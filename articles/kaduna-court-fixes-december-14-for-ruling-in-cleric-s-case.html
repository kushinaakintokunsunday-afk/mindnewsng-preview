Kaduna court fixes December 14 for ruling in cleric’s case

By Vanguard Vanguard 16 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG

By Ibrahim Hassan-Wuyo

KADUNA — An Upper Sharia Court sitting along Daura Road, Kaduna State, has fixed December 14, 2026, for ruling in a case involving Islamic cleric, Sheikh Sunusi Khalil, over allegations of sexual relations outside marriage and threats against a woman who accused him of fathering her pregnancy.

The court fixed the date on Monday, November 16, after the presiding judge, Justice Auwal Abdullahi, said the ruling had not been brought to his attention and was not on his schedule.

“The ruling was not brought to my notice. It is not in my diary,” the judge said.

The new date was subsequently secured by the defendant’s lead counsel, Abass Machika. Counsel to the complainant, Hafsat Uwa Usman, was reportedly absent when the date was fixed.

The case followed a direct criminal complaint filed by Usman on September 21, 2026, in which she accused the cleric of engaging in sexual relations with her outside marriage, resulting in pregnancy, and subsequently threatening her life when she declined to terminate it.

In her complaint, Usman alleged that she first met the defendant in 2024 around Kawo Bus Stop, Kaduna, where he reportedly approached her through one of his aides and introduced himself as an Islamic cleric.

She claimed their initial discussions centred on religion, dressing and general conduct before the relationship allegedly developed into a romantic and intimate one.

The complainant, who said she was formerly known as Faith Usman and practised Christianity, alleged that the cleric encouraged her to embrace Islam and promised to provide for her needs if she agreed to an intimate relationship.

She said she converted to Islam in early 2025 at Juma’at Mosque, Rigachikun, Kaduna, where she adopted the name Hafsat Uwa Usman.

According to her, the defendant’s religious standing and the circumstances surrounding her conversion contributed to the trust she placed in him.

She further alleged that their relationship became intimate and resulted in sexual intercourse without a valid Islamic marriage.

Usman maintained that no wali, mahr, marriage witnesses or formal solemnisation of nikah took place between them.

She also claimed that she had no sexual relationship with another man during the period the pregnancy was conceived and attributed the pregnancy to the cleric.

On the alleged threat, the complainant stated that Khalil visited her residence in Millennium City, Kaduna, sometime in August 2026, ostensibly to discuss the pregnancy.

She alleged that during the visit, he asked her to terminate the pregnancy and threatened to kill her when she refused.

Usman said the alleged threat made her fear for her life and the safety of her unborn child.

She further accused the cleric of subsequently withdrawing from the relationship, avoiding her telephone calls and failing to provide financial, medical or other assistance during the pregnancy.

In her complaint, she expressed readiness to present telephone records, messages and other available evidence in support of her allegations. She also indicated willingness to undergo a lawful DNA examination with the child after birth to establish paternity.

The complainant urged the court to take cognisance of the allegations and issue a criminal summons against the defendant.

She cited alleged offences of zina under Section 121 of the Sharia Penal Code Law of Kaduna State, 2002, and criminal intimidation under Sections 232 and 233 of the same law.

The court is expected to deliver its ruling on December 14.

However, the allegations against Sheikh Sunusi Khalil remain unproven, and the court has yet to determine the merits of the complaint.

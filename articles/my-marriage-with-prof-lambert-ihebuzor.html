My marriage… With Prof Lambert Ihebuzor

How did you meet your wife?

I went to my village, and I was coming out of my father’s house and heading to my own house when I saw a young woman passing by. I approached her, and it turned out that she was the daughter of a man who was the secretary to the Anglican Bishop. She had come to visit her aunt from Plateau State. That was how we became friends. She went back to Jos, while I returned to Ilorin as a corps member. Since 1989, when I married Oluchuckwu, we have remained friends. God has blessed our marriage with four children, two boys and two girls.

What qualities attracted you to her?

She is homely and generally a pretty person. She is a fantastic cook. I like homemade food. I don’t eat out. I also like the way she acclimatises herself. She was an Anglican, while I am a Catholic, but she is now a leader of women in the Catholic Church. She is not from my place, but now she speaks the language better than some people who are from there.

How did you ask her out?

I just told her straightforwardly that she was going to become my friend and that I was going to marry her. That was it. That day, I followed her to her aunt’s place. Her aunt then told her who I was. My mother was a women’s leader, and her aunt knew her. I think her aunt did the rest of the work; she encouraged her. I don’t think there was any special asking out like we have people do today because three days after we met, we went to our various locations. When we came back, my father was already making arrangements because he wanted us to marry before we attained the age of 28. At that time, I was 28, and she was 23. My father helped, and then we got married.

What event on your wedding day was most memorable?

But I was happy that both my parents were alive to witness my wedding, which we had here in Ibadan.

How has being a father and a husband changed you?

It has humbled me because this thing called parenting can humble an adult. There are challenges in marriage, but so far, so good; I am grateful to God. God has also saved me at precarious times.

What is your advice to youths planning to get married?

Look properly before you leap. Marriage is not a bed of roses, but you can make it comfortable for you to sleep on. And it is not a picnic. Sometimes, your wife can come up with a particular attitude, and you would wonder where it is coming from. Don’t bother. They are all the same. My mother did it to my father. The thing is, when they do that kind of thing to you, you just smile and move on, remembering the fact that this is a woman and she has to behave like one. If she is not behaving like a woman, then something is wrong, and you have to investigate what is wrong. Also, this thing they say about marrying an entire family when you marry someone is true. Be married to your wife and watch her siblings carefully. Don’t draw a line of battle, but be careful because some of them may not be your friends.

Oyebanji signs three laws, revises Ekiti ₦415.5bn budget

By Rotimi Ojomoyela

ADO-EKITI – Ekiti State Governor, Biodun Oyebanji, has signed three new laws, including the 2026 Revised Appropriation Law, the Pension Reform (Second Amendment) Law and the Right of Way Law.

The laws were presented to the governor for assent in his office in Ado-Ekiti by the Speaker of the State House of Assembly, Rt. Hon. Adeoye Aribasoye, following their passage by the legislature.

The 2026 Revised Appropriation Law retains the state’s total budget at ₦415.57 billion but adjusts the allocation between recurrent and capital expenditure.

Under the revised budget, recurrent expenditure increases from ₦221.87 billion to ₦268.76 billion, while capital expenditure decreases from ₦193.69 billion to ₦146.80 billion.

The adjustment represents a reallocation of funds from capital to recurrent expenditure to accommodate the state’s financial and operational obligations without increasing the overall budget approved in December 2025.

The revised appropriation is expected to sustain government operations while maintaining funding for infrastructure, social development, economic growth and institutional efficiency.

The second legislation, the Ekiti State Pension Reform (Second Amendment) Law, 2026 (No. 4 of 2026), amends the existing Pension Reform Law No. 3 of 2022 to formally accommodate the designation of a Director-General alongside the Permanent Secretary in the administrative and governance structure of the commission responsible for the Contributory Pension Scheme in the state.

The amendment also makes consequential adjustments to relevant provisions of the principal law, including the composition of the commission’s board, staff administration, remuneration and allowances.

The third legislation, the Ekiti State Right of Way Law, 2026 (No. 5 of 2026), establishes a regulatory framework for approving and administering permits for the installation and maintenance of telecommunications infrastructure in the state.

The law fixes the maximum Right of Way fee at ₦145 per linear metre for ducts and cables and ₦700,000 per site for a Base Transceiver Station.

It also requires telecommunications operators to comply with approved engineering standards, protect public safety and the environment, minimise damage to roads and public utilities, and restore affected infrastructure after installation.

The signing of the three laws underscores the Oyebanji administration’s efforts to strengthen public institutions, improve the regulatory environment for infrastructure development and ensure effective management of public resources.

Reforms unlock household credit but borrowing costs high – Experts

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Banking and finance experts have said that the Central Bank of Nigeria’s reforms, particularly the banking sector recapitalisation exercise, have begun strength

They said the improvement reflected stronger bank balance sheets, rising liquidity and increased financial inclusion, although the high cost of borrowing remained a major obstacle to the full benefits of expanding credit.

The experts spoke against the backdrop of the CBN’s Q3 2026 Credit Conditions Survey, which showed increased demand for secured, unsecured and corporate loans, alongside higher approval rates and lower default rates across major lending categories.

The survey showed that the demand index for secured lending stood at 25.6, driven largely by consumer loans to households, which recorded 42.9, and credit for house purchases at 28.0. Mortgage and remortgage lending recorded 12.0, while lending to small businesses by households declined to -19.7.

Speaking with The PUNCH, the Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of Optimus Bank, Dr Ademola Odeyemi, said banks had increased lending following the recapitalisation exercise, noting that stronger capital positions would enable lenders to expand credit and improve profitability.

“Lending has actually picked up truly because banks raised capital, and capital can only be utilised profitably by lending. If you don’t lend, you don’t make money. So, lending is actually, truly picking up,” Odeyemi said.

He, however, stressed the need for a supportive economic environment that would enable borrowers to deploy credit productively and repay their loans.

“The important thing is to have a very good environment through which customers will effectively use that money. If the economy is not conducive, the loan will go bad. So, we need a very workable economy, we need a growing economy, we need a conducive economy where customers can borrow effectively, use the money effectively, and be able to pay back,” he said.

Odeyemi warned that a weak business environment could trigger another wave of bad loans, undermining the gains recorded in the banking sector.

He also commended the CBN Governor, Yemi Cardoso, for restoring confidence in the financial system through reforms and stronger regulatory supervision.

“The recapitalisation exercise was also very successful because almost every bank succeeded in getting it. So, which was also a major challenge that people have overcome. So, right now banks are well capitalised, and again, the level of supervision is quite high,” he said.

He added that sustaining the reforms would strengthen the banking industry and provide the financial foundation needed to support economic growth.

Similarly, the Chief Executive Officer of Economic Associates, Dr Ayo Teriba, said Nigeria was experiencing a liquidity expansion that was beginning to spread beyond the foreign exchange and capital markets into bank lending to households and businesses.

In a phone interview, Teriba attributed the development to the impact of economic reforms and the appreciation of the naira, arguing that the resulting improvement in liquidity was beginning to translate into increased access to credit.

He said, “There are four liquidity buckets: there’s foreign reserves, there’s market cap, there’s the bond market, and there’s the banking system. And the point is that in past tides, you’ve seen the rising tide in 2006 to 2007. A rising tide of reserves, a rising tide of market cap, there’s a rising tide of banking loans.”

The economist noted that the current expansion in liquidity is increasingly reaching households and businesses through higher bank lending, unlike previous periods when stock market gains largely benefited listed companies and investors.

Teriba maintained that the growing availability of credit would support business expansion, employment and household incomes, while predicting that borrowing costs would eventually decline to single-digit levels.

He added that the current recovery differed from the liquidity expansion recorded in 2006 and 2007 because the latest improvements stemmed from domestic policy reforms rather than favourable international oil prices.

Teriba added that the reforms had helped strengthen the naira, moderate inflation and improve liquidity across the economy, creating conditions for a more sustained expansion in lending.

Also, the Founder of the Centre for the Promotion of Private Enterprise, Dr Muda Yusuf, attributed the improvement in household credit partly to the expansion of financial inclusion, particularly through financial technology companies and digital lending platforms.

He said fintech companies and commercial banks had widened access to consumer credit through small loans and overdraft facilities, helping households meet spending needs and supporting demand for goods and services.

“The level of financial inclusion in the last few years, driven largely by the fintechs, has led to an increase in consumer credit, and that has made a lot of difference in terms of consumer credit,” Yusuf said.

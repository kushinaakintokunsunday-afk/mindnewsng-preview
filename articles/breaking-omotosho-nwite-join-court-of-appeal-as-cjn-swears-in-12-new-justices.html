BREAKING: Omotosho, Nwite join Court of Appeal as CJN swears In 12 new Justices

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 17 Nov 2026 3 min read

The Chief Justice of Nigeria (CJN), Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, has sworn in 12 new Justices of the Court of Appeal, including two Federal High Court judges who handled several high-profile criminal cases.

The newly elevated judges include Justices James Omotosho and Emeka Nwite, whose appointments were recommended by the National Judicial Council (NJC) at its meeting on May 13.

Justice Omotosho convicted and sentenced the detained leader of the proscribed Indigenous People of Biafra (IPOB), Nnamdi Kanu, to life imprisonment on November 20, 2025, after finding him guilty on a seven-count terrorism-related charge filed by the Federal Government.

Similarly, Justice Nwite handled the N80.2bn fraud case involving former Kogi State Governor, Yahaya Bello.

He also presided over the trial of four members of the Al-Shabaab terrorist group, whom he sentenced to death by hanging on June 3 for their roles in the June 5, 2022 attack on St. Francis Catholic Church in Owo, Ondo State.

The other judges sworn in by the CJN on Tuesday are Justices Yakubu Abdulhammeed Mohammed, Abodunde Monisola Oluwatoyin, Ajuwa Raphael, Abua Elias Ojie, Ijohor Mbalamen Jennifer, Shuaibu Sabiu Bala, Buba Dauda Njane, Kado Sanusi, Ademola Enikuomehin and Dadom Julcit Veronica.

Following their elevation, criminal cases previously handled by the judges, including the trial of former Governor Bello, are expected to be reassigned to other judges and heard afresh, a process known as de novo.

Speaking at the swearing-in ceremony, Kekere-Ekun said the appointments brought together judicial experience from different parts of the country and courts with distinct jurisdictions and responsibilities.

She disclosed that some of the new appellate court justices were selected from Gombe, Bayelsa, Cross River, Benue, Kebbi, Taraba and Ondo, following their service at various High Courts, the National Industrial Court and the Customary Courts of Appeal in Ekiti and Plateau states.

The CJN urged the new justices to understand that their responsibilities at the Court of Appeal would differ from their previous roles, as they would now sit in panels, deliberate collectively and reach decisions through judicial engagement.

She said their duties extended beyond affirming or setting aside decisions of lower courts, stressing that they were expected to promote consistency in the application of the law, clarify legal principles, correct judicial errors and contribute to the development of Nigeria’s jurisprudence.

Kekere-Ekun cautioned them against treating appeals as opportunities to rehear cases simply because they might have reached different conclusions from the judges of lower courts.

She advised them to carefully examine the grounds of appeal, records of proceedings, findings and reasoning of the lower courts before deciding whether there were sufficient legal grounds to intervene.

The CJN also stressed the need for caution when reviewing findings of fact and the evaluation of evidence, particularly in cases where appellate judges did not have the opportunity to observe witnesses.

She urged the new justices to ensure that their judgments were clear, well-reasoned and guided strictly by the law, regardless of public opinion or criticism.

Kekere-Ekun further warned against political, financial, social and personal pressures, including those arising from professional relationships and familiarity.

She charged them to protect their judicial independence and conduct themselves in ways that would leave no reasonable doubt about their impartiality.

“The law must be applied consistently, fairly and without prejudice,” the CJN told the new appellate court justices.

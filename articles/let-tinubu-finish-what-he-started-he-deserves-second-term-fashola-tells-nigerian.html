‘Let Tinubu finish what he started, He deserves Second Term’ — Fashola tells Nigerians

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: VeryNigerian

Former Minister of Works and Housing, Babatunde Fashola, has defended President Bola Tinubu’s re-election bid, arguing that the President deserves a second term

Former Minister of Works and Housing, Babatunde Fashola, has defended President Bola Tinubu’s re-election bid, arguing that the President deserves a second term to complete the projects and reforms initiated by his administration.

Fashola made the remarks on Tuesday during an interview on Channels Television’s Politics Today, where he was asked to assess Tinubu’s performance, economic reforms and prospects ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The former Lagos State governor said his close working relationship with Tinubu gave him firsthand knowledge of the President’s leadership style, resilience and capacity to govern.

According to him, he worked with Tinubu for several years, including four and a half years as his Chief of Staff, spending at least 14 hours daily working closely with him.

“I’ve worked with him. And I was his chief of staff for four and a half years. So, I worked with him on a minimum basis of over 14 hours a day. So, I saw him at close quarters,” Fashola said.

He described resilience, intelligence and determination as some of Tinubu’s major strengths, arguing that the President had demonstrated the ability to remain focused despite criticism and political challenges.

Fashola also defended the administration’s economic policies, particularly the removal of petrol subsidy and the unification of the foreign exchange rates, saying the decisions were necessary to address longstanding economic challenges.

He argued that although the reforms had generated difficulties for Nigerians, the country could have faced a worse economic situation without them.

The former minister maintained that the policies needed sufficient time to translate into the desired economic benefits, insisting that Tinubu should be allowed another four years to consolidate the ongoing reforms.

“Governance is not about sainthood, it’s about efficiency, it’s about performance, it’s about delivery,” he said.

To support his argument, Fashola referred to Tinubu’s eight-year administration as governor of Lagos State between 1999 and 2007.

He claimed that Tinubu increased the state’s Internally Generated Revenue from about N600 million monthly to more than N7 billion, while laying the foundation for reforms that influenced governance in other parts of the country.

Fashola also highlighted some of the major projects and initiatives introduced under Tinubu’s administration, including the Lagos-Calabar Coastal Highway, the Sokoto-Badagry Superhighway, the Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND) and ongoing housing and infrastructure programmes across the states.

He urged Nigerians to assess the administration based on its policies and measurable achievements rather than what he described as propaganda.

“You underestimate his resilience and intelligence at your own peril,” Fashola added.

On the 2027 presidential election, the former governor argued that changing the country’s political arrangement midway through an eight-year tenure would be inappropriate.

He noted that Tinubu had overcome several challenges throughout his political career, suggesting that his emergence and continued political journey could serve a greater purpose.

Fashola further urged Nigerians to allow Tinubu to complete his constitutionally permitted second term, arguing that the South should be allowed to complete its eight-year presidential tenure, just as the North did under the late President Muhammadu Buhari.

“Let President Tinubu finish what he started. That is why he deserves a second term,” he said.

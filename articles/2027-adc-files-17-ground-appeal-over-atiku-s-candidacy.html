2027: ADC files 17-ground appeal over Atiku’s candidacy

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 18 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has filed a 17-ground appeal against the Federal High Court judgment on its leadership dispute, asking the Court of Appeal

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has filed a 17-ground appeal against the Federal High Court judgment on its leadership dispute, asking the Court of Appeal to restrain the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) from altering its leadership records and the nomination particulars of its presidential and governorship candidates pending determination of the appeal.

The party is challenging the November 5, 2026 judgment of Justice Peter Lifu, describing it as fundamentally flawed and arguing that the trial court lacked jurisdiction to entertain the suit.

In a statement on Tuesday by the Director of Strategic Communication of the ADC Campaign Council, Phrank Shaibu, the party said it was also challenging an earlier ruling granting Bala Gombe leave to amend his originating summons while its objection to the court’s jurisdiction remained unresolved.

The ADC argued that allowing INEC to implement the judgment before the appeal is determined could undermine the legal challenge and create uncertainty for its candidates ahead of the 2027 general elections.

It urged the appellate court to preserve its existing leadership structure and candidate records pending the resolution of the dispute.

Shaibu said the 17 grounds of appeal challenged the jurisdiction of the Federal High Court, Gombe’s standing to institute the suit, the assessment of disputed evidence and the orders made against the party.

“The 17 grounds challenge the foundation, findings and orders of Justice Lifu’s judgment.

“The ADC has asked the Court of Appeal to examine whether the trial court had jurisdiction, whether the claimant had standing, whether disputed evidence was properly assessed, and whether the sweeping orders made against the party can stand in law,” he said.

The party is also appealing the July 24, 2026 ruling in a separate case, arguing that the trial court allowed Gombe to amend his originating summons while deferring its decision on the ADC’s preliminary objection challenging jurisdiction.

According to the statement, the party contends that the amendment altered the description of parties and the capacity in which a defendant was sued without prior leave of court.

The appellant’s brief challenging the ruling was prepared by Chief J.S. Okutepa (SAN), who leads the party’s legal team in the appeal.

The ADC is asking the Court of Appeal to set aside the amendment order and determine the outstanding objection to the trial court’s jurisdiction.

It further argued that Justice Lifu proceeded to deliver judgment on a court process whose validity was already being challenged before the appellate court.

The party expressed concern that INEC could implement the judgment before the appeal is decided, potentially creating a situation that could be difficult to reverse should the appellate court rule in its favour.

“INEC must not make changes that could preempt the court’s decision or put the party’s candidates in jeopardy while our applications are pending.

“We are asking the court to protect the subject of the appeal so that its eventual decision has practical effect,” Shaibu said.

The ADC said it made the legal challenge public to clarify the issues before the appellate court and reassure its members and supporters that it would pursue the dispute through lawful judicial processes.

The dispute centres on the party’s leadership and the validity of the processes through which its leadership records and related affairs are being contested.

Justice Lifu’s November 5 judgment in the suit filed by Gombe has thrown the party’s leadership structure and preparations for the 2027 general elections into fresh legal uncertainty.

The ADC maintained that earlier court decisions recognising its leadership transition remained valid and had not been set aside, insisting that no administrative changes should be made on the basis of a contested judgment while its appeals were pending.

The party’s appeals and applications are before the Court of Appeal for determination.

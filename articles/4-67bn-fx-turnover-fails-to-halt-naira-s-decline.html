$4.67bn FX turnover fails to halt naira’s decline

By Punch Punch 17h ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria’s foreign exchange market recorded $4.67bn in transactions last week, but the increased trading activity failed to halt the naira’s depreciation, exposi

The turnover, the second-highest weekly level recorded in 2026, increased by 36.59 per cent from $3.42bn in the preceding week, according to FMDQ data.

However, the naira depreciated by 2.50 per cent to close at N1,394 per dollar, its weakest level in eight months, suggesting that higher transaction volumes were not translating into sufficient dollar supply to stabilise the currency.

Cordros Securities said foreign portfolio investor demand outweighed the CBN’s reported $1.30bn intervention during the week, indicating the extent of demand pressure in the FX market.

Spot transactions accounted for $4.64bn, or 99.40 per cent, of total turnover, representing an increase of 43.08 per cent from the preceding week.

Forward transactions, meanwhile, fell by 83.88 per cent to $28.19m, leaving them with just 0.60 per cent of total turnover.

The sharp increase in spot transactions pushed average daily turnover to $934.50m from $684.18m a week earlier.

Despite the increase in market activity, the naira weakened from N1,360 per dollar on Monday to N1,394 on Friday, while the parallel market rate depreciated by 2.33 per cent to N1,458 per dollar.

The one-month forward rate also weakened by 2.7 per cent to N1,414.99, while the three-month contract depreciated by 2.7 per cent to N1,451.81.

The six-month forward rate fell by 2.1 per cent to N1,495.98, while the one-year contract weakened by 2.0 per cent to N1,597.57 per dollar.

The figures suggest that increased trading activity alone may not be sufficient to ease pressure on the naira, particularly as FX demand continues to outpace available supply.

However, FMDQ’s turnover data do not establish whether the increase in transactions was driven by additional dollar supply or stronger demand, making it difficult to determine the extent to which market liquidity improved.

Daily interbank turnover at the CBN’s Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market window rose from $68.66m on Monday to $815.57m on Friday, while calculations based on daily CBN figures showed approximately $2.55bn in turnover during the week.

The figures differ from FMDQ’s $4.67bn because they reflect different reporting scopes and should not be combined.

Cordros Securities expects FX demand pressures to persist through the remainder of the year, citing weaker net foreign inflows and investor caution ahead of the 2027 general elections.

the Chief Executive Officer of the Centre for Promotion of Private Enterprise, Dr Muda Yusuf, said domestic factors could be contributing to the successive decline of the naira against the dollar.

The economist identified political spending, capital flight and policy uncertainty as possible factors behind the weakening currency and declining foreign reserves.

He said political spending could inject liquidity into the economy and put pressure on the naira, noting that the Central Bank of Nigeria had previously raised concerns about the impact of political activities on exchange rate stability.

“The political spending liquidity may be already putting pressure on the naira. The CBN had expressed that fear before during the latest Monetary Policy Committee meeting,” Yusuf said.

He argued that foreign exchange demand associated with political activities could be channelled through the parallel market because such transactions might not be formally documented or conducted through official channels.

Yusuf also warned that political uncertainty and statements by some politicians about reversing the economic reforms of the President Bola Tinubu administration could trigger capital flight.

An emerging markets expert and economist, Ike Ibeabuchi, attributed the weakening naira and declining reserves to reduced dollar supply and rising demand for foreign exchange.

He said lower inflows from exporters, investors and other sources might be reducing dollar availability in the market, while demand for foreign currency to finance imports and international transactions continued to exert pressure on the naira.

“Lower foreign exchange inflows from exporters, investors and other sources reduce dollar supply in the market, while demand for foreign currency for imports, international payments and other transactions continues to exert pressure on the naira,” Ibeabuchi said.

He said the festive season typically increases demand for foreign exchange, while the influence of election-related spending on the currency could be greater than initially anticipated.

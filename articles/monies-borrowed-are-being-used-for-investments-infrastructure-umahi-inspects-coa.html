‘Monies Borrowed Are Being Used For Investments, Infrastructure’, Umahi Inspects Coastal Road Bridges

The Minister for Works and Infrastructure, David Umahi, has defended the external borrowings of President Bola Tinubu, saying the monies are being invested in the building of infrastructure across the country.

Umahi revealed this as he inspected the Coastal Road Bridges on Sunday.

According to him, the road projects are not the only investments President Tinubu is spending the borrowed monies on, as the Federal Government also has the vision of investing in housing for rentals.

“As far back as six months, section one was already fully completed. Section two, which is about 53 kilometers, is also already completed. It has road architecture such as solar lights, underground drainage, and the walkway.

“So, it’s something that is out of this world in terms of performance, where you have over 200 kilometres plus being done within two and a half years. It’s a performance that no other contractor in Africa has been able to achieve.

“I have bragging rights as the Minister of Works over these projects because I am very proud of them. I have bragging rights for the President because when you are on this coastal way, it is quite relieving.

“Imagine if we can go all the way from Lagos to Calabar on this kind of road. All these roads are investments of President Bola Ahmed Tinubu. And when you ask about the monies which are being borrowed. They are being used for infrastructure. They are being used for investment in infrastructure.

“And investors are asking how they can return the investments on this road. But we are still considering their request because we want to install CCTV, we want to rent houses. We want to buy land where we can have a lot of facilities for tourism, factories, and others.

“And these projects are profit-driven for the Federal Republic of Nigeria.

This is as President Tinubu has asked the House of Representatives to authorise FG to access $7.71bn (N10.79 trillion) in external loans already captured in the 2026 Appropriation Act, with Eurobond issuance expected to be a major funding option.

The request was contained in a letter to the Speaker of the House, Tajudeen Abbas, and was read during last Wednesday’s plenary.

Tinubu said the proposed borrowing would help finance part of the Federal Government’s budget deficit and fund projects across critical sectors, including defence, transportation, agriculture, power, education and health.

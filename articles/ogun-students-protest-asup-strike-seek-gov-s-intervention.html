Ogun students protest ASUP strike, seek gov’s intervention

By Punch Punch 4d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Students of polytechnics across Ogun State on Thursday staged a peaceful protest, mounting fresh pressure on Governor Dapo Abiodun to urgently resolve the linge

The protest, organised under the auspices of the National Association of Polytechnic Students, followed what the students described as the government’s failure to respond decisively to repeated appeals and ultimatums issued to secure the reopening of their institutions.

The students, who marched in their hundreds to the Governor’s Office, Oke Mosan, on Thursday, carried placards bearing various messages, including, “Gov Dapo Abiodun, Pls end ASUP strike”, “Gov Abiodun, save polytechnic education now”, “Our lecturers must return to classroom” and “Attend to our lecturers demand”, among others.

Speaking during the demonstration, the NAPS Chairman, Ogun State Liaison Council, Comrade Joshua Opatunmibi, popularly known as Lush, said the students had exhausted diplomatic channels and were no longer prepared to accept repeated assurances without corresponding action.

Opatunmibi maintained that the protest was not intended to create hostility between the students and the state government but to make their frustrations known and demand accountability over the prolonged disruption of their academic calendar.

The NAPS leader stressed that the continued closure of the institutions was taking a heavy toll on students, whose academic programmes and future plans had been put on hold, warning that the government must demonstrate the political will to bring the situation to an end.

He explained that, “On November 12th, we issued a 48 hours ultimatum to the government appealing for the quick intervention of the Gov Dapo Abiodun to help end the ongoing ASUP strike.

“The 48 hours elapsed and there was nothing done. So, on Monday again, we had another press conference and begged the government to come to our aid over this strike which has stalled our education and we gave another 48 hours ultimatum.

“The 48 hours expired yesterday, Wednesday afternoon. We are really not happy that the government has done nothing over our request. Is it that the government doesn’t care about our grievances or what? We are definitely not happy because this closure of all the polytechnics in the state is affecting us.

“So, we are here to show that our voice matters. It is not that we are fighting the government but we are saying that the matters involved should be resolved.

“We can’t continue to put our future in jeopardy, we call on Gov Abiodun to please attend to the demands of our lecturers so that we can all go back to our various schools to complete our education”.

Also speaking, Kolade Musbau, a NAPS member, decried the 10-day-old strike, saying that the students were unhappy that the government had not demonstrated serious commitment to ending the industrial action and allowing them to resume their studies.

Musbau said, “The students are not happy that when they are supposed to be in school they are at home wasting their time because of this ASUP strike and despite repeated appeal, it seems government is not ready to do the needful.

“So, we are left with no choice than to embark on this protest to draw government’s attention to our plight and demand urgent intervention to end this strike. We are all eager to go back to our schools and complete our studies within the record time. We hereby beg Gov Abiodun to please end this strike without further delay”.

Speaking on behalf of the state government, the Permanent Secretary, Bureau of Cabinet and Special Services, Mr Dolamu Idowu-Agida, assured the students that the government was already taking steps to resolve the disagreement between it and the lecturers.

Idowu-Agida disclosed that the government had held two meetings with relevant stakeholders as part of efforts to address the issues surrounding the strike. He added that approval had also been granted for an increase in subvention to all the polytechnics in the state, expressing hope that the strike would be called off within the next 48 hours.

He explained that, “I want to tell you that the State Government has held meetings with relevant stakeholders on this matter, twice. So government is seriously addressing your concerns. As at yesterday, approval has been granted for an increase of subvention to all polytechnics.

“The issue of your lecturers is being given serious consideration. I want to assure you. I want to assure you that very soon the matter will be resolved. And your lecturers will go back to classes. Be rest assured that your lecturers should be back to classrooms within the next 48 hours”.

It would be recalled that lecturers in the six state-owned polytechnics embarked on an indefinite strike last Wednesday following the expiration of the 15-day ultimatum given to the state government to address their concerns over the non-implementation of the Consolidated Academic Tools Allowance and Earned Academic Allowance.

The union also lamented the inadequate funding of polytechnic education, saying the government had refused to review the N5m monthly subvention given to some of the state-owned polytechnics for more than a decade.

The union had, among other demands, urged the government to pay the outstanding CATA and EAA allowances and to “immediately review and increase the monthly subvention of all the state-owned polytechnics to ₦100 million to reflect current economic realities and the operational needs of these institutions”.

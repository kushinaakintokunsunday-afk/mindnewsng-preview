Tinubu’s pardon of convoy attack suspects divides opposition

By Punch Punch 2d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Opposition parties have expressed divergent views over President Bola Tinubu’s decision to grant clemency to 78 suspects arrested over the alleged attack on his

While some political parties condemned the incident and demanded prosecution, others faulted the President’s intervention, describing it as politically motivated and a missed opportunity to investigate the circumstances surrounding the incident.

The parties stated their positions in separate interviews with Sunday PUNCH on Friday, following an attack on Tinubu’s motorcade shortly after he observed Jumu’ah prayers at the Shehu Shagari Central Mosque in Abuja’s Garki District on November 13.

The incident initially generated conflicting accounts, with the Presidency disputing reports that the President’s convoy was attacked.

Tinubu’s Senior Special Assistant on Digital Engagement, Strategy, and New Media, O’tega Ogra, had dismissed the claims, describing them as an opposition narrative.

However, the Federal Capital Territory Police Command confirmed the incident on November 16, saying 78 suspects had been arrested following intelligence-led operations.

Police spokesperson for the FCT command, Josephine Adeh, said the suspects were being processed for release after the President extended clemency to them and directed that they be warned and freed.

Commenting on the development, the National Publicity Secretary of the Social Democratic Party, Rufus Aiyenigba, described the attack as unacceptable and an assault on democracy, saying those responsible for promoting political violence should be identified and prosecuted.

Aiyenigba said although the incident reflected growing public discontent over poverty and insecurity, citizens had a responsibility to respect the office of the President.

“The SDP condemns, in very strong terms, the recent attack on the convoy of President Tinubu. The party considers the incident, which was carried out by those unruly fellows, unacceptable, and it is considered not only a national embarrassment, but a major assault on our democracy.

“Although it undeniably speaks to the growing public discontent, particularly regarding the high and pervasive poverty level, and unabating insecurity that have enveloped a greater part of the nation, nonetheless, Nigerian citizens have a duty to protect and respect the office of our President, which is the symbol of the nation.

“Individuals and political figures who are found to be culpable in the act of promoting election violence have to be identified and prosecuted to serve as the much-needed deterrence. Crime and criminality in any form must be met with appropriate punitive measures,” he said.

Aiyenigba warned against the growing culture of political intimidation, threats, and violence, particularly as parties began preparations for the 2027 general elections.

He said political actors must promote peaceful campaigns and electoral processes, adding that the SDP believed political parties should seek power through popular support rather than violence.

The party called for stronger legislation and stiff sanctions against political actors and parties found culpable in promoting violence.

“Our democracy must be truly democratic in form and practice and be safeguarded. Nigerian people must have the freedom to exercise their fundamental rights, free of any form of intimidation or harassment. That is what guarantees the survival of our democracy and enhances national stability, particularly as we enter the 2027 general elections,” he said.

However, the National Publicity Secretary of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, Osa Director, questioned the circumstances surrounding the arrests and subsequent release of the suspects, accusing the ruling All Progressives Congress of double standards in its handling of attacks involving opposition figures.

Director said NDC was surprised that the APC had acknowledged the incident through the arrests after initially disputing reports that Tinubu’s convoy was attacked.

“It is surprising that the APC is claiming that they arrested some hoodlums who have now been set free on the order of the President. Was it not the same APC that said there was no incident where the President was pelted with stones by hungry Nigerians over his non-performance?

“When will APC ever learn to tell the truth and play straight? Why is it that it was only when the President’s convoy was stoned that they are suddenly arresting people? Peter Obi and the NDC have been attacked several times, but to date no one has been arrested. Atiku was attacked in Sokoto, but nobody was arrested.

“This double standard is uncalled for in a democracy,” he said.

Similarly, the Labour Party National Publicity Secretary, Ken Asogwa, questioned the legal basis for the President’s intervention, arguing that the decision to release the suspects could undermine the investigation.

Asogwa queried whether the President could exercise his constitutional power of pardon over suspects who had not been charged and convicted by a court.

“There are two salient questions to be asked here. First, does the President have the power to grant a pardon or clemency to someone who has not been charged and convicted by a court?

“Section 175 of the 1999 Constitution (as amended), which confers the power of prerogative of mercy or pardon on the President, as well as established legal practice and judicial interpretation, contemplates clemency as a post-conviction measure.

“So, the action of Mr President in ambushing the police investigation through his hasty pardon is not helpful to the intelligence community,” he said.

The LP spokesman also questioned the motive behind the attack, saying the police investigation could have provided answers to the circumstances surrounding the incident.

“Secondly, we need to understand why someone would suddenly defy the paraphernalia of security surrounding the President and Commander-in-Chief and decide to embark on a suicide mission by attacking the President’s convoy with stones.

“Is this a suicide mission borne out of frustration and despair? The investigation should have been able to reveal some of these motives. Unfortunately, the President has thwarted that opportunity.

“In all, I enjoin the President to engage in deep introspection and soul-searching. Why would someone decide to stone the convoy of the President of Nigeria in Abuja, not elsewhere? That is the deeper question,” he said.

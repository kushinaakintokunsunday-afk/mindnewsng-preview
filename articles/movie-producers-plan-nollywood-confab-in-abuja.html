Movie producers plan Nollywood confab in Abuja

From Fred Itua, Abuja

The Association of Movie Producers (AMP), Federal Capital Territory Chapter, has announced plans to host the Nollywood Film Conference and Awards, a national gathering aimed at exploring the strategic role of Nigeria’s film industry in promoting national security, peacebuilding, social cohesion and sustainable development.

The conference, scheduled for November 27, 2026 in Abuja, will be held under the theme, “Film for National Security and Sustainable Development,” with the sub-theme, “Harnessing Nollywood’s Creative Power for Peace, National Re-orientation and Sustainable Development.”

The Chairman of the Association of Movie Producers, FCT Chapter, Desmond Utomwen, made this known during an interview with journalists in Abuja, explaining that the conference would bring together filmmakers, government officials, security agencies, development organisations, investors, broadcasters and other stakeholders to explore opportunities for deploying cinema as an instrument of national transformation.

Utomwen said the initiative was informed by the growing recognition of film as a powerful medium for public enlightenment, behavioural change, social mobilisation and the promotion of responsible citizenship.

He observed that Nigeria’s security challenges require multidimensional approaches extending beyond conventional security operations to include strategic communication, youth empowerment, civic education, community engagement and national re-orientation.

According to him, Nollywood possesses enormous potential to complement these efforts through compelling storytelling that challenges harmful social practices, promotes tolerance and encourages peaceful coexistence.

“Every film carries the possibility of influencing how people see themselves, their neighbours and their country. Our responsibility as filmmakers is to recognise that influence and apply it with creativity, integrity and care. With this conference, we want to move the conversation towards practical partnerships. We want to examine how compelling Nigerian stories can support peace, strengthen public understanding, create livelihoods and help communities confront the pressures that threaten their security and wellbeing.” Utomwen stated.

He identified violent extremism, human trafficking, irregular migration, gender-based violence, drug abuse and youth involvement in criminal activities as critical issues that could be addressed through purposeful filmmaking.

Utomwen noted that cinema could also deepen public understanding of the experiences of security personnel, survivors of violence, internally displaced persons and communities affected by insecurity. The AMP-FCT Chairman further emphasised the relationship between national security and sustainable development, noting that a vibrant film industry could generate employment, stimulate entrepreneurship and contribute significantly to economic diversification.

He explained that Nollywood’s value chain provides opportunities across acting, directing, cinematography, screenwriting, animation, editing, sound engineering, costume design, logistics, hospitality and other production-related services.

According to him, the conference would examine film financing, production infrastructure, professional standards, technical capacity development, distribution, emerging technologies and institutional partnerships necessary to strengthen the industry’s contribution to national development.

Utomwen disclosed that the awards component would recognise outstanding contributions to filmmaking, celebrate professional excellence and encourage creativity, innovation and socially responsible storytelling. He stressed that collaboration between filmmakers and public institutions must respect artistic independence, factual accuracy, human dignity and professional ethics.

The AMP-FCT Chairman consequently called on government institutions, security agencies, corporate organisations, development partners, investors and industry practitioners to support and participate in the conference.

“Nollywood must be recognised beyond entertainment as a strategic contributor to national development, cultural diplomacy, economic empowerment and social transformation.

“Film can help a nation understand its challenges, imagine solutions and mobilise the will to act. This conference seeks to turn that potential into purposeful collaboration and lasting opportunities for Nigerians,” Utomwen declared.

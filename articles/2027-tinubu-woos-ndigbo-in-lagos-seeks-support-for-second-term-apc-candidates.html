2027: Tinubu woos Ndigbo in Lagos, seeks support for second term, APC candidates

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 1d ago 3 min read

President Bola Tinubu has appealed to members of the Igbo community in Lagos State, market leaders and traders to support his re-election bid in 2027, urging them to vote for the All Progressives Congress (APC) and its candidates across the country.

Tinubu made the appeal on Monday while addressing the Ndigbo Lagos State Town Hall, organised by the Ndigbo National Dialogue and Development Initiative (NNDDI) at the Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and Creative Arts, formerly National Theatre, Iganmu, Lagos.

The President, who adopted a conciliatory tone in his engagement with the Igbo community, acknowledged that a significant number of Ndigbo in Lagos did not support his presidential ambition in the 2023 election.

He, however, urged them to reconsider their political position, obtain their Permanent Voter Cards (PVCs) and support his administration for another four years, citing ongoing infrastructure development and economic opportunities across the country, particularly in the Southeast.

Addressing a large gathering of traders and socio-cultural leaders drawn from major commercial centres, including Alaba International Market, Radio Market and ASPAMDA, Tinubu said another term would enable him to consolidate his administration’s achievements and continue serving Nigerians.

“The critical aspect in our national anthem is what I came here for. I appeal for the job and I am ready to serve; to serve you as I did in my first term. The Constitution allows me to serve for another term, so I want a second term,” he declared.

The President urged Nigerians to rise above ethnic and religious differences, insisting that the country should be regarded as a common home where everyone could pursue legitimate aspirations and prosper.

“Let’s see ourselves as one people living in the same house geographically called Nigeria. So, if you are in Nnewi, Onitsha or anywhere, grow up there and you come to Lagos, you are just answering the call of business and opportunity that God-given country, Nigeria, has given to you,” he said.

Tinubu cautioned against individuals and groups seeking to exploit ethnic differences for political advantage, stressing that national development depended on unity, mutual understanding and collective responsibility.

“Don’t let anyone come to divide us. In brotherhood we stand,” he added.

The President also made references to his family’s multi-ethnic connections, including his wife, Senator Oluremi Tinubu, who is of Itsekiri heritage, as an illustration of the cultural diversity and interconnectedness that define Nigeria.

He maintained that Nigerians, irrespective of their ethnic backgrounds or places of origin, should be able to live, work and conduct business anywhere in the country without discrimination.

Lagos State Governor, Babajide Sanwo-Olu, who attended the town hall alongside his deputy, Dr Obafemi Hamzat, described Tinubu’s presence as a significant recognition of the Igbo community and its contributions to Lagos State.

Sanwo-Olu expressed appreciation to the President for honouring the gathering, noting that the engagement provided an opportunity to strengthen relationships between the government and the Ndigbo community.

He also acknowledged political leaders, business associates and other stakeholders at the event, recognising their contributions to the political and economic development of Lagos and Nigeria.

The governor made references to Nigeria’s political journey, particularly the elections of 1999, 2003 and 2007, while acknowledging the roles played by various political leaders in shaping the country’s democratic development.

The town hall brought together Igbo community leaders, traders, business owners, political stakeholders and government representatives to discuss issues affecting Ndigbo in Lagos and explore ways of strengthening their relationship with the state and Federal Government.

It also provided a platform to promote dialogue, inclusion and peaceful coexistence, with participants underscoring the importance of mutual respect and cooperation in sustaining the unity and development of Lagos State and Nigeria.

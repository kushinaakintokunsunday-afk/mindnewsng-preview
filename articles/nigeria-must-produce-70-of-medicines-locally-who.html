Nigeria must produce 70% of medicines locally – WHO

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The World Health Organisation and the Nigerian Academy of Pharmacy have called for stronger local pharmaceutical manufacturing, improved regulation and increase

They made the call on Tuesday at the Academy’s 2026 Annual General Meeting and Investiture of New Fellows, held under the theme, “Pharmacy for National Health Security: Advancing Innovation, Medicine Sovereignty and Sustainable Healthcare.”

In a keynote address delivered on behalf of the WHO Country Representative in Nigeria, Dr Pavel Ursu, by Dr Akinola Fatiregun, the organisation stressed the need for Nigeria to reduce its dependence on imported medicines and build a resilient pharmaceutical sector.

He said, “Nigeria needs to strengthen local pharmaceutical manufacturing, reduce dependence on imported medicines and build a resilient pharmaceutical sector capable of safeguarding national health security. The Presidential Initiative for Unlocking the Healthcare Value Chain targets 70 per cent local production of pharmaceuticals by 2030.”

Ursu said medicine sovereignty was essential to national health security, noting that disruptions to global supply chains could expose countries heavily dependent on imported medicines to serious risks.

He identified six areas of potential collaboration between the WHO and the Academy: policy development, implementation research, regulatory science, workforce development, pharmacists’ involvement in health emergencies, and antimicrobial resistance and pharmacovigilance.

He also emphasised the importance of strengthening quality standards, supporting active pharmaceutical ingredient manufacturing and exploring opportunities under the African Continental Free Trade Area to expand access to locally produced medicines.

The WHO representative reiterated that pharmacists had a critical role to play in achieving universal health coverage, adding that “there is no universal health coverage without pharmacy.”

The President of the Academy, Prof. Lere Baale, urged the newly inducted fellows to use their expertise to transform the pharmaceutical sector and contribute to national development.

“Let your fellowship become a platform for collaboration, for mentorship, innovation and service. Do not merely wear this honour of fellowship. Let that honour define your actions,” he said.

Baale said Nigeria had the human resources and scientific capacity to become a major pharmaceutical manufacturing hub but needed stronger collaboration among professionals, government and industry.

“The time for isolated excellence is over,” he said, calling for collective efforts to strengthen the industry and position Nigeria to serve the wider African market.

He added, “Nigeria must work towards producing 70 per cent of its medicine requirements locally to strengthen medicine security, reduce dependence on imports and build a sustainable pharmaceutical manufacturing industry.”

The recipient of the Lifetime Achievement Award, Pharm. Stella Okoli, expressed appreciation to the Academy, describing the recognition as an encouragement to continue contributing to the profession.

“Quote me anywhere: Nigeria is the best country in the world. Nobody should feel sorry that they are Nigerian. The only thing we have to do is push hard,” she said.

Okoli urged pharmacists to remain committed to developing the country’s pharmaceutical industry and encouraged young professionals to gain practical exposure to manufacturing.

She also recalled periods when government policies supported local pharmaceutical production, saying sustained government engagement was necessary to help manufacturers grow.

A former President of the Pharmaceutical Society of Nigeria and Vice-President of the Academy, Pharm. Ahmed Yakasia, said professional recognition must be matched with integrity, competence and commitment to public health.

“It is not just enough to have knowledge; you have to have character,” he said.

Yakasai urged pharmacists to intensify efforts against fake and substandard medicines through improved pharmacovigilance, stronger professional standards and collaboration across the pharmaceutical supply chain.

He also stressed the need for pharmacists to take responsibility for protecting the public and strengthening confidence in medicines produced and distributed in Nigeria.

The Academy inducted 11 new fellows at the ceremony. They are Dr Shakirat Adeshiyan Adeosun, Dr Solomon Aigbavboa, Pharm. Ignatius Kenechukwu Anukwu, Dr Josephine Ihanene Ehimen, Pharm. Bankole Aminu Ezebuilo, Rasheed Oladele Faworaja, Dr Ukamaka Gladys Okafor, Pharm. Prof. Henry Akpobor Okeri, Professor Ogbonna Okorie, Prof. Ibrahim Adekunle Oreagba and Pharm. Dr Titilayo Abidemi Onedo.

The Academy also conferred honorary fellowships on Dr Obiora Anthony Chukwuka and Alhaji Auwalu Abdullahi Rano, while a Distinguished Award was presented as part of the ceremony.

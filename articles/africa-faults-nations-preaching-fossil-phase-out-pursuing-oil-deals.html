Africa faults nations preaching fossil phase-out, pursuing oil deals

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The African Group of Negotiators on Climate Change has accused countries advocating a complete phase-out of fossil fuels of hypocrisy, alleging that some of the

The group on Thursday said countries pushing for an end to fossil fuel production and consumption must demonstrate consistency between their public climate commitments and private energy arrangements, warning that the conflicting positions were undermining trust in the global climate negotiations.

The African negotiators also insisted that the transition away from fossil fuels must reflect the continent’s economic realities, protect workers and farmers, and allow African countries to process and utilise their natural resources rather than impose additional development burdens on them.

The position comes as negotiations at the 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference in Antalya, Türkiye, approach their conclusion, with disagreements over fossil fuels, climate finance and the pace of the global energy transition remaining major issues.

His remarks followed reports of a new trilateral declaration issued by China, the United States and Russia on Thursday after an unprecedented summit hosted by Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen, attended by US President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

The declaration reportedly included commitments to pursue talks on nuclear risks, increase petroleum supplies and advance reforms of the United Nations.

The proposed cooperation on petroleum supplies was linked to efforts to strengthen global refined petroleum availability amid energy market pressures arising from the conflict involving Iran and disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz.

The reported agreement drew criticism at COP31 from the European Union’s Climate Chief, Wopke Hoekstra, who described the development as unfortunate and urged countries to invest in domestic clean energy to avoid repeating energy supply shocks.

But speaking during a press conference, the Chair of the African Group of Negotiators, Nana Dr Antwi-Boasiako Amoah, described the contradiction between countries’ climate rhetoric and their pursuit of petroleum supplies as hypocrisy in the global climate process.

Although he declined to comment specifically on the reported petroleum supply arrangement involving China, the United States and Russia, Amoah maintained that his criticism was directed at what he described as inconsistencies in countries’ overall approach to fossil fuel production and supply.

He said, “For China and U.S. issues, I don’t have information about it. But I’m just commenting on fossil service in total as parties. This is the hypocrisy in the whole process. And so at one point, you see some countries talking about taking fossil out completely, and the underground making deals to get fossils service in their pipelines. And that is the hypocrisy that we have in this whole process.

“So, if fossil service is something that we want to do away with as parties, let’s be honest to our commitments. Let’s stay with it head on, not to be going back doors to do certain things and later come back and make it look awkward in the face of the other people. That is hypocrisy.”

Amoah added that the continent’s participation in COP31 was guided by the need to protect multilateralism, secure the means to implement climate commitments and ensure that the transition was just and credible.

He said, “Our first point of call is the protection of multilateralism. Africa feels safe to be under multilateralism because this is the only place that we can function properly as a people. And therefore, any attempt, any plans to destroy this process is a red line for us as Africa.”

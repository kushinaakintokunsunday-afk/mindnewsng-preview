Kogi youths demand power shift

From Romanus Ugwu, Abuja

A coalition of youths drawn from Kogi State’s three senatorial districts has rejected Governor Usman Ododo’s bid for a second term in the 2027 governorship election, warning that his re-election would extend the hold of one zone on the governorship to 16 consecutive years, contrary to their demand for equitable power rotation.

The youths, comprising representatives of Igala, Ebira, Okun and other ethnic groups, insisted that the governorship should shift to another zone after the eight-year tenure of former Governor Yahaya Bello and the current administration of Ododo, both from Kogi Central.

They also accused the state government of favouritism in appointments and contracts, failure to promote civil servants with corresponding salary increases, inadequate employment opportunities and lack of accountability in the management of public funds.

Addressing a press conference in Abuja yesterday under the auspices of De Group, in collaboration with the Igala Youth Council and other socio-cultural and political organisations, the coalition described the proposed 16-year zonal arrangement as unjust and unacceptable.

In a statement jointly signed by spokesperson of De Group, retired Colonel Kabiru Ndamadu, and National President of the Igala Youth Council Worldwide, Onogu Collins Monday, the youths argued that allowing Ododo to complete another four-year term would consolidate the dominance of one senatorial zone in the state’s political leadership.

“Governor Ahmed Usman Ododo assumed office January 27, 2024, succeeding former Governor Yahaya Bello, who ruled for eight years from the same zone.

“That means one zone has already held power for eight years, and is now in its third year under Governor Ododo. Any plan to extend that hold to 16 continuous years for one zone is unjust, undemocratic and unacceptable to the people of Kogi State.

“No zone is superior to another. No zone was created to rule forever while others only watch. Power must rotate. Power must shift now — in the interest of justice, equity and fairness for all Kogites,” the coalition declared.

Beyond the power rotation controversy, the youths questioned the state’s development record, alleging that substantial revenues received by the government had not translated into improved welfare, employment opportunities and infrastructure.

They claimed that Kogi had received more than N385 billion in Federation Account Allocation Committee (FAAC) disbursements and N71.67 billion in internally generated revenue within two years, figures they said should have supported better public services and workers’ welfare.

“With over N385 billion FAAC plus N71.67 billion IGR in two years, Kogi has no excuse to be poor, no excuse not to promote workers, no excuse not to employ our youths, and no excuse to persecute its own leaders,” they stated.

The coalition accused the administration of concentrating government appointments, contracts and empowerment opportunities among a small circle of political associates, describing the situation as a betrayal of the broader population.

“What we have in Kogi State today is not a government for all Kogites. It is a paddy-paddy government — government for friends, for family, for loyalists, for a small cabal.

“Contracts go to the same few people. Appointments go to the same few people. Empowerment goes to the same few people. Money circulates among the same few people.

“While the masses — the workers, the farmers, the market women, the students, the pensioners — suffer and beg to survive. Kogi State belongs to all of us, not to a few paddy-paddy friends,” the statement alleged.

On workers’ welfare, the coalition accused successive administrations of failing to implement promotions with corresponding salary increases, claiming that some civil servants had remained on the same salary grades for years despite rising living costs.

“For over nine years, civil servants in Kogi State have been denied real promotion with corresponding increase in salary. Workers are stagnated on the same level, doing the same work, with no growth, no motivation, no reward for service.

“A worker who joined service in 2016 is still suffering on the same salary grade in 2026, while cost of living has multiplied by five times,” the youths claimed.

They also alleged that successive administrations had failed to undertake meaningful recruitment of teachers, health workers and other categories of public servants, leaving thousands of graduates without employment.

“From Bello till date, there has been no meaningful mass employment for our youths. Thousands of graduates in Kogi East, Central and West are roaming the streets. No teachers recruited to fill our empty schools. No health workers recruited to fill our empty hospitals. No jobs for our graduates,” they said.

The coalition demanded an immediate end to what it described as political exclusion and victimisation of advocates of power rotation, specifically mentioning Senators Jibrin Isah Echocho and Sunday Karimi.

It also called for the implementation of outstanding civil service promotions with full salary benefits, mass recruitment of qualified youths, teachers and health workers, and equitable distribution of government appointments, contracts and empowerment opportunities.

The youths further demanded a forensic audit of local government allocations received since January 27, 2024, urging the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) to investigate the expenditure of public funds at both state and local government levels.

“We specifically call on the EFCC to investigate the former council chairmen from all the local governments in Kogi State. Let every former chairman come and account for the billions that entered his local government during his tenure.

“No former chairman should be allowed to walk away with the money of the people. They must explain what they did with FAAC, what they did with IGR, and what they did with every kobo that was meant for the grassroots.

“If they are innocent, the investigation will clear them. If they are guilty, they must face the law. Kogi people deserve to know where their local government money went,” the coalition said.

The youths maintained that their agitation was directed at what they considered inequitable political arrangements and governance practices rather than any individual.

“We are not fighting any individual. We are fighting injustice. We are fighting bad governance. We are fighting a paddy-paddy system that enriches a few and impoverishes millions. We are fighting a system that wants to silence all opposition so that it can keep power forever,” they declared.

The state government’s response to the allegations was not contained in the statement.

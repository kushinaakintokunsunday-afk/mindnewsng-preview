ADC accuses INEC of bias, demands mock exercise postponement

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The African Democratic Congress Presidential Campaign Council has accused the Independent National Electoral Commission of bias over the timing of its invitatio

The council also faulted the Commission’s reported position that results from Ward, Local Government and State collation levels would not be electronically transmitted and uploaded to its Result Viewing Portal (IReV) during the 2027 general elections.

In a statement issued in Abuja on Wednesday, the ADC-PCC Director of Media and Publicity, Kola Ologbondiyan, described INEC’s notice as hasty and unfair, alleging that the Commission deliberately delayed notifying the party until November 18, the final day for submitting the names of participating agents.

According to the council, INEC’s letter, dated November 16, gave political parties barely two days to meet the deadline for submitting agents’ names for an exercise scheduled to cover all 774 local government areas and two polling units in each of the country’s 8,838 Registration Areas.

It argued that the timeframe was inadequate for political parties to mobilise, train and deploy agents for an exercise intended to test the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System ahead of the 2027 elections.

“How does INEC expect political parties to adequately prepare, mobilise, train and submit their agents for an exercise of this magnitude within such a short time? This is not preparation; it is an ambush,” the council said.

It added that a mock exercise designed to test the credibility of the electoral system could not achieve its purpose if critical stakeholders were effectively excluded through an unreasonable timeline.

“INEC cannot test electoral credibility through an incredible process,” it declared, urging the Commission to postpone the exercise and provide political parties with sufficient time to participate.

The ADC-PCC’s demand comes amid growing political attention to INEC’s preparations for the 2027 general elections, particularly the deployment of technology in voter accreditation, results management and the electronic transmission of election results.

On electronic transmission, the council expressed concern over comments reportedly made on Tuesday by INEC’s Director of Information and Communication Technology, Lawrence Bayode, that the Commission had neither developed nor tested the necessary system for uploading results from Ward, Local Government and State collation levels.

It said the position contradicted previous assurances by the Commission’s former Chairman, Professor Mahmood Yakubu, and his successor, Professor Joash Amupitan, SAN, on extending electronic publication of results beyond the polling unit level.

The council maintained that Nigerians had consistently demanded real-time electronic transmission and public access to results to reduce opportunities for manipulation during the collation process.

“Nigerians fought for and demanded real-time electronic transmission of election results precisely to eliminate the manipulation that often occurs between the Polling Unit and subsequent collation levels,” it added.

It specifically questioned why Forms EC8B, EC8C and EC8D, which contain results at the Ward, Local Government and State collation levels, respectively, would not be uploaded to IReV when polling unit results recorded in Form EC8A could be electronically captured and published.

“If Form EC8A, the polling unit result, can be captured and uploaded to IReV, there is no convincing reason why the corresponding results at subsequent collation levels cannot also be made publicly accessible,” the council said.

It warned that limiting public access to polling unit results would leave gaps in the transparency of the collation process and could undermine confidence in the 2027 elections.

The campaign council further urged INEC to demonstrate its independence and impartiality, cautioning against policies that could give the ruling All Progressives Congress an undue advantage.

“INEC is not an extension of the ruling party. It is an independent electoral umpire, and it must act like one. Its credibility rests on its ability to treat all political parties fairly and transparently,” it said.

The council also appealed to Amupitan to ensure that the Commission’s actions matched its assurances, warning that the credibility of the 2027 elections would be a major test of his leadership.

“The world is watching the INEC Chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan. He must be mindful of the weight of responsibility before him. His name, his legacy and the peace of this country are at stake. History will judge him not by assurances, but by the credibility of the elections he conducts,” it added.

The campaign council reaffirmed its commitment to peaceful democratic engagement but insisted that it would continue to demand transparency and accountability in the electoral process.

“We will not be intimidated. We will not be silenced. We will continue to insist that every Nigerian’s vote must count,” the statement further read.

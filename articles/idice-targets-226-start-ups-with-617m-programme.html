iDICE targets 226 start-ups with $617m programme

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 3 min read

Lagos advises founders to focus on customer needs

______________________________________________________________________

By Chinenye Anuforo

The Federal Government’s Investment in Digital and Creative Enterprises (iDICE) programme is targeting at least 226 Nigerian start-ups for support through a $617 million initiative, its National Coordinator, Ife Adebayo, has said.

Adebayo spoke on Wednesday at the southern preliminary of the iDICE Startup Summit in Lagos, where founders in the creative and digital technology sectors met investors, operators and policymakers.

He said the programme was designed to address gaps in skills, access to finance and policy support for businesses in the two sectors.

“We want to ensure that we have a conveyor belt of skills that continues to churn out people with the right level of skills for the sector,” Adebayo said.

He added that the programme would provide “patient capital and blended finance options for start-ups” and work with regulators and policymakers to promote policies that support the sectors.

The initiative is backed by the Federal Government, the African Development Bank, the French Development Agency, the Islamic Development Bank and the Bank of Industry.

Adebayo said Ventures Platform Limited, the programme’s technology fund manager, had launched its fund with an $84 million close, while Kuramo Capital was raising funds. He said iDICE was also concluding the process of appointing a creative fund manager.

The programme includes a $45 million Bank of Industry debt fund, with loans offered at a 10 per cent rate, he said. An Islamic finance option, funded by the Islamic Development Bank, is also available.

“About four start-ups have benefited from the tech fund,” Adebayo said, adding that applications for the debt funds were being processed through partner banks.

He said the application portal remained open, while banks assessed applications to determine which businesses qualified for funding.

On risk-sharing, Adebayo said the Federal Government had offered fund managers a 30 per cent first-loss position on equity investments. “If any of the start-ups fail, the Federal Government takes a 30 per cent first-loss position with the fund managers,” he said.

Lagos State Commissioner for Innovation, Science and Technology, Olatunbosun Alake, urged founders to identify a clear market niche and build products that meet customer needs.

“Start-ups need to identify a particular niche they want to deliver value in,” Alake said. “If you are delivering a service or a good better than what people are doing now, trust me, you will find consumers, customers and financiers.”

He said Lagos had supported nearly 100 start-ups through direct funding and programmes offering access to internet, electricity and office space.

Alake, however, warned that “it’s not just having an idea”; founders needed to research their markets, understand customer groups and build a structure capable of delivering their products or services.

Creative economy lawyer and managing partner at Zaeda Oracle Solicitors, Foza Oyinkansola Fawehinmi, advised founders to delegate as their businesses grow.

“Start-up mentality can work for one year, but after that year, you need to move away from that start-up mentality and begin to function as a CEO,” she said. “You can be a founder and not have CEO skills.”

Fawehinmi also urged entrepreneurs to seek professional advice and stay informed about government support schemes. “Hire professionals that understand the context and industry you work in and spend money looking for information,” she said.

The summit series continues in Ilorin on 23 November, with the national final scheduled for Abuja on 30 November.

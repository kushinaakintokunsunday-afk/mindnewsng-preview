Obi denies seeking US lawmakers’ backing for 2027 race

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria Democratic Congress presidential candidate, Peter Obi, has said his recent engagements with American political figures in Washington, DC, were not inten

Obi said his visit to the United States was primarily to discuss Nigeria’s development, citizens’ welfare, and the country’s democratic future with American stakeholders.

The former Anambra State governor stated this in a post on his X handle on Wednesday, responding to media reports suggesting that his meetings with members of the United States Congress amounted to an endorsement of his presidential ambition.

Obi said he did not describe the engagements as endorsements by the US Congress or any of its members.

“As I expressly noted in my remarks, my visit to the United States was not intended to secure an endorsement, and I did not characterise my meetings as endorsements of my political aspirations by the US Congress or any of its members,” he said.

He explained that the discussions focused on Nigeria’s development, the welfare of its citizens and the consolidation of democracy.

“I am especially concerned with strengthening democratic institutions, advancing accountability, and ensuring that Nigeria’s electoral process produces elections that are free, fair, transparent and credible. These matters should concern every Nigerian who is committed to our country’s future,” he added.

The NDC candidate also encouraged other Nigerian political leaders, particularly those seeking public office, to engage international stakeholders on issues affecting the country.

“Our democracy can be strengthened through constructive dialogue, the exchange of experiences and a shared commitment to credible elections,” he said.

Obi warned against sensational headlines and inaccurate interpretations of his engagements.

“Nigeria’s democratic future is far too consequential to be distorted by sensational headlines or inaccurate interpretations. My mission centred on Nigeria, its people and the future of our democracy—not on endorsing any individual,” he stated.

Obi had, in an earlier post on Tuesday, disclosed that he arrived in Washington, DC, on Monday, November 16, for engagements with American political figures, including members of the US House of Representatives.

He listed Congressman Marlin Stutzman, Congresswoman Beth Van Duyne, former Congressman Todd Tiahrt, Suzanne Bock Grisham and retired US Army Colonel Albert V. Short among those he met.

He also disclosed that he granted an interview to American television commentator Glenn Beck, during which they discussed Nigeria’s development challenges, accountable leadership and resource management.

Obi said he also addressed a gathering on Capitol Hill, where he highlighted the need for international cooperation on Nigeria’s security, national unity, economic prosperity and democratic development.

However, Karl Von Batten, a representative of Washington-based lobbying firm Von Batten-Montague-York, which was hired by former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar, disputed suggestions that the US Congress officially met with or endorsed Obi.

In a statement on Wednesday, Von Batten said the event attended by Obi was a private engagement and that none of the individuals featured in the circulated video belonged to the House Foreign Affairs Committee or its Subcommittee on Africa.

He acknowledged the presence of Stutzman and Van Duyne but said neither served on the House Foreign Affairs Committee.

“Congress did not meet with or endorse Mr Obi. The event he attended was private, and no one in the video is a member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee or its Subcommittee on Africa,” he said.

Von Batten added that no official from the US State Department’s Bureau of African Affairs was present at the event.

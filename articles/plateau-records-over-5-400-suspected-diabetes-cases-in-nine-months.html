Plateau Records Over 5,400 Suspected Diabetes Cases In Nine Months

By Channels TV Channels TV 16 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Channels TV

The Plateau State Government says 5,457 suspected new cases of diabetes were recorded across health facilities in the state between January and September 2026,

The Plateau State Government says 5,457 suspected new cases of diabetes were recorded across health facilities in the state between January and September 2026, raising concerns over the need for increased screening, early diagnosis and proper management of the disease.

The Commissioner for Health, Dr. Nicholas Ba’amlong, disclosed this on Monday during a press briefing in Jos to commemorate the 2026 World Diabetes Day, noting that the figure followed 5,546 suspected cases recorded in 2025.

Ba’amlong, however, clarified that the figures represented suspected cases and not confirmed diagnoses, stressing that diabetes remained a major public health concern because many people were living with the condition without knowing their status.

He also disclosed that findings from the 2024 nationwide Know Your Number, Control Your Number screening campaign showed that 14,359 people were screened in Plateau State, with 2,610, representing approximately 18 per cent, recording elevated blood glucose levels.

According to him, the findings highlight the need to expand access to screening, diagnosis and treatment, but do not represent the prevalence of diabetes in the state.

As part of activities marking this year’s World Diabetes Day, the Ministry of Health, in collaboration with the Plateau State chapters of the Diabetes Association of Nigeria and the Endocrine and Metabolic Society of Nigeria, conducted a free workplace screening exercise at the Joseph Gomwalk Secretariat Complex, Jos.

The exercise, which targeted 1,000 civil servants, provided blood glucose and blood pressure checks, weight and body mass index assessments, health education, counselling and referrals for further medical attention where necessary.

The commissioner said the state government, under Governor Caleb Mutfwang, was also working towards extending diabetes education and screening services to underserved communities across the 17 local government areas through existing primary healthcare facilities and community outreach programmes.

He added that the government was exploring partnerships and appropriate medical technologies to improve access to diagnostic services, particularly at the primary healthcare level, while strengthening follow-up and referral systems for individuals with elevated blood glucose levels.

“Screening is only the beginning. Early detection must lead to appropriate diagnosis and treatment,” Ba’amlong said.

He urged residents, particularly those with a family history of diabetes, excess weight or high blood pressure, to regularly check their blood glucose levels and seek medical attention when necessary.

The commissioner also advised residents to maintain healthy lifestyles through balanced diets, regular physical activity and weight management, while avoiding tobacco and limiting alcohol consumption.

He identified excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss and persistent fatigue as some of the symptoms that could indicate diabetes, urging people living with the condition to adhere to prescribed treatment and attend regular medical reviews.

World Diabetes Day is observed annually on November 14 to raise awareness about diabetes, promote prevention and encourage access to quality care for people living with the condition.

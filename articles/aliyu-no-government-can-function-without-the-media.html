Aliyu: ‘No government can function without the media’

By Bianca Iboma-Emefu

Ambassador Dr. Dahiru Aliyu, President of the Nigeria Law Society, has described the media as indispensable to effective governance and nation-building, declaring that no government can function meaningfully without a strong and independent press.

Aliyu made the assertion while receiving an Award of Excellence in Legal Leadership and Professional Development at the 2026 Global Affairs Magazine Awards (GAMA 2026), held on Friday, November 20, at Eko Hotels and Suites, Victoria Island, Lagos.

Emphasising the media’s critical role in holding institutions accountable and disseminating information, the senior lawyer said journalists make significant sacrifices to ensure that citizens remain informed and society benefits from access to knowledge.

“If you remove the media, to me, that’s no government,” he declared, insisting that the media must be adequately empowered to discharge its responsibilities to society.

Aliyu, who travelled from Abuja to attend the ceremony, said he considered the recognition bigger than his individual achievements, dedicating the award to the Nigerian legal community, members of the Nigeria Law Society and the country at large.

“The award is for the institution, the members, the governing council. It’s for Nigerian lawyers. Because the recognition given to me is not for me alone,” he said.

He added that the honour should be seen as recognition of the collective contributions of legal practitioners to the development of the profession and the nation.

The lawyer also stressed that awards should reflect genuine service, commitment and contributions to society rather than being treated as entitlements.

His remarks brought the relationship between the media, the legal profession and democratic governance into focus, particularly the responsibility of both institutions to promote accountability, protect citizens’ rights and strengthen national development.

The 2026 GAMA awards brought together personalities from different professional fields to recognise contributions to their respective sectors.

Aliyu’s recognition in legal leadership and professional development also highlighted the importance of institutional leadership and the role of the legal profession in strengthening Nigeria’s democratic and professional institutions.

Meanwhile, a Lawyer and philanthropist, Dr. Valentine Mbamalu, was honoured for his humanitarian deeds. He builds homes for widows, award scholarship to their children and equally empower young entrepreneurs.

He recently built two houses for widows and provided ₦20 million in business support to 20 young entrepreneurs as part of his humanitarian interventions aimed at improving livelihoods and addressing economic vulnerabilities.

The interventions, carried out through the Touch of Valentine Foundation, also include the construction of another house for a widow, which is currently ongoing.

Under the youth empowerment programme, each of the 20 beneficiaries received ₦1 million last year to establish or expand their businesses, bringing the total financial support to ₦20 million.

Mbamalu, who was recently honoured with an Award of Excellence by the Global Affairs Magazine Awards (GAMA) for his humanitarian deeds, highlighted some of his interventions while speaking after receiving the recognition.

He said his philanthropic activities were driven by the desire to improve lives rather than the expectation of public recognition or rewards from beneficiaries.

According to him, some beneficiaries have voluntarily returned to share accounts of how the support has improved their circumstances, although the foundation does not compel them to provide feedback.

“There is nothing you do that is not seen. No matter how you think nobody is seeing you, they see what we do in society and the job that we have done, how we have impacted lives,” he said.

Mbamalu, who heads TOV Chambers and chairs TOV Group of Companies, said he was encouraged by the recognition of his humanitarian efforts.

His interventions underscore the importance of targeted private philanthropy in tackling housing insecurity and youth unemployment, particularly through practical support that enables vulnerable women to secure shelter and young people to build sustainable livelihoods.

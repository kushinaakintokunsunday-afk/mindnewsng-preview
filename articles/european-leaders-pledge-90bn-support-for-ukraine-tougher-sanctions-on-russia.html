European leaders pledge €90bn support for Ukraine, tougher sanctions on Russia

By Punch Punch 4d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

European leaders have pledged €90bn in military and financial assistance to Ukraine by the end of 2027 and vowed to impose tougher sanctions on Russia, includin

The commitments were contained in a joint statement issued on Friday by the German Embassy in Nigeria, following a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, Ukraine and the United States in Berlin.

The leaders of Germany, France, the United Kingdom and Ukraine reaffirmed their determination to sustain support for Kyiv and increase economic pressure on Moscow over its ongoing war against Ukraine.

They said the €90bn support package under the European Union’s Ukraine Support Loan would cover Ukraine’s financial needs well into 2027, providing both military and financial assistance.

The statement also reaffirmed NATO allies’ commitment to provide €70bn in military equipment, assistance and training to Ukraine in 2026, with at least equivalent levels of support planned for 2027.

The statement said they reaffirmed, “their commitment to fulfil in its entirety the pledge made by NATO Allies to provide 70 billion Euro in military equipment, assistance
and training for Ukraine in 2026 and at least equivalent levels in 2027.

“Under the European Union’s Ukraine Support Loan, 90 billion Euros will be
provided for military and financial assistance by the end of 2027, covering
Ukraine’s financial needs well into 2027”.

The leaders declared that Russia would not be able to outlast the coalition’s commitment to Ukraine, stressing that its resilience would be matched by sustained international assistance.

“Russia will not be able to outlast the steadfast support of the Coalition’s members and Ukraine’s resilience,” the statement said.

On economic measures, the leaders pledged to intensify sanctions and introduce broader measures to restrict Moscow’s ability to finance its military campaign.

They specifically agreed to pursue additional measures targeting Russian oil and gas revenues, cryptocurrency transactions and illicit financial flows, while disrupting the country’s military-industrial complex.

The coalition also pledged to strengthen efforts to obstruct Russia’s shadow fleet, which it said enables Moscow to circumvent existing sanctions.

The statement added that the leaders would work to safeguard maritime security by enforcing applicable international rules and standards established through the International Maritime Organization.

The renewed pressure on Russia comes amid growing concerns over its attacks on Ukrainian civilians and critical infrastructure.

The leaders condemned what they described as Russia’s systematic escalation of deliberate and indiscriminate missile and drone attacks against civilians and civilian infrastructure.

They expressed condolences to families of victims and reiterated Ukraine’s right to defend itself against what they called Russia’s war of aggression.

The statement also argued that Moscow was paying an increasingly unsustainable price for the conflict, despite its continued attacks.

“Despite suffering record losses, Russia has made only minimal territorial gains this year and has failed to take a single strategically important town or city,” the leaders said.

They added that Russia’s escalating attacks on Ukrainian civilians could not compensate for its failures on the battlefield.

The leaders further condemned alleged Russian hybrid attacks in Europe, including sabotage, information operations and cyber-attacks, which they said were intended to weaken international support for Ukraine.

They insisted that such intimidation would not deter their commitment but instead strengthen their resolve to reinforce the defence of Ukraine, Europe and NATO.

On military assistance, the coalition identified strengthening Ukraine’s air defence capabilities, particularly systems capable of intercepting ballistic missiles, as a priority.

Ahead of winter, the leaders pledged to work with third countries to provide additional missile defence capabilities to protect civilians and critical infrastructure.

Beyond financial and military assistance, the statement outlined plans for legally binding security guarantees for Ukraine once a ceasefire takes effect.

The proposed guarantees, to be defensive in nature and closely coordinated with the United States, could include sustained military, financial and technical support to maintain Ukraine’s armed forces.

They could also involve Ukraine’s participation in a proposed US-led ceasefire monitoring and verification mechanism, alongside a European-led Multinational Force for Ukraine, supported by Washington.

The force would provide reassurance measures in the air, at sea and on land, while helping to rebuild Ukraine’s military capabilities.

The leaders also proposed a legally binding commitment, subject to national procedures, to take measures to restore peace and security if Russia launches another armed attack against Ukraine.

On diplomatic efforts to end the war, the coalition reaffirmed its commitment to a comprehensive, just and lasting peace, building on ongoing US diplomatic initiatives.

It commended Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s continued willingness to accept an immediate ceasefire and engage in meaningful negotiations.

The leaders also supported direct dialogue between Ukraine and Russia, with the participation of the United States and European countries.

They insisted that any peace agreement must respect Ukraine’s sovereignty and territorial integrity, maintaining that international borders must not be altered through force.

“Ukraine’s sovereign right to choose its own security arrangements and alliances must be fully respected,” the statement said.

The leaders further maintained that Russia’s sovereign assets would remain frozen until Moscow ended its war of aggression and compensated Ukraine for the damage caused by the conflict.

They warned that negotiations on broader European security arrangements could not proceed while Russia continued its military aggression and violated international agreements, including the United Nations Charter, the Helsinki Final Act and the Paris Charter.

The statement also stressed that any negotiations involving the European Union and NATO would require the consent of their respective member states and allies.

As an immediate step towards ending hostilities, the leaders called on Russia to accept an energy ceasefire throughout the winter.

They also demanded a ceasefire arrangement in the Black Sea to guarantee safe passage and freedom of navigation, particularly to sustain food exports and protect global food security.

EFCC arraigns Lagos socialite Halimat Tejuosho over alleged N1.1bn scam to secure victim NNPC GMD position

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: VeryNigerian

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has arraigned socialite Halimat Adenike Tejuosho before the Federal Capital Territory High Court in Gwagwala

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has arraigned socialite Halimat Adenike Tejuosho before the Federal Capital Territory High Court in Gwagwalada over an alleged N1. 1 billion job scam.

Tejuosho was arraigned on Wednesday before Justice A. S. Adepoju on a five-count charge bordering on advance fee fraud, obtaining money by false pretence and other alleged criminal activities.

According to a statement published on the EFCC’s X account, the commission accused Tejuosho and another defendant, identified as Gidado Ibrahim, of obtaining N603.4 million from Oluseye Yomi-Sholoye between August and October 2024.

The prosecution alleged in the first count that the defendants collected the money under the pretence that they would facilitate Yomi-Sholoye’s appointment as Group Managing Director of the Nigerian National Petroleum Corporation (NNPC), knowing that the claim was false.

The charge was brought under Section 1(a) of the Advance Fee Fraud and Other Fraud Related Offences Act, 2006.

In another count, the EFCC accused Tejuosho of receiving N259.9 million, allegedly derived from criminal conduct, into her Access Bank account during the same period.

The fourth count alleged that she received an additional N50 million on September 30, 2024, into an Access Bank account belonging to Haleems Integrated Services Limited.

Tejuosho pleaded not guilty to all five counts.

Following her plea, defence counsel, Chuks Ugwuru, applied for bail on liberal terms, arguing that the offences were bailable and that his client’s health condition constituted special circumstances deserving the court’s consideration.

Ugwuru relied on a medical report which he said indicated that Tejuosho was critically ill. He urged Justice Adepoju to exercise discretion in her favour.

However, the EFCC opposed the application. Its lead prosecution counsel, Yahaya Tarfa, told the court that the commission had filed a 12-paragraph counter-affidavit, alongside an exhibit and written address.

Although Tarfa acknowledged that bail was at the court’s discretion, he urged the judge to impose conditions that would ensure the defendant remained available throughout the trial.

In his ruling, Justice Adepoju held that the offences were bailable and granted Tejuosho bail in the sum of N100 million, with one surety in like sum.

The judge directed that the surety must reside within the court’s jurisdiction and own land or property in the Federal Capital Territory.

He further ordered that the surety’s property documents be verified by the FCT Land Registry, with a copy deposited with the court registrar.

Tejuosho was also ordered to surrender her international passport to the registrar pending the hearing and determination of the case.

The judge, however, directed that she remain at the Kuje Correctional Centre until she fulfils the bail conditions.

The case was adjourned until February 1, 2027, for trial.

The EFCC had declared Tejuosho wanted in April 2026 over an alleged case of obtaining money by false pretence.

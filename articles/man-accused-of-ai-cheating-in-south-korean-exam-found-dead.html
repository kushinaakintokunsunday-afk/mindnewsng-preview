Man accused of AI cheating in South Korean exam found dead

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A 32-year-old South Korean man accused of using artificial intelligence to cheat in a law school entrance examination has been found dead at a motel in Seoul, a

The man, identified as Lee Jin-woo, was found at a motel near Cheonho Station at about 1:12 pm on Wednesday, according to The Chosun Daily.

Police had been searching for Lee after receiving a report at 8:42 am expressing concern about his safety.

His discovery came hours after he failed to appear for a planned live-streamed test intended to address allegations that he had used AI to obtain a perfect score in this year’s Legal Education Eligibility Test, known as LEET.

Lee had reportedly promised to demonstrate his abilities by solving Korean-language questions from South Korea’s College Scholastic Ability Test in real time on YouTube at a private institute in Daechi-dong, Seoul.

The exercise was scheduled to begin at 8 am, but Lee did not arrive. His phone was also switched off.

The institute cancelled the exercise after he failed to show up by 8:35 am, when the examination papers were due to be distributed.

The institute reportedly said there was still a possibility that an accomplice inside an actual examination centre could relay questions to a candidate, even during a live verification exercise.

Police and fire officials visited Lee’s home in Bongcheon-dong, Gwanak-gu, after the institute raised concerns about his whereabouts, but found that he had not returned home since leaving on the evening of November 17.

Police subsequently traced his movements after confirming that he had checked out of a motel in Gwangjin-gu on Wednesday morning.

A packaging box that had contained a multi-purpose rope was reportedly found in the room he had occupied. Officers later tracked his movements towards the Achasan area and then Songpa-gu and Gangdong-gu before locating him at the motel near Cheonho Station.

A note allegedly written by Lee was also found at the scene. It reportedly contained the words, “My body and mind are exhausted,” but did not mention or apologise for the alleged examination cheating.

The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency said the cause of death would be established through an autopsy and other investigations.

“Once the death is finalised, the cause of death will be confirmed through the autopsy and other investigations,” the agency said.

However, the police investigation into the alleged AI-assisted cheating will continue despite Lee’s condition.

The Financial Crimes Investigation Unit of the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency’s Metropolitan Investigation Division said, “Despite the suspect’s death, we will continue the investigation to confirm the substantive truth related to this case.”

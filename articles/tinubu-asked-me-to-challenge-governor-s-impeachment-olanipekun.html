Tinubu asked me to challenge governor’s impeachment – Olanipekun

By Punch Punch 4d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Chief Wole Olanipekun, a Senior Advocate of Nigeria, has revealed that President Bola Tinubu personally urged him to assemble a legal team to challenge the impe

Olanipekun said Tinubu called him at home following the impeachment and directed him to mobilise lawyers to challenge the process, insisting that the country’s democratic system must be protected.

The senior lawyer made the disclosure on Thursday at the presentation of his six-volume book, Wole Olanipekun Through the Cases, which documents cases handled by his law firm, Wole Olanipekun & Co.

The event, held at the Body of Benchers Complex in Abuja, also marked his 75th birthday, 50 years at the Nigerian Bar and 35 years as a Senior Advocate of Nigeria.

Tinubu attended the ceremony, unveiled the books and conferred the Grand Commander of the Order of the Niger on him.

Olanipekun made the remarks while recounting his longstanding relationship with Tinubu and the President’s involvement in defending democratic institutions.

According to him, Tinubu contacted him after learning about the governor’s impeachment and asked whether he was aware that the President had accused him of being responsible for the governor’s removal.

He recalled that he was asleep when the call came and was surprised by the allegation.

Olanipekun said Tinubu told him, “Son, have you heard that the President said you have removed the governor of your state?”

He added, “He said, ‘What are you doing?’ I said, ‘I’m sleeping.’ He said, ‘There is no sleep for the wicked.’

“I said, ‘Am I a wicked person? How did I become a wicked person? Because somebody was impeached? Goodness me.’”

The senior lawyer said Tinubu subsequently instructed him to constitute a team to challenge the impeachment, warning that failure to resist such a development could undermine democracy.

He quoted the President as saying, “Please go and set up a team to fight it.

“This democracy will collapse if you allow governors to be removed that way.”

Olanipekun said he and other lawyers took up the challenge, describing the eventual Supreme Court decision as a significant moment in the country’s democratic history.

He said the apex court rejected the circumstances surrounding the governor’s removal, adding that the case demonstrated the importance of judicial intervention in protecting constitutional democracy.

“That was how it started,” he said.

Olanipekun, who was the Attorney-General of the old Ondo State, said Nigerians needed to understand that democracy went beyond elections and the transfer of political power.

He described democracy as a system built on public confidence, leadership and the protection of people’s rights.

“Democracy is the government of the people. It’s more than that. Far more than that.

“It is God. It is confidence. It is leadership,” he said.

The senior lawyer also recalled Tinubu’s involvement in challenging the dissolution of the National Assembly following the 1993 military coup led by the late General Sani Abacha.

He said Tinubu, alongside some other senators, approached him and the late G.O.K. Ajayi, SAN, at a meeting held at the residence of the late Senator Odi Adewumi on Agoro Doyin Street.

According to Olanipekun, the politicians briefed them to challenge the dissolution of the legislature in court.

He said, “At that nocturnal meeting at Agoro Doyin Street, in the residence of late Senator Odi Adewumi, you briefed my boss, one of my bosses in the profession, G.O.K. Ajayi, and myself that we should challenge the dissolution.

“We did file an action, but later withdrew it for exigencies of that time. Shortly thereafter, we didn’t see you because we couldn’t locate our clients and couldn’t be fighting any cause on behalf of a client that we don’t see, because you had disappeared. You had to travel; you had to leave.”

Olanipekun said Tinubu later returned and assigned him and other lawyers to work with senior lawyer Chi Williams on his legal matters.

He also recalled that Tinubu wrote him a letter in July 2003 after the conclusion of a legal case involving Mitchell Williams and Tinubu.

The senior lawyer said the letter contained commendations that humbled him and would be included in his memoirs.

“I’m not doing any campaign, but I’m talking as a lawyer. I’m talking of what I’ve seen.

“I’m not talking of what I’ve witnessed or what I’ve experienced. At first hand, I did not only work for you, Mr President, but you’ve always appreciated lawyers. This is very important to our profession,” he said.

Although Olanipekun did not mention the governor’s name or the state involved during his remarks, his account appears to relate to the 2006 impeachment crisis involving former Oyo State Governor, Senator Rashidi Ladoja.

In January 2006, 18 members of the Oyo State House of Assembly moved to impeach Ladoja and replace him with his deputy, Christopher Alao-Akala.

The impeachment was challenged in court, with Olanipekun among the lawyers involved in the legal battle.

The Court of Appeal nullified the removal, and the Supreme Court, on December 7, 2006, dismissed an appeal by the lawmakers who had sought to overturn the decision.

The apex court held that the impeachment process was unconstitutional.

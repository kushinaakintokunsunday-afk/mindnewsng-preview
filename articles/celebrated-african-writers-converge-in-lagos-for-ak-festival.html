Celebrated African writers converge in Lagos for Aké Festival

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

African writers, including Nnedi Okorafor, Chika Unigwe, Wole Talabi and A. Igoni Barrett, have converged in Lagos for the 14th edition of the Aké Arts and Book Festival, which is exploring the relevance of indigenous African knowledge in an increasingly technology-driven world.

The three-day festival, which opened on Thursday, November 19, at the Silkwood Hotel, Ikeja GRA, will end on Saturday, November 21, with the theme, ‘Native Intelligence’.

Organised by the Book Buzz Foundation, the 2026 edition features 24 curated events, including book discussions, panel sessions, film screenings and the popular Palmwine and Poetry Night.

Speaking with Sunday Scoop, the festival’s founder and director, Lola Shoneyin, said the theme was inspired by the growing attention given to AI, which she believes has overshadowed the importance of indigenous knowledge.

She said, “There’s so much noise about AI, and to be honest, I didn’t think Native Intelligence was getting the kind of traction and celebration that it deserves.”

Shoneyin explained that the festival would provide a platform to examine different forms of intelligence, particularly knowledge passed down through generations and preserved in African cultures.

“There’s the intelligence that comes from education. Then there’s the intelligence from the people. This allows us to talk about that kind of intelligence.

“As you know, with the Aké Festival, we are not shy. We will have important conversations. We are constantly in pursuit of the unsayable. And that means we want to talk about the matters that are important to us, the things that we want people to take away and remember for months and years after leaving that festival,” she said.

Highlights of the festival include conversations on the future of creative work in the age of artificial intelligence, the relationship between memory and technology, and the global prospects of Nigerian comics and animation.

Ahead of the festival, the United States Consulate General in Lagos hosted a literary salon featuring Okorafor, who discussed her novel, Death of the Author.

Okorafor is also scheduled to discuss the novel at the festival, while Talabi will present The Fist of Memory. Unigwe will engage participants on her book, Grace.

Other writers and creatives featured include Fatima Bala, Oyin Olugbile, Bisi Adjapon, Suyi Davies Okungbowa, Nana Darkoa Sekyiamah, Wana Udobang and Dami Ajayi.

Shoneyin also reiterated her commitment to strengthening African publishing and literary institutions, stressing that writers on the continent should not depend on Western validation to establish their worth.

She commended the Lagos State Government for increasing its support for literary activities, particularly through an initiative offering N20,000 book grants and festival access to 100 residents below the age of 25.

The state government, through the Office of the Special Adviser on Sustainable Development Goals and the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture, partnered with the festival.

A representative of the state government reaffirmed Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu’s commitment to making Lagos a leading creative hub in Africa.

“Lagos remains the city where every heritage finds its place, and where creativity does not simply exist; it thrives,” the representative said.

The official added that the partnership and book grant initiative were designed to encourage young people to embrace indigenous stories, critical thinking and cultural identity.

Founded in 2013 by Shoneyin through the Book Buzz Foundation, the Aké Arts and Book Festival has grown into a gathering for writers, poets, filmmakers and cultural thinkers from Africa and the diaspora.

This year’s edition seeks to deepen conversations about African identity, heritage and creativity while examining how indigenous knowledge can remain relevant in a world increasingly shaped by technology.

37 miners’ deaths: Senate demands sanctions against NSCDC officers

By Punch Punch 6d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Senate on Wednesday called on the Federal Government to prosecute or appropriately punish officers found culpable in the deaths of 37 young artisans who die

It also urged the government to pay compensation to the bereaved families and implement the recommendations of the independent investigative committee set up to probe the incident.

The resolutions followed a motion sponsored by Senator Mohammed Musa (Niger East), who raised concerns over the circumstances surrounding the deaths of the artisans on September 17, 2026, after their arrest during an operation against suspected illegal mining activities.

Presenting the motion, Musa described the incident as a serious human rights concern, saying it raised questions about the treatment of detainees and accountability within security agencies.

He said, “The incident should not be treated as an ordinary tragedy but as a serious matter requiring transparency, accountability and institutional reforms to prevent a recurrence.”

The senator told the chamber that the Federal Government had constituted a 10-member independent investigative committee to establish the circumstances surrounding the deaths.

He said the committee had completed its assignment and submitted a report which reportedly identified serious lapses and recommended sanctions, institutional reforms and compensation for the victims’ families.

Musa urged the Senate to intervene by demanding the immediate implementation of the recommendations, particularly those concerning officers found culpable and reforms within the NSCDC.

The deaths sparked public outrage, mourning and protests in Niger State, prompting calls for an independent investigation into the treatment of the young men while in detention.

The Senate, during Wednesday’s plenary, observed a minute of silence in honour of the deceased and commended President Bola Tinubu, the Federal Government and Niger State Governor, Mohammed Umaru Bago, for facilitating the establishment of the investigative committee.

It also resolved to constitute a delegation led by the Senate President to pay a condolence visit to the families of the deceased in Niger State.

The chamber directed its committees on Interior, Human Rights, Police Affairs and Judiciary to monitor the implementation of the investigative committee’s recommendations and report back.

The Acting Chief Whip, Senator Diket Plang (Plateau Central), moved an amendment to include the Senate Committee on Solid Minerals among the committees overseeing the implementation process.

The amendment was seconded by Senator Abdul Ningi (Bauchi Central) and adopted.

Contributing to the debate, former Edo State Governor, Senator Adams Oshiomhole (Edo North), called on the Senate to condemn illegal mining, arguing that it should be treated with the same seriousness as illegal oil bunkering.

He said, “Illegal mining should be treated with the same seriousness as illegal oil bunkering, given its implications for national revenue, economic stability and the exploitation of the country’s natural resources.”

Oshiomhole urged traditional rulers, state governments and local government authorities to intensify public enlightenment campaigns against illegal mining.

He described the activity as an economic crime, arguing that it deprived the government of legitimate revenue while allowing individuals and criminal networks to exploit the country’s mineral resources for personal gain.

He also called for stringent penalties for those involved in illegal mining but cautioned against interpreting the Senate’s position as an endorsement of the deaths of the 37 artisans.

He stressed that the need to tackle illegal mining should be separated from the circumstances surrounding the deaths, particularly as the victims’ involvement in the activity had not been conclusively established.

His additional prayer was seconded by Senator Simon Lalong (Plateau South).

Lalong also urged the Senate to commend state governors and the 36 State Houses of Assembly for supporting the constitutional amendment on state police.

He described the approval of the State Police Bill as a significant achievement, noting that the proposal had generated controversy over the years before securing unanimous support from the states.

The Senate President welcomed the additional prayers but cautioned lawmakers against assuming that the deceased were illegal miners.

He said the victims were in NSCDC custody when they died, adding, “The Senate must be careful not to make assumptions about their guilt or innocence.”

His clarification reinforced the need to establish the circumstances of the deaths and determine the responsibility of those involved in the detention of the artisans.

However, Ningi raised a point of order under Order 56, questioning the relevance of Oshiomhole’s additional prayer on illegal mining to the substantive motion.

He argued that the motion concerned the deaths of 37 Nigerians in the custody of a government security agency and that the Senate should focus on justice, human rights and compassion for the bereaved families.

Ningi maintained that although illegal mining was a serious national concern, it should not distract from the immediate issue of the deaths and the need to hold those responsible accountable.

The Senate subsequently adopted the prayers, including the demand for the implementation of the investigative committee’s recommendations, sanctions against culpable officers, compensation for the families and reforms within the NSCDC.

The PUNCH reported in September 2026 that 37 artisanal miners died in the custody of the NSCDC in Minna, Niger State, triggering public outrage, mourning and protests.

A presidential investigative panel subsequently concluded that the deaths resulted from severe overcrowding, extreme heat, dehydration and neglect, rather than a disease outbreak initially claimed by the NSCDC.

The incident raised concerns over the treatment of detainees and the accountability of security agencies, with families and rights advocates demanding justice for the victims.

Norway Is Short of About 34,000 Workers as Healthcare and Construction Struggle to Recruit

By Mindnewsng Mindnewsng 6 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Mindnewsng

Norway labour authorities estimate a national shortage of roughly 34,000 workers in 2026, with health and care the largest gap and thousands of unfilled roles i

Norway is facing a national shortage of about 34,000 workers, according to the country’s labour administration, with health and care the largest shortage area and thousands of roles unfilled in construction. The figures come from the 2026 employer survey by NAV, Norway’s labour and welfare authority, which highlights persistent gaps in sectors that keep the country’s public services and building programmes running.

Where the shortage is deepest

Health and care remain the hardest-hit area, reflecting long-standing pressure from an ageing population and rising demand for care services. Construction is another major gap: recent reports put unfilled construction roles at around 4,750, including roughly 1,150 carpenters, as Norway continues large public investment in roads, rail and other infrastructure.

Occupations frequently listed as in demand

  • Healthcare workers and nurses — the largest single cluster of reported shortages.
  • Construction trades — carpenters, electricians and other skilled trades.
  • Industrial and technical roles — engineering and manufacturing positions.
  • IT and skilled professional roles — persistently reported as shortage areas.

What this means — and doesn’t mean

A shortage figure is not the same as an open door. Norway has language expectations (Norwegian is often required, especially in care and regulated health roles), regulated professions that may need recognition of foreign qualifications, and strict documentation standards. Outreach and recruitment do happen, but migrants still need to meet qualification and licensing requirements.

Practical checks for Nigerians

  • Check recognised qualifications: verify whether your qualification needs recognition in Norway, especially for health and regulated trades.
  • Start language early: Norwegian language ability dramatically widens the range of roles you can be considered for.
  • Use official channels: NAV runds public labour-market information; use it, plus embassy and EU-wide EURES resources, rather than unofficial agents.
  • Be wary of “guaranteed jobs” promises on social media — legitimate recruitment does not work that way.

This article is general information, not legal, career or immigration advice. Always confirm current requirements through official Norwegian sources before making decisions.

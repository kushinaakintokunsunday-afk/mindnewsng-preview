Firm targets 10 million Kenyan homes with cooking gas

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Sahara Group, through its subsidiary, Asharami Synergy Kenya Limited, is developing a 30,000-metric-tonne liquefied petroleum gas import, storage and handling t

The development was disclosed in a statement made available to PUNCH Online on Tuesday, which said the facility was scheduled for completion by 2028 and would occupy 23.19 acres at the Kenya Petroleum Refineries Limited site.

According to the statement, the project is expected to support Kenya’s LPG Growth Strategy and wider clean-cooking ambitions.

Speaking at the groundbreaking ceremony, the Country Manager of Asharami Synergy Kenya, Bayo Sotomi, said the additional storage capacity would enable larger LPG cargoes, improve vessel discharge efficiency and help reduce shipment and demurrage costs.

“The 30,000-metric-tonne LPG storage facility is a major economic boost that will accelerate the energy supply chain. It will ensure lower prices for distribution of clean energy in schools and hospitals and restore forests from firewood cutting,” he said.

The statement quoted Sotomi as saying Asharami would work with the government, regulatory authorities and local technical institutions to support internships, industrial attachments and training in LPG handling, engineering and safety.

He added that the terminal could strengthen Mombasa’s role as an LPG gateway for East and Central Africa, serving markets including Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan and eastern Democratic Republic of Congo.

Also speaking, the Principal Secretary for the State Department for Petroleum, Harsama Kello, said the investment would transform the energy sector’s supply chain.

Kello said 70 per cent of Kenyans still relied on kerosene, charcoal and firewood, adding that the government planned to ensure access to clean energy through the National LPG Growth Strategy Programme.

He said the project would create employment opportunities for residents of Changamwe and help lower the cost of cooking gas for households.

“By 2028, we want to make that a reality in homes. Launching Asharami LPG is one of the plans to make Kenya self-sufficient in gas supply,” he said.

The statement also quoted the General Manager of Kenya Petroleum Refineries Limited, Tom Mailu, as saying the company would ensure compliance with environmental requirements and that residents would benefit from the project, which would take 24 months to complete.

The Chair of the KPRL Board, Lillian Mahiri Zaja, said the facility would help protect the company’s assets.

“The future of the energy sector will be shaped by institutions we build for the country and for the future of our people,” Zaja said.

The Director-General of the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority, Edward Kinyua, said the multi-billion-shilling facility would stimulate economic activity.

“We are losing 25,000 lives because of indoor pollution; therefore, this is about saving lives,” Kinyua said, adding that the project supported the government’s plan to move households from biomass to LPG.

The statement added that Sahara Group continued to invest in LPG shipping, storage and distribution infrastructure in Nigeria, Côte d’Ivoire, Senegal, Tanzania and Ghana.

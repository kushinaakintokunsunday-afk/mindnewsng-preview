TikTok deploys AI tools to curb election misinformation

By Punch Punch 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

TikTok is deploying artificial intelligence tools, specialist moderation teams and independent fact-checking partnerships to monitor election-related content in

The measures include an election task force comprising specialists in platform integrity, cybersecurity, deceptive behaviour, misinformation and election integrity, supported by automated moderation systems and local language moderators.

The platform disclosed that its preparations also include collaborations with the Independent National Electoral Commission, the Nigeria Union of Journalists and fact-checking organisations such as Code for Africa and DUBAWA to enhance access to credible electoral information and strengthen the verification of content shared online.

TikTok revealed this in a statement to The PUNCH detailing its election integrity framework for Nigeria, noting that the measures draw on its experience in managing platform-related risks during more than 300 elections worldwide.

The Global Integrity Programmes Lead at TikTok, Lorenzo Andreozzi, said the company was working with electoral authorities, independent fact-checkers, and civil society organisations to address information challenges during the election period.

“As important civic events such as Nigeria’s elections unfold, upholding platform integrity is at the heart of our mission. We recognise that no platform can tackle modern information challenges alone; that is why we actively deepen our partnerships with independent fact-checkers, the Independent National Electoral Commission, and civil society experts such as DUBAWA, ensuring that we lean into local context and understanding of the realities on the ground,” he said.

As part of the initiative, TikTok has introduced an in-app Election Centre in partnership with INEC and DUBAWA, providing users with information on voting procedures, important electoral dates and other details about the election process.

The centre also connects users to an artificial intelligence media literacy hub designed to help them identify AI-generated content, understand responsible AI use, and recognise the platform’s measures for promoting transparency around synthetic media.

The Director of Voter Education and Publicity at INEC, Victoria-Eta Messie, said access to credible electoral information was important for improving voter awareness and understanding of the electoral process.

She said the initiative could help counter misinformation and disinformation, particularly among young people, who constitute a significant segment of the voting population.

TikTok is also working with the NUJ and Code for Africa to train more than 300 media professionals across Lagos, Abuja, Ogun and Oyo states on digital platforms, information verification and responsible online journalism.

The Vice Chairman of the NUJ Lagos State Council, Philip Nwosu, said the training would strengthen journalists’ capacity to provide accurate and credible election coverage.

The platform said it removes content that violates its election misinformation policies, including false claims about voting procedures and accounts impersonating politicians or news organisations.

Where election-related claims cannot immediately be verified, TikTok said it could attach labels identifying the information as unverified, warn users before they share it, and restrict its recommendation on the For You feed.

It also works with more than 20 International Fact-Checking Network-accredited organisations globally, while Code for Africa supports its fact-checking efforts in Nigeria. The organisations assess claims circulating on the platform and provide feedback to inform content moderation decisions.

Between April and June 2026, TikTok said it removed more than 210,000 videos for violating its policies on civic and election integrity, misinformation and harmful misleading AI-generated content. More than 99 per cent of the content was removed before users reported it.

In Nigeria, the platform removed more than four million videos for violating its community guidelines during the second quarter of 2026. It said 98 per cent of the videos were taken down before they were reported, while 91.7 per cent were removed within 24 hours.

The company is also strengthening safeguards around AI-generated content, requiring creators to label such material and deploying tools including Content Credentials, developed under the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity standard, and invisible watermarking technology.

TikTok said more than five billion videos had been labelled through its AI-generated content tools and technologies since the introduction of the labelling feature.

Political advertising is also prohibited on the platform, including paid promotions by creators for politicians and political parties. Accounts belonging to political figures and parties are restricted from advertising and accessing monetisation features, while government and political accounts are subject to additional security requirements, including mandatory two-step verification.

Official election management bodies are, however, permitted to advertise essential voter information, such as voter registration guidance, polling details and participation requirements.

TikTok said it would continue investing in platform security, content moderation and partnerships with local institutions to address emerging threats and provide users with access to reliable information during Nigeria’s electoral process.

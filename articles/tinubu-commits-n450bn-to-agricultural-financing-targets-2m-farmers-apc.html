Tinubu commits N450bn to agricultural financing, targets 2m farmers –APC

The All Progressives Congress (APC) Presidential Campaign Council has said President Bola Tinubu’s administration has committed N450 billion to agricultural financing programmes aimed at boosting domestic food production, improving food security and expanding access to credit for smallholder farmers across the country.

The council also disclosed that the President had approved a N1.5 trillion recapitalisation of the Bank of Agriculture (BOA) to strengthen its capacity to provide affordable financing to farmers, cooperatives, processors and other operators in the agricultural value chain.

In a statement issued by its spokesperson, Dele Alake, the campaign council said more than 500,000 smallholder farmers across 16 states, including over 70,000 women, had benefited from agricultural interventions under the Renewed Hope Agenda, while the government was targeting two million farmers through an expanded support programme.

According to the council, the N450 billion intervention comprises the N250 billion Renewed Hope Smallholder Support and Value Chain Fund and the N200 billion Guaranteed Minimum Price Programme, designed to address farmers’ financing challenges, improve access to agricultural inputs and reduce losses arising from unstable commodity prices.

It explained that the N250 billion fund was being implemented through Farmer Aggregation Companies responsible for coordinating farmer registration, input distribution, extension services and market linkages.

The programme, it said, had facilitated the distribution of approximately 2.5 million bags of fertiliser and 300,000 bags of certified seeds, with plans to expand coverage to two million farmers and distribute more than 10 million bags of fertiliser.

The council maintained that the interventions were particularly important in Northern Nigeria, where agriculture remained a major source of employment and household income.

It listed Kaduna, Kano, Katsina, Niger, Borno, Gombe, Taraba, Adamawa, Yobe and Bauchi among states receiving attention under the agricultural financing programmes.

On efforts to protect farmers against price fluctuations and distress sales, the campaign council said the Bank of Agriculture was implementing the N200 billion Guaranteed Minimum Price Programme, under which approximately 170,000 metric tonnes of maize, rice, sorghum and cassava had been procured.

It explained that the initiative was intended to strengthen agricultural commodity markets and provide farmers with greater certainty over returns on their produce.

The council also highlighted a financing arrangement for onion farmers, developed by the Bank of Agriculture in partnership with the National Onion Producers, Processors and Marketers Association of Nigeria (NOPPMAN), representing more than 11,800 farmers.

Under the Onion Financing Programme, farmers are expected to receive support for improved seeds and fertiliser, while processors would have access to commodity purchase financing and facilities for storage and solar-powered cooling to reduce post-harvest losses.

The arrangement requires participating cooperatives to contribute 30 per cent of their input costs, with the Bank of Agriculture financing the remaining 70 per cent.

On the proposed N1.5 trillion recapitalisation, the APC campaign council said the intervention would strengthen the bank’s financial position and enable it to expand lending to underserved farming communities, agricultural cooperatives, processors and commercial producers.

It described the recapitalisation as part of Tinubu’s commitment to strengthening agricultural financing and transforming the sector into a major contributor to economic growth and employment.

The council further disclosed that the bank was working with the All Farmers Association of Nigeria on a dry-season financing programme targeting 400,000 farmers during the 2026 farming cycle.

The initiative covers a proposed 120,000 hectares of wheat cultivation and another 300,000 hectares for rice, maize, sorghum, millet, cassava and soybeans.

It also cited the Nigeria Ginger Recovery Programme, which it said had supported 3,200 farmers in Kachia, Kaduna State, with blight-free seeds and recovered more than 9,000 bags for multiplication, with plans to extend the intervention to 20,000 hectares across 16 states.

For women farmers, the council said the Bank of Agriculture was targeting at least 50,000 beneficiaries through insured cooperative production financing at an interest rate of nine per cent, in collaboration with the Federal Ministries of Women Affairs and Agriculture.

The statement also disclosed that 1,571 tractors had been deployed to 23 states under the Renewed Hope National Agricultural Mechanisation Programme, with a target of mechanising 1.2 million hectares of farmland through qualified mechanisation service providers.

Other interventions highlighted included a N385 billion productive-use-of-electricity programme involving 44,000 electric cargo tricycles and 100,000 solar irrigation pumps, intended to support approximately 500,000 farming families.

The campaign council maintained that the combined interventions demonstrated the administration’s commitment to addressing agricultural financing constraints, increasing domestic production and strengthening food security.

It said expanding access to credit, improved seeds, fertiliser, mechanisation, irrigation and reliable commodity markets would help farmers increase productivity and improve their incomes.

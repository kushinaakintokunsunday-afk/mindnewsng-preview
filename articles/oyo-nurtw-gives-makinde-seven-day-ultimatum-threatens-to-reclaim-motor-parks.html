Oyo NURTW gives Makinde seven-day ultimatum, threatens to reclaim motor parks

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 4 min read

From Taiwo Oluwadare, Ibadan

The National Union of Road Transport Workers (NURTW), Oyo State chapter, has issued a seven-day ultimatum to Governor Seyi Makinde, declaring that its members will resume full operations at motor parks across the state on November 23, 2026, irrespective of government approval.

The union said the decision followed what it described as seven and a half years of illegal proscription, injustice, hunger, humiliation and the alleged loss of several members since the state government suspended its activities.

In a statement jointly signed by its Chairman, Alhaji Abideen Olajide, popularly known as Ejiogbe, and Secretary, Comrade Lekan Alesinloye, on Sunday, the union described the ultimatum, which runs from November 16 to November 23, as its final warning to the state government.

The union also said it had alerted the Federal Government, the General Officer Commanding (GOC), the Department of State Services (DSS), the Oyo State Commissioner of Police and the Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC) of its intention to enforce a court judgment restoring its operations.

“We are alerting the Federal Government; GOC; Director of DSS and the Commissioner of Police that we will implement the court judgment by ourselves because the State Governor has failed us,” the statement read.

The union added that it was prepared for any eventuality, including the possible declaration of a state of emergency, insisting that it had suffered enough under the present administration.

“We don’t mind if the State of Emergency is declared. We are ready. Enough is enough. We are putting the State NLC on notice too,” the leaders said.

The NURTW said its decision was anchored on the unanimous judgment of the Court of Appeal, Ibadan Division, delivered on September 26, 2025, in Appeal No. CA/IB/263/2022.

In the judgment, Justice Kenneth Amadi held that the state government had failed to justify the suspension of the union’s activities on the grounds of breach of peace, law and order.

The appellate court consequently allowed the appeal, set aside the suspension of the union’s operations in Oyo State and overturned the judgment of the lower court.

The union said it had obtained the Certified True Copy of the judgment, dated October 3, 2025, but accused the Makinde administration of failing to implement the court’s decision.

It also cited a Notice of Preliminary Objection dated April 23, 2026, filed by the Attorney-General and Commissioner for Justice, Mr Abiodun Aikomo, in which the state government reportedly stated that it had not done anything to prevent the NURTW from operating in the state.

“With the Attorney-General now on record that government is not preventing NURTW from operating, we expect full compliance without further delay, victimisation or harassment of our members at motor parks,” the union said.

However, the NURTW accused the Attorney-General of misrepresenting the appellate court’s judgment during a recent multi-stakeholder meeting convened by the Commissioner of Police following the union’s request for security protection ahead of its planned return to the parks.

The union said it had earlier written to the Attorney-General through Falana & Falana’s Chambers in a letter dated June 26, 2026, describing his interpretation of the judgment as a calculated misrepresentation of a binding court decision.

It maintained that the appellate court’s decision setting aside the suspension of its operations was sufficient to restore its operational status without requiring another court order.

Citing Section 287(2) of the 1999 Constitution, the union argued that all authorities in the country were constitutionally bound to enforce decisions of the Court of Appeal.

It warned that any continued obstruction of its members’ activities would amount to deliberate and continuing contempt of court.

The union consequently demanded that the Attorney-General withdraw his interpretation of the judgment, advise the state government and persons acting as park managers to vacate the motor parks, and publicly acknowledge the binding nature of the appellate court’s decision.

It also called on the Commissioner of Police to provide immediate security for its members, direct Area Commanders and Divisional Police Officers to ensure compliance with the judgment, and arrest anyone found obstructing its activities.

Meanwhile, the union raised concerns over an alleged acquisition of firearms and ammunition by the Park Management System (PMS) to confront its members during their planned resumption.

“We have it on good authority that the PMS in the state have acquired guns to fight our union members. We are ready for them,” the union leaders alleged.

The NURTW insisted that its planned return to the motor parks was not an attempt to foment violence but an effort to resume lawful activities following the appellate court’s decision.

It described its members as breadwinners who deserved dignity and the opportunity to earn a living, rather than continued exclusion from their means of livelihood.

“An injustice to one is an injustice to all,” the union leaders declared, adding that “enough is enough.”

EU And UK Eye ‘Reset’ Deal At Burnham’s Brussels Summit

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham and the EU’s top officials were set to announce deals to ease trade and youth mobility at a Brussels summit Friday, billed as a first step in a push to rebuild ties after Brexit.

But in a potential spoiler for Burnham’s “reset”, Brussels said the deals coming into force would be conditional on reaching an agreement to cut university fees for EU students in Britain.

Since taking power this summer, the prime minister has smashed through the taboos in Britain’s toxic debate over relations with Europe — going as far as to float rejoining the bloc one day.

For now, the summit with EU chiefs Ursula von der Leyen and Antonio Costa will focus on much smaller steps to help turn the page a decade after the UK voted to leave — while Burnham saves for later the bigger decisions on what could come next.

“Britain and Europe share values, interests and a global neighbourhood. In a more dangerous world, we are stronger together. A closer partnership with Europe is good for working people, good for Britain and good for Europe,” a statement from Burnham said as he arrived in Brussels.

On the table in Brussels are three main agreements: a deal cutting red tape for food exports, another linking the two sides’ emissions-trading schemes and a third on a “youth experience scheme”.

Burnham argues those moves — which were in the pipeline long before he entered Downing Street — will help fix some of the damage Brexit inflicted.

British firms will export to Europe more easily again, and 100,000 under-30s from each side will be allowed to reside for up to three years in each other’s territory for work, study or travel.

But while the mood music at the meeting may be much improved, old habits still die hard.

Brussels and London wrangled ferociously down to the wire over the details.

One key sticking point was pinning London down to a future agreement on cutting fees for EU students at UK universities to the level paid by their British counterparts — with the EU making the other details entering into force contingent on striking a deal.

“We have not yet reached an agreement on tuition fees, and we have given ourselves six months to reach that agreement,” said an EU official, adding that the three core deals would be signed “in parallel” with those talks.

‘Tread Carefully’

The big question that the Brussels meeting won’t answer is what Burnham plans next.

“After the summit, we will initiate a discussion with our EU partners about options for our long-term relationship,” Burnham said earlier this week.

Having previously said he’d like to see Britain rejoin the EU in his lifetime, the former Manchester mayor has promised to lay out options that could range from leaving things as they are to entering the customs union or becoming a member again.

The strategy is in part because polls now regularly indicate most Britons regret Brexit and would vote to re-enter the bloc if a referendum were held tomorrow.

It is also reflective of Britain’s weak economy, damaged by Brexit, and how much the world has changed since 2016, with Europe firmly behind Ukraine in its war against Russian aggression.

Burnham hopes friendlier EU relations will help build a broad centre-left coalition to win the next general election, not anticipated until 2029.

But the strategy comes with huge risks, not least because his own party — never mind the country — is divided on how to proceed.

For now, the prime minister appears to have tempered his early urgency.

“I’m happy with closer ties, but he does need to tread carefully,” one Labour MP told AFP, speaking on condition of anonymity.

Burnham’s push leaves him open to accusations of Brexit betrayal from the opposition Conservatives and hard-right Reform UK party of Nigel Farage — who will give his own press conference in Brussels Friday.

In Brussels meanwhile, there is little expectation the UK will return to the fold any time soon.

“I doubt he’d take the risk of a major step that would be so radioactive,” said one senior EU diplomat.

“He’ll probably keep on trying to nibble away around the edges to improve ties — and we are open to having as close a relationship as possible.”

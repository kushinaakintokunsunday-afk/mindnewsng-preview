British Council awards grants to seven Nigerians for community projects

By Punch Punch 1d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The British Council has awarded grants of up to £5,000 each to seven Nigerians to implement community projects addressing flooding, electronic waste management,

The projects, which will run for five months, include training young women to collect flood-related data in Bayelsa State, equipping solar technicians in Rivers State to safely manage discarded solar batteries and providing learning support to disadvantaged girls in Lagos and Imo states.

According to a statement by the British Council on Monday, the grants were announced in Lagos on Thursday November 19, under the 2026–27 Alumni UK Grant Programme.

The awards were presented by its Deputy Country Director in Nigeria, Chilufya Besa, who said the funding was tied to specific projects and measurable outcomes.

“These are not personal awards. The money is tied to a project, an agreed budget and clear results,” the statement quoted Besa as saying.

It added that the beneficiaries would be required to report on what they delivered, the people reached and the lessons learnt from implementing their projects.

“We are not simply recognising seven people for what they have already achieved. We are backing them to do something practical,” he said.

Four of the projects focus on climate action and environmental sustainability, while three address inclusion, reducing inequality and empowerment.

In Bayelsa State, May Tamara Stow will train 40 young women aged 16 to 24 in Otuoke to collect, analyse and present flood-related data from more than 200 households.

The project, which will be implemented in partnership with the Federal University Otuoke and community leaders, will also produce community flood-preparedness stories and a practical toolkit to help households prepare for flooding.

In Rivers State, Peter Abiye Banigo’s Solar Second Life Project will train 30 informal-sector solar technicians, including women and young people, to repair, restore and safely manage discarded solar equipment and lithium batteries.

The initiative will also develop open-access training materials and establish a community recovery network in partnership with a licensed electronic waste recycler.

Another beneficiary, Adelopo Abdulganiyu Omobolaji, will implement CircuRise in Lagos, targeting 100 informal waste workers at selected dumpsites and waste-picker communities.

The project will equip participants with digital and carbon literacy skills while testing multilingual learning tools, artificial intelligence-assisted waste identification and mobile reporting systems.

In Abuja, Bethel Ikenna Okeke’s Green Cycle Initiative will support 100 adolescent girls and young women aged 15 to 24 in the Durumi and Lugbe camps for internally displaced persons.

The initiative will provide menstrual health education and training in reusable sanitary pad production, entrepreneurship and financial literacy, alongside mentorship and business support to help participants establish small enterprises.

Under the inclusion theme, Charity K. Usifoh-Chenge will expand The Next Hundred Public Health Stewards programme to Gombe and Nasarawa states, supporting between 40 and 50 young people and early-career professionals through training and mentorship.

The project will focus on women and underserved groups, drawing on the programme’s 2025 evaluation to guide possible expansion across the country.

David Chukwuma Izuogu’s Exam Equity for Girls will support 150 girls from low-income families in two underserved secondary schools in Lagos and Imo states.

Led by Africa of Our Dream Education Initiative in partnership with Aspiralane, the project will combine offline-accessible digital learning, artificial intelligence-supported tutoring, female mentorship in science, technology, engineering and mathematics, and scholarship guidance.

It will also provide teachers with support to sustain digital learning beyond the project period.

The seventh initiative, Bridging Digital Divide for Equal Disability Employment, led by Opeolu Stephen Akinola, will equip 50 undergraduates and graduates with visual, hearing and physical disabilities with digital and employment skills.

Implemented by Accesstech Innovation and Research Centre in partnership with six organisations of persons with disabilities, the project will provide training in digital literacy, assistive technologies, CV preparation and interview skills.

Training materials will be available in Braille, large print and screen-reader-compatible formats, with sign language interpretation also provided.

Speaking on the rationale for the grants, Besa acknowledged that the funding would not independently resolve the challenges being addressed but could help beneficiaries test their ideas and attract further support.

“A grant of up to £5,000 will not solve a national problem. It can, however, test an idea,” the statement quoted him as saying.

He added that climate change affected several aspects of people’s lives beyond the environment, including employment, healthcare, energy, transportation and the ability of communities to withstand economic pressure.

“Climate change is not only about the environment. It affects jobs, health, energy, transport and the ability of communities to cope with economic pressure,” he said.

On social inclusion, Besa said it was important to ensure that people could access education, technology, employment and healthcare and participate fully in society.

“Inclusion is about whether people can enter a classroom, use technology, find work, access healthcare and participate properly in society,” he added.

The seven Nigerian projects are part of a wider British Council initiative supporting 61 alumni-led projects across 15 countries. Of these, 35 focus on climate action and sustainability, while 26 address inclusion, inequality and empowerment.

The statement said applicants in Nigeria were required to be Nigerian nationals living in the country, have studied at a UK institution for at least one term and be independent of political affiliations.

It added that the seven beneficiaries emerged through a competitive selection process.

Explaining the importance of the programme to Nigeria-UK relations, Besa cited British Council research involving more than 4,000 international alumni of UK institutions.

According to him, the research found that nearly three-quarters of the alumni had maintained contact with people they met while studying in the UK, while more than half of the alumni entrepreneurs surveyed were collaborating or trading with the UK.

“A UK education, therefore, does not end at graduation,” he said.

The British Council said it would work with the beneficiaries throughout the five-month implementation period, with a global showcase of the projects scheduled for April 2027.

It also said the recipients would be expected to provide honest accounts of their achievements and challenges during implementation.

“Honest evidence is more useful than a success story that leaves out the difficult parts,” Besa said.

He urged organisations to support the initiatives through technical advice, access to communities, equipment, data and other forms of collaboration.

“Not every partnership begins with a large cheque. Sometimes, the right connection is what allows a good idea to move forward,” he said.

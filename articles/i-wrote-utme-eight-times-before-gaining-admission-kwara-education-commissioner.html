I wrote UTME eight times before gaining admission – Kwara education commissioner

By Punch Punch 5d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Kwara State Commissioner for Education and Human Capital Development, Dr Lawal Olohungbebe, has revealed that he sat for the Unified Tertiary Matriculation

Olohungbebe disclosed this while recounting his academic journey in a video obtained from the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board’s X handle on Thursday, which he said spanned eight years, from 1997 to 2005, before he finally secured admission into the university.

Reflecting on his experience, the commissioner said his repeated attempts did not stop him from pursuing his educational ambitions, adding that he has risen to become the Kwara State Commissioner for Education and Chairman of Commissioners of Education in Nigeria.

“I wrote UTME eight times before I passed. From 1997 to 2005, and to God be the glory, I’m the commissioner today and the chairman of all commissioners of education in Nigeria,” he said.

The commissioner also cautioned prospective university students against accepting admission offers outside the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board’s (JAMB) Central Admissions Processing System (CAPS), insisting that the platform remains the recognised channel for confirming admission offers.

“Anything that falls out of CAPS, forget about it, it’s not going to happen. It is only JAMB, through CAPS, that can offer you admission that you can be rest assured that yes, you have admission. Anything outside that, forget about it, it’s not going to work,” Olohungbebe stated.

Speaking about his academic qualifications, he disclosed that he gained admission into the University of Maiduguri, where he studied Sociology and Anthropology.

He subsequently studied Social Work and Community Development at Ladoke Akintola University of Technology before proceeding to obtain a Bachelor of Laws (LLB) degree. He also earned a PhD in Community Development and Adult Education from Bayero University, Kano.

Olohungbebe’s account highlights his prolonged struggle to gain university admission and his subsequent academic progression, which he described as a journey that eventually led him into public service.

His experience also serves as a reminder to prospective students that setbacks in the pursuit of education do not necessarily mean the end of their academic ambitions.

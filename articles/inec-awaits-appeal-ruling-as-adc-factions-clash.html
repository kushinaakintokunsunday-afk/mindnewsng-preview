INEC awaits appeal ruling as ADC factions clash

By Punch Punch 4d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Independent National Electoral Commission has said its position on the leadership crisis rocking the African Democratic Congress will be guided by the outco

However, the Nafiu Bala Gombe-led faction has urged the commission to immediately comply with the subsisting judgment of the Federal High Court by recognising its leadership and removing the names of Mark and former Minister of Interior, Rauf Aregbesola, from its records.

INEC National Commissioner, Mohammed Haruna, disclosed the commission’s position to The PUNCH on Wednesday, saying it had been formally served notice of the appeal and would await the appellate court’s decision before determining its next course of action.

“The case is already in the Court of Appeal; they’ve already appealed. We’ve been served notice of the appeal, so we can’t pretend that we don’t know what is happening,” Haruna said.

He added, “So, that in itself serves in a way, as a kind of scale, so we’ll have to await the outcome of the Court of Appeal.”

However, counsel to Gombe, whose suit led to the judgment against the Mark-led leadership, insisted that INEC should comply with the subsisting court orders rather than await further correspondence from the commission.

One of Gombe’s lawyers, Senior Advocate of Nigeria, Haruna Musa, told The PUNCH that INEC had yet to respond to a letter written by the factional chairman.

Asked whether the commission had responded to the letter, Musa replied, “Not yet.” He, however, maintained that the commission was not required to respond to the letter but to implement the court’s directives.

“They don’t need to reply but to comply with the orders of the court,” he said.

Asked where Gombe was likely to operate from, amid allegations that the ADC national headquarters building belonged to former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar, Musa said, “Ask him (Nafiu Bala), we have done our bit.”

Meanwhile, the Gombe-led Board of Trustees of the ADC has asked INEC to immediately enforce the November 5, 2026 judgment of the Federal High Court concerning the party’s leadership dispute.

The board also called on the commission to remove the names of Mark and Aregbesola from its records as the party’s leaders and delist candidates uploaded to its portal by the Mark-led leadership.

The BoT, in a statement issued by its Chairman, Chief Rufus Ikanmi, said the judgment delivered by Justice Peter Lifu in Suit No. FHC/ABJ/CS/1819/2025, filed by Hon. Nafi’u Bala Gombe against the ADC and four others, remained valid and binding.

Ikanmi said, “The judgment of the Honourable Court is valid, binding, and subsisting. It has not been stayed, varied, or set aside by any superior court. INEC, as a statutory body bound by the rule of law, has a constitutional duty to give full effect to it without further delay.”

He urged the commission to recognise and deal only with the leadership headed by Gombe, which he said was affirmed by the court.

He specifically asked INEC to immediately remove the names of the Mark- and Aregbesola-led leadership from its records as leaders of the ADC and delist all candidates uploaded to its portal by the faction.

The board argued that the uploads were made in violation of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria (as Amended), the Electoral Act 2022 and the party’s constitution.

It also urged former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar and other persons allegedly acting with the Mark-led leadership to suspend campaign activities, rallies and public engagements organised in the name of the ADC pending compliance with the judgment.

“Any continued defiance of the judgment of the Federal High Court amounts to contempt of court and a direct assault on constitutional democracy and the rule of law,” Ikanmi said.

The board called on party members and supporters to remain calm and law-abiding, adding that it was committed to peace, unity and the constitutional order of the party.

The dispute followed a judgment of the Federal High Court in Abuja, which nullified the leadership of the ADC led by Mark and invalidated decisions taken under the caretaker committee, including the process that produced Atiku as the party’s presidential candidate for the 2027 general elections.

Justice Lifu declared the Mark-led Caretaker Committee illegal and nullified the process through which it conducted the party’s presidential primary.

The court also invalidated the emergence of Aregbesola as National Secretary, alongside the appointments of other national officers and the nominations of candidates presented by the party for the 2027 elections.

The judgment followed a suit marked FHC/ABJ/CS/1819/2025, instituted by Gombe, a former Deputy National Chairman of the ADC (North-East), who challenged the process through which Mark and other members of the caretaker committee assumed control of the party.

Gombe argued that following the resignation of the former National Chairman, Ralph Nwosu, he was constitutionally entitled to take over the party’s leadership.

He consequently asked the court to invalidate the actions of the Mark-led leadership and restrain Mark and Aregbesola from parading themselves as National Chairman and National Secretary of the party, respectively.

Upholding Gombe’s case, Justice Lifu held that the emergence of the Mark-led leadership violated the ADC constitution, relevant provisions of the Electoral Act and the 1999 Constitution.

The judge also ordered INEC not to recognise or act on decisions taken at the party’s 99th National Executive Committee meeting held on July 27, 2025.

However, the Mark-led ADC has appealed the judgment and disclosed that it had filed an application for a stay of execution pending the hearing and determination of the appeal.

A report by Arise News on Thursday, citing a senior official of the commission, said the continued listing of the candidates was connected to a subsisting Notice of Appeal and an application for a Stay of Execution relating to the judgment.

The report added that the commission could not disregard the pending appellate proceedings while considering requests for recognition arising from the ruling.

Efforts to reach the Chief Press Secretary to the INEC Chairman, Dayo Oketola, for comments were unsuccessful as of the time of filing this report.

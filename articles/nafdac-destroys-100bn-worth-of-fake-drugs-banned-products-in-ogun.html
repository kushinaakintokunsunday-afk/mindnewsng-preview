NAFDAC Destroys ₦100bn Worth Of Fake Drugs, Banned Products In Ogun

By Channels TV Channels TV 3d ago 3 min read

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has destroyed fake, banned, expired, substandard and falsified products worth an estimated ₦100 billion in Ogun State.

The destruction exercise was carried out on Friday, 20 November 2026, at Ogwama Dumpsite, Ogere Remo, as part of the agency’s efforts to prevent unsafe and unregistered products from re-entering circulation.

Speaking at the flag-off of the exercise, NAFDAC’s Director-General, Mojisola Adeyeye, said the products included banned and unregistered medicines, alcoholic beverages in sachets and PET bottles below 200ml, banned Analgin injections, expired Codeine cough syrups, Oxytocin injections, counterfeit cosmetics and unwholesome food products.

She said the destruction was necessary to eliminate the risk of the products finding their way back into the Nigerian market.

“As the lead agency and chair of the Federal Task Force on Fake and Counterfeit Drugs and Unwholesome Processed Foods, NAFDAC is resolutely committed to ensuring that food, drugs, cosmetics, medical devices, chemicals, packaged water and drinks are safe, wholesome and effective for human consumption,” she said.

Adeyeye explained that the products were recovered during enforcement operations across the country. They also included items voluntarily surrendered by compliant companies and pharmaceutical products handed over to the agency by the Nigeria Customs Service (NCS).

She disclosed that NAFDAC operatives recently uncovered illegally manufactured cosmetics and unregistered pharmaceutical products in a warehouse in Lagos.

According to her, the facility, which was meant for tools and hardware, had a penthouse where cosmetic products were being manufactured. Officers also recovered several cartons of Augmentin and Ampicillin packets, leaflets and other unregistered cosmetics.

The agency also uncovered a facility where counterfeit versions of popular alcoholic beverage brands were being produced, while expired energy drinks were allegedly being revalidated and stored for distribution.

Adeyeye said the suspects arrested during the operations would face the full wrath of the law.

She added that NAFDAC officers, working with relevant authorities, had coordinated the removal of more than 100,000 cartons of banned alcoholic beverages in sachets and PET bottles from manufacturers, wholesalers and retailers in Lagos and Ogun states.

The Director-General further disclosed that officers uncovered a residential building being used to manufacture and sell large quantities of unauthorised and unregistered herbal mixtures.

Adeyeye commended the Nigeria Customs Service for its collaboration, particularly the handover of pharmaceutical products, including cartons of Codeine-containing cough syrups, which were among the items destroyed.

She also acknowledged the contributions of the Nigeria Police Force, Nigerian Army, Department of State Services, Pharmacists Council of Nigeria, National Drug Law Enforcement Agency and Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, as well as other stakeholders.

The NAFDAC chief urged community and religious leaders, health practitioners, the media and members of the public to educate their families and communities about the dangers of patronising unlicensed vendors and unauthorised drug sellers.

She called on Nigerians to support the agency’s enforcement efforts by providing credible information about the production, sale and distribution of fake, unregistered and other violative products.

“We seek your continued cooperation in providing valuable information to help us prevent and ultimately eradicate unregistered, dangerous and other violative products from our society. Together, we can protect the health and safety of all Nigerians,” she said.

We focus on things that matter to Edo; I’m ready to supervise execution of convicted kidnappers — Okpebholo

By Ozioruva Aliu 

GOVERNOR Monday Okpebholo just celebrated his administration’s second year anniversary in Edo State. In this interview with some editors in his office in Benin-City, Okpebholo speaks about his administration’s commitment to good governance for the people and how he wants to leave the state among other issues.

How do you assess your administration so far?

Construction is ongoing all over the state. Road construction is ongoing. Schools are being constructed. Also construction is ongoing in the primary healthcare area, we are also doing marvelously well in that sector. There are many areas that have been neglected in the past where you can’t even remember that roads ever existed in some of these places but we are working there. I can remember vividly that when you were coming from Abuja, from Auchi, then Ewu to Ekpoma, that road was completely washed off. I spoke to the Minister of Works to please allow me do this work so that I could be reimbursed. He approved it and talked to Mr. President and Mr. President agreed that I should do it and then forward the bill, we are already on that road. I really don’t talk much because I know I am saving my energy to work. For me, talking is not the business, working is the business.

So I prefer doing the job than talking so that when you come you see. When they are crying, shouting on social media, it doesn’t bother me because the people that live in those areas know that work is ongoing. We are also working in the villages because there are also human beings there and they also contribute to the economy. If they don’t go to the farm, where will you see food to eat and when you don’t eat, there will be hostility. That is the truth and that is why I feel that everybody should be carried along. There are villages that you never think of, I will just go there and do my work. I don’t need any politician to lobby me. I don’t need any influencer to lobby me, all I need to do is just to do the will and the wish of the people.

Is it the policy of the state government now that your roads will be concrete pavement?

We are not only just doing the concrete pavement, one of the reasons we are doing it is because of the swampy nature of many of our areas. We are in the rainforest zone and we need to sustain the roads. We have carefully studied to see that without concrete pavement, we will have problems within a short time. We are preparing for the future so that by the time when this government leaves, the next government will come and concentrate on other areas so that development will spread.

Look, we are not only working on infrastructure, we are also working on security. We are trying to make Edo very safe, safer than we met it. When we came in, within a week I recorded about 11 people dead not in an accident, somebody will get into a shop and kill the person there, somebody will just see someone on the road and shoot him. Why? The killers were cultists. I remember when the BBC guys came here and asked: Do you fear them? I said “I don’t fear because I am working for the people and I want to save Edo people”.

Today, you can hardly hear somebody die as a result of cult issues because we took a stiff measure that when you kill, we go to your family house or your personal house or wherever you live, whether you left your hotel to that place, I will demolish that hotel. If you leave your father’s house to that place to kill, I will demolish your father’s house. So these are stiff measures we have taken that are really working for us and, to be honest with you, if not that we have done that, maybe you won’t be able to stay in Benin for two nights.

Today nightlife is back, business is booming at night, even supermarkets are beginning to open at night; in the next few weeks, it will be safer such that everywhere, at every corner of the state, there will be the police to watch everyone. They will be equipped even with motorbikes so that when somebody thinks he can run, then they will follow the person. Whether you take a bush path, they will follow you up. So we are prepared to secure the city. We are prepared to secure the state.

Are you ready for state police?

Yes, we are prepared. We were one of the first states to pass the bill; when others were talking, we said no, it is working for us and, right now, to be honest with you, even with our vigilantes, we already have state police because it is working for us. Edo is safe.

Remember there was this kidnapping that happened and within 24 hours which has never happened in Nigeria, we got the guys, so that is it. We are even prepared to go to other states when they take any of our citizens. We did it in Kogi, then something happened in Ogun, I called the governor, I said, “Please, I don’t want my person to stay with this kidnapper for more than five days”, and, as God would have it, he was able to get them out within a short time. So somebody has to be prudent with everything.

How are you managing the burden of debt servicing versus project implementation in the state?

Many times when I say I am rooting for Mr. President’s 2.5 million votes, people wonder why I am saying this. It is because of the evidence of what we are seeing today. The FAAC allocation has increased and our own internally generated revenue has also increased. So what we are doing is prudent spending. We focus on things that matter to the people and we are not interested in ourselves, so what matters to the people is what we spend money on.

Is there any plan to float an airline considering the traffic coming in and out of the state?

We are already on it. If you look at the newspapers a few days ago, there was widespread publication on Edo Air; so, very soon, you will see it.

There is the challenge of gully erosion in several places in the state. What is your government doing about this?

We have actually set up an ecological agency to handle such cases. We have to do our best to see who will secure our place for the teachers.

You recently employed some teachers, but there is still shortage of teachers in several schools. What are you doing about this?

We are still going to hire more teachers to be honest with you, because when we were growing up, if there were no teachers, we wouldn’t be where we are today. So, the education for the children is very key and that is one of the reasons when I came in I was so interested to see that we fixed our educational system.

There was EdoBEST but it was EdoBEST for Obaseki and not for Edo people. That is the truth and they just used that to take away Edo money because when I looked at the system called EdoBEST based on what I saw, it was just like a tablet and, first of all, before you could even use the tablet, there should be internet which is 3 or 4 5G but maybe the children were just buying little airtime and load on their phones to operate it. And the curriculum they were using was actually not our own curriculum. It was Kenya and I frowned at it and so I stopped it and some governors that were convinced to do it but didn’t know what it was all about now got in touch with me and asked me how I got out of it. In Edo, we are concentrating on a school access programme and, for me, that is the way to go because, first of all, you need classrooms for the children. They should have at least water in school, they should have toilets and they should also have teachers.

So once you have all these on ground children want to go there. Now in Edo State, many children don’t want to go to private schools, they want to come into government schools because the basic amenities for them are being provided and teachers are also there.

What is the situation of those recently sentenced to death for kidnapping?

I pray our brothers are not involved in kidnapping because I will sign the death warrant, even if it is my father, I will sign it. The truth is this, we just have to get some bad elements out of the system for the system to stand, and these people have been killing people. Just look at the way they were pushing that woman that day, supposing she died? Because of fear, people have gone through trauma.

Even our chief judge, if you see him, one of his fingers was cut off by kidnappers. I am just waiting, but a few days ago I asked my Attorney General “what happened? I am waiting for the paper”, and he said no, that they have appealed. We still follow them up on the appeal, we won’t just leave them to go like that, we’ll get to wherever they stop, we’ll take them from there, we will not spare anybody. If I see this paper today now I will sign it and I will even supervise the killing.

What are your plans to take the state from subsistence farming considering its potentials and your plans for the health sector?

 I remember when I met Mr. President in France, I was showing him in my phone what we have done with the primary health care, and he was so happy and very thankful to God for the time that we have stayed in office and what we have done in terms of health care. Apart from rebuilding the health care system, we are also providing free treatment for patients especially on malaria.

We are also doing a lot in the area of secondary healthcare, trying to source for equipment and build more facilities. I prefer practical governance. That is why when I came, I told the people I was going to provide them with practical governance which is what you are seeing. We have completed over 82 primary health care centres across local government areas, across all the communities. In agriculture, naturally Edo State is a home for food. Apart from the mechanization equipment that we are providing, we are also encouraging the people to go into farming.

The Commissioner for Agriculture yesterday sent me a design on producing our own rice. We have very big farms in Edo North and Edo Central. We have rice farm, we have corn farm, we have cassava farm, our rice farm is over 57 acres, we’re expanding, the corn farm to about 400 acres. So we are doing well in terms of agriculture. I am not talking about the private sector people who are coming in every day to ask for land for farming and they are doing well too. Presco is here, Okomu is there and there are many other private companies. We are also supporting our farmers.

What are you doing to make the local governments replicate what you are doing in the state in their localities?

 The first day I met Mr. President, he told me about local governments and he was so particular about them. He said that local government is the closest to the grassroots, which I believe is true. I can tell you that since I came into office, I have never had any JAC meeting with local governments, and I have never sent my Commissioner for Finance or anybody to tell them “Look, I need this money from you, let’s do this joint project together”. No, it has never happened. Ask any of the local government chairmen.

When we came in, they were all PDP, so you can ask the opposition whether we have done that. I have not done that and I don’t intend to touch their money. They are doing very well. Do you know that they task themselves? They build and renovate schools, even primary health centers across the 18 local governments and they are paying salaries. They are not owing anybody. So they are also doing other things. Like a few months ago, I commissioned earth moving equipment. So they are introducing maintenance agencies back to local governments. To me it is key. It reduces spending, they can also be focused. They can call their own maintenance agencies to where they are needed to work. The day I was sworn into office, the first thing that came to my mind was that I realised that out of four years a day is gone. So there is no time to waste. That is why you see what we are doing and we continue to do it. We are hungry to work.

In the face of all that you are doing, how do you feel when you go to bed for the day?

I feel satisfied that I have done my best and I just know that this is a task that I have to carry and I have to fulfill my promise to the people. When I went out to campaign to the people and tell them, “Look, when I win, I will do this”, they thought “when you win, you turn your back to them”. But I don’t know how I will feel turning my back at them.

Sometimes when I go around the city and they start giving me names like Okpe’s Baby, I feel like crying because I think I don’t deserve it; after all, I am using Edo money to work for them, it is the taxpayer’s money.

How were you able to navigate your administration especially considering your claim that your predecessor did not give you any hand over note?

First of all, let me tell you that winning an election is to me a privilege from God and also the people were expecting so much from me because of the trust they have reposed in me. I asked for the job. And my goal is to do the job. So whatever I am doing, I am here to serve. I am not here just because I want to be a fine boy. No, I want to serve the people. And that is my aim and that is my goal. I think it will be more honourable to me that when I leave government, I should be remembered for the things I did and not the amount of money I have.

When I was growing up, I kept hearing about Professor Ambrose Alli, now that I have grown up, I still see Alli’s sticker on vehicles. It is not Alli that printed those stickers but his fans; so, these are the things I want to be remembered for. By the time I am no longer in this world, people should still remember me and also put my sticker somewhere.

Real estate experts task Nigerian govts on housing crisis

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 1d ago 5 min read

…demand active market intervention

By Bianca Iboma-Emefu

Real estate professionals have called on federal and state governments to move beyond passive regulation and take deliberate steps to address Nigeria’s widening housing deficit, rising rents and declining public confidence in the property market.

The experts, who spoke at the third edition of the CWA Real Estate Academy in Lagos, argued that government must deploy its control over land allocation, title processing, planning approvals, infrastructure and housing incentives to influence market outcomes and make accommodation more affordable.

Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of West Norwood Realty, Alfred Osagie, said the housing crisis required governments to adopt a proactive approach rather than merely react to market developments.

Osagie, a senior real estate surveyor and facility manager, maintained that rising rents were symptoms of deeper structural problems that required a proper understanding of market behaviour, supply gaps and emerging trends.

“Rent is only a symptom. Value is a symptom of the behavioural pattern in the market,” he said, stressing that real estate professionals must understand the factors driving the market and identify gaps that government intervention could address.

He proposed that governments designate specific portions of land exclusively for social housing and provide developers operating in the segment with reduced entry costs, tax incentives and access to cheaper building materials.

He also recommended linking cement manufacturers’ corporate social responsibility programmes to discounted supplies for social housing projects, arguing that such incentives could reduce construction costs and improve affordability for low-income earners.

To prevent abuse, Osagie called for strict residency requirements to ensure that beneficiaries of subsidised housing were genuinely low-income households rather than property investors or individuals who already owned homes.

He further urged Lagos and Ogun state governments to strengthen collaboration on infrastructure development, particularly along their shared boundaries, where growing populations face inadequate transport links and limited access to essential institutions and commercial facilities.

According to him, improved road and rail connectivity would enable more workers to live in relatively affordable communities outside Lagos while maintaining access to employment opportunities in the state.

Also speaking, Akinropo Abraham, a registered estate surveyor and fellow, said neither government nor private developers could independently resolve Nigeria’s housing challenges.

He advocated a coordinated approach that would combine increased housing supply with affordability measures, including the release of properly titled government land, mandatory social housing components in private developments and faster processing of land titles.

Abraham also called on major employers to invest in staff housing, noting that such developments could contribute to the country’s overall housing stock.

Similarly, Akin Opatola identified insecure land titles, inadequate infrastructure and low purchasing power among working Nigerians as major obstacles to housing delivery.

He observed that much of the country’s land remained without secure title documentation, limiting its use as collateral and restricting access to development finance.

Opatola also criticised the practice of selling land before providing essential infrastructure, warning that the disconnect between property development and infrastructure delivery continued to undermine the sector.

He cautioned that without deliberate measures to address these structural weaknesses, housing demand would continue to outstrip supply, putting further pressure on rents and property prices.

Beyond government intervention, the experts identified poor professional standards and inadequate understanding of the real estate value chain as factors contributing to public mistrust.

Senior Operations Administration and Project Lead at CWA, Aramide Olopo, said the industry needed to address the misconception that advertising properties on social media automatically qualified individuals to operate as real estate professionals.

She explained that the academy was designed to expose participants to the different disciplines involved in property transactions and development, including quantity surveying, architecture, town planning, law and project management.

According to Olopo, the programme brings together emerging practitioners and experienced professionals to deepen their understanding of ethics, market intelligence, negotiation, risk management and the legal dimensions of real estate transactions.

She added that technology and artificial intelligence should strengthen professional judgement and operational efficiency rather than replace the expertise required to deliver credible services.

Osagie also called for mandatory periodic ethics training and assessments for members of regulatory institutions, including the Lagos State Real Estate Regulatory Authority (LASRERA), to strengthen accountability and restore public confidence.

He argued that continuous professional development should form an essential part of regulatory oversight, citing the standards maintained by the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors as a model.

The third edition of the CWA Real Estate Academy, held from November 18 to 20, 2026, at the CW Real Estate headquarters in Ikoyi, Lagos, was themed “The Modern Real Estate Playbook.”

The three-day programme brought together professionals from the real estate, investment, legal, construction, technology and business sectors to examine the challenges and opportunities shaping the industry.

Discussions on the first day focused on market realities, property valuation, identifying investment opportunities, deal fundamentals, sales and negotiation.

The second day examined transaction security, legal due diligence, property documentation, professional responsibility and risk management, alongside the growing application of artificial intelligence and digital tools in real estate operations.

The final day addressed business structures, financing, operational systems, customer experience and sustainable growth, including how real estate companies could build organisations capable of expanding beyond their founders.

The programme also featured an interactive session, “Ask Delani,” with CW Real Estate Founder and Director, Delani Adewuyi, who shared insights on leadership, career development, professional reputation and building businesses for long-term value.

Other speakers included Adeniji Adele, Munachi Ejinkeonye, Yemi Stephens, Tope Adekoya, Adekunle Osude, Olamiju Olayemi, Ijeoma Odunukwe, Mehrez Boutros, Oladapo Runsewe, Tola Akinhanmi, Olamide Oshikoya, Ife Bakare, Jonathan Millard, TPL Rasheed Osinowo and QS John Agbezin.

The discussions underscored the need for stronger collaboration between government and private sector operators, improved land administration, infrastructure investment and stricter professional standards.

The experts maintained that addressing Nigeria’s housing crisis would require more than increasing property transactions, insisting that deliberate public policies and responsible industry practices were necessary to expand access to decent and affordable housing.

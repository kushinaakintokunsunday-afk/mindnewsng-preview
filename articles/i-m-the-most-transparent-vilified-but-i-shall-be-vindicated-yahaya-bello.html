I’m The Most Transparent, Vilified But I Shall Be Vindicated — Yahaya Bello

By Channels TV Channels TV 3d ago 3 min read

Photo: Channels TV

Former Kogi State Governor, Yahaya Bello, has expressed confidence that he will eventually be vindicated over the allegations of financial misconduct levelled against him by the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC).

He also described himself as the most transparent individual.

“I am the most transparent and the most vilified. But at the end of the day, I shall be vindicated,” Bello said on Channels Television’s Politics Today on Friday.

Bello, who has consistently denied wrongdoing, is facing multiple cases instituted by the anti-graft agency over alleged misappropriation of public funds during his eight-year tenure as governor of the state.

The EFCC Chairman, Ola Olukoyede, said in an interview with Channels Television in January 2026 that the commission had filed three cases against the former governor and fulfilled its mandate by investigating and prosecuting him.

According to Olukoyede, the responsibility for determining Bello’s guilt rests with the judiciary.

One of the cases involves a 16-count charge bordering on alleged criminal breach of trust and money laundering involving ₦110.4 billion.

Bello, who was the governor from 2016 to 2024, is standing trial alongside two former state government officials.

The Federal Government alleges that the defendants diverted state funds to acquire properties in highbrow areas of Abuja and Dubai, United Arab Emirates.

Bello is also facing a separate 19-count charge involving an alleged ₦80.2 billion fraud and money laundering before the Federal High Court in Abuja.

However, some prosecution witnesses have given evidence that has featured prominently in the proceedings.

In February 2026, an EFCC witness, Olomotane Egoro, told the Federal High Court that Bello’s name did not appear as a beneficiary, signatory, or party to transactions involving some companies allegedly awarded local government contracts in Kogi State.

Similarly, in March, another prosecution witness, Mahmoud Abdulazeez, maintained that Bello’s name was not linked to the transactions involving property documents tendered before the court.

In June 2026, the 17th prosecution witness, Shehu Bala, also told the court during cross-examination that Bello was not involved in the property transactions under examination.

Meanwhile, the EFCC secured a significant legal victory in August 2025 when the Court of Appeal in Lagos restored an interim forfeiture order covering 14 properties allegedly linked to the former governor.

The appellate court directed the EFCC to proceed with the final forfeiture proceedings, holding that properties suspected to be proceeds of unlawful activities could be investigated and preserved pending the determination of the case.

Despite the legal challenges, Bello, who is the senatorial candidate of the All Progressives Congress (APC) for Kogi Central in the 2027 general elections, has maintained his innocence and expressed confidence that the allegations against him will not stand the test of judicial scrutiny.

The cases remain before the courts, and no final determination of his guilt has been made.

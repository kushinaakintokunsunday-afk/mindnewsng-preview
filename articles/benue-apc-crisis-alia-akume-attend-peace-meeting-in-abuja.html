Benue APC crisis: Alia, Akume attend peace meeting in Abuja

By Punch Punch 3d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Benue State Governor, Hyacinth Alia, has met with key stakeholders of the All Progressives Congress in Abuja as part of efforts to reconcile and strengthen unit

Alia disclosed this in a statement on Saturday, saying the meeting was convened by the North-Central Presidential Reconciliation Committee, chaired by former Katsina State Governor, Senator Ibrahim Shema.

The meeting, which lasted about two hours, was attended by the Secretary to the Government of the Federation,

; former Benue State Governor, Senator Gabriel Suswam; Senators Barnabas Gemade and Jack Gyado; and Elder Shango, among other party stakeholders.

The governor thanked Shema and members of the committee for bringing the party leaders together, noting that reconciliation required willingness among members to listen, speak frankly and work together despite their differences.

“I thank Sen. Shema and his committee for bringing us together. Reconciliation takes a willingness to listen, to speak frankly and to keep working together even where differences remain,” he said.

Alia reaffirmed his commitment to engaging with APC leaders and stakeholders to promote unity within the party, while ensuring that peace, security and development remained central to his administration.

“My commitment is clear. I will continue to engage with our leaders and stakeholders in the interest of a united APC, while keeping the peace, security and development of Benue State at the centre of my work,” he added.

He said the responsibility entrusted to the government by the people of Benue should guide the actions of political leaders and stakeholders.

“The people of Benue entrusted us with the responsibility to serve, and that responsibility must continue to guide us,” the governor said.

The meeting comes amid a prolonged leadership crisis in the Benue APC, which has divided the party into factions loyal to Alia and Akume. The disagreement, which dates back to shortly after Alia assumed office in 2023, has centred on control of the party’s structures and the influence of key stakeholders in the state.

The crisis has led to the emergence of parallel party executives, disagreements over party congresses and repeated reconciliation efforts. In November, Alia blamed the divisions on his refusal to allow some party chieftains access to state funds for personal interests, while Akume’s loyalists have accused the governor of sidelining established party leaders.

Previous peace efforts have also encountered disagreements, including a May reconciliation meeting over automatic tickets for elected APC officials.

LASG targets 6,000km fibre network, 10% startup procurement

By Punch Punch 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Lagos State Government is targeting an expansion of its fibre-optic network from 2,400 kilometres to 6,000 kilometres and plans to reserve 10 per cent of pu

Deputy Governor Obafemi Hamzat disclosed the plans at a fintech town hall in Lagos on Friday, where he urged technology companies, financial institutions and investors to work with the government to develop the state’s digital infrastructure and innovation ecosystem.

Hamzat said the proposed fibre expansion would be pursued through partnerships with telecommunications companies and other private-sector stakeholders, while the startup procurement initiative would give smaller businesses access to government contracts and improve their ability to grow.

He said the government was also working towards ensuring that startups engaged through the procurement programme received payment within 15 days, reducing the cash-flow difficulties that often constrain small businesses.

The deputy governor added that women accounted for about 54 per cent of startups that had undertaken work under the initiative.

The town hall, themed “The Lagos Digital Economy Blueprint: Co-Authoring the Lagos Digital Economy Agenda”, brought together fintech operators, technology companies, investors and government representatives to discuss the policies and infrastructure required to strengthen Lagos’s position as a technology and financial services hub.

Hamzat challenged businesses to help bridge the gap between research and commercial investment, arguing that many innovations developed by young Nigerians failed to reach the market because of inadequate funding and weak connections with industry.

“We have seen a lot of ideas and young people bringing innovations, but how do we bring them to capital? How do we ensure that we understand the needs of the private sector and connect them to research?” he asked.

The former Commissioner for Science and Technology urged companies to identify challenges within their operations that could be addressed through academic research, and to fund universities and researchers to develop practical solutions.

He cited his doctoral research, which he said was funded by an oil company in the United Kingdom and resulted in a reported 33 per cent reduction in energy consumption in oil production, as an example of the benefits of industry-backed research.

On digital infrastructure, Hamzat said 680 of Lagos’s 1,028 public schools were connected to the internet, with the government working to extend connectivity to the remaining 348 schools.

He also outlined plans to integrate government databases through Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) to allow agencies to share information and reduce repeated requests for documents already held by the government.

According to him, an integrated data system would reduce duplication, improve public service delivery and make interactions with government agencies easier for residents.

The deputy governor urged fintech operators and technology companies to identify areas where their expertise could support the government’s digital agenda, rather than wait for the government to develop solutions independently.

Earlier, the convener of the town hall and Executive Director of MyCard Global, Aderemi Fagbemi, said the meeting was designed to bring government and industry stakeholders together to shape policies for the state’s financial technology and digital innovation sectors.

She said sustainable innovation required cooperation between public institutions and private businesses, adding that the town hall was intended to move discussions beyond individual corporate interests to a shared digital economy agenda.

“As someone proudly serving on Your Excellency’s Formal Sector Campaign Council, I see firsthand the immense passion and dedication driving this vision. We gathered because Lagos means business,” Fagbemi said.

She identified tax harmonisation, the integration of digital payment infrastructure into municipal systems and the development of innovation corridors as issues requiring attention if Lagos were to compete effectively as a global technology hub.

Also speaking, the co-founder of Flutterwave, Gbenga Agboola, said predictable policies and regulatory stability were essential to attracting investment and enabling fintech companies to expand.

He called for better coordination among regulatory agencies, particularly in licensing and approval processes, arguing that businesses should not have to navigate duplicative procedures to secure authorisation from different regulators.

Agboola said approvals granted by one relevant agency should, where applicable, help facilitate requirements from other regulators, reducing the time and cost of compliance for businesses.

He also urged the government to create room for startups to test new ideas and develop products without unnecessary regulatory obstacles, while operating within established legal frameworks.

The Flutterwave co-founder said more efficient regulation, consistent policies and tax-efficient business operations would encourage entrepreneurship, attract investment and support the growth of Nigeria’s fintech sector.

The town hall was organised by MyCard Global in collaboration with the Fintech Association of Nigeria, also known as FintechNGR. Participating organisations included Flutterwave, Paystack, PiggyVest, Kuda, PalmPay, Mastercard, Visa, Oracle, Interswitch, OPay, Moniepoint, Paga and Meristem.

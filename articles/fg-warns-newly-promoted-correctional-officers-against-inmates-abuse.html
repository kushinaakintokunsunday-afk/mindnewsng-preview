FG warns newly promoted correctional officers against inmates’ abuse

By Vanguard Vanguard 16 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Vanguard

….Entrenches performance management system

By Omeiza Ajayi

ABUJA: The Federal Government has warned officers of the Nigerian Correctional Service against abuse of authority and inmates’ rights, insisting that the powers vested in them must be exercised “within the confines of the law, respect for human dignity and the highest standards of professional conduct.”

Minister of Interior, Hon. Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo, gave the charge on Monday in Abuja at the decoration of 6,207 newly promoted personnel at the service’s National Headquarters in Abuja

Represented by the Permanent Secretary in the ministry, Dr Magdalene Ajani, the minister told the newly promoted controllers that they would now be held responsible for failures in their commands.

“You are not going to be waiting for directives while things are going bad, because you will now be held responsible squarely because you are now management staff.

“We do not imprison people anymore. We bring you in for corrections, we try to reform you and rehabilitate you, and send you back to the society much better than you were when you approached or came to our institution,” she said.

Ajani urged the officers to “play the managerial role of the positions you now occupy,” take initiatives “based on extant rules” and “make sure that problems are solved immediately, you see them.”

Controller General of Corrections, Mr Sylvester Ndidi Nwakuche, announced that the Performance Management System PMS is being institutionalised as the yardstick for future promotions.

Nwakuche said the promotion covered 4,924 senior officers and 1,283 junior officers. Among them, 1,281 Inspectors of Corrections moved to Assistant Superintendents of Corrections II, and 743 Chief Correctional Assistants were converted to Inspector of Corrections after completing the prescribed conversion course.

He said the PMS would change the basis on which officers are judged. “It is a framework that will help us move from merely assessing the time officers have spent in service to objectively evaluating what they have contributed to the achievement of our institutional objectives.

“Let me emphasise that going forward, your performance records, demonstrated competencies, achievement of assigned targets and measurable contributions to the objectives of the Service will constitute major considerations in assessing your suitability for future career progression, in accordance with extant regulations and guidelines. Promotion must increasingly reflect not just how long an officer has served, but also how well that officer has performed,” he said.

The Controller General told the officers to own their appraisals, adding that the PMS should not be regarded as an annual paperwork exercise or something to be attended to only when an appraisal is due. It must become part of our everyday work culture, he said.

On conduct, Nwakuche said: “Never allow your new rank to become a licence for arrogance, intimidation or abuse of authority. Rank should deepen your sense of responsibility, not diminish your respect for others. Our uniform must inspire confidence, our conduct must command respect, and our institutions must reflect the values and aspirations of a modern correctional system.”

Nwakuche thanked President Bola Tinubu for the administration’s support for the service and the Civil Defence, Correctional, Fire and Immigration Services Board (CDCFIB) for facilitating the exercise.

Tinubu pledges 50,000 hostel beds in federal tertiary institutions

By Punch Punch 2d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

President Bola Tinubu has pledged to provide 50,000 additional hostel bed spaces across Federal tertiary institutions by the end of 2027 to address accommodatio

He made the pledge during the commissioning of Block E of the College of Medicine Students’ Hostel, University of Ibadan, Ibadan, Oyo State, on Saturday.

The president, who was represented by the Minister of Education, Tunji Alausa, said the project was part of the N400 billion Student Hostels Programme, to be implemented through the Tertiary Education Trust Fund and private-sector partnerships.

Tinubu said, “My administration will deliver 50,000 new bed spaces across federal tertiary institutions by the end of 2027, through our N400 billion Student Hostels Programme. Our purpose is to give more students safe, decent accommodation that supports their education.

“The newly commissioned facility would accommodate 192 medical and dental students in 48 rooms across four floors, with each room designed for four occupants. The rooms have private toilets, showers and kitchenettes, alongside four reading rooms.”

The president stressed that the college previously faced a shortage of 490 beds, representing 44.5 per cent of its accommodation requirement, following a 2020 directive by the Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria requiring clinical students to reside within the University College Hospital premises.

He noted that the shortage exposed students to security challenges and affected the institution’s ability to utilise its admission quota fully.

Tinubu also said more than 36 universities had benefited from the Renewed Hope Energising Education Programme, with power installations ranging from 2.5 to 12 megawatts.

The president added that 20 Compressed Natural Gas conversion centres had been completed, while another 40 were expected by the end of 2028.

“Reliable power supports laboratories, libraries, hostels and the clinical facilities on which medical education depends,” he said.

Earlier, the university’s Vice-Chancellor, Prof. Peter Olapegba, described the commissioning as another milestone in efforts to improve accommodation for medical students.

He recalled that Blocks C and D, named after Philip Ozuah and T.Y. Danjuma, respectively, had also been commissioned as part of the initiative.

Olapegba thanked the Chagoury brothers — Gilbert and Ronald– for their contribution, saying, “Our expectation is that the improved learning environment will contribute to producing healthcare professionals distinguished by competence, integrity and a strong sense of national responsibility.”

In his address, the Provost, College of Medicine, Prof. Temidayo Ogundiran, said the college accounted for about one-third of the university’s institutional footprint, while its researchers generated approximately 80 per cent of the institution’s grant income.

The provost commended the Federal Government’s N110bn Special High Impact Intervention Project, launched in April 2025 to rehabilitate 18 medical schools across the six geopolitical zones through TETFund.

Ogundiran appealed for government support to construct a 1,000-seat student auditorium, expand the college’s administrative building and complete hostel Blocks A and B.

The varsity don also disclosed that the college spent about N18 million monthly on diesel and electricity bills, describing the cost as unsustainable.

“The college currently spends about N18m monthly on diesel and as payment to IBEDC for electric power supply. This expenditure is unsustainable and is placing severe pressure on our operations.”

Ogundiran urged the Minister of Power, Joseph Tegbe, to facilitate the implementation of an approved solar hybrid mini-grid project for the university and UCH, as well as the installation of four 5MVA transformers.

“The ultimate measure of these investments will not be the number of buildings commissioned, but the quality of healthcare professionals, researchers and leaders whose lives they help shape,” the provost said.

The hostel, named the President Bola Ahmed Tinubu GCFR Block, was donated by the Chairman of Chagoury Group, Gilbert Chagoury, and his brother, Ronald Chagoury.

Reps to summon NAFDAC, DSS over fake drugs, deaths

By Punch Punch 5d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The resolution followed the adoption of a motion of urgent public importance moved on Wednesday by the member representing Obi/Oju Federal Constituency of Benue State, Mr David Agada, following reports that the consumption of a suspected poisonous herbal medicine had claimed four lives in the constituency.

The motion, titled “Urgent need to investigate the alleged distribution and administration of suspected poisonous, unregistered/substandard herbal medicinal products in Obi/Oju Federal Constituency, Benue State to prevent further loss of lives,” called for urgent intervention to establish the circumstances surrounding the incident and prevent further casualties.

Moving the motion, Agada reminded lawmakers of their responsibility to protect the lives and welfare of their constituents.

He noted that the NAFDAC Act empowers the agency to regulate and control the manufacture, distribution, sale and use of drugs, herbal medicinal products and other regulated health-related products in Nigeria.

He also cited the Food and Drugs Act and the Counterfeit and Fake Drugs and Unwholesome Processed Foods (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, which prohibit the manufacture, distribution and sale of unsafe, adulterated, counterfeit or unwholesome drugs and related products.

Speaking on the incident, Agada said, “The House is concerned that on October 31 2026, three individuals identified as Muhammad Sani Adamu, Musa Alhaji Kala who claim to come from Sokoto State and Alhaji Muhammad Sule from Bauchi States allegedly introduced and distributed a herbal medicinal product in Oju/Obi Federal Constituency, Benue State, claiming that the product could cure typhoid fever, waist pain and other ailments.

“We are concerned that several residents reportedly purchased and consumed the said product, after which a number of them developed severe symptoms within hours and were rushed to different hospitals for emergency medical attention.

“The House is disturbed that the incident has reportedly resulted in the deaths of four persons, namely-Awugo Joseph Oyije, who died on 2nd November, 2026; James Ogah Onma, who died on 4th November, 2026, following referral for further medical treatment; Ode Friday Ogah died on November 5, 2026; and Mr Agi Edugbeke who died on the 11th day of November, 2026.”

He added that 11 other persons were reportedly affected and were receiving medical attention, with two referred to hospitals in Makurdi, the Benue State capital.

The affected persons were identified as Otumala Ochele, Binta Sule, Onu Oboh, Adoga Onu, Upa James, Igbo Pius, Ochele Genesis, Otumala Amos, Uwa Baku, Ate Vivian Okpune and Roman Ehile.

Agada further warned that other residents might have consumed the suspected product without being identified or could be receiving treatment outside recognised health facilities, raising the risk of additional casualties.

According to him, “The House is worried that other residents within the affected communities may have consumed the suspected product without being identified or may be receiving treatment outside recognised health facilities, thereby increasing the possibility of further casualties.

“The House is further worried that the composition, source, manufacturing conditions, NAFDAC registration status, safety, dosage and possible adverse effects of the product remain unverified, raising serious concerns about the possible circulation of poisonous, adulterated, unregistered or substandard herbal medicinal products.”

He said the incident constituted a serious public health concern requiring immediate intervention by relevant federal and state authorities to establish the circumstances surrounding the deaths, prevent further exposure and ensure that those responsible, where culpability was established, were brought to justice.

Contributing to the debate, the House Leader, Prof. Julius Ihonvbere, said the proliferation of fake drugs and drinks had become a national challenge requiring a broader response beyond NAFDAC’s current capacity.

He said the agency was struggling with inadequate personnel, noting that its workforce of about 2,000 was insufficient to effectively police the country’s drug distribution network.

“This issue has gone beyond NAFDAC which has only a staff strength of about 2,000 people. The agency requires at least 10,000 staff members to be able to execute its mandate. NAFDAC does not have a presence everywhere; it is not in my village in Edo State.

“I think we need to have a holistic conversation about this problem because it is getting out of hand. If we do not do something quickly, more Nigerians will continue to die of fake drugs and drinks.”

Ihonvbere urged the House to recommend stiffer penalties for manufacturers and distributors of fake and contaminated products.

“Mr Speaker, I think we can suggest life imprisonment or even the death penalty for those involved in this criminal enterprise and may bring the DG of NAFDAC to come and tell us where they are on this matter,” he added.

Similarly, Ali Shettima (APC, Yobe) urged lawmakers to look beyond the Benue incident and address the nationwide consequences of the circulation of fake drugs.

“Let us look beyond Benue and focus on what the issue of fake drugs is doing throughout the country. NAFDAC needs to intensify its work in regulating the activities of herbal medicine producers. Selling fake products to innocent citizens in the name of drugs and drinks is very unfortunate. An enabling law should be enacted to ensure that those involved in this are penalised if they are found guilty,” he said.

Another Yobe lawmaker, Fatima Talba, however, urged caution, arguing that many Nigerians patronised unregulated herbal medicine sellers partly because of limited access to affordable and reliable healthcare alternatives.

She said, “Some of the people who patronise these do not have any other place to go to. It is not as if these people are forced to buy these products because even in the hospitals, there are fake drugs. We cannot tell these people not to patronise these fake herbal drug sellers because we have not given then alternatives.”

Peter Uzoekwe (NDC, Anambra) proposed holding market leadership accountable for the sale and distribution of counterfeit drugs, suggesting that the executives of markets where such products were manufactured or sold should be removed.

“Let us make it a duty that any market where fake drugs are manufactured or sold, we will depose the executives. If we do this, you will see that those executives would act as taskforce,” he said.

Also contributing, Ismaila Haruna, who represents Toro Federal Constituency of Bauchi State, called for an investigation into deaths allegedly linked to the use of sexual performance-enhancing drugs.

“There are existing laws in place guiding the regulation of herbal medicine. What is left is enforcement. Is NAFDAC doing its job? Again, we need to investigate why men are dying in their numbers because many times, we hear of men dying in hotel rooms and what have you,” he said.

At the end of the debate, the House resolved to invite NAFDAC, the Nigeria Police Force and the DSS to brief its relevant committees on measures being taken to curb the circulation of fake herbal medicines and adulterated drinks across the country.

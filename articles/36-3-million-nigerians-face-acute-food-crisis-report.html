36.3 million Nigerians face acute food crisis – Report

By Punch Punch 3d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

About 36.3 million Nigerians face crisis or worse levels of food insecurity, according to a report by the Alliance for a Green Revolution in Africa. The documen

The document, Food Security Monitor (Edition 74, October 2026), obtained by Saturday PUNCH on Friday, puts the figure at 36,289,939 people, the highest number of any country in Africa covered in the report.

The report, supported by the United Kingdom government’s Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, tracks food security and staple prices across East, Southern and West Africa.

According to the report, Nigerians facing food crisis represent 16.65 per cent of the analysed population, classified in Phase 3 or higher of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification/Cadre Harmonisé scale.

The report said: “Nigeria continues to carry by far the largest absolute burden, with 36.29 million people facing Crisis or worse food insecurity, followed by South Sudan with 7.83 million.”

A Saturday PUNCH analysis of the report showed that Nigerians facing food crisis were about 7.1 million more than the combined 29.18 million recorded for the 13 other countries listed in the survey.

Nigeria accounted for about 55 per cent of the 65.5 million people counted across the table.

After South Sudan, the ranking included Uganda (2.94 million), Malawi (2.61 million), Kenya (2.55 million), Zimbabwe (2.50 million) and Niger (2.42 million).

The rest were Mozambique (1.84 million), Mali (1.56 million), Ghana (1.50 million), Côte d’Ivoire (1.33 million), Zambia (1.25 million), Tanzania (507,498) and Togo (332,147).

South Sudan had the highest incidence at 55 per cent, followed by Mozambique at 22 per cent and Nigeria at 16.65 per cent.

Uganda and Zimbabwe recorded 16 per cent each, while Kenya and Malawi had 14 per cent each.

In West Africa, Niger recorded nine per cent, Mali six per cent, Côte d’Ivoire and Togo five per cent each, and Ghana four per cent.

The report said the figures emphasised a difference between absolute and proportional burdens, with Nigeria carrying the largest number and South Sudan the highest incidence.

On the outlook, the report said Nigeria’s food security situation would remain “highly uneven” through January 2027.

It said Emergency (Phase 4) conditions would persist in inaccessible parts of Borno State, while conflict-affected areas of the North-West and North-Central would stay in Crisis as insecurity disrupted farming, harvesting, livelihoods and market access.

In other parts of the North-East, the report said harvests should bring temporary improvement from Crisis to Stressed (Phase 2), although many displaced and land-constrained households would remain in Crisis.

It added that high fuel and transport costs, and staple prices “well above the five-year average,” meant harvest gains were unlikely to substantially improve food access for poor and conflict-affected households.

The report also showed wide differences in food prices.

It noted that milled rice fell 9.1 per cent in October to N452.87 per kilogramme, and was 46.7 per cent lower than a year earlier, which the report attributed to good market supply.

White maize fell 5.8 per cent in the month to N333.10 per kg and was 12.7 per cent lower year-on-year.

However, white sorghum was 47 per cent higher than a year earlier at N486.23 per kg, while millet was up 23.3 per cent at N503.25 per kg.

Across West Africa, the report said food insecurity was expected to worsen into the 2027 lean season, particularly in conflict-affected parts of the central Sahel and northern Nigeria.

It named Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger and north-eastern Nigeria as the most severe cases, with humanitarian needs expected to peak between March and May 2027.

It said crop conditions were rated poor in conflict-affected parts of northern Nigeria, and that a very strong El Niño was compounding shocks across the continent.

The report follows similar data from the Cadre Harmonisé update for the June–August 2026 lean season, published on July 2.

The analysis said Nigeria accounted for nearly 66 per cent of the 54.8 million people in Crisis or worse across the Sahel, West Africa and Cameroon, followed by Chad (3.2 million), Cameroon (2.8 million) and Niger (2.4 million).

It also put about two million Nigerians in Emergency, with roughly 10,000 people in Catastrophe (Phase 5) in Borno State.

The earlier Cadre Harmonisé, presented in Abuja in late 2025, had put 27.2 million Nigerians in Crisis or worse between October and December 2025 and projected 34.7 million for June to August 2026.

The Food and Agriculture Organisation said the 36.3 million estimates represented a deterioration from 2025, when more than 30.6 million people were in Phase 3 or above.

The FAO also said over 3.7 million people were displaced nationwide as of early August 2026, about two-thirds of them in Borno.

The World Food Programme said in July that more than 17 million people in nine conflict-affected northern states were experiencing crisis, emergency or catastrophic hunger, and that it could support only 740,000 of 6.2 million food-insecure people in three North-East states, down from 1.3 million at the height of the 2025 lean season.

According to the National Bureau of Statistics, food inflation stood at 19.13 per cent year-on-year in October 2026, even as the food price index fell 0.75 per cent from September.

Simon Kolawole reveals intrigues behind Tinubu’s 2023 victory in new book

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Veteran Nigerian journalist, Simon Kolawole, is set to release a book examining the political manoeuvres, rivalries and alliances that shaped the 2023 president

Kolawole is the founder of TheCable, an online newspaper launched in 2014.

The 296-page book, titled ‘Winning Formula: The Inside Story of Nigeria’s 2023 Presidential Election,’ will go on sale on Monday, November 23, according to a statement on Friday by its publishers, Cable Books.

In the book, Kolawole examines how President Bola Tinubu secured victory, the rivalry between former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar and ex-Rivers State Governor Nyesom Wike, and the emergence of Peter Obi and the Obidient movement as major forces in the election.

Describing the book’s central themes, Kolawole said it would explain “how Bola Tinubu outmanoeuvred Muhammadu Buhari, how Atiku Abubakar and Nyesom Wike destroyed each other’s presidential ambition, how Peter Obi and the Obidient movement became a phenomenon — and how the winning formula that decided it all was hiding in plain sight.”

The author said the book drew on insider briefings, court filings and polling data to examine the election and the political calculations behind its outcome.

It also explores voting patterns in presidential elections since 1999, the controversy surrounding the Muslim-Muslim presidential ticket and how former Vice-President Yemi Osinbajo lost out in the succession contest ahead of the 2023 poll.

“This is a story of naivety, cat-and-mouse games, cold calculations — and subterfuge,” Kolawole said.

The 2023 presidential election had four candidates who each polled more than one million votes: Tinubu, Atiku, Obi and New Nigeria Peoples Party candidate Rabiu Kwankwaso.

The poll was also followed by controversy over delays in uploading presidential polling-unit results to the Independent National Electoral Commission’s Result Viewing Portal and legal challenges by candidates who disputed the outcome.

A central argument in the book is that no candidate has won Nigeria’s presidency in the Fourth Republic without building a competitive coalition across at least two of the country’s three largest voting blocs — Hausa-Fulani, Igbo and Yoruba — alongside support from a minority geopolitical zone.

The book combines political history, electoral analysis and accounts of events behind the scenes to explain how alliances and voting patterns influenced the outcome of the election.

It will be distributed nationwide by Rovingheights bookstore.

Kolawole began his journalism career at Complete Football magazine in 1993 and later worked with TheNews/TEMPO group and City People.

He joined THISDAY in 1997 and served as editor of the newspaper from 2007 to 2012.

In 2022, he published ‘Fellow Nigerians, It’s All Politics,’ a collection of essays on leadership, democracy and national development.

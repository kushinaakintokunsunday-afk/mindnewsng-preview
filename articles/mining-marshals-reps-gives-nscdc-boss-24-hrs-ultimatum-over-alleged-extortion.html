Mining marshals: Reps gives NSCDC boss 24 hrs ultimatum over alleged extortion

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The House of Representatives Committee on Solid Minerals has directed the Commandant-General of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), Ahmed Audi

The House of Representatives Committee on Solid Minerals has directed the Commandant-General of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), Ahmed Audi, to appear before it over allegation of extortion and harassment of licensed mining operators by mining marshals.

The panel issued the directive, on Tuesday, at at investigative hearing on the operations and funding of mining marshals, following
allegations bordering on abuse of power, extortion, unlawful detention, financial irregularities and alleged misconduct by personnel of the special security unit established to combat illegal mining across the country.

The Chairman of the committee, Gaza Gbefwi, while speaking at investigative hearing, explained that the probe was aimed at establishing the facts surrounding allegations of unlawful arrests and harassment of licensed mining operators, and alleged ₦2 billion transaction linked to the bank account of a former state commandant of the NSCDC.

According to him, “We see this inquiry as essential to confidence in Nigeria’s solid minerals sector. We want our mineral wealth to sustain livelihoods, attract responsible investment and strengthen national revenue. We must therefore protect mining communities and lawful enterprise, while ensuring that security powers serve the public interest.

“Our nation’s mineral wealth must translate into opportunity and security for its people.
Our investigation arises from the House’s resolution to examine serious concerns about the Mining Marshals’ performance, legal status, funding and alleged compromise.

“We must determine whether the unit’s conduct, structure and funding fulfil that purpose and comply with Section 3 of the NSCDC Act and the Nigerian Minerals and Mining Act 2007.

“We will examine allegations of unlawful arrests and harassment of licensed mineral title holders; the alleged ₦2 billion linkage to a former State Commander’s bank account and his reported redeployment to Enugu State; alleged syndicate compromise across the 36 states and the FCT; and vehicle procurements for which verifiable legislative appropriation has been questioned.

“We will establish the sources and legal authority for the unit’s funding, including external inflows. We have serious reservations about the initial response of the head of the Mining Marshals to the Speaker, imputing ulterior motives to the mover of the motion and, by implication, casting doubt upon the House’s purpose.

“We respect the right to respond and disagree. A resolution adopted by the House, however, engages the responsibility of the institution, and its questions deserve substantive answers. We will assess every explanation on its merits; speculation about motives cannot settle the issues before us.”

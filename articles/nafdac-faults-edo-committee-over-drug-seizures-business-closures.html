NAFDAC faults Edo committee over drug seizures, business closures

Seeks Okpebholo’s intervention over Arden Table Water

By Chioma Obinna

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has faulted the Edo State Consumer Protection Committee (ESCPC) over the alleged seizure of pharmaceutical products, closure of business premises and imposition of fines on operators without proper regulatory authorisation.

The agency also appealed to Edo State Governor, Senator Monday Okpebholo, to intervene in the reported shutdown of Chepfets Mega Investment Nigeria Limited, manufacturer of Arden Table Water, insisting that the product’s registration was undergoing renewal and that this did not establish that it was unsafe or substandard.

The Director-General of NAFDAC, Prof. Mojisola Adeyeye, raised the concerns while clarifying the agency’s position on the committee’s activities in the state.

She expressed concern over reports that the committee had been using NAFDAC’s product authentication platforms to verify regulated products and make public declarations about their authenticity and quality.

Adeyeye explained that access to the agency’s authentication platforms did not automatically confer regulatory authority on users or provide the technical basis for declaring products counterfeit, adulterated, injurious, safe or unsafe.

NAFDAC said it recognised the importance of consumer protection and welcomed collaboration with state governments and other stakeholders in the fight against counterfeit, falsified, adulterated and substandard products.

However, it stressed that such interventions must respect established regulatory boundaries and applicable laws.

The agency said it was particularly concerned by reports that the committee had seized regulated products, publicly labelled some products fake or adulterated, sealed business premises and imposed fines on operators under the guise of working for NAFDAC without its authorisation or guidance.

It also raised concerns over the reported deployment of NAFDAC’s Automated Product Administration and Monitoring System (NAPAMS), QR code scanner, Short Messaging Service (SMS) verification platform and other regulatory tools by the committee to assess products and make public pronouncements on their identity, authenticity, efficacy and effectiveness.

According to the agency, failed QR code scans, missing registration details or inconsistencies in product packaging do not, on their own, constitute sufficient evidence that a product is counterfeit or substandard.

It advised consumers and other stakeholders to report such irregularities to NAFDAC for investigation and possible laboratory analysis, urging the public to await the agency’s findings before making claims about products.

On the reported enforcement activities, NAFDAC condemned what it described as the unilateral seizure of pharmaceutical products, closure of establishments and imposition of fines by the committee.

It maintained that the regulation of drugs, medicines and pharmaceuticals falls under the exclusive legislative jurisdiction of the Federal Government.

The agency said details of seized products, including quantities, estimated value, storage conditions, documentation and circumstances of seizure, should be properly recorded, with impounded regulated products subsequently handed over to NAFDAC.

Arden Table Water registration

Clarifying the status of Arden Table Water, Adeyeye said the product, manufactured by Chepfets Mega Investment Nigeria Limited at 1, Afegbua Street, Ekenwan Road, Benin City, Edo State, was available in sachet and bottled forms.

She explained that the sachet water registration was valid, with registration number D1-3016L, while the bottled water registration, number D1-3065, was undergoing renewal in line with the agency’s NAPAMS procedures.

NAFDAC expressed serious concern that the committee reportedly shut down the company’s operations, imposed a fine and made an arrest without first seeking clarification from the agency on the product’s registration status.

Adeyeye said, “The absence of a status update on the NAPAMS portal provides no basis for concluding that the product is unsafe, ineffective or substandard.”

The agency consequently appealed to Okpebholo to review the reported actions and direct the committee to seek formal clarification from NAFDAC in similar circumstances.

NAFDAC said it had commenced investigations to establish the facts surrounding the developments, including the circumstances of the reported intervention by its Zonal Enforcement Team in Asaba, Delta State, and whether any formal working relationship or authorisation existed between the committee and the agency.

It added that it would engage relevant authorities to clarify the committee’s statutory mandate, particularly regarding products regulated under federal law.

The agency also urged manufacturers with active NAFDAC registration licences to begin renewal processes at least six months before expiration to allow sufficient time for processing.

Adeyeye advised traders, distributors, manufacturers, healthcare providers, pharmacists, doctors and members of the public to report suspected counterfeit, adulterated, fake, unregistered or substandard products through its official helplines: +234(0)700-1-623322 and +234(0)800-1-623322, or via email at reforms@nafdac.gov.ng.

She said complaints could also be submitted through the agency’s online complaints form or at its state offices, adding that the Edo office was located at the Federal Secretariat Complex, Benin-Auchi Road, Aduwawa, Benin City.

NAFDAC reaffirmed its commitment to protecting public health and ensuring that enforcement activities were conducted in accordance with the law and established technical procedures.

It stressed that while consumer protection was a shared responsibility, regulatory accountability must remain clearly defined.

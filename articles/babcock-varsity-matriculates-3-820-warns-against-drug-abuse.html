Babcock varsity matriculates 3,820, warns against drug abuse

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The President/Vice-Chancellor of Babcock University, Ilishan-Remo, Ogun State, Professor Afọlarin Ojewole, has charged newly admitted students to uphold discipl

Ojewole gave the charge on Friday during the university’s undergraduate matriculation ceremony held at the University Amphitheatre, Ilishan-Remo, where a total of 3,820 students, including 550 part-time students, were formally admitted into various first-degree programmes.

Addressing the matriculating students, the Vice-Chancellor described the ceremony as more than a formal university tradition, saying it represented a commitment to the institution’s values, academic responsibilities and broader mission of producing graduates equipped to contribute meaningfully to society.

He urged the students to make purposeful use of the opportunities available to them, noting that their eventual success would depend significantly on their commitment, diligence and willingness to embrace personal development.

Ojewole stated that the university’s educational philosophy went beyond the award of academic certificates to the development of individuals with sound knowledge, moral conviction, practical competence, leadership abilities and concern for humanity.

“At Babcock University, we believe education must go beyond the acquisition of certificates. We are interested in the whole person. We want you to graduate with strong academic knowledge, strong moral conviction and practical competence, leadership capability and genuine concern for the welfare of other people,” he stated.

He, however, reminded the students that providing opportunities was only one part of the university’s responsibility, insisting that they must take personal responsibility for maximising the resources available to them.

The Vice-Chancellor urged the matriculating students to attend lectures regularly, arrive early, respect lecturers and other members of the university community, participate actively in academic activities and develop the habit of reading beyond their lecture notes.

While charging the newly admitted students, Prof Ojewole said, “Please come to class. Come early. You have heard the saying that first class begins with a first class. So come prepared to learn. Respect your lecturer.

“Respect the rules. Respect your colleagues and every member of staff. Be punctual. Participate actively in academic activities. Ask probing questions. It’s okay to do so here in Babcock,”

He added, “Do not allow the desire for quick success to compromise your integrity. Reject examination malpractice, plagiarism, bullying, violence, cultism, drug use and every form of misconduct that undermines the values of this institution and the great values that you were brought up with.

“Don’t let no one deter you from your goal. Let no one make you stray from the path. Don’t let no one pump you up to do wrong. They may come to you with emotional manipulation. Please don’t listen.

“Your character is as important to us as your academic performance. Indeed, when the applause of graduation is over and the certificate is free, it is your character that will sustain you at the top and speak for you everywhere.”

Ojewole assured the students that the university had support structures, including pastors, chaplains, faculty members, advisers, counsellors and the Student Support Centre, to provide assistance and guidance.

He emphasised that students in need could seek help confidentially and would not be subjected to public embarrassment for opening up about their struggles, urging them not to suffer in silence.

Addressing parents and guardians, the Vice-Chancellor acknowledged their sacrifices, prayers, financial commitments and personal investments in securing admission for their children.

He thanked them for entrusting the university with the responsibility of educating and nurturing their wards, describing students’ success as a shared responsibility between the institution and their families.

Ojewole said the university remained committed to producing globally relevant, research-focused and data-driven graduates who would retain their Seventh-day Adventist identity and uphold high ethical standards.

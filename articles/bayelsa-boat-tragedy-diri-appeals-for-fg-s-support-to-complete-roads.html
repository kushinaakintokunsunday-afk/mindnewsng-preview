Bayelsa boat tragedy: Diri appeals for FG’s support to complete roads

By Punch Punch 5h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Bayelsa State Governor, Senator Douye Diri, has made a fresh appeal to the Federal Government to deepen its collaboration with the state in the construction and

The ongoing projects include the Ekeremor-Agge road in Bayelsa West, Yenagoa-Oporoma-Ukubie road in Bayelsa Central, and the Nembe-Brass road in the eastern district.

While the state had undertaken the construction of phase one of the Nembe-Brass road, with the project nearing completion, the Federal Government has awarded the contract for the second phase.

The other two senatorial roads are being constructed solely by the state government.

Diri renewed his appeal in a statement issued by his Chief Press Secretary, Mr Daniel Alabrah, on Tuesday, following the tragic boat incident of November 20, 2026, in the Southern Ijaw Local Government Area of the state, which claimed no fewer than 29 lives.

During Vice-President Kashim Shettima’s condolence visit to the state on Sunday, the Bayelsa governor lamented the slow pace of work on the second phase of the Nembe-Brass project.

He also noted that tragic boat incidents in the riverine state would be reduced or averted if the road projects were completed.

He added that completing the roads would unlock economic benefits for the state and the country, considering the huge natural resources in Bayelsa’s maritime domain.

“Work on the second phase of the Nembe-Brass road is too slow. We believe that if that road is completed, Brass Island will be one of the best in Nigeria,” he said.

On the Yenagoa-Ukubie road, Diri explained that his administration continued its construction from where previous governments stopped around Igeibiri community and completed one of the longest bridges in the state at Angiama, which was commissioned by President Bola Tinubu on April 10 this year.

“Today we drive to Oporoma, the headquarters of Southern Ijaw Local Government Area. This road continues to Ukubie, where these victims were heading as their final destination.

“I have less than two years to go. Because of the cost of construction, I plead and again bring to attention the necessity of a federal government collaboration with the state to take this road from Oporoma to Ukubie. I am very sure that if there was a road to Ukubie, this accident might not have happened.”

On the West Senatorial Road, Diri said Agge in Ekeremor Local Government Area has a natural deep seaport and that the Federal Ministry of Marine and Blue Economy was collaborating with the state to develop the Agge Deep Seaport into another economic hub, not only for Bayelsa but for the country.

“Agge has a natural deep seaport and ships are already berthing there. So it is an economically viable area. We have gone very far in making sure that it becomes another economic hub, not only for our state but for our country,” Diri said.

He further stated that the Ekeremor-Agge road serves as the critical land link connecting Bayelsa’s riverine communities to the Atlantic Ocean, unlocking maritime trade, lowering transport costs and spurring industrial growth, which would in turn contribute to the nation’s GDP.

According to him, the state government was actively constructing the key access route to the proposed $3bn seaport at Agge.

The Bayelsa governor urged the Federal Government to sustain its support for the state, noting that the completion of the senatorial roads would improve connectivity and open up more communities for economic activities.

“Your Excellency, Mr President, if you collaborate with our state government on these roads, you would have written your name in gold in Bayelsa State,” he said.

FG moves to fast-track drug trials, seize traffickers’ assets

By Punch Punch 23h ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has pledged to strengthen the prosecution of drug-related offences through the speedy disposal of cases, confiscation of illicit assets a

The Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, Lateef Fagbemi (SAN), disclosed this on Monday in Abuja at the inauguration of the National Drug Control Master Plan (NDCMP) 2026–2030.

Fagbemi said the Federal Ministry of Justice would collaborate with the judiciary to facilitate the speedy disposal of drug cases in accordance with the Administration of the Criminal Justice Act.

He said the ministry would also strengthen efforts to ensure that convictions for drug-related offences were accompanied by the confiscation of assets acquired through illicit activities.

“The government will intensify mutual legal assistance, extradition processes and cross-border asset recovery to disrupt the financial networks sustaining organised drug trafficking.

“These measures are necessary to strengthen Nigeria’s criminal justice response and ensure that drug traffickers did not continue to benefit from the proceeds of their illegal activities,” he said.

Fagbemi stressed that effective prosecution and asset recovery were critical to dismantling criminal enterprises and discouraging the financing of illicit drug markets.

He also commended the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) for promoting early collaboration between investigators and prosecutors.

He said the approach had helped strengthen evidence gathering, prevent procedural errors and improve the effectiveness of criminal proceedings.

The Attorney-General described the NDLEA as the bedrock of Nigeria’s national drug control architecture, citing its contributions to intelligence gathering, interdiction, investigation, prosecution and public sensitisation.

He particularly commended the NDLEA Chairman, retired Brig.-Gen. Buba Marwa, for his leadership and commitment to strengthening the country’s drug control efforts.

“The agency remains the bedrock of our national drug control architecture, delivering vital results across intelligence operations, interdiction, investigation, prosecution and public sensitisation.

“I specifically commend Brig.-Gen Buba Marwa, who has been a silent achiever and has consistently made this administration and country proud,” he said.

Fagbemi said the NDCMP 2026–2030 would consolidate the agency’s achievements within a broader whole-of-government framework.

He explained that the framework was designed to strengthen collaboration among law enforcement agencies, public health institutions, educational authorities, social development agencies, financial investigators and community stakeholders.

According to him, the multidimensional nature of drug-related challenges required an integrated approach that addressed both criminal activities and the social and health consequences of substance abuse.

The minister stressed that Nigeria’s legal and policy response must distinguish between major drug traffickers and persons experiencing substance use disorders.

He said while criminal kingpins must face the full weight of the law, individuals struggling with drug dependence should have access to appropriate medical and psychological support.

Fagbemi emphasised that effective drug control must combine firm law enforcement with prevention, treatment, rehabilitation and social reintegration.

He said such an approach would help ensure that the criminal justice system addressed organised trafficking without neglecting the health and welfare needs of persons affected by substance dependence.

The Attorney-General also commended the NDLEA’s alternative development initiatives aimed at replacing the cultivation of illicit drugs with legitimate agricultural activities.

He said the agency’s efforts to encourage the cultivation of lawful cash crops and other agricultural enterprises demonstrated the importance of addressing the economic factors associated with illicit drug production.

“As we saw in the documentary earlier, the NDLEA is delivering in that regard through successful efforts at replacing the planting and cultivation of illicit drugs with that of licit cash crops, in addition to other legitimate agricultural activities.

“This is a commendable move, in line with the agenda and philosophy of President Bola Tinubu to turn adversity into opportunities for progress,” he said.

Fagbemi said the initiative reflected the importance of providing sustainable economic alternatives to communities vulnerable to involvement in illicit drug cultivation.

He described the new master plan as an important instrument for strengthening institutional coordination and ensuring that drug control interventions were implemented consistently across relevant sectors.

The minister urged stakeholders to translate the provisions of the document into practical programmes and measurable outcomes.

He stressed that the plan should not be regarded merely as a policy statement but as a guide for the daily operations of institutions responsible for its implementation.

Fagbemi reiterated the commitment of the Federal Ministry of Justice to strengthening legal cooperation and supporting measures aimed at improving the effectiveness of Nigeria’s drug control system.

In a goodwill message, the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) Commission commended Nigeria for developing the NDCMP 2026–2030, describing it as an important contribution to regional drug control efforts.

The commission’s representative, Prof. Nassirou Bako-Arifari, Commissioner for Human Development and Social Affairs, said the master plan aligned with regional commitments towards a safer, healthier and more resilient West Africa.

Bako-Arifari said the success of the policy would depend on sustained political commitment, adequate funding and cooperation among governments, civil society organisations, communities, the private sector and international partners.

He stressed that drug trafficking and substance abuse were transnational challenges requiring coordinated responses across West African countries.

“Nigeria’s progress in implementing the master plan will contribute significantly to strengthening regional cooperation against illicit drug trafficking and its associated consequences.

“The ECOWAS Commission will stand with Nigeria at every step, sharing experience, mobilising support and linking this national effort to the work of other member states.

“Nigeria’s progress will guide the region because no country can win this fight alone,” he said. (NAN)

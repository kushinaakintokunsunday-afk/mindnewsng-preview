Ghana win first Amputee Football World Cup title

By Punch Punch 1d ago 2 min read

Ghana’s national amputee football team, the Black Challenge, have won their first-ever Amputee Football World Cup title after beating Morocco 1-0 in Sunday’s final in San Juan de los Lagos, Mexico.

Captain Richard Opentil scored the decisive goal in the 32nd minute to secure the historic victory.

The Ghana Football Association disclosed this in a statement published on its website on Monday.

In its report titled, “Ghana make history as Black Challenge beat Morocco to win 2026 Amputee Football World Cup in Mexico”, the association said the team had “etched their names into football history” after being crowned champions.

It said, “In a fiercely contested encounter between two of Africa’s finest sides, the Black Challenge demonstrated remarkable determination, resilience and defensive discipline to protect their slender advantage and hold off Morocco until the final whistle.”

The association added that the encounter was a rematch of the 2024 African Amputee Cup of Nations final and the 2023 African Para Games final.

Ghana’s victory made the country the second African nation to win the competition after Angola, who lifted the trophy in 2018.

The team’s achievement was also highlighted by forward Mubarik Mohammed, who claimed the tournament’s Golden Boot after finishing as the leading scorer with seven goals.

The GFA said the Black Challenge’s journey to the championship was built on “a series of impressive performances against formidable opposition”.

Ghana began their knockout campaign with a 3-1 victory over the United States in the round of 16 before defeating Egypt 2-0 in the quarter-finals to reach the last four.

The team then faced defending champions Türkiye in the semi-finals. After the encounter ended 1-1 following extra time, Ghana won 4-3 on penalties to book their place in the final.

They carried that momentum into Sunday’s showdown against Morocco, where “Opentil’s first-half strike proved decisive in delivering the title.”

‘Rig and die’ rhetoric fuelling Delta tension – Keyamo

By Punch Punch 1d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, has called on those promoting the ‘Rig and Die’ slogan in Delta State to withdraw it publicly

Keyamo said the growing wave of political violence could make anyone a victim, following the killing of four members of the campaign organisation of former Deputy Senate President, Ovie Omo-Agege, in Ughelli, Delta State.

The former lawmaker had alleged that gunmen attacked his campaign team in Uduere, Ughelli North Local Government Area, repeatedly shouting his name and threatening to kill him.

Omo-Agege said he escaped because of the intervention of his Department of State Services escorts.

According to him, the attackers opened fire less than five minutes after he began addressing supporters at the campaign venue.

Reacting to the incident in a statement on Monday, Keyamo, who described Ughelli as his town of birth, said he received disturbing reports of the attack, which left four persons dead and several others injured.

“Those who have promoted the mantra of ‘Rig and Die’ in Delta State and elsewhere should have a rethink, publicly withdraw it, and apologise for overheating the political temperature in the state and country. As can be seen, anyone can be a victim,” he said.

He expressed condolences to the families of the deceased and wished those injured a quick recovery.

The minister condemned the killings and called on security agencies to identify and arrest those responsible, including perpetrators of an earlier killing in Asaba.

He said, “I condemn in the strongest possible terms the killing of these persons, just as I also condemned the killing of a tricycle operator in broad daylight by identified supporters of a political party in Asaba on November 7, 2026.

“I call on the law enforcement agencies to fish out the perpetrators of these killings, both in Asaba and Ughelli, and bring them to book.”

Keyamo disclosed that he had been engaging security officials over the rising political tension in the state.

“I have been in touch with the top hierarchy of law enforcement agencies in the last one month over the rising wave of political violence and violent rhetoric in Delta State,” he said.

He noted that Delta had maintained relative political peace since the return to democracy in 1999, arguing that political disagreements among residents had never previously degenerated into killings.

“Since 1999, Delta State has been a relatively peaceful state, politically. Our disagreements have never led to violence and killings. We are all brothers and sisters who went to the same primary, secondary and tertiary institutions, and we all know ourselves and our families so well. There is absolutely no need for a descent into violence,” he said.

The minister also cautioned political actors against encouraging supporters to resort to violence, insisting that only security agencies had the authority to deal with offenders.

“It is wrong to tell your supporters to kill other citizens under whatever guise. It smacks of desperation,” he added.

He appealed to Governor Sheriff Oborevwori to address residents of the state and warn individuals threatening violence.

“I call on our leader and my elder brother, the Governor of Delta State and the Chief Security Officer of the State, Rt. Hon. Elder Sheriff Oborevwori, to urgently address Deltans and read the Riot Act against those who are threatening fire and brimstone,” Keyamo said.

He ended the statement with the words, “Enough is enough.”

In a statement issued through his media adviser, Sunny Areh, Omo-Agege’s campaign organisation described the incident as an assassination attempt on the former deputy senate president during his campaign tour.

It called on the Nigeria Police Force to conduct an independent and thorough investigation into the attack and appealed to the Inspector-General of Police, the Department of State Services and the Independent National Electoral Commission to ensure the safety of political candidates and their supporters.

The organisation also urged supporters to remain calm and avoid retaliation, saying its ward-to-ward campaign activities across Delta Central would continue.

Omo-Agege, who is seeking election to represent Delta Central Senatorial District in the 2027 general election, said four members of his campaign organisation were killed, while about 15 others sustained injuries, some of them critically.

Among those reportedly killed were Moses Anaughe, a former member of the Delta State House of Assembly and personal assistant to Omo-Agege, and Akima Efemena Umukoro.

The controversy surrounding the slogan dates back to October 23, when Omo-Agege used it during the flag-off of his campaign for the Delta Central Senatorial District seat in Uvwie Local Government Area.

“Anybody who rigs the election on that day, we will kill him! If you don’t value your life, come and rig the election. They’re rigging your future. They’re rigging the future of your children. We’re waiting for them. Let them try it. It is rig and die,” he said.

Following the backlash, his Special Adviser on Strategy and Communications, Godwin Anaughe, defended the remarks, saying they were directed at electoral offenders who manipulate results and steal electoral materials, not voters or innocent citizens.

Keyamo had also reacted to the slogan on November 5 in a post on X, sarcastically arguing that its promoters would have to explain how many alleged riggers they killed if they lost an election conducted freely and fairly.

The slogan has featured amid political rivalry in Delta Central, where Omo-Agege is challenging incumbent Senator Ede Dafinone of the All Progressives Congress for the senatorial seat in the 2027 elections.

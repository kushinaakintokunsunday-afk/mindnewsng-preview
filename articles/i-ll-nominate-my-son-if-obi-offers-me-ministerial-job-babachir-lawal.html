I’ll nominate my son if Obi offers me ministerial job – Babachir Lawal

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former Secretary to the Government of the Federation, Babachir Lawal, has said he would give his 40-year-old son a ministerial appointment if Peter Obi offered

Lawal, a chieftain of the Nigerian Democratic Congress, said he was no longer interested in serving in government, citing his age and saying he had achieved all he wanted in life.

Speaking on Channels Television’s Politics Today on Friday, Lawal said he would rather return to his farm than take up a public office in an Obi-led administration.

“Go back to my farm and watch Nigeria grow,” he said when asked what he would do if Obi won the presidency.

Explaining his position, Lawal said, “See, I keep telling you I’m 72 years old. I have a 40-year-old son. What is wrong if Peter says, ‘BD, give me, I’m going to make you Minister of Agric.’ I said, ‘Peter, instead give my son Minister of Agric.’ Is that wrong?

“In those days when we were young, that’s how things were done. When they approach you, ‘You are going to be this,’ you say, ‘No, I’m too old for this, there are young generation.’ Pick and give! It’s doable.”

He described himself as an elder statesman and suggested that having served as SGF under former President Muhammadu Buhari, ministerial appointment would be a demotion.

“I’m 72! Past 72. Yes, very. An elder statesman. And God has done well for me. I’ve achieved everything that I need to achieve,” he said.

Lawal said he was not in the party to seek political office but to support what he described as a good man capable of rescuing Nigerians from hardship.

He expressed confidence that Obi would win the 2027 presidential election, insisting that the circumstances surrounding the 2023 poll would not be repeated.

The former SGF argued that the 2023 presidential election should have proceeded to a runoff, claiming that Obi secured the majority of votes but failed to meet the geographical spread requirement.

“Peter got the majority of votes; he didn’t get the spread. Bola Tinubu got the spread and came second. There should have been a runoff in that election,” he said.

Lawal predicted that Obi would win Kogi, Benue and Plateau states, as well as defeat former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar of the African Democratic Congress in his home state of Adamawa.

“I give it to you that Peter didn’t have more spread. This time, Peter will get the spread in Nigeria.

“Atiku will come third in Adamawa State this time around,” he added.

He also questioned Atiku’s chances in the election, arguing that the former vice-president would not have sufficient ethnic support to secure the presidency.

Comparing the NDC ticket with those of other political parties, Lawal described Obi and his running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso, as a better option.

He disclosed that the party’s campaign strategy would prioritise town hall meetings, which he considered a more effective way of engaging voters.

According to him, Nigerians would support Obi because of his character and competence.

“First of all, he is incorruptible. Second, he is detribalised. Third, he is a businessman that understands market forces. And fourth, he is a man who sticks to his words,” Lawal said.

Asked whether Obi had made any promises to him, the former SGF replied, “No, he doesn’t need to.”

Lawal served as SGF under Buhari from 2015 to 2017. He joined the NDC in September 2026 after leaving the African Democratic Congress and declared his support for the Obi-Kwankwaso ticket.

Obi, a former governor of Anambra State, is the NDC’s presidential candidate for the 2027 election, with former Kano State Governor Kwankwaso as his running mate.

In the 2023 presidential election, Obi, who contested on the platform of the Labour Party, finished third with 6,101,533 votes, behind Atiku, who polled 6,984,520 votes, and President Bola Tinubu, who won with 8,794,726 votes.

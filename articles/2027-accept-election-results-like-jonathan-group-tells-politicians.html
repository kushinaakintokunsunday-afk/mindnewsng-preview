2027: Accept election results like Jonathan, group tells politicians

By Punch Punch 6d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Ahead of the 2027 general elections, politicians seeking elective offices have been urged to emulate former President Goodluck Jonathan by accepting the outcome

The Journalists Monitoring Performance for Democratic Governance, led by its President, Vincent Akpomefure, made the call at a press conference held in Maryland, Lagos, on Wednesday.

Jonathan, who contested the 2015 presidential election on the platform of the Peoples Democratic Party, conceded defeat to the candidate of the All Progressives Congress, Muhammadu Buhari, after the poll.

His decision to accept the outcome paved the way for Nigeria’s first transfer of power from an incumbent ruling party to an opposition party through the ballot box.

Akpomefure urged political actors across the country to learn from Jonathan’s decision to concede defeat in the 2015 presidential election and facilitate a peaceful transfer of power to the opposition.

The concerns come amid recent attacks on political gatherings and leaders ahead of the 2027 elections.

On November 12, suspected political thugs disrupted a town hall meeting organised by the Nigeria Democratic Congress for its presidential candidate, Peter Obi, in Oregun, Lagos, attacking supporters and damaging vehicles.

In a separate incident on November 13, President Bola Tinubu’s motorcade was attacked with stones and other dangerous objects shortly after he observed Friday prayers at a mosque in the Garki area of Abuja.

Also, the Chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission, Prof. Joash Amupitan, was confronted by Nigerian protesters in London on November 12, 2026, who blocked his movement and demanded his resignation over allegations of irregularities in the preliminary voters’ register and fears about the neutrality of the commission ahead of the elections.

Akpomefure said the warning was prompted by growing political intolerance, attacks and threats of violence ahead of the elections, which could undermine the country’s stability.

He said the government and opposition must understand that they had a responsibility to protect democracy.

Akpomefure said, “A few months ahead of this all-important election, we have seen evidence of intolerance, violent attacks and threats of even greater violence.
“We are calling on politicians to learn from Dr Jonathan in the sense that they need to accept the outcome of the 2027 elections at the local, state and federal levels.”

He, however, pointed out that the appeal was not a call on President Bola Tinubu to hand over power to the opposition but a reminder that both winners and losers in the 2027 elections should respect the people’s choice.

He urged Nigerians to defend free and fair elections, stressing that the group was not advocating a situation in which the ruling party must lose power but one in which all political parties would respect a credible electoral outcome.

He said, “The important point is not that a ruling party must hand over to the opposition only to prove a free and fair election. No. What we seek is a free and fair election that Nigerians need to accept. If the ruling party wins, let the opposition respect the outcome of the election; if the opposition wins, let the ruling party respect the outcome.”

Akpomefure said the group was using the 2027 elections to draw attention to Jonathan’s decision to concede defeat in 2015, describing it as an example of humility and personal sacrifice for democratic stability.

He said the former President’s action demonstrated that the country’s interest should take precedence over the desire to retain political power, regardless of the ethnic background of the incumbent.

Akpomefure stressed, “We want an election that will leave us with confidence, peace, trust and stability, without which we can neither speak of democracy, liberty nor press freedom.

“As journalists, we need to insist on peaceful campaigns devoid of threats and violence, and devoid of religious and ethnic sentiments. We need to remember what the civil war cost us as a nation. We do not want to see a repeat of that ugly history.”

He said the 2027 elections would be significant because they would take place 60 years after the first military coup in Nigeria, which occurred on January 15, 1966, and warned against a repeat of the events that plunged the country into the civil war.

“The January election is significant in many ways. It will be taking place exactly 60 years after the first military coup,” he said.
He recalled that the 1964 general election contributed to the crisis that preceded the coup and the civil war, which lasted 30 months and claimed more than one million lives.

The media conference also marked the commencement of activities towards Jonathan’s 70th birthday celebration, during which the group announced plans to unveil a book titled “Goodluck Jonathan: The President Who Ruled With Golden Fingers.”

Akpomefure said the book would examine Jonathan’s political journey, presidency and contribution to Nigeria’s democratic development, with particular attention to the 2015 elections and the peaceful transfer of power that followed.

He said the publication would also explore the former President’s background, rise to power, electoral reforms, education policies and commitment to democratic values.
He added that the public presentation of the book would take place in 2027, after the general elections, as part of activities marking Jonathan’s 70th birthday.

The group also announced that the Ibenanawei of Bomo Kingdom in Southern Ijaw Local Government Area of Bayelsa State, King Joshua Igbugburu, would serve as the royal father of the occasion.

The monarch, who ascended the throne in 1958 at the age of 20, was described by the group as a longstanding traditional ruler with a place in the history of Bayelsa State and Nigeria.

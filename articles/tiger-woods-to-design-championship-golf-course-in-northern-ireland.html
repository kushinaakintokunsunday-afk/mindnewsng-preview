Tiger Woods to design championship golf course in Northern Ireland

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Golf legend Tiger Woods is set to design a new championship links golf course in Northern Ireland, marking a major development for the country’s golf tourism in

Woods and his golf course architecture firm, TGR Design, have unveiled plans for Bellarena Golf Links, which will be built along the spectacular Causeway Coast in the north-west of Northern Ireland.

The proposed course will be located approximately 20 miles west of Royal Portrush, a three-time host of The Open Championship.

The project will be Northern Ireland’s first new championship links course in more than a century and TGR Design’s first golf course development in Europe.

The 15-time major champion described the opportunity to create a course in one of the world’s most celebrated golfing destinations as a special experience.

As reported by BBC Sport on Thursday, Woods was quoted as saying, “Northern Ireland is home to some of the greatest links golf in the world, and building one on the Causeway Coast is a special opportunity for our team,” Woods said.

“We want Bellarena to feel as though it has always been part of this coastline, with a course that challenges the best players and is fun for everyone who plays it.”

The development is a partnership between Galgorm Collection and US-based Reef Destinations. Construction is expected to begin in 2027, subject to planning approval, with a target opening date of July 2029.

Galgorm Collection chief executive Colin Johnston described Woods’ involvement as a remarkable opportunity for the region.

“To have Tiger Woods and his company design a golf course in Northern Ireland is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity, it’s unbelievable,” Johnston said.

“It’s bringing together fantastic companies and partners and creating something that will benefit Northern Ireland for many years to come.”

Johnston said the location’s natural beauty was a major attraction, highlighting the views of Binevenagh Mountain, Lough Foyle and the Donegal hills.

“The backdrop of Binevenagh Mountain and the views over Lough Foyle and into the Donegal hills are just stunning,” he said.

Beyond its sporting appeal, the project is expected to strengthen tourism in Northern Ireland’s north-west by attracting more international golf visitors.

Johnston said golf tourism was already experiencing strong demand, making additional championship-level facilities important for the region.

“We know golf tourism is huge and we know it’s close to capacity,” he said.

“If we want to bring more people to this part of the world, we need to increase that capacity, and it needs to be at the elite level people expect when they come here.”

He added that Bellarena would complement established golfing destinations such as Royal Portrush and Royal County Down, while bringing more visitors to the region.

Unlike some of Northern Ireland’s exclusive championship venues, Bellarena is expected to operate as a non-member, pay-and-play course, giving visiting golfers greater access.

With Woods’ international profile, the spectacular coastal setting and the prospect of a new championship venue, Bellarena Golf Links is expected to become an important addition to Northern Ireland’s golfing landscape when it opens in 2029.

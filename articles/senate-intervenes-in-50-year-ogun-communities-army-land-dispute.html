Senate intervenes in 50-year Ogun communities, Army land dispute

By Punch Punch 20h ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Chairman of the Senate Committee on Ethics, Privileges and Public Petitions, Senator Neda Imasuen, has assured residents of at least 16 communities borderin

The Army had reportedly given the communities one week to vacate their ancestral land earlier this year, claiming that the expanse of land, said to measure more than 500 hectares, had been acquired over the last 50 years.

The action of the Army was said to have caused confusion and anxiety, with thousands of residents wondering where else to call home.

However, during the committee’s visit to Apunren-Ijebu and Ijebu Imushin on Monday, Imasuen said the Senate was working round the clock to ensure that the conflict was resolved in a way that would be acceptable to all parties involved.

He attributed the committee’s visit largely to the determination and sustained efforts of Senator Gbenga Daniel, who represents Ogun East Senatorial District, commending him for his commitment to bringing the matter before the Senate and ensuring that the concerns of the affected communities received the necessary attention.

The committee chairman acknowledged that the dispute had persisted for more than 50 years, describing the visit as an important step towards resolving an issue that had remained a source of concern to successive generations.

“We are here today largely because of the doggedness of our leader, Senator Gbenga Daniel. I am sure you are aware that members of the Committee have had to contend with several engagements across their respective states and senatorial districts. Despite these commitments, he worked tirelessly to ensure that we could come here at this time.

“I am glad that the people appreciate what he has done in this regard. This matter has been on for over 50 years, and Senator Gbenga Daniel appears to be bringing a special 50th-anniversary gift to the people through this intervention,” Imasuen said.

“We have come with a genuine commitment to see things for ourselves. We want to inspect the boundaries, the affected houses, the schools and other facilities so that we can properly appreciate the situation on the ground.

“Interestingly, as I alighted from the bus, I was informed that even this palace was, so to speak, is on Army land. I immediately asked: if the palace is on Army land, then who owns the land?” he queried.

While acknowledging the concerns raised by the communities, Imasuen commended the Nigerian Army for its cooperation and willingness to engage with the committee and other stakeholders in addressing the matter.

He said the Army’s approach had helped facilitate the committee’s work, noting that its leadership had demonstrated an understanding of the human and communal dimensions of the dispute.

“I must also express our appreciation to the Nigerian Army for its cooperation. They have been very helpful and have made our work easier. We appreciate the leadership of the Army for the way it has handled this engagement and for its efforts to give the matter a human face.

“We are all Nigerians, and we live together as one people. It is important that issues of this nature are approached with understanding, cooperation and a genuine commitment to finding solutions,” he stated.

Imasuen further assured the communities that the committee would make every effort to prepare its report promptly upon returning to Abuja, so that the document could be presented to the Senate for consideration and the necessary legislative action.

He expressed optimism that the committee’s intervention, coupled with Daniel’s sustained efforts, would bring the prolonged dispute closer to a satisfactory conclusion.

The senator, however, urged Apunren-Ijebu and other affected communities to maintain unity, cooperation and mutual understanding throughout the resolution process.

He cautioned that the interests of the various communities involved must be carefully harmonised to avoid resolving one dispute while inadvertently creating another.

Speaking during the committee’s visit, Daniel said he was deeply concerned by what he personally witnessed during the inspection of the affected areas.

“I came here to see things for myself, and I must confess that I was not particularly happy with what I saw. Many people’s houses have been marked by the Nigerian Army.

“From the information available to me, this land was acquired over 50 years ago, yet the matter has remained unresolved for so long,” he said.

He disclosed that he had successfully moved a motion on the floor of the Senate, drawing attention to the prolonged situation and the need for the National Assembly to intervene.

Daniel explained that the Senate subsequently authorised the appropriate committee to undertake an on-the-spot assessment of the affected communities and investigate the issues surrounding the land dispute.

“With the support you have given me as your senator, I was able to successfully present a motion on the floor of the Senate, drawing attention to what has been happening in our community.

“The National Assembly could no longer simply look away while this situation continued. Consequently, the appropriate committee was mandated to come here, see things for themselves and examine the circumstances surrounding the matter,” he stated.

Daniel expressed satisfaction that those engagements had begun to yield some positive developments, including the Army’s commencement of certain activities towards addressing the situation.

The senator thanked President Bola Tinubu, the Senate President, Godswill Akpabio, and the Chairman of the Senate Committee on Ethics, Privileges and Public Petitions, Senator Imasuen, among others, for their support and cooperation towards resolving the conflict.

Daniel urged the residents to remain calm and patient, assuring them that the committee had now personally inspected the affected areas, listened to the communities’ concerns and received relevant explanations from the military authorities.

“All the efforts we have made would amount to very little if we do not take this opportunity to move the matter forward. That is why we are here today.

“We have gone round the affected areas. You should therefore remain calm and patient. The members of the Committee have seen what is happening with their own eyes, and we have also listened to the explanations provided by the Army,” he said.

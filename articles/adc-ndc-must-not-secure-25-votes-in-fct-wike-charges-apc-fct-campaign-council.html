ADC, NDC Must Not Secure 25% Votes In FCT, Wike Charges APC FCT Campaign Council

By Channels TV Channels TV 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Channels TV

The Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Barr. Nyesom Wike, has charged members of the All Progressives Congress (APC) Presidential Campaign Council

The Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Barr. Nyesom Wike, has charged members of the All Progressives Congress (APC) Presidential Campaign Council and the Rainbow Coalition to ensure that opposition parties, particularly the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), do not secure 25 per cent of the votes in the FCT during the January 16, 2027 general election.

Wike gave the charge on Monday in Abuja during the inauguration of the APC Presidential Campaign Council/Rainbow Coalition FCT at the Bola Ahmed Tinubu International Conference Centre, where he urged members to intensify grassroots mobilisation and take the achievements of President Bola Tinubu’s administration to voters across the six area councils.

The minister, who is the coordinator of the FCT Presidential Campaign Council, said the forthcoming election presented an opportunity for the APC to demonstrate its political strength in the territory, insisting that members must not take victory for granted.

He challenged campaign council members to move beyond political rhetoric and present verifiable evidence of the achievements of the Tinubu administration, particularly in infrastructure, institutional reforms and policies designed to improve governance in the FCT.

“You don’t need to go and see a prophet. You don’t need to go and see any doctor or anybody to tell you what to do. It is obvious; it is factual. Look at what Mr President has done for the people of the FCT. These are the tools you need to campaign,” Wike said.

The minister argued that President Tinubu had demonstrated an unprecedented commitment to the development and political inclusion of the FCT, noting that several institutional reforms introduced by the administration had addressed longstanding demands of residents and indigenous communities.

According to him, 50 years after the creation of Abuja in 1976, Tinubu became the first president to ensure that the FCT was represented at the Federal Executive Council, where major national policies and decisions affecting the country are made.

He said the decision reflected a recognition that the FCT should not merely serve as the nation’s administrative capital but should also have a voice in national governance.

Wike further listed the establishment of the FCT Civil Service Commission, the appointment of a Head of Service and the elevation of FCT civil servants to permanent secretary positions as major milestones under the Tinubu administration.

He also highlighted the creation of the Mandate Secretariats for Women Affairs and Youth Development, saying the initiative had provided platforms for women and young people to participate more meaningfully in governance and channel their concerns to government.

The minister said the administration had also granted the FCT greater financial independence through its removal from the Treasury Single Account arrangement, enabling it to access loans to finance critical infrastructure projects.

On infrastructure, Wike challenged opposition politicians to compare projects executed under previous administrations with those delivered since Tinubu assumed office in 2023.

He maintained that the scale of ongoing projects across the city centre and satellite communities provided sufficient evidence of the president’s commitment to the territory.

Wike particularly cited the administration’s record in the issuance of Certificates of Occupancy (C of O), claiming that while previous administrations between 1999 and 2023 issued about 7,000 to 8,000 certificates, the Tinubu administration had issued more than 20,000 within three years.

“How can governments from 1999 to 2023 sign only about 8,000 C of Os, while the government of 2023 to 2026, in three years, has signed over 20,000? How?” he queried.

He urged campaign council members to take the message of the administration’s achievements to communities, wards and polling units, particularly in the satellite towns, where residents could assess the impact of government projects.

Earlier, the FCT APC Chairman, Ishaku Tanko Yamawo, expressed confidence that the party would secure an overwhelming victory for Tinubu and its National Assembly candidates in the forthcoming elections.

Yamawo described the infrastructure transformation under Wike as a major campaign advantage, saying the projects had strengthened public confidence in the APC-led administration.

“Your projects have already become our strongest campaign message. The goodwill and confidence you have earned among our people, both indigenes and residents of the FCT, are exceptional,” he told the minister.

He also commended the Rainbow Coalition for bringing political interests together, stressing that the alliance would strengthen the party’s mobilisation capacity across the six area councils.

Yamawo urged members of the campaign council to work as a united team, maintain effective coordination and ensure that the APC’s message reached every ward and polling unit.

Also speaking, the FCT PDP Chairman, Ismaila Mohammed Dogara, expressed confidence in the capacity of political leaders and stakeholders in the territory to mobilise support for Tinubu and other endorsed candidates, even in Wike’s absence.

Dogara commended the minister for building a strong political structure capable of sustaining mobilisation and coordinating electoral activities without his physical presence.

He described the cooperation between APC and PDP leaders under the Rainbow Coalition as a significant political development, expressing optimism that the alliance would translate into victory at the polls.

“Even if you are not in the FCT, our executive chairman does not allow any space, not even for one minute, before taking it from where you stopped. That shows that on the faithful day of the election, if the Honourable Minister is not in the FCT, those leaders are ready and capable of delivering Mr President, Senator Philip Aduda, Tanimu and others,” Dogara said.

He expressed confidence that members of the coalition would return to the conference centre on January 17, a day after the election, to celebrate their anticipated victory.

“The big day is January 16, and the return day is January 17. By the special grace of God, we are going to return to this hall to celebrate,” he declared.

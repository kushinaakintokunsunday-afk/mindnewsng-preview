ECOWAS court president seeks improved justice delivery, harmonised judicial standards

By Vanguard Vanguard 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG

By Innocent Anaba

The President of the Community Court of Justice of the Economic Community of West African States, ECOWAS, Justice Charlyne Brumskine, has called for stronger institutional collaboration, harmonised judicial practices and improved justice delivery across the West African sub-region.
Brumskine made the call while declaring open the Court’s 2026 Judicial Retreat in Niger State, Nigeria.
She described the five-day retreat as an important opportunity for judges and judicial support staff to review existing practices, address emerging institutional challenges and develop strategies for improving the administration of justice.
The ECOWAS Court president said the retreat was taking place at a critical stage in the institution’s development, following the adoption of a harmonised legal framework under Supplementary Act SA.1/12/25, the deployment of the Electronic Case Management System, ECMS, and the commencement of the implementation of the Court’s Strategic Plan 2026–2030.
She noted that the first strategic goal of the plan was to guarantee timely and impartial justice.
According to her, the developments require clear and harmonised institutional standards to ensure that the Court’s legal framework, technological infrastructure and judicial practices work together effectively.
“Our task this week is to build on that foundation, consolidate the best practices and experiences, and improve upon the standards where needed,” she said.
Brumskine stressed the importance of collaboration among the Judges’ Chambers, the Registry, the Research and Documentation Department and other support departments, noting that effective judicial administration depended on the collective efforts of all units.
“It is our collective responsibility to build a Court whose standards, systems and procedures consistently support judicial independence, efficiency, accountability and public confidence,” she added.
Earlier, the acting Deputy Chief Registrar of the Court, Mrs. Marie Saine, outlined the objectives and expected outcomes of the retreat.
She said the overall objective was to adopt harmonised standards, guidelines and working procedures to promote the effective and efficient administration of justice.
Saine identified the adoption of standardised judgment templates, a judgment-drafting style guide and quality-assurance procedures, as well as the establishment of an Editorial Committee, among the key objectives.
Others include strengthening legal research support and collaboration between the Judges’ Chambers and the Research and Documentation Department.
She said the retreat would also consider the 2027 judicial calendar, external court sessions, guidelines for virtual sittings and priorities for further development of the Electronic Case Management System.
The review and determination of Instructions to the Chief Registrar and Practice Directions are also expected to form part of the deliberations.
Saine added that participants would develop an implementation framework with clearly assigned responsibilities to ensure that recommendations from the retreat were effectively executed.
Expected outcomes include harmonised judgment templates and drafting standards, a quality-assurance framework, improved legal research support and stronger institutional collaboration.
The retreat is also expected to produce an agreed 2027 judicial calendar, guidelines for external and virtual court sessions, recommendations on procedural instruments and measures to improve the utilisation of the electronic case management system.
The 2026 Judicial Retreat is aligned with the ECOWAS Court’s Strategic Plan 2026–2030, which seeks to strengthen judicial efficiency, expand access to justice, promote regional integration and reinforce the rule of law and protection of human rights across West Africa.

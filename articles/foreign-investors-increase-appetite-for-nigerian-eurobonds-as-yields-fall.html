Foreign investors increase appetite for Nigerian Eurobonds as yields fall

By Punch Punch 17h ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Foreign investors increased their exposure to Nigerian dollar-denominated bonds amid renewed buying interest in emerging and African debt markets, pushing the a

The decline reflected broad-based price gains across Nigeria’s external debt securities, with investors showing particular interest in bonds maturing in February 2028, February 2030 and September 2033.

Trading activities in the international debt market were largely positive last week, as investors increased their positions in African sovereign bonds amid changing global market conditions.

Nigeria attracted renewed attention as the Federal Government sought parliamentary approval for plans to raise more than $20bn from external markets.

Other African sovereign issuers also recorded improved investor demand, with Angola’s dollar-denominated bonds attracting buying interest, while yields on Ghanaian and Egyptian Eurobonds also moderated.

Market sentiment was supported by expectations of easing inflationary pressures and further monetary policy adjustments, which improved foreign asset managers’ appetite for emerging-market debt.

However, developments in the United States continued to influence global fixed-income markets, as investors assessed stronger-than-expected employment data and its implications for the Federal Reserve’s interest rate decisions.

US Treasury yields rose on Friday following the release of October employment figures, strengthening expectations that the Federal Reserve could maintain a tighter monetary policy stance.

The benchmark 10-year Treasury note yield increased by more than two basis points to 4.864 per cent, while the 30-year bond yield climbed by more than one basis point to 5.263 per cent.

Similarly, the two-year Treasury yield advanced by more than four basis points to 4.522 per cent.

The contrasting movements in Nigerian Eurobond and US Treasury yields highlight the influence of changing global risk appetite and country-specific demand on sovereign borrowing costs.

For Nigeria, stronger foreign demand could support Eurobond prices, ease external borrowing costs and improve the government’s access to international debt markets.

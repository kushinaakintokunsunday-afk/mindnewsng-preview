Alleged forgery: Court orders lawyer to surrender passport before December 2

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The Federal Capital Territory (FCT) High Court sitting in Apo Abuja, on Wednesday November 18, 2026 ordered a lawyer, Victor Giwa who is put on trial alongside Ibitade Bukola Olugbenga for alleged forgery and impersonation to return his international passport on or before December 2, 2026, after questioning his failure to comply with an earlier directive to surrender the travel document which was part of the conditions of his bail granted 8 October 2026.

Justice Jude Onwuegbuzie issued the fresh order after the first defendant (Giwa) in the criminal case, told the court that his passport was being used to process his expired visa.

The matter, Charge No. CR/150/2025, was brought by the Inspector-General of Police brought against Victor Giwa and Ibitade Bukola, scheduled for the hearing of an application by a subpoenaed non-party witness, Chief Awa U. Kalu, SAN, seeking to set aside the subpoena issued against him.

At the commencement of proceedings on Wednesday, the prosecution counsel, Stanley Medugu, with E.T. Emmanuel, informed the court that the subpoenaed witness was absent.

He argued that a witness served with a subpoena was required to remain in court until formally discharged.

The prosecution also asked the court to establish whether Giwa had complied with its November 9, 2026 directive requiring him to return his international passport within 24 hours or show cause.

Rather than address the issue, the first defendant (Giwa), who appeared in person, sought the transfer of the case from Justice Onwuegbuzie’s court.

“I have no confidence in this court,” he declared, adding that he had filed an application seeking the judge’s recusal.

Counsel to the subpoenaed witness, Dr. Sunny Ajala, described the allegation as serious but urged the court to suspend proceedings pending the Chief Judge’s response to the petition.

Counsel to the second defendant, Ogbu Aboje, aligned himself with the position.

However, Justice Onwuegbuzie declined to suspend proceedings, stating that he had received no communication from the Chief Judge.

“The Honourable Chief Judge has not written me any letter. It is a criminal case. Have your motion,” the judge said.

Shortly afterwards, Giwa requested permission to use the toilet, complaining that there was no toilet facility within the court premises.

He subsequently left the courtroom and remained away for approximately one to two hours, during which the judge, prosecution and defence counsel, as well as other lawyers in the matter, waited for his return.

The delay interrupted the hearing of the application, which had previously suffered several adjournments.

Upon resumption, Ajala adopted the processes filed in support of his client’s application and urged the court to set aside the subpoena, describing it as excessively broad, oppressive, a fishing expedition and an abuse of court process.

The application, filed on October 30, 2026, was supported by an 11-ground argument and a 19-paragraph affidavit with two exhibits, alongside a further 10-paragraph affidavit filed on November 16, 2026.

Ajala argued that two subpoenas had been issued against his client, contending that the process interfered with his professional responsibilities as a legal practitioner under Rule 19 of the Rules of Professional Conduct for Legal Practitioners 2023.

He cited the Supreme Court decision in Josiah v. The State (1985) 1 NWLR (Pt. 1) 125.

In opposition, Medugu adopted the prosecution’s 41-paragraph counter-affidavit, deposed to by Salami Adewale, alongside five exhibits marked AKA to AKE and a written address dated November 10, 2026.

He urged the court to dismiss the application.

Giwa also attempted to oppose the application, relying on Section 398 of the ACJA, but the judge overruled his objection.

The court adjourned the application to December 2, 2026, for ruling.

It subsequently returned to the issue of Giwa’s international passport.

When Justice Onwuegbuzie asked where the document was, Giwa explained that it had been submitted as part of the documents required to process his expired visa.

He also argued that the earlier order concerning the passport was on appeal and that the record of appeal had been transmitted.

Giwa further contended that the prosecution was in possession of the document and that the court lacked jurisdiction to make further pronouncements on the matter.

The judge, however, questioned his failure to comply with the earlier directive.

“Are you aware that you are in contempt of this court for not returning the passport? I granted you the passport to enable you to go and see your family, with the expectation that you would return when you came back,” Justice Onwuegbuzie said.

Justice Onwuegbuzie consequently ordered Giwa to return the international passport to the court on or before December 2, 2026.

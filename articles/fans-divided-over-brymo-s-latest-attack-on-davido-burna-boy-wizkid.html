Fans divided over Brymo’s latest attack on Davido, Burna Boy, Wizkid

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Brymo’s latest remarks about Afrobeats stars Davido, Burna Boy and Wizkid have sparked debate among music fans, with some backing his views while others have fa

In a recent interview with Afromixx, Brymo, whose real name is Olawale Olofooro, questioned the criteria for describing the trio as Afrobeats’ so-called ‘Big Three’.

He argued that commercial success and mainstream recognition should not give any artiste the impression that they own Afrobeats.

Brymo also questioned whether their recognition was based on songwriting, vocal performances, live performances or their willingness to speak up for themselves.

“My real problem with those so-called Big Three before is that they’ve forgotten that it’s called the mainstream.

“If you want to nail yourself down somewhere and say, ‘I’m the king, I’m the…’ Then go and create something. First of all, Afrobeats is not yours. Yours is a derivative of the real thing. So what’s Big Three? Big Three as what? As songwriting? As vocal performances? As live performances? As speaking up for yourself?” he queried.

The singer also appeared to take a swipe at one of the artistes over his dependence on family members, questioning his maturity and independence, although he did not mention any name.

“You get a woman pregnant, your father is on live TV defending you. You can’t be the biggest artiste in Nigeria. Your mum carries you around the whole world, you say you’re the biggest artiste in Nigeria? You’re not a man.”

Brymo, however, offered a different perspective on Wizkid, suggesting that the singer’s decision to live abroad was influenced by challenges in Nigeria.

“I know Wizkid for sure, and I know why he’s here out here. If things were really working out in Nigeria, he probably would be back home full-time, just like me.”

His comments have since generated mixed reactions among music lovers. While some supported his views, arguing that musical talent should be measured by songwriting, vocal ability and stage performances rather than popularity, others accused him of attacking successful artistes instead of concentrating on his career.

Supporting Brymo, an Instagram user wrote, “I don’t care about their lifestyles or life choices. That is their kettle of fish. Each one will pay for his actions or inactions. But when it comes to meaningful music, vocal power, lyrical expertise, and relatable cultural content, Brymo beats all the so-called Big 3 hands down! I listen to all four, but Olawale Olofooro is in a class of his own.”

Another commenter, @kingmaikudimufasa, described Brymo as underrated, while @emry_yx said, “Brymo is really good.”

However, another user, @VOsimhen144, defended the trio, highlighting their contributions to the industry and support for others.

According to the commenter, Burna Boy involves his family in his business, Davido is known for supporting others, while Wizkid also helps people despite keeping his activities private.

“The Big 3 are making us proud. Brymo should stop crying,” the user wrote.

Some fans also suggested that Brymo was using the controversy to promote his upcoming show in London.

An Instagram user, @adebayo_fejiro, wrote, “Wahala promo your show and leave Davido abeg. This format is old.”

Similarly, @zeyzey8747 asked, “But why is Wizkid’s name always on your lips?”

Another commenter, @Cyzzy2000, questioned why Brymo frequently criticised other artistes, urging him to concentrate on his music.

“I believe he is talented and should just focus on his craft. He makes beautiful soul music that people appreciate, has a niche to himself. Why all this unnecessary controversy and bad-mouthing?” the user queried.

Similarly, @Negroid001 suggested that the singer was seeking relevance.

The latest development comes months after Brymo urged established Afrobeats stars to make way for younger artistes, accusing some of them of dominating the industry through the ‘Big Three’ and ‘Big Four’ labels.

This is not the first time Brymo has attracted attention for his comments about prominent Nigerian artistes.

Two years ago, in an interview with Chude Jideonwo on #WithChude, he claimed that popular Nigerian artistes who believed they had made it in the industry were mistaken, arguing that most did not own their masters or music catalogues.

He also boasted that no Nigerian artiste, dead or alive, could command two hours of listening without interruption in the way he could.

ADC crisis: Keyamo accuses Ojudu of misrepresenting his comments

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, has accused former lawmaker Senator Babafemi Ojudu of misrepresenting his comments on the leadership crisis in the African Democratic Congress out of what he described as hatred for President Bola Tinubu.

Keyamo, through his Special Adviser on Media and Research, Gabriel Emameh, said Ojudu’s criticism was part of a sustained campaign against the President, despite his continued claim to membership of the All Progressives Congress.

In a statement issued on Monday, Emameh said the minister’s earlier remarks on the ADC crisis were intended to defend the judiciary and caution opposition figures against making inflammatory statements following a Federal High Court judgment nullifying the party’s leadership.

The court, presided over by Justice Peter Lifu, had ruled against the leadership structure led by former Senate President David Mark, a development that has raised questions about the validity of decisions taken by the affected party leadership, including the process that produced former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar as its presidential candidate.

Ojudu had criticised Keyamo in a piece titled ‘Festus, Let Us Preserve the Country’, accusing him of taking a position that could heighten political tensions and undermine democratic tolerance.

However, Emameh maintained that the minister did not call for the suppression of opposition voices or advocate violence, but merely explained the implications of court judgments and the need for political actors to respect judicial decisions.

He quoted Keyamo as citing the Supreme Court’s decisions on the 2019 Zamfara governorship and legislative elections, as well as the 2020 Bayelsa governorship election, where the ruling APC lost victories initially secured by its candidates.

According to him, the party accepted those judgments despite their political consequences.

Keyamo was also quoted as saying that the heavens would not fall if Atiku did not appear on the ballot in the 2027 presidential election.

Emameh argued that the minister’s position was that the ADC could remain on the ballot while the eligibility of Atiku to contest under the party’s platform could be affected by the court’s decision on the leadership that conducted the nomination process.

He said there was nothing undemocratic about drawing attention to the possible legal consequences of the judgment.

The presidential politics of 2027 has increasingly brought the ADC into focus as opposition figures seek to build a formidable coalition against the ruling APC. However, the party’s leadership dispute has introduced fresh uncertainty into those efforts, particularly over its internal processes and the authority to conduct party activities.

The statement read in part, “As a senior member of the Bar and indeed a member of the Inner Bar, Mr Keyamo was only discharging a professional duty by clarifying the letter and spirit of the law and defending the sanctity of the courtroom. Whilst cautioning against such threats, he affirmed that court pronouncements are sacrosanct, however unpleasant some might find them.

“On February 13, 2020, when the Supreme Court nullified the victory of the APC governorship candidate in Bayelsa State, David Lyon, a day to his swearing-in (when he was in fact inspecting the stadium to be used), the APC took it with equanimity on the chin. When the Supreme Court in 2019 stripped all the APC victorious candidates from top to bottom (Governor to House of Assembly) of their victories at the polls in Zamfara State and replaced them with the PDP candidates that all lost at the polls, this is what Atiku Abubakar said at the time (JUSTICE HAS PREVAILED IN ZAMFARA). That judgment was against the ruling party. We took it on our chins. You cannot abuse judges and blackmail the judiciary when it doesn’t go your way. Heavens will not fall if a certain Atiku Abubakar is not on the ballot in 2027.”

Taking a swipe at Ojudu, Emameh recalled that the former senator, who served as political adviser to former Vice-President Yemi Osinbajo, had openly declared in 2022 that he would not support Tinubu if he emerged as the APC presidential candidate.

He also alleged that Ojudu had “repeatedly criticised the President on social media while continuing to identify as an APC member.”

Emameh further argued that Ojudu’s continued political engagement without harassment was “evidence of the administration’s tolerance of dissent.”

He noted that the former senator still held a political appointment in Ekiti State, governed by the APC, “while his daughter is also serving in an appointment under the Tinubu administration.”

The minister’s aide consequently dismissed Ojudu’s allegation of growing intolerance under Tinubu, insisting that “opposition figures have continued to organise, campaign and express their views without state-sponsored intimidation.”

He said the country had not witnessed state-sponsored political assassinations or midnight arrests of political opponents under the current administration.

On Ojudu’s reference to a property transaction involving Keyamo in Wuse, Abuja, Emameh said the minister purchased the property in 2008 from Independent Communications, the company associated with the ownership of TheNEWS and PM News.

He described Keyamo as “a long-standing real estate investor” and accused Ojudu of failing to provide the full context of the transaction.

Emameh, however, concluded by wishing Ojudu well in exercising his freedom of expression, insisting that the former senator remained free to criticise the President without hindrance.

Bayelsa boat accident: Reps demand safer boats, compulsory life jackets

By Punch Punch 2h ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The House of Representatives has called for the provision of safer boats, compulsory use of life jackets and stricter enforcement of water transportation safety

The lawmakers also urged the Federal Government to intensify rescue operations, provide relief materials to affected communities and introduce subsidised modern boats to reduce the risks associated with water transportation.

The resolutions followed a motion of urgent public importance moved by Rodney Ambaiowei, representing Southern Ijaw Federal Constituency, during plenary on Tuesday.

Moving the motion, Ambaiowei said more than 150 people, mostly indigenes of Southern Ijaw, were involved in the accident on November 20, 2026, while travelling from Ayama community to Ukubie for a condolence visit.

He said the boat capsized at the river junction between Oweikorogha and Otuan, throwing all its occupants into the water.

According to the lawmaker, rescue operations began immediately, with the Bayelsa State Government, security personnel and other relevant agencies joining efforts to save the victims.

As of November 22, he said, 51 people had been rescued alive with varying degrees of injuries and discomfort, while 29 others, including two children, had been confirmed dead.

The rescued victims were taken to medical facilities in Southern Ijaw Local Government Area and government hospitals in Yenagoa for treatment, while search operations continued for missing persons and possible survivors.

Ambaiowei expressed concern over the number of people still unaccounted for, warning that the scale of the tragedy required a more extensive search and rescue operation.

He said the accident had plunged families and residents of Southern Ijaw, particularly Ukubie and neighbouring communities, into mourning.

The lawmaker attributed the vulnerability of riverine communities to the poor condition of boats used for transportation, noting that many vessels were old, overworked and frequently overloaded because of their scarcity.

He also lamented the widespread absence of life jackets, floaters and other water safety equipment, as well as the failure to enforce their use, despite waterways being the major means of transportation in the constituency.

Ambaiowei noted that many communities had limited access to motorable roads, leaving residents heavily dependent on waterways for their daily movement, despite ongoing road construction projects by the Bayelsa State Government.

He warned that failure by the National Emergency Management Agency and other relevant bodies to intensify rescue operations and provide relief materials could worsen the hardship facing affected families.

The lawmaker added that fishing, which provides food and income for many residents, had also been disrupted by the tragedy.

He commended President Bola Tinubu for sending Vice-President Kashim Shettima to the affected communities to sympathise with bereaved families, noting that the Federal Government had also donated ₦250m to the victims.

Contributing to the motion, lawmakers called for a broader review of water transportation in riverine communities, arguing that government interventions had largely focused on road transportation at the expense of people who depend on waterways.

The House Minority Leader, Fred Agbedi, said the tragedy had exposed the difficulties faced by riverine communities and called for the introduction of subsidised modern boats, similar to the Federal Government’s intervention in road transportation through Compressed Natural Gas buses.

“Mr Speaker, this incident brings to the fore what people in riverine communities are suffering. I think it is an opportunity for us to address these challenges.

“I am aware that the Federal Government is providing CNG buses, which are more economical. But whenever we talk about mass transit, there is no provision for river craft.

“In this century, people are still using dugout boats with weak roofs that could collapse on passengers. If the government provides modern vessels for transportation at subsidised rates, as it does with mass transit and CNG buses, I am sure incidents like this could be prevented,” he said.

Agbedi described the motion as an opportunity to address the longstanding neglect of riverine communities, insisting that federal transportation policies should also accommodate people living in areas with little or no access to roads.

He urged the Federal Government to include modern boats in its transportation interventions and future budgetary provisions to make travel to remote riverine communities safer and more affordable.

On his part, Shina Oyedeji urged the government to stop paying lip service to safety standards, describing the accident as a wake-up call for authorities to strengthen the regulation of transportation operations.

He questioned the training and certification of boat operators and called for proper inspection of vessels and other transport facilities.

“I think this is an opportunity for us as a nation to start doing things the right way.

“The time to stop playing games is now. The government meant well by introducing CNG buses to make transportation cheaper, but the implementation must be properly regulated to ensure safety.

“Many of these drivers and operators, I do not know who trained them or who certified them. When was the last time their certifications were reviewed?” he said.

Oyedeji warned against adopting practices from other countries without establishing and enforcing appropriate standards to reflect Nigeria’s circumstances.

He urged the House committee responsible for safety standards to examine the matter and ensure that relevant regulations were properly enforced.

“Safety standards must be taken seriously in Nigeria,” he added.

The Deputy Majority Whip, Isiaka Ibrahim, said responsibility for safety on inland waterways rested with both federal and state governments, noting that creeks and offshore waters were on the concurrent legislative list.

He called for an investigation into the accident and urged lawmakers to examine the safety standards observed by boat operators, particularly in comparison with multinational companies operating in Nigeria’s offshore waters.

Ibrahim said the safety measures adopted by multinational companies operating in offshore waters should serve as a reference for improving safety practices in the country’s creeks.

He cautioned against relying on swimming ability as a substitute for basic safety precautions, particularly the use of life jackets.

“I know how to swim. I have been swimming for 40 years. But you do not know the condition of the boat, the water, its depth or what may have happened to the boat.

“We ignore life vests and life jackets, and then we talk about accidents. This motion should provide an opportunity to learn from what has happened and address the causes,” he said.

However, the House Leader, Prof. Julius Ihonvbere, argued that while federal intervention was important, communities, maritime associations, local governments and state authorities must also be held accountable for safety failures.

He said television footage of the accident showed passengers boarding boats without life jackets, describing the practice as unacceptable and calling for tougher penalties against those who violated safety regulations.

“Mr Speaker, I think our aim should be to extend the leadership’s deep sympathies to the people of Bayelsa over this unfortunate incident.

“But this is not an issue for the Federal Government alone. We must look at the grassroots, including community associations, maritime associations, local government councils, state governments and the relevant agencies.

“Even as we watched reports on television, we saw people boarding boats and canoes without life vests. How does somebody get on a boat without a life vest?” he asked.

Ihonvbere insisted that familiarity with waterways or swimming experience should not be an excuse for disregarding safety requirements.

He also questioned the responsibility for inspecting watercraft, describing the condition of some boats operating in riverine communities as unacceptable.

The House Leader called for stronger enforcement at jetties and other boarding points, warning that recurring accidents would continue unless authorities and community-level institutions took responsibility.

He said penalties and enforcement were necessary to ensure compliance with safety regulations, adding that the responsibility for preventing such incidents should not rest solely on the Federal Government.

The House unanimously adopted the motion and mandated its Committee on Emergency and Disaster Management to work with NEMA and the Bayelsa State Government to investigate the immediate and remote causes of the accident and recommend measures to prevent future occurrences.

It also urged NEMA, the Niger Delta Development Commission, the Nigerian Inland Waterways Authority and the South-South Development Agency to provide additional relief materials to affected communities to cushion the impact of the tragedy and the loss of livelihoods.

The lawmakers further called for the urgent provision of safer boats, life jackets, floaters and other water transportation safety equipment to riverine communities, with measures to ensure their compulsory use.

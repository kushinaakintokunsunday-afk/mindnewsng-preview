Electoral violence: No life worth wasting over politics, Kalu warns

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

• Dismisses opposition coalition, says Tinubu has no formidable challenger • Denies supplying arms to IPOB, disowns allegations against Abia deputy gov From Ade

• Dismisses opposition coalition, says Tinubu has no formidable challenger  • Denies supplying arms to IPOB, disowns allegations against Abia deputy gov

From Adesuwa Tsan, Abuja

Former Abia State governor and Senator representing Abia North, Orji Uzor Kalu, has warned politicians and their supporters against turning the 2027 general elections into a battlefield, declaring that no political ambition or electoral victory is worth the loss of human lives.

Kalu, who condemned the growing wave of attacks on political leaders and their supporters, said political contests should be fought on the strength of candidates’ achievements, leadership records and capacity to deliver good governance rather than through intimidation, thuggery and violence.

He also dismissed the emerging opposition coalition as belated and divided, declaring that President Bola Tinubu had no formidable challenger in the forthcoming presidential election and describing the contest as a battle of “Tinubu versus Tinubu.”

Speaking with journalists shortly after receiving the Best Impactful Representation Award from the Senate Press Corps at the National Assembly, Abuja, on Tuesday, Kalu expressed concern that political violence, which had troubled Nigeria since independence, was again threatening to overshadow democratic competition.

“Nobody has asked anybody to go and fight or engage in violence. Campaigns should be fought based on what candidates have done for the country. No life is worth wasting because of politics.”

He urged political leaders to restrain their supporters and discourage inflammatory rhetoric, stressing that political disagreements should never be allowed to destroy relationships or threaten national unity.

According to him, leading politicians across party lines maintained cordial personal relationships despite their political differences, a lesson he said their supporters should learn rather than resort to violence in defence of individual political ambitions.

“Tinubu, Atiku, Obi, Makinde and others are all friends who eat at the same table. Nigeria needs unity. We don’t need fighting,” he said.

His warning came against the backdrop of mounting concerns over political attacks and intimidation ahead of the 2027 elections, with the Senate also debating incidents involving President Tinubu and opposition political leaders.

Turning to the emerging opposition coalition, Kalu argued that the parties seeking to challenge the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) had wasted valuable time and remained divided over leadership and the choice of a presidential candidate.

He said a genuinely united opposition could have presented a more difficult contest for the APC but maintained that the ongoing consultations had come too late to substantially alter the political calculations ahead of the election.

“If they had sincerely come together, it would have given APC a headache, but not a win. The game is already too late for them because they cannot even agree on who will be their presidential candidate.”

Kalu dismissed the possibility that consultations involving the African Democratic Congress (ADC), Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) and other opposition platforms would produce a coalition capable of defeating the ruling party.

“We are not afraid of any party. APC is the ruling party, and we have massive grassroots support,” he declared. The senator, however, cautioned against making unsubstantiated allegations of sponsorship of political violence, particularly against public office holders.

Reacting to claims by the Abia State Tinubu Support Group that the state’s deputy governor was sponsoring thugs to attack supporters of the President, Kalu said anyone found culpable of recruiting, financing or training political thugs should face the consequences of the law.

He stressed that such conduct should not be associated with persons entrusted with public responsibilities but maintained that he had no verified evidence linking the deputy governor to the allegation.

Kalu said he did not personally believe the claims, describing the deputy governor as a well-trained engineer with whom he had maintained a longstanding relationship.

He nevertheless appealed to Governor Alex Otti to engage his deputy and encourage actions that would promote political stability and peaceful coexistence in the state.

The senator said security agencies, including the Department of State Services (DSS), could investigate the allegations and invite anyone considered necessary for questioning, stressing that accusations of political violence should be established through credible investigations rather than public speculation.

On the controversy generated by his earlier remarks about international arms transactions, Kalu denied ever supplying weapons to the proscribed Indigenous People of Biafra (IPOB), insisting that he was neither a defence contractor nor involved in illegal arms dealings.

He explained that international arms transactions could constitute legitimate commercial activities when undertaken by licensed and authorised entities in accordance with applicable regulations, but stressed that his comments should not be interpreted as support for supplying weapons to terrorists, bandits or private individuals.

“I have never supplied arms to IPOB. I have never seen arms in my life,” he maintained.

Responding to speculation about his possible presidential ambition following a prophecy by Prophet Ebuka Obi of the Zion Prayer Movement that he would become Nigeria’s president before the age of 70, Kalu said the fulfilment of divine will could not be determined by human calculations.

He recalled contesting the presidential election in 2007 but said he had no immediate intention of seeking the office again, preferring to concentrate on his legislative responsibilities.

“The will of God is always the will of God, and nobody can stop it. I am capable, and I ran for president when I was much younger, but for now, I have no ambition. I want to sit quietly in the Senate,” he said.

Earlier, Kalu dedicated the Senate Press Corps’ Best Impactful Representation Award to the people of Abia North, describing the recognition as an acknowledgment of the development efforts undertaken across the senatorial district since his election to the National Assembly in 2019.

The award citation highlighted projects and interventions across the five local government areas of Bende, Ohafia, Isuikwuato, Umunneochi and Arochukwu, particularly in road construction, education, healthcare, agriculture and economic empowerment.

According to the organisers, Kalu had facilitated the construction and rehabilitation of more than 160 rural and agricultural access roads, alongside over 200 school structures and classroom blocks across the district.

Other interventions cited included scholarships, payment of Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) examination fees, provision of educational materials, rehabilitation of community health centres, installation of solar-powered streetlights and provision of boreholes in more than 150 communities.

The citation also recognised the distribution of agricultural inputs and more than 1,000 cassava processing machines, as well as the facilitation of cashew processing factories and tricycle assembly plants.

Under his constituency empowerment programmes, Kalu was credited with distributing motorcycles, sewing machines, generators, refrigerators, tricycles, laptops and financial grants to beneficiaries across Abia North.

In his acceptance speech, the senator disclosed that more than 49 additional roads were under construction across the district, with progress dependent on the release of relevant budgetary funds.

“If the budget has been released, there are more than 49 new roads currently being constructed in the district,” he said.

Kalu explained that his approach to representation prioritised infrastructure and human capital development over the routine distribution of material items, arguing that investments in roads, education and skills acquisition would deliver more enduring economic benefits.

“What is meant for the public is for the public. My duty is to serve the people.

“I don’t mostly do empowerment because empowerment is not the way to save the economy. We have to go back to providing roads, sending children to school and sending people to study so they can be useful to their communities.”

He reaffirmed his commitment to extending development projects across the five local government areas of Abia North, saying his legislative responsibilities remained focused on improving infrastructure, expanding educational opportunities and supporting economic activities in the district.

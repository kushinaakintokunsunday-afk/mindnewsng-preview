2027: INEC plans mock election in 638 Rivers polling units

By Punch Punch 6d ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Independent National Electoral Commission said it will conduct a mock accreditation and election results upload exercise in 638 polling units across Rivers

INEC said the move is part of preparatios for the general elections in 2027.

INEC Resident Electoral Commissioner in Rivers State, Dr Johnson Sinikiem, disclosed this during the State-Level Stakeholders’ Meeting on the 2027 General Elections at the commission’s headquarters in Port Harcourt on Wednesday.

Sinikiem said two polling units would be selected in each of the state’s 319 registration areas, also known as wards, for the exercise.

He said the exercise will test the performance of the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System and network connectivity under real election conditions.

The INEC REC explained that the exercise was designed to identify possible technical challenges and assess the efficiency of the technology ahead of the general elections.

Sinikiem called on security agencies, political parties and the electorate to support the exercise to ensure its smooth conduct, saying adequate information would be provided to the public on the selected polling units and other arrangements for the exercise.

He said INEC was intensifying preparations for the 2027 elections, noting that Rivers State had approximately 3,736,434 registered voters spread across 23 local government areas, 319 registration areas and 6,866 polling units.

He disclosed that the commission currently had 6,399 fully functional and configured BVAS devices in its inventory.

He added that additional units would be deployed from the national headquarters to cover all polling units and provide operational backups.

The REC also announced that the commission had received 173,241 Permanent Voter Cards in different batches ahead of the elections.

Sinikiem further said 99,381 PVCs, comprising previously uncollected and newly printed cards, had been collected by voters as of Tuesday, November 17, urging those whose cards were ready to visit INEC offices in their respective local government areas.

He stated, “I strongly urge citizens who cards are available to visit our 23 LGA offices to claim them. Nationwide mock accreditation and results upload in Rivers State.

“INEC will be conducting a nationwide mock accreditation and result upload exercise on the 28th November 2026 to test the bimodal voter accreditation system, BVAS, ahead of the 2027 general elections.

“This exercise will span across all the 23 local government areas and 319 registration areas which is the wards across Rivers State.”

He noted that the commission had also taken delivery of essential non-sensitive election materials, including voting cubicles, mats, ballot boxes and other operational accessories, which had been audited and distributed to the 23 local government areas for safe storage.

He urged security agencies to strengthen surveillance around INEC offices to protect the materials against theft or damage.

On personnel mobilisation, Sinikiem said recruitment of ad hoc staff for the elections, which opened on September 18, would close at midnight on Wednesday, November 18.

He identified members of the National Youth Service Corps as critical partners in the electoral process, adding that the commission had sustained engagements with corps members to prepare them for their election duties.

The REC further disclosed that intensive training was ongoing for INEC personnel in electoral risk management, Election Monitoring and Support Centres, and the Collation Support and Result Verification System.

He said the training would improve risk assessment, field monitoring, rapid response and transparency in the collation of election results.

Sinikiem reiterated the commission’s commitment to conducting free, fair, credible, transparent and inclusive elections across Rivers State, stressing that the success of the electoral process required the cooperation of all stakeholders.

He said presidential and National Assembly elections were scheduled for January 16, 2027, while governorship and State House of Assembly elections would hold on February 6, 2027.

In his goodwill message, the Rivers State Commissioner of Police, Olugbenga Adepoju unveiled unveiled security measures to prevent electoral violence during the elections, including round-the-clock protection of INEC facilities, intelligence-led monitoring of flashpoints and deployment of more than 5,000 trained police officers.

CP Adepoju warned politicians, political parties and their supporters against thuggery, ballot snatching, vote buying and voter intimidation, declaring that anyone found sponsoring electoral violence would be arrested and prosecuted regardless of status.

He disclosed the measures during a stakeholders’ engagement on preparations for a peaceful and violence-free election in Rivers State.

Adepoju said it had concluded security planning in collaboration with other security agencies under the Inter-Agency Consultative Committee on Election Security, covering the state’s 23 local government areas, 319 wards and 6,866 polling units.

He said joint operational plans, patrol routes and deployment arrangements had been developed to secure the electoral process before, during and after the elections.

On the protection of electoral facilities and personnel, the police said arrangements had been concluded for 24-hour security at INEC offices, Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) vaults and Registration Area Centres.

He disclosed that more than 5,000 officers earmarked for election duties were undergoing refresher training on electoral laws, human rights and rules of engagement.

To improve public engagement and emergency response, he said the police had established an election situation room and control room, alongside dedicated telephone hotlines through which residents could report security incidents.

“Area commanders and Divisional Police Officers have also been directed to organise town hall meetings with stakeholders in their respective areas to identify community concerns and address potential threats before election day”, he said.

The police urged voters not to be intimidated by political interests, assuring them of adequate security to exercise their franchise.

He stressed that the responsibility for peaceful elections extended beyond security agencies, calling on INEC to ensure transparency in the electoral process and prompt transmission of results.

“Political parties and candidates are urged to sign and enforce peace accords, discipline their supporters and focus their campaigns on issues rather than personal attacks”, he said.

Adepoju also called on traditional rulers and religious leaders to use their influence to discourage do-or-die politics, mediate disputes and encourage communities to adopt peace commitments ahead of the elections.

He urged civil society organisations to intensify voter education, monitor the electoral process and report violations, particularly at the grassroots.

Also speaking, The National Orientation Agency in Rivers pledged to intensify grassroots mobilisation and voter education to discourage electoral violence, vote buying, hate speech and voter intimidation ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The agency said it would take its peace advocacy campaigns to communities, markets, schools, churches, mosques and youth groups to promote peaceful participation and responsible conduct before, during and after the elections.

The State Director of NOA, Dr Edward Banigo, described the 2027 general election as a collective responsibility rather than the exclusive business of INEC or political parties, stressing that its success would depend on cooperation among electoral authorities, security agencies, political actors and citizens.

He commended the level of preparedness demonstrated by INEC, security agencies, civil society organisations, the media, political parties and other stakeholders, particularly in early planning, voter education, deployment of materials and the adoption of technology to strengthen electoral transparency.

He said, “The 2027 general election is not just an INEC election. It is not a political party election. It is our election. And its success will depend on how prepared we are, how united we are, and how committed we are to peace.”

The NOA director warned political actors and their supporters against treating the elections as a do-or-die contest, urging them to reject violence, hate speech, vote buying, intimidation and misinformation.

He said credible elections could only be achieved through sustained collaboration among INEC, security agencies, political parties, candidates, observers, the judiciary, traditional and religious leaders, youth and women groups, and the media.

The meeting was attended by political party representatives, security agencies, youths, religious leaders, civil organisations and women groups among others.

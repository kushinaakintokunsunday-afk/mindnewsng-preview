Centre calls for probe into alleged diversion of firearms by Anambra security outfit

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Rule of Law and Accountability Advocacy Centre has called on the Anambra State governor, Prof. Chukwuma Soludo and the Inspector-General of Police, Tunji Di

RULAAC, also called for witness protection, an immediate firearms audit and a review of the outfit’s leadership in light of pending allegations and recurring reports of misconduct.

The group stated this in a press statement signed by its Executive Director, Okechukwu Nwanguma, and made available to our correspondent in Awka, on Tuesday.

Nwanguma said a social critic and public commentator, Dr Uche Nworah, drew public attention to the allegations, which he attributed to a commercial transport operator from Eziowelle, Idemili North Local Government Area of the state.

He said the gravity of the claims called for immediate preservation of evidence, protection for the person who came forward, and an investigation independent of the police and its chain of command.

Recall that the Anambra State Government established Agunechemba under the Anambra State Homeland Security Law, 2025, and launched Operation Udo Ga-Achi as a security intervention to complement regular policing.

RULAAC, however, said the government has a responsibility to account for the weapons entrusted to its operatives and to ensure that residents can report suspected misconduct safely.

The group, therefore, calls for the following action, “The Inspector-General of Police should appoint an independent investigative team.

Investigators should obtain the transport operator’s account confidentially, seek corroborating evidence and determine whether any officially issued firearms can be linked to reported kidnappings or armed robberies.

“The police should provide a secure reporting channel and assess the witness’s protection needs promptly. The Governor should order an immediate firearms and ammunition audit and preserve relevant records.

“The state should secure armoury registers, weapons issue and return records, and relevant duty rosters, while auditors should reconcile the records with a physical inventory, document discrepancies and make their findings available to investigators.

“The Governor should review the outfit’s leadership and appointment process. Leadership appointments should follow rigorous checks of integrity, competence and disciplinary history. The review should examine pending allegations, recurring reports of misconduct and how commanders handled earlier complaints.

“Where credible evidence warrants interim action, the state should consider temporarily removing implicated personnel from armed duties or supervisory roles, with due process.

“The state should strengthen recruitment, periodic vetting and oversight. Every operative entrusted with a firearm should be identifiable in the records, and supervisors should routinely verify weapons custody and review misconduct complaints. Residents should have a clear channel for lodging complaints outside the outfit’s chain of command.

“The authorities should inform the public of the outcome.* The Governor and the Inspector-General should announce who will conduct the investigation and provide updates consistent with witness safety and the integrity of the inquiry.

“Where evidence supports criminal charges, the police should refer the case for prosecution. Any proven supervisory or disciplinary failures should also be addressed.

“People who report suspected abuses by security operatives should be able to come forward without fear of retaliation. The immediate test for the authorities is whether they can protect the witness, account for the weapons and establish what happened through an independent investigation.”

However, efforts to get the reactions of the Operation Udo-Ga-Chi through their Spokesman, Nweke Nweke, proved abortive as he did not take several calls to his phone and failed to reply to messages as at the time of filing this report.

NEC approves additional N130bn for police training institutions

By Punch Punch 16h ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

This brings the total funding to N230bn as the country prepares for the establishment of state police and the recruitment of 50,000 new officers.

The Council also endorsed two major development projects backed by proposed World Bank financing of $850m, covering disaster preparedness and early childhood development, and received an economic update showing that inflation had fallen for the fifth consecutive month to its lowest level since September 2020.

These were among the decisions taken at the Council’s 162nd meeting, chaired by Vice President Kashim Shettima, according to a statement signed on Monday by the Vice President’s Senior Special Assistant on Media and Communications, Stanley Nkwocha.

“With state police on the horizon, the National Economic Council has committed an extra N130bn to overhaul Nigeria’s police training institutions, bringing the total funding to N230bn,” the statement read.

Presenting an update on the rehabilitation of police training institutions, the NEC Ad-Hoc Committee, chaired by Enugu State Governor Peter Mbah, disclosed that interventions were currently underway in 13 locations across the country, with 30 per cent of the rehabilitation work completed.

The committee said the additional N130bn would accelerate rehabilitation works, upgrade training facilities and strengthen the capacity of police institutions to accommodate increased recruitment and training requirements.

The Council had earlier approved N100bn for the rehabilitation programme following the establishment of the committee in October 2025, on the recommendation of President Bola Tinubu.

The intervention is also expected to support preparations for the recruitment of 50,000 police personnel and improve the country’s security training infrastructure ahead of the rollout of state policing.

On the state of the economy, Minister of Finance and Coordinating Minister of the Economy, Taiwo Oyedele, informed the Council that Nigeria’s real Gross Domestic Product grew by 4.43 per cent in the second quarter of 2026, compared with 4.23 per cent in the corresponding period of 2025.

Oyedele disclosed that headline inflation declined for the fifth consecutive month to 14.06 per cent in October 2026, its lowest level since September 2020, while external reserves stood at $54.87bn as of November 12, 2026.

He added that Nigeria’s trade surplus doubled from N10.2tn in the first half of 2025 to N20.1tn in the corresponding period of 2026, while net revenue distributed through the Federation Account Allocation Committee rose by 44 per cent, from N15.2tn in 2024 to N21.9tn in 2025.

Oyedele, however, cautioned that the improving macroeconomic indicators had yet to translate sufficiently into relief for households and businesses, particularly in the areas of food prices, electricity costs, access to affordable credit and employment. He noted that food inflation remained elevated at 19.13 per cent in October, while high lending rates and energy costs continued to place pressure on businesses and household incomes.

The Council also approved two memoranda presented through the Federal Ministry of Budget and Economic Planning, headed by Senator Atiku Bagudu.

The first memorandum concerned the proposed $350m Accelerating Resilience through Investment Systems and Engagement, ARISE-Nigeria, project, a six-year World Bank-supported programme designed to strengthen the country’s capacity to anticipate, prepare for and respond to floods, droughts, fires and other natural disasters.

The programme will improve emergency operations centres, early warning systems, weather monitoring and disaster response financing at federal and state levels.

Nkwocha revealed that between five and 10 states were expected to participate in the initial phase, with selection based on disaster risk exposure, institutional readiness and commitment to emergency preparedness.

The second memorandum concerned Nigeria’s State-Led Early Childhood Development Programme, designed to improve children’s access to healthcare, nutrition, early learning and protection services across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory.

The initiative is expected to receive support from a proposed $500m World Bank financing arrangement, with the Global Financing Facility indicating potential additional grant support of $30m.

Under the programme, early childhood development hubs will connect primary healthcare facilities with childcare, preschool education and other essential social services, particularly in underserved communities. State governors are expected to provide leadership for implementation, resource mobilisation and accountability, while existing institutions will coordinate service delivery.

On the proposed NIMC Act 2026, the Council called for stronger collaboration between the National Identity Management Commission and state governments to accelerate the registration of Nigerians and improve the integration of the National Identification Number into public services.

The Council urged state governments to fast-track identity registration and data capture to ensure that all Nigerians are covered by the national identity system, saying the initiative would strengthen public service delivery, improve tax administration, eliminate duplicate records and reduce fraud in government payrolls, pensions and social intervention programmes.

On pension reforms, the Director-General of the National Pension Commission, Omolola Oloworaran, presented a proposal titled ‘Securing the Retirement of Nigerian Workers While Strengthening State Finances,’ calling for the full implementation of the Contributory Pension Scheme across the federation.

The Council approved the proposal and urged state governors to formally adopt implementation timelines for the scheme, while directing their administrative and finance teams to work with PenCom on the necessary arrangements.

In his opening remarks, Vice President Shettima said Nigeria’s economic future and the lasting success of President Tinubu’s reforms depended on building strong institutions capable of outlasting any single administration.

“While His Excellency, President Bola Tinubu, GCFR, makes the difficult reforms to stabilise the economy, we must all participate in establishing institutions capable of sustaining the benefits of those reforms. This requires ministries and states to settle practical responsibilities without losing sight of the people affected. To achieve this, we must cooperate,” he said.

Shettima urged Council members to consider the Nigerian who would depend on the decisions they made years from now. He said sound practice in one jurisdiction should inform decisions elsewhere while respecting differences in capacity and circumstance across the country.

“The quality of our cooperation will shape the inheritance we leave,” he said.

On the ARISE disaster resilience project, the Vice President said the fundamental obligation was “to reduce the damage that disruption can do to incomes, enterprises and public services,” noting that resilience begins with decisions made before a shock rather than after it.

“This matters especially where a family or business has little room to absorb another loss. The capacity to recover should never depend entirely on the resources a person already possesses.

“People affected by an intervention must also have a place in shaping it. Their experience can reveal what a proposal overlooks. The strength of a project rests partly on the institutions and citizens that will carry it,” Shettima said.

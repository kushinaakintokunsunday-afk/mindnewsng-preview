Abbas urges fairer global narrative on Nigeria, says country is more than its challenges

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 4 min read

• Insists call for balanced reporting not an attempt to shield government from scrutiny

Speaker of the House of Representatives, Tajudeen Abbas, has urged the international community to adopt a fairer and more balanced narrative about Nigeria, warning against portraying the country exclusively through its economic difficulties, insecurity and other challenges.

Abbas said while the hardship confronting Nigerians was real and demanded urgent attention from governments, Nigeria should not be defined solely by its problems at the expense of its human capital, economic potential and development progress.

He spoke on Monday at the ongoing Global Alliance World Public Relations Forum in Abuja, where he addressed issues bordering on responsible communication, democratic accountability, governance and institutional reforms.

The Speaker, who was represented by the House Majority Leader, Prof. Julius Ihonvbere, said there was a distinction between reporting Nigeria’s challenges and reducing the country’s identity to those difficulties.

“The difficulties confronting households and businesses are real and deserve serious attention,” he said.

He added: “There is a difference between reporting a country’s difficulties and reducing the country to those difficulties. There is also a difference between legitimate scrutiny and the construction of narratives that oversimplify complex realities.

“We do not seek immunity from scrutiny. We seek fairness in the exercise of scrutiny. This is not an argument for managing Nigeria’s image at the expense of truth.”

Abbas urged public relations practitioners and other communication professionals to promote fairness, accuracy and balance in their work, noting that the narratives they shaped could influence public understanding of countries, institutions and democratic processes.

He said the government must, however, match its demand for fair international representation with concrete action to improve the living conditions of citizens.

According to him, Nigerians expect improved security, more employment opportunities, efficient public services and better governance, while the benefits of ongoing economic reforms must begin to reflect in their daily lives.

He said the reforms being implemented by the President Bola Tinubu-led administration were intended to address structural weaknesses, restore economic stability and lay the foundation for sustainable growth.

Acknowledging the immediate costs of the reforms, Abbas said government had a responsibility to ensure that their eventual benefits translated into tangible improvements in the lives of ordinary Nigerians.

On insecurity, he called for decisive and sustained action against perpetrators of violence, describing every innocent life lost as a tragedy requiring justice and accountability.

“Every innocent life lost is a tragedy that calls for justice and accountability,” he said.

He urged the country to strengthen its security institutions and protect every citizen, irrespective of religion, ethnicity or place of origin.

Reflecting on Nigeria’s democratic journey since 1999, Abbas said the country had sustained constitutional governance through successive elections, changes in leadership and the continued operation of democratic institutions.

He, however, acknowledged that the democratic process remained a work in progress, identifying institutional capacity, electoral confidence, public accountability and political intolerance as challenges requiring attention.

According to him, democracy depends not only on strong institutions but also on the space it provides for disagreement, representation, negotiation and peaceful political competition.

“Democracy cannot thrive on institutions alone. It also depends on the quality of public discourse,” he said.

The Speaker called for access to reliable information to enable citizens to make informed decisions, urging political actors to focus debates on ideas, policies and evidence rather than insults, intimidation and deliberate falsehoods.

He also called for the protection of press freedom, saying journalists must be allowed to scrutinise those in authority while maintaining professional standards. Public institutions, he added, must embrace transparency and accountability.

Abbas described parliament as the constitutional platform for representation, deliberation and accountability, saying its legitimacy depended not only on the laws it enacted but also on public confidence in its processes.

He said the House of Representatives, with 360 members representing constituencies across the federation, had a responsibility to accommodate the diverse interests and aspirations of Nigerians.

He listed national security, economic growth, education, healthcare, social inclusion, institutional strengthening and an open parliament among the legislature’s priorities.

The Speaker said legislative performance should ultimately be measured by its impact on citizens’ welfare, security and opportunities.

He also disclosed that the National Assembly had advanced constitutional reforms aimed at strengthening the country’s security architecture, including a proposal for the establishment of state police.

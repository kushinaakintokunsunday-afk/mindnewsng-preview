Stay at polling units to protect votes, Obi urges voters

By Punch Punch 4d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, Peter Obi, has urged Nigerians to remain at polling units after voting to ensure their votes are

Obi said his supporters and other Nigerians must take responsibility for protecting their votes by staying at polling units throughout the voting, counting and transmission processes.

He spoke on Friday at a town hall engagement organised by the NDC at the Main Bowl of the Archbishop C.J. Patterson Building, All Saints Cathedral, Onitsha, Anambra State, as part of his engagements ahead of the 2027 general elections.

Addressing supporters, the former Anambra State governor urged voters to make the election day a priority and prepare to spend the day at their polling units.

“Go out there on that day, sacrifice one day. Nigeria is asking for one thing. Sacrifice one day for Nigeria. That day of voting,” he said.

He advised voters to go to their polling units with food and other necessities, cast their ballots, and remain there until the votes were counted and transmitted.

“Carry your food. Carry everything. Go to a police station. Vote. Stay there. They count the votes. Transmit it,” Obi said.

He further urged voters not to leave their polling units after voting, describing them as agents responsible for protecting the electoral process.

“You are our agent. Don’t look at any party agent. You are the party agent. Everybody is a party agent,” he said.

Obi, who is seeking the presidency alongside his running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso, said the NDC was prepared for the election and would resist attempts to manipulate the outcome.

“We are prepared. Nobody will rig this election. Nobody will rig this election. We are prepared for it,” he declared.

The former governor also outlined his electricity target for the country if elected, promising that his administration would increase power generation, transmission and distribution to at least 10,000 megawatts.

“Today Nigeria only generates, transmits and distributes about 4,000 megawatts of electricity. Kwankwaso and I assure you that within our tenure, Nigeria will generate, transmit and distribute at least 10,000 megawatts,” he said.

He cautioned against making promises that could not be fulfilled, adding, “Take note of everything I said here; we don’t want to make fake promises.”

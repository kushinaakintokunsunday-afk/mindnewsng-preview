Ogun poly to graduate 4,817 students at 50th anniversary

By Punch Punch 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Ogun State Polytechnic of Health and Allied Sciences, Ilese-Ijebu, is set to graduate 4,817 students on Thursday at its 4th Combined Convocation Ceremony as the

The polytechnic has also received N339.5m from the Nigerian Education Loan Fund for about 1,300 students across three academic sessions: 2023/2024, 2024/2025 and 2025/2026.

The development comes as the institution commended Governor Dapo Abiodun for increasing its monthly subvention from N5m to N30m.

The Rector of the polytechnic, Dr Rasaki Yinka Akinbo, disclosed this on Monday at a briefing ahead of the institution’s 4th combined convocation and 50th anniversary celebrations.

Akinbo used the occasion to highlight the institution’s major academic, infrastructural and administrative achievements while appealing to the state government for increased funding and inclusion in the Tertiary Education Trust Fund intervention programme.

The rector said the graduating students comprised 2,191 from the 2024/2025 academic session and 2,626 from the 2025/2026 session, with a combined total of 291 students graduating with distinctions.

He explained that 191 students from the 2024/2025 session earned distinctions, while another 100 achieved the same academic feat in the 2025/2026 session.

Giving a breakdown of the graduating students, Akinbo said, “The 2024/2025 session produced 377 Higher National Diploma graduates, 56 of whom earned distinctions; 606 National Diploma graduates, with 25 distinctions; 1,109 Diploma graduates, including 35 distinctions; and 99 certificate graduates, of whom three earned distinctions.

“For the 2025/2026 session, 395 students graduated with HND qualifications, including 33 with distinctions, while 1,146 obtained National Diplomas, with 26 earning distinctions.”

He added that 980 students graduated from the Diploma programme, including 39 with distinctions, while 105 completed certificate programmes, with two earning distinctions.

The rector identified Saliu Fatimah Abisola of the Health Information Management Department as the overall best graduating student of the combined convocation, having recorded a cumulative grade point average of 3.88 in the 2024/2025 academic session.

He said Monsurat Onifade, also of the Health Information Management Department, emerged as the best graduating student for the 2025/2026 academic session with a CGPA of 3.85.

Akinbo also disclosed that the institution had recorded a significant increase in students’ participation in the Federal Government’s student loan scheme, NELFUND, with beneficiaries rising from 40 in the 2023/2024 academic session to more than 1,000 in subsequent sessions.

He explained that “Initially, our students did not want to embrace NELFUND, but after a lot of enlightenment, they have embraced it because it remains a very wonderful initiative that the Federal Government has introduced to ensure that no one is deprived of tertiary education because of lack of funding.

“In the 2023/2024 academic session, we had 40 applications processed, and that amounted to N5,413,000. Then, for 2024/2025, we had another 109 beneficiaries, and that amounted to N28,141,000.

“In 2025/2026, about 1,149 students benefited, and that amounted to N211,122,000. And then we also had another one for 2025/2026, which amounted to N94,824,000. The total amount received from NELFUND so far is N339,500,000.”

Reflecting on the institution’s five-decade journey, Akinbo recalled that the polytechnic was established on September 28, 1976, as Ogun State School of Health Technology, initially operating from a temporary site at Itamogiri under its pioneer principal, Dr V.B. Odunlami.

The rector disclosed that the management had secured resource accreditation for 15 new programmes and accreditation for five existing programmes, describing the development as part of efforts to strengthen the institution’s academic standing and meet the standards of the National Board for Technical Education.

He also said the institution had improved its entrance and internal examination processes, while its admission procedures had been upgraded to align with Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board requirements.

Despite the progress recorded, Akinbo identified inadequate funding, the need for additional facilities and infrastructural expansion as some of the major challenges confronting the institution.

He appealed to the Ogun State Government to increase its financial support, particularly for programme accreditation, noting that the process required substantial resources.

Among the institution’s pressing needs, he listed the construction of a 1,000-seat ICT centre, a 2,000-capacity auditorium, a teaching hospital and entrance and adjoining roads within the campus.

He also appealed to the state government to facilitate the institution’s inclusion as a beneficiary of TETFund interventions.

Akinbo, however, commended Abiodun for increasing the polytechnic’s monthly subvention from N5m to N30m, saying the gesture would help offset some of the backlog of allowances that forced lecturers at the institution to embark on a recent strike.

INEC warns youths against electoral violence, manipulation

By Punch Punch 5d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Independent National Electoral Commission has urged young Nigerians to reject electoral violence, political manipulation, hate speech and misinformation ahe

The commission made the call during its South-South zonal engagement with youth organisations and other critical stakeholders, following an earlier engagement in the South-West.

According to a post on INEC’s X handle on Thursday, the engagement focused on strategies to promote peaceful participation, electoral integrity and credible elections, while encouraging young people to take greater responsibility for protecting Nigeria’s democratic process.

In his welcome address, the Resident Electoral Commissioner (REC) in Akwa Ibom State, Prof. Anugbum Onuacha, called on youths and other stakeholders to take ownership of the electoral process by actively participating in election planning and implementation.

He stressed that professionalism, transparency and stakeholder inclusion were essential to strengthening public confidence in elections.

Onuacha reaffirmed the commission’s neutrality, declaring that, “INEC has no political party and no candidates.”

He further noted that the commission remained committed to improving its electoral processes, stressing that, “No two elections are exactly the same.”

According to him, lessons learnt from previous electoral exercises would continue to guide the commission in improving subsequent elections.

In his goodwill message, the Akwa Ibom State Director of the National Orientation Agency (NOA), Mr. Mkpoutom U. Noa, commended INEC for organising the engagement, describing it as an important platform for addressing youth involvement in electoral violence.

He urged young Nigerians to understand the consequences of electoral offences and resist attempts by politicians to use them as instruments of violence.

“Youths must understand the consequences of electoral offences and avoid allowing themselves to be used as instruments of political violence,” he said.

Noa also expressed confidence in INEC’s commitment to improving the conduct of the 2027 elections, calling on young people to support efforts aimed at safeguarding the nation’s democratic process.

Similarly, the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC), through its Akwa Ibom State Coordinator, conveyed a goodwill message from its Director-General, Brigadier-General U. O. Ahmed, who commended the collaboration between INEC and the NYSC.

The message urged young Nigerians to embrace peace, demonstrate political maturity and take collective responsibility for protecting democracy.

It cautioned youths against allowing political differences, personal interests or financial inducements to turn them into agents of intimidation and electoral violence.

“Young Nigerians must not allow political differences, personal interests or financial inducements to turn them into agents of intimidation and electoral violence,” the message stated.

The engagement forms part of INEC’s efforts to strengthen youth participation, promote peaceful elections and encourage responsible civic behaviour ahead of the 2027 General Elections.

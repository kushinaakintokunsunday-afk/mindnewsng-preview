Wabara rejects A’Court ruling on PDP leadership crisis, warns against making Tinubu sole presidential candidate

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 17 Nov 2026 3 min read

Ex- Senate President and Chairman of the Board of Trustees (BoT) of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP), Senator Adolphus Wabara has rejected the Appeal Court’s ruling in the PDP leadership crisis, describing it as travesty of justice.

This is even as Wabara has warned against using the courts to make Tinubu a sole presidential candidate in 2027, saying that will not in its own save the APC.

Wabara accused the All Progressives Congress (APC) of systematic weakening of opposition parties using public institutions and warned the ruling party to accept responsibility should Nigeria’s democracy suffer any setback.

The Court of Appeal, in a unanimous decision by a three-member panel, had declined to upturn the July 17, judgment of the Federal High Court in Abuja, which dismissed the suit filed by the Wabara-led BoT faction.

The appellate court held that the trial judge, Justice Salim Ibrahim, was right to have dismissed the suit seeking to validate actions taken on behalf of the PDP by the Tanimu Turaki, SAN-led faction.

Those actions included a national convention organised by the faction, which subsequently announced former President Goodluck Jonathan as its consensus presidential candidate for the 2027 election.

In the lead judgment delivered virtually by Justice R. O. Ayoola, the appellate court held that the Wabara-led group lacked the locus standi to institute the action on behalf of the PDP.

It consequently struck out the party’s name as the eighth appellant and held that the appeal had become academic and amounted to an abuse of judicial process.

However, expressing strong reservations over the judgment, Wabara accused the APC of benefiting from the protracted crises in opposition parties and warned that attempts to weaken political alternatives ahead of the 2027 general elections could undermine Nigeria’s democratic foundations.

He described the appellate court’s refusal to compel the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to recognise his faction’s position as a travesty of justice, insisting that the circumstances surrounding the emergence of the Alhaji Abdulrahman Muhammad-led faction raised serious questions about democracy in Nigeria.

He warned that accepting the Muhammad-led faction’s position without adequately addressing the issues surrounding its emergence amounted to “standing truth in the head”.

Wabara expressed surprise at what he described as the Appeal Court’s questionable legal reasoning, wondering how it arrived at what he called an inverted interpretation of the issues before it.

He cautioned the judiciary against becoming entangled in political disputes in ways that could further erode public confidence in the institution.

“The growing legal battles within opposition parties, particularly the PDP, the African Democratic Congress (ADC), and the Nigeria Democratic Congress, (NDC), have raised troubling questions about the ability of opposition parties to participate effectively in the 2027 elections”.

Wabara alleged that the APC was benefiting from the weakening of opposition parties and urged the judiciary to protect its independence and credibility by ensuring that its decisions do not create the impression of favouring any political interest.

“The opposition should be allowed to breathe. Nigeria cannot afford a situation where formidable opposition parties are weakened to the point that nobody can effectively challenge the ruling party in 2027.

“Tinubu should not be made a sole presidential candidate in 2027. That would be anti-democratic and a dangerous precedent capable of bringing avoidable embarrassment to our country.

“Using the courts to make Tinubu the sole presidential candidate in 2027 will not save the APC,” he declared.

Wabara recalled that the APC itself benefited from a competitive political environment before taking power in 2015, and challenged the ruling party to demonstrate its confidence in its performance by allowing opposition parties to operate freely.

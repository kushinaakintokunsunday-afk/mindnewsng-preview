UNN to introduce random drug tests after deaths of four students

By Punch Punch 5d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka, said it will introduce random drug testing for students, including those living off campus, following the deaths of four stud

According to a statement posted on the school’s Facebook page on Wednesday, the Vice-Chancellor, Prof Simon Ortuanya, disclosed this on Tuesday when he received a condolence delegation of traditional rulers led by the Chairman of the Enugu State Traditional Rulers Council, HRM Igwe Samuel Ikechukwu Asadu.

The university said the proposed measure would be implemented in partnership with the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency, while its recently established Directorate of Community Relations would coordinate engagement with landlords and other community stakeholders.

Ortuanya said the university was also strengthening its existing safety measures and regulatory framework to protect students, regardless of where they lived.

He explained that the tests would be conducted randomly, without following a predictable pattern.

“Students of a particular department or faculty could be randomly selected for the test,” he said.

The Vice-Chancellor said the university recognised the challenges of monitoring students living outside its campuses but expressed optimism that drug testing and closer collaboration with landlords would help address misconduct among students.

He urged students to learn from the tragedy, recalling that the university had prohibited formal sign-out celebrations and similar activities on its campuses in Nsukka, Enugu and Ituku-Ozalla since 2025.

“It is our collective responsibility to protect the lives and welfare of our students and preserve the sanity, sanctity and integrity of the University as a centre of learning,” he said.

Ortuanya also said the university was cooperating with relevant government and security agencies investigating the house party incident and was awaiting the outcome of their investigations.

He expressed condolences to the families of the deceased students and assured them of the university’s continued concern and support.

The traditional rulers, who visited the university to commiserate with its management and community, described the deaths as a major loss to the institution, the bereaved families and society.

Asadu said the council considered it important to identify with the university in its moment of grief and offer support.

“On behalf of the Traditional Rulers Council in Enugu State, we express our deep sympathy to the University management, the bereaved families and the entire University community over this painful loss.

“The loss of young lives, particularly at a time when their parents had invested so much in their upbringing and education, is heartbreaking,” he said.

He commended the Vice-Chancellor for his response to the incident and the steps taken to support affected students.

The traditional rulers also pledged to work with the university and other stakeholders to prevent a recurrence.

The proposed drug-testing policy follows an incident on November 11, when four students died and six others were hospitalised after a gathering at an off-campus lodge in the Hilltop area of Nsukka.

The party, held at Praise Lord Lodge, was reportedly organised to celebrate a friend who had completed his final-year examinations. The university said 10 students were involved.

The Vice-Chancellor had earlier said preliminary findings suggested possible alcohol and drug abuse but stressed that the university had not reached a conclusive determination on the cause of death.

He said the institution was working with security agencies to establish what happened.

The Enugu State Police Command also opened an investigation into the deaths. Its spokesperson, SP Daniel Ndukwe, said the four victims, comprising two males and two females, reportedly took ill after consuming alcoholic beverages at the gathering.

Six others were taken to hospital, while the four bodies were deposited in a mortuary for autopsy.

The police said the investigation was intended to establish the circumstances surrounding the deaths.

The incident came despite the university’s existing restrictions on sign-out celebrations.

Ortuanya said UNN had prohibited formal sign-out activities and similar celebrations on its campuses in Nsukka, Enugu and Ituku-Ozalla since 2025.

The latest incident, however, occurred at a private facility outside the university campus.

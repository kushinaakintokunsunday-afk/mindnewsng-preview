Independent judiciary checkmates government, excesses – Nwifuru

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 2d ago 3 min read

From Uchenna Inya, Abakaliki

Ebonyi State Governor Francis Nwifuru has stressed the importance of an independent judiciary in sustaining constitutional democracy, saying it checkmates the government and its excesses.

He also advocated the adoption of technology-driven processes in the administration of justice.

He noted that the judiciary serves as a critical institutional safeguard against possible excesses of government.

Nwifuru stated this at the weekend at the State High Court, Abakaliki, during the Special Court Session marking the commencement of the 2026/2027 Legal Year in Ebonyi State.

He said an efficient justice system must be capable of resolving disputes promptly and minimising unnecessary expenditure of resources.

“A justice system that cannot resolve disputes timely wastes resources. Technology offers us the opportunity to resolve some of these things. Digital filings and digital transcriptions help.

“The significance goes beyond governance. It helps to transact with various registries without recourse to location.

“An independent judiciary checkmates the government as well as its excesses. As a Governor, I recognise that the independence of the judiciary must be respected and allowed to perform its constitutional and professional mandate,” he stated.

He explained that the deployment of digital technology in judicial administration would reduce the challenges associated with physical processes, improve efficiency and enable legal practitioners and litigants to interact with relevant court registries more conveniently.

The Governor noted that his People’s Charter of Needs Agenda was committed to the ongoing reforms in the justice sector, pointing to several initiatives introduced to strengthen the administration of justice and improve the delivery of judicial services in the state.

According to him, the ongoing adoption of electronic filing, the establishment of the Moot Court, the creation of the Office of the Public Defender and the adoption of the Sexual Assault Bill are evidence of his government’s commitment to justice sector reforms.

He maintained that the reforms were designed to strengthen institutional capacity, promote access to justice and ensure that the justice system remained responsive to the needs and expectations of the people.

Earlier, the Chief Judge of Ebonyi State, Hon. Justice Elvis Ngene, informed the Governor that the state judiciary had recorded progress in several areas of judicial administration under his leadership.

Justice Ngene identified the modernisation of the machinery of justice through the deployment of technology, the protection of judicial integrity, the improvement of access to justice and the appointment of deserving judicial officers as some of the areas receiving attention.

Justice Ngene stated that official visits to correctional centres, regular human capital development, professional capacity building, infrastructural development and the improvement of court facilities were significant components of the judiciary’s activities within the last one year.

The Chief Judge emphasised that judicial integrity remained fundamental to the credibility and effectiveness of the justice system, stressing the need for all judicial officers and personnel to uphold the highest ethical standards.

He assured that the judiciary would continue to invest in professional development and institutional reforms despite the financial constraints confronting the institution.

“As part of our efforts to promote this, we shall, notwithstanding the leanness of our resources, continue to encourage continuous legal education and professional learning, strengthen administrative competence, promote responsible use of technology and insist upon the highest ethical standards from every person entrusted with judicial responsibilities,” he said.

He said the judiciary would continue to recognise the dedication of its workforce and prioritise measures that promote professionalism, institutional discipline and effective service delivery.

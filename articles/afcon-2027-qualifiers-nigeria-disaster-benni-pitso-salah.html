AFCON 2027 Qualifiers - Nigeria Face Disaster, Benni v Pitso, Salah Fresh Start - Check Below For Full Details

By Mindnewsng ESPN 12 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: ESPN

AFCON 2027 qualifying resumes with decisive double-headers. Nigeria risk disaster after a 3-0 home loss to Guinea-Bissau; Egypt, Ghana, Senegal, Kenya and South

AFCON 2027 qualifying resumes this week with potentially decisive double-headers for several continental giants - including Nigeria and South Africa. For some, November offers a chance to move within touching distance of qualification; for others, another poor international break could be disastrous.

Can Nigeria avoid another catastrophe?

Nigeria's campaign is already in jeopardy after a wretched opening pair of fixtures left them three points off Group L leaders Guinea-Bissau. A humiliating 3-0 home defeat by the West Africans has given Eric Chelle's side little margin for error - especially with Tanzania automatically qualifying as co-hosts, leaving just one automatic qualifying berth in the group.

The Super Eagles now face the Taifa Stars twice - in Uyo and Dar es Salaam - knowing that further dropped points could leave them with too big a gulf to close. They must also navigate both fixtures without Victor Osimhen (again), while midfield duo Frank Onyeka and Raphael Onyedika are both absent. The hope is that Tolu Arokodare, in magnificent goalscoring form for Ajax Amsterdam, can finally make up for lost time and fire Nigeria back into contention.

Can Egypt restore harmony with Salah?

Hossam Hassan was unceremoniously dismissed on November 7, exactly four months after Egypt's unforgettable World Cup quarter-final defeat by Argentina. His increasingly contentious relationship with Mohamed Salah attracted heavy scrutiny, particularly after the forward was substituted against Angola and departed camp before the trip to South Sudan. New boss Ali Maher must establish a relationship with Salah, get the most out of a talented squad and restore stability - starting with a double-header against Malawi, where domestic hotshot Mahmoud Mamdouh earns a maiden call-up and Trezeguet returns.

Can returning Kudus ignite Ghana's campaign?

Like Nigeria, Ghana are in a tough spot after defeats by Cote d'Ivoire and The Gambia left them pointless after two games. Carlos Queiroz has departed, with women's coach Kim Bjorkegren holding the reins in an interim capacity for the double-header against Somalia. The comeback of Mohammed Kudus, who missed the World Cup through injury but has shown glimpses of form at Tottenham, should lift a squad that cannot afford another AFCON absence.

Is Vieira really the author of Senegal's revival?

Patrick Vieira's appointment replaced Pape Thiaw, but his latest squad suggests the transition could be more substantial than first thought. Edouard Mendy and Ismail Jakobs have retired internationally, while Sadio Mane and Idrissa Gana Gueye are absent for the fixtures against Sudan - Mane staying home as he awaits the birth of a child. Their absences open the door to an uncapped quartet, with Saudi-based Dion Lopy the chief beneficiary of Gana's absence.

Benni vs Pitso

Two of South Africa's most celebrated football personalities collide when Benni McCarthy's Kenya face Pitso Mosimane's South Africa in a compelling Group D double-header. Kenya are guaranteed to be at AFCON 2027 as hosts but must show readiness after a troubled international break in which McCarthy revealed players allegedly refused to train over unpaid allowances. Mosimane's side, evolving after the World Cup, began qualifying with two wins.

Other storylines to watch

  • Mali vs Rwanda: both sides took maximum points from their openers; Lassine Sinayoko has nine goals in 10 Ligue 1 games for Paris FC.
  • Cape Verde: consecutive defeats after World Cup heroics; coach Bubista left for RS Berkane; now face Liberia in must-win fixtures.
  • Amad Diallo: among 10 nominees for African Footballer of the Year; Ivory Coast's new boss Herve Renard could unlock the Manchester United winger against The Gambia.

CAF has also reversed its four-year AFCON plan and restored the two-year cycle - a reminder of how quickly the continental landscape keeps shifting.

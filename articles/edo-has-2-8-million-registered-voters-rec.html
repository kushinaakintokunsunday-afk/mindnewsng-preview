Edo has 2.8 million registered voters — REC

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Edo State Resident Electoral Commissioner, Obo Effanga, on Friday, said the state currently had 2.8 million registered voters following the latest Continuou

He said the final figure would be released after the Independent National Electoral Commission completed its recent data clean-up exercise.

INEC had earlier announced that 131,165 new voters were registered during the Continuous Voter Registration exercise, which ran from August 2025 to July 2026.

Giving an update on the collection of Permanent Voter Cards, Effanga said 23,401 PVCs had been collected across the state since distribution began on October 9.

He said the figure comprised 6,319 old PVCs, 5,128 newly produced cards and 11,954 cards relating to voter transfers.

“The state currently has 2.8 million registered voters after the last registration exercise. However, the final figure would be released after the recent clean-up exercise by the national body,” Effanga said.

The REC disclosed that PVCs for voters who registered during the first and second phases of the CVR exercise were available for collection at INEC offices across the state’s 18 local government areas.

He recalled that the first phase of the registration exercise ran from August to December 2025, while the second phase took place between January and April 2026.

Effanga said the commission was working towards ensuring a smooth electoral process.

Speaking on the mock accreditation exercise scheduled for November 28, the REC said it would be conducted in 384 polling units across the state.

He explained that Edo had 192 registration areas, also known as wards, with two polling units selected from each for the exercise.

“The exercise would provide an opportunity to test the commission’s electoral technology and assess the readiness of its personnel ahead of the elections.

“The state office had submitted the list of proposed polling units to the commission’s headquarters for approval,” he added.

He said the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System would be deployed during the exercise to demonstrate how voters were accredited, how results were captured and how they were transmitted to the INEC Result Viewing Portal.

The REC explained that INEC did not have enough permanent staff to conduct elections across the state’s 4,519 polling units, making the recruitment of ad hoc personnel necessary.

He said recruitment would commence after the closure of the application portal, after which the commission’s headquarters would process applicants’ details and forward their names to the respective states based on their preferred locations of service.

Successful applicants would subsequently be invited for screening and training before deployment, he added.

Effanga identified supervisory presiding officers and collation officers as other categories of ad hoc personnel.

He explained that supervisory presiding officers were usually selected from among senior civil servants, particularly federal civil servants who met the required grade-level qualifications.

Responding to concerns about the safety of ad hoc staff, particularly in view of previous incidents of electoral violence, the REC said INEC was working with security agencies to ensure adequate protection for everyone involved in the electoral process.

He said the security arrangements would cover election officials, voters, journalists and other stakeholders.

According to him, the commission had been holding regular meetings of the Inter-Agency Consultative Committee on Election Security, with the latest meeting held on Tuesday.

He added that he would participate in a national summit on election security organised by the Office of the National Security Adviser in collaboration with INEC.

However, Effanga said maintaining electoral security required the cooperation of all stakeholders, particularly politicians, whom he accused of sometimes encouraging violence through their actions and campaign rhetoric.

He said politicians should understand that elections were civil exercises and should not threaten or intimidate voters into supporting particular candidates.

The REC condemned statements suggesting that people who refused to vote for certain candidates should not visit particular communities, describing such rhetoric as unacceptable.

On whether election personnel were covered by insurance in the event of injuries or other incidents during elections, Effanga confirmed that the commission had insurance arrangements for those engaged in the electoral process.

He said the coverage included life insurance, adding that INEC had previously taken responsibility for the medical treatment and other necessary support for corps members injured while performing electoral duties.

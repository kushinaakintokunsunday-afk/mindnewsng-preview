FCT upgrades 20 PHCs, expands free maternal healthcare to pregnant women

By Punch Punch 23h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Capital Territory Administration has upgraded 20 primary healthcare centres across the six area councils and expanded free maternal healthcare servi

The Mandate Secretary of the FCT Health Services and Environment Secretariat, Dr Dolapo Fasawe, disclosed this on Monday during a community engagement at the Owner Occupier Primary Healthcare Centre, Kubwa, Abuja.

Fasawe said the upgraded facilities had been equipped with electricity, water, staff accommodation, medical equipment and essential medicines to enable round-the-clock services.

She said the centres provided maternal and child healthcare, immunisation, nutrition services, breastfeeding education, malaria testing and treatment, as well as emergency referrals.

According to her, the administration was also providing free maternal healthcare and one-year health insurance coverage to eligible residents, with at least 1,000 women already enrolled in the programme.

“We are addressing the three delays that contribute to maternal deaths: the delay in deciding to seek care, the delay in reaching a health facility and the delay in receiving appropriate treatment,” she said.

To address transportation challenges, Fasawe said the FCTA had introduced emergency transportation arrangements involving commercial drivers and transport workers to ensure that pregnant women and other patients could reach health facilities when they needed care.

She said the arrangement complemented the FCT Emergency Medical Services and Ambulance System, which was linked to the national emergency response system.

The mandate secretary also disclosed that women who delivered in recognised FCT health facilities received baby-care kits, while delivery kits containing essential medical supplies were provided to reduce the financial burden on expectant mothers and encourage institutional childbirth.

She attributed the decline in malaria incidence in the territory from 18.7 per cent in 2023 to 7.4 per cent to free testing, treatment, immunisation and other interventions.

Fasawe further said health insurance enrolment in the FCT had “increased by nearly 70 per cent”, adding that the administration was strengthening healthcare workers’ welfare and facility-level funding through the Basic Health Care Provision Fund.

She urged community leaders and residents to monitor health facilities and report any that demanded payment for services designated as free.

A World Bank Senior Health Specialist and Task Team Leader of the Immunisation Plus and Malaria Progress by Accelerating Coverage and Transforming Services project, Dr Onoriode Ezire, said the initiative was being integrated into government programmes to ensure its sustainability beyond the project’s lifespan.

Ezire said the FCT had demonstrated ownership of the interventions by committing government resources to emergency services and other healthcare programmes, while the World Bank continued to provide technical support.

Also speaking, the Senior Special Assistant to the President on Community Engagement (North-Central), Dr Abiodun Essiet, said more than 16 million Nigerians had been enrolled in health coverage since 2023, while over N235bn had been invested in primary healthcare upgrades.

She said the Basic Health Care Provision Fund was supporting the upgrade of at least one primary healthcare centre in each ward across the 774 local government areas.

Essiet added that about 48,000 women had benefited from free maternal and child healthcare packages, while 14 million girls had received human papillomavirus vaccinations.

She said the Federal Government was targeting 90 per cent primary healthcare coverage by 2027, alongside the expansion of free emergency childbirth services.

Meanwhile, a deaf beneficiary of the free Caesarean section intervention commended the programme but called for sign-language interpreters in health facilities to help persons with hearing impairments access antenatal information and other healthcare services.

The FCT APC Woman Leader, Farida Suleiman, also commended the facility’s upgrade and called for similar interventions across the six area councils and 62 wards.

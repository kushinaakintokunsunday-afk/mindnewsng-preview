SERAP sues NDPC, INEC over AI manipulation of voters’ register

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project has sued the Nigeria Data Protection Commission and the Independent National Electoral Commission over alle

SERAP said it filed the suit at the Federal High Court in Abuja last week, seeking an order compelling the data protection commission to independently investigate allegations of irregularities in the voters’ register.

The organisation disclosed this in a statement issued on Sunday by its Deputy Director, Kolawole Oluwadare.

In the suit, marked FHC/ABJ/CS/2863/2026, SERAP is asking the court “an order of mandamus to direct and compel the NDPC to obtain and examine relevant records, documents and electronic data held by INEC to establish the nature and extent of the reported irregularities and determine whether the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 has been violated.”

It is also seeking “an order of mandamus to direct and compel the NDPC to exercise its enforcement powers under the Act, including issuing corrective orders, imposing appropriate fines and sanctions, and facilitating remedies or compensation for voters affected by any established violations.”

The organisation said the alleged irregularities raised concerns about the security of Nigerians’ personal information and the reliability of the voters’ register ahead of the 2027 general elections.

It added that the accuracy of the register was essential to protecting voters’ personal data and their right to participate in public affairs.

“Nigerians should not have to worry that their personal information could be altered, manipulated or accessed without proper safeguards,” it said.

SERAP argued that INEC’s acknowledgement of questionable records and suspected AI-generated photographs made an independent investigation necessary.

“The public needs to know what happened, whether the law was breached and what measures are being taken to prevent a recurrence,” the organisation said.

It also urged the NDPC to conduct an independent forensic examination of INEC’s affected database and systems to establish whether personal data were unlawfully accessed or altered.

“The NDPC should exercise its statutory powers independently and without delay. Any investigation must establish the facts, protect affected voters and ensure that those responsible for any unlawful conduct are held accountable,” it said.

The suit, filed on behalf of SERAP by its lawyers, Oluwakemi Agunbiade, Kehinde Oyewumi, Andrew Nwankwo and Valentina Adegoke, also asks the court to compel the NDPC to determine whether the incidents constitute a personal data breach under Section 65 of the Nigeria Data Protection Act.

The organisation said the investigation should establish the number and geographical distribution of affected records, when and how they were created or altered, and the persons or institutions responsible.

It argued that the alleged irregularities could involve the unlawful alteration or processing of personal data, misuse of authorised access credentials and failures to protect information held by INEC.

SERAP further said any compromise of the voter database could undermine confidence in the electoral process and expose eligible voters to wrongful exclusion.

The suit also cited Section 37 of the Nigerian Constitution, which guarantees the right to privacy, as well as provisions of the African Charter on Human and Peoples’ Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights on political participation.

The legal action follows INEC’s acknowledgement of suspected AI-generated photographs and questionable registration records in its preliminary Register of Voters.

In a statement issued on October 27, the commission said some questionable records were identified and flagged for further examination during the nationwide physical display of the register from October 9 to 15.

INEC said it had commenced a review and was taking corrective action, adding that the online publication did not reflect all its ongoing data-cleanup activities.

The commission also said it was examining the circumstances surrounding the suspicious photographs, including possible technical or procedural lapses.

On November 1, INEC’s Director of Information and Communication Technology, Lawrence Bayode, disclosed that its Automated Biometric Identification System had flagged 268 records for review over suspected AI-generated photographs and other irregularities.

The commission maintained that the preliminary register was not the final certified version and said it would complete its verification, adjudication and data-cleanup processes before certifying the final Register of Voters on December 15, 2026.

SERAP said an internal review by INEC could not replace an independent investigation by the data protection commission.

No date has been fixed for the hearing of the suit.

Enugu Assembly caps rent increase at 2%, bans hikes for 4 years

From Jude Chinedu, Enugu

The Enugu State House of Assembly has passed a bill seeking to regulate rent increases and check exploitative practices in the state’s housing sector.

The Houses restricted landlords to a maximum rent increment of two per cent in not less than four years, subject to specified exceptions.

The proposed legislation is expected to provide significant relief to tenants, particularly residents of Enugu metropolis, by addressing arbitrary rent increases, excessive agency charges and other financial demands imposed on prospective tenants when securing accommodation.

The bill, sponsored by Member representing Nkanu East Constituency, Hon. Okey Mbah, followed growing public outcry over rising rents and exploitative practices by some landlords and estate agents.

Governor Peter Mbah had, during a recent interactive television programme, Enugu Kwenu, hosted by Afia TV, promised to engage the State Assembly to expedite action on pending legislation aimed at addressing the rent crisis in the state.

The bill was subsequently given renewed attention, culminating in its passage yesterday following the adoption of a report presented by a special committee constituted by the House to review the proposed amendments to the state’s Landlord and Tenant Law. Its passage followed the successful completion of the third reading after lawmakers considered and adopted the committee’s recommendations.

The committee comprised members of the House Committees on Housing and Investment, Public Petition, Ethics and Privileges, and Judiciary.

The motion for consideration of the report was moved by the member representing Nkanu West Constituency, Hon. Iloabuchi Abugu, and seconded by the member representing Awgu North Constituency, Rt. Hon. Jane Eneh. Presenting the report, the committee chairman, Hon. Okey Mbah, said the recommendations were the outcome of extensive consultations with relevant stakeholders.

He described the proposed amendment as a people’s-oriented intervention designed to address exploitative practices in the housing sector, protect tenants’ interests and reduce the financial burden associated with securing accommodation. He said the legislation would substantially reduce the cost of renting properties, particularly by regulating ancillary charges that have become additional sources of financial pressure on prospective tenants.

“This bill will subsidise over 60 percent of what residents used to pay while trying to secure accommodation,” he said.

Under the proposed law, landlords would be permitted to review rents only after four years, with any increment capped at two per cent of the prevailing rent. However, an exception of not more than 10 per cent is provided where a landlord has undertaken reasonable renovation of their property.

In his remark after the passage, Speaker of the Assembly, Hon. Uche Ugwu, described the legislation as part of the good governance initiatives of the Peter Mbah administration. He said the proposed law would give tenants across Enugu State a new lease of life by providing legal safeguards against arbitrary rent increases and other exploitative practices.

Speaking with journalists after the passage, the sponsor, Okey Mbah, described the legislation as a people’s bill, saying its spirit and intent were directed at addressing the concerns and yearnings of residents. He expressed concern over the activities of some estate agents who, according to him, impose exorbitant charges on prospective tenants beyond the actual rent payable.

Mbah cited instances where accommodation advertised at N300,000 annually attracts a total payment of N950,000, while a N500,000 apartment could require as much as N1.4 million in overall charges. He said the legislation had addressed such additional financial demands by introducing specific limits on agency, legal and caution fees.

Under the proposed provisions, agency fees would be restricted to five per cent of the annual rent, while legal fees would be capped at 10 percent. Caution fees would also be limited to 10 percent of the annual rent and made refundable.

The lawmaker further explained that the bill provides for a bond account domiciled under the custody of the State Attorney-General to ensure that caution fees are safeguarded and refunded to tenants at the end of their tenancy, subject to an assessment of the property’s fittings and fixtures.

He said the arrangement would prevent the practice whereby tenants lose their caution deposits after vacating rented apartments, regardless of the condition in which they leave the properties.

The bill, titled A Bill for a Law to Amend the Landlord and Tenant Second Amendment Bill, Cap. 101, Revised Laws of Enugu State 2024, House Bill No. 3, 2025, passed its second reading on March 11, 2026, and subsequently underwent a public hearing on March 26, 2026.

With its passage by the House, the bill now awaits the assent of Governor Peter Mbah before it can become law and take effect in the state.

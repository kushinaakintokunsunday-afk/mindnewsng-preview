Binnari: Can Taraba community shed its Special Security Concern tag?

As grief grips Binnari after a deadly massacre, residents of Karim-Lamido seek lasting peace amid persistent insecurity and questions about the effectiveness of the area’s Special Security Concern designation, TERVER DOOMGA writes

For the people of Binnari, a community in Karim-Lamido Local Government Area of Taraba State, October 23, 2026, will remain a date associated with grief, bloodshed and the collapse of the fragile sense of security they had struggled to maintain.

It was a Friday, a market day when residents and traders from neighbouring settlements converged on the community to buy and sell farm produce, food and other necessities. The market, ordinarily a centre of commercial and social interaction, became a scene of horror when armed men invaded the community, shooting residents, looting shops and setting properties ablaze.

For hours, the attackers operated with little resistance, sending residents fleeing in different directions for safety. Some ran towards nearby bushes, while others headed for the Benue River. In the desperate attempt to escape, several people drowned.

By the time the attackers withdrew, families were searching for missing relatives, traders were counting their losses and survivors were struggling to comprehend the scale of the tragedy.

The Taraba State Emergency Management Agency subsequently reported at least 62 deaths and 52 injuries, while thousands of residents were displaced. The police initially reported a lower casualty figure as recovery operations continued. The Senate, in a motion seeking urgent security intervention, cited approximately 80 people killed.

Although the final death toll remains subject to reconciliation, the figures tell a disturbing story of a community that suffered one of the deadliest attacks in Taraba’s recent history.

The tragedy also renewed questions about the government’s capacity to protect rural communities, particularly those located along difficult terrain and state boundaries where criminal groups can exploit inadequate infrastructure and weak security coverage.

For Karim-Lamido, however, Binnari was not the beginning of the crisis. It was another painful chapter in a prolonged security challenge.

A troubled security corridor

Karim-Lamido occupies a strategic position in Taraba, sharing boundaries with parts of Plateau, Bauchi and Gombe states. Its geographical location, extensive rural settlements, farming communities and difficult terrain have created security and administrative challenges over the years.

The local government has experienced recurring communal clashes, farmer-herder conflicts, banditry, kidnapping and attacks on rural settlements.

An assessment by the European Union Agency for Asylum, drawing on conflict-monitoring data, identified Karim-Lamido as the local government area with the highest number of recorded security incidents in Taraba between January 2024 and August 2025.

The assessment recorded 26 incidents in Karim-Lamido, compared with 24 in Wukari and 23 in Takum.

The figures illustrate the extent to which insecurity has affected the state beyond isolated attacks. They also demonstrate that the problem confronting Karim-Lamido predates the recent massacre.

In May 2025, violence escalated in Munga Dosso and surrounding communities, where armed attacks reportedly claimed several lives and forced residents to flee their homes.

The attacks affected farming activities, destroyed property and increased the number of displaced households.

For rural residents, insecurity is not measured only by the number of people killed. It is also reflected in abandoned farms, deserted settlements, disrupted markets, missing livestock and the inability of families to travel freely between communities.

The economic implications are particularly serious in a local government where agriculture remains a major source of livelihood.

When farmers cannot safely access their fields, food production suffers. When traders cannot attend markets, rural commerce declines. When families flee their homes, their ability to earn an income and educate their children is also affected.

It was against this background of recurring violence that the Taraba State Government introduced a special security intervention in January 2026.

On January 2, 2026, Governor Agbu Kefas signed Executive Order No. 1, declaring Karim-Lamido a “Special Area of Security Concern.”

The measure followed consultations and security assessments arising from repeated communal clashes, bandit-related attacks and violent farmer-herder conflicts in the local government.

According to the government, the declaration was intended to strengthen security coordination, protect lives and property, restore peaceful coexistence and create an enabling environment for economic and social activities.

The designation was a recognition that Karim-Lamido required sustained security attention rather than occasional responses to individual attacks.

However, the Binnari massacre exposed the continuing vulnerability of communities within the designated area.

Following the October 23 attack, Kefas convened an emergency security meeting at the T.Y. Danjuma House in Jalingo and announced a further six-month extension of the designation, effective October 26.

At the meeting, the governor acknowledged the loss of lives and the trauma suffered by affected families.

“We have gathered to mourn those we have lost, support those affected, and agree on the actions required to protect our people,” he said.

He also assured residents that the government was determined to identify and apprehend those responsible for the violence.

“Our long-standing peace must not be disrupted by those who seek to destroy it. We are on track to identify and apprehend the criminals who have vowed to threaten our peace and security,” Kefas stated.

The governor directed relevant authorities to account for missing persons, establish their whereabouts and provide support to affected families.

He also tasked security agencies with investigating the movement of the attackers, their possible motives and any links to other criminal activities.

The renewed designation was accompanied by instructions for a coordinated security response involving security agencies, traditional institutions, community leaders and residents.

Yet, for communities that have experienced repeated attacks, the central question is whether the renewed declaration will translate into a security arrangement capable of preventing another massacre.

The road to security

Perhaps one of the most significant lessons from the Binnari tragedy is the relationship between poor road access and the ability of security agencies to respond to emergencies.

The challenge was highlighted during deliberations in the House of Representatives following the attack.

A motion moved by Mohammed Lauya representing Karim-Lamido, Lau, Ardo-Kola Federal constituency, called for urgent federal security and humanitarian intervention.

The lawmaker argued that the absence of adequate access roads had compounded the vulnerability of communities in Karim-Lamido and made it difficult for security personnel to reach affected settlements quickly.

He also urged the Federal Government to complete the abandoned Wase-Karim-Lamido road project to improve security operations and facilitate the rapid deployment of personnel.

“This type of deadly attack has never taken place in Karim Lamido Local Government; the people of Karim Lamido have suffered a lot. No access road leading to all these areas,” Lauya said.

The lawmakers consequently called for additional security camps, improved surveillance and intelligence sharing among security agencies in Taraba, Plateau and Bauchi states.

The road question has since become a major component of the state government’s response.

Following further assessments of the affected communities, the Kefas administration approved six emergency road projects intended to open up vulnerable settlements and improve access for security agencies and rural residents.

The people have mourned their dead. Families are still nursing their wounds, and many displaced residents are hoping that life will soon return to normal.

Yet, beneath the grief, there remains a desire to reclaim the farms, reopen the markets, rebuild homes and restore the relationships that once made their communities places of belonging.

For them, lasting peace will not be measured by official declarations or the number of security meetings held, but by the freedom to live, work, worship and raise their children without fear.

And that is the hope Binnari is holding on to: that the tragedy of October 23 will become a turning point in the collective determination to secure, rebuild and restore the communities of Karim-Lamido.

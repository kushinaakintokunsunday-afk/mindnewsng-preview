Customs suspends recruits’ training amid FCC legal action over recruitment process

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 3d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: VeryNigerian

The Nigeria Customs Service (NCS) has suspended ongoing training activities at its training colleges to allow for further consultations with the Federal Character Commission (FCC) over concerns surrounding its recruitment exercise.

The suspension was announced in a statement on Friday by Abdullahi Maiwada, the NCS national spokesperson.

According to Maiwada, the decision was taken to create room for further discussions with the FCC “towards the amicable resolution of concerns raised in connection with ongoing recruitment exercise”.

He explained that the development followed engagements between the FCC and a delegation from the Customs Service.

Maiwada added that both institutions had reaffirmed their commitment to resolving the issues through constructive dialogue, mutual understanding and continued institutional collaboration.

The suspension is expected to allow both agencies to continue consultations towards resolving the concerns surrounding the recruitment process.

“In consideration of the concerns raised by the Commission and in line with the series of engagements, the Comptroller-General of Customs (CGC) Bashir Adewale Adeniyi MFR has reaffirmed the Service’s commitment to institutional collaboration and constructive engagement with relevant government institutions in the overall national interest,” the statement reads.

“Furthermore, the CGC acknowledged the critical role of FCC in promoting national unity, fairness and equitable representation in public service appointments, noting the importance of its constitutional mandate in strengthening inclusiveness and ensuring that the principles of federal character are upheld across the Federation.

“The NCS values its longstanding relationship with the FCC and remains confident that the ongoing dialogue will provide an opportunity to address the concerns raised in a mutually beneficial manner, while reinforcing the shared commitment of both institutions to fairness, national cohesion and effective public service delivery.

“The Service, under the leadership of the CGC and his management team, remains committed to upholding the highest standards of institutional accountability and transparency in the discharge of its responsibilities.”

The suspension comes days after the FCC filed criminal charges against NCS and Bashir Adeniyi, the agency’s comptroller-general, over alleged failure to provide information on its 2025 recruitment exercise.

The FCC instituted a four-count charge against NCS and Adeniyi at the federal high court in Abuja.

According to the charge sheet dated November 12, 2026, the FCC alleged that the customs CG “failed to furnish” requested information and records relating to the NSC’s 2025/2026 recruitment exercise.

BACK STORY

In July, NCS released the list of successful candidates in its recruitment exercise.

The service selected 3,852 candidates from 573,680 applications across three entry cadres; superintendent, inspectorate, and customs assistant.

Speaking about the list in August, Adeniyi said he was prepared to defend the selection on its merits.

He said the successful candidates were distributed equally across the 36 states of the federation.

In September, the NCS invited the successful candidates for physical screening and documentation at the Nigeria Customs Command and Staff College, Gwagwalada, Abuja.

Some of the candidates were subsequently invited for training.

However, allegations emerged that some applicants whose names appeared on the list of successful candidates had been screened out and replaced people who did not participate in the recruitment exercise.

In a statement released on October 19, NCS said it had acknowledged the complaints from applicants who participated in the recruitment exercise.

The agency said 397 applicants were screened out over issues relating to substance abuse, age, physical fitness, and documentation.

The NCS added that some candidates were rejected because of “identified irregularities in their selection process during the screening exercise”.

In October, the FCC called for the suspension of the NCS recruitment exercise over alleged violations of federal character principles.

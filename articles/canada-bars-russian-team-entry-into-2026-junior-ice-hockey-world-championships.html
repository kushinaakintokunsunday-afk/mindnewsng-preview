Canada bars Russian team entry into 2026 Junior Ice Hockey World championships

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 16 Nov 2026 3 min read

Russia’s bid to return to international sports has suffered another setback after Canada announced plans to prevent Russian athletes from entering the country for the 2026 Junior Ice Hockey World Championships.

The decision effectively rules out Russia’s Under-20 national team from participating in the tournament, scheduled to take place from December 26, 2026, to January 5, 2027, in Edmonton and Red Deer.

According to BBC Sport on Monday, the International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) confirmed Russia’s exclusion in a statement, citing restrictions communicated by the Canadian government.

“Due to the position and restrictions formally communicated by the Canadian government, the Russian Under-20 national team is unable to participate in the World Junior Championship,” the IIHF said.

The federation added that, after considering alternative arrangements, it had decided to retain Canada as the host nation.

“After carefully assessing the feasibility of alternative arrangements, the IIHF Council has determined that Canada will remain as host and the tournament will proceed from 26 December 2026 to 5 January 2027 in Edmonton and Red Deer,” it said.

Russia’s exclusion has drawn criticism from the Russian Ice Hockey Federation, which described the decision as “unacceptable and unlawful” and vowed to appeal to the International Olympic Committee (IOC).

“The IIHF’s decision was made solely due to political reasons.

“The exclusion of the Russian national team violates fundamental legal principles, Olympism, the principle of non-discrimination, equality among athletes and the integrity of international tournaments,” the Russian federation said in a statement.

Russia had been suspended from IIHF competitions since its invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

However, the Court of Arbitration for Sport overturned the ban last month, ruling that the federation’s safety and security arguments were “not supported by sufficient evidence”.

The court subsequently ordered the IIHF to allow Russia to participate in its competitions.

The ruling was welcomed by Russia but opposed by Ukraine and some sponsors of the Junior World Championships.
The situation became more complicated after six European ice hockey federations threatened to boycott matches against Russia if the country were allowed to participate.

Canada’s decision to deny entry to Russian athletes has now effectively ended hopes of Russia featuring in the tournament.

The development is part of a wider debate over Russia’s possible return to international sport following its invasion of Ukraine.

Norway, Finland and Estonia have also announced plans to block visas for Russian and Belarusian athletes.

Norway is preparing to host the Cross-Country World Cup next month, while Finland is scheduled to stage the World Figure Skating Championships in March.

The International Skating Union (ISU), however, has defended the participation rights of athletes, saying it believes they should not be penalised for the actions of their governments.

“The ISU believes that athletes should not be punished for the actions of their governments in a world where, sadly, many conflicts are ongoing,” the organisation said, adding that it was “seeking clarifications” from Finnish officials.

Meanwhile, several Russian and Belarusian athletes were allowed to compete under their national flags at the Winter Paralympics in March after the International Paralympic Committee lifted some restrictions.

In July, the IOC also provisionally reinstated Russia to its competitions, declaring that a ban introduced in 2023 was “no longer applicable”.

However, individual international sports federations retain the authority to determine their own eligibility requirements.

While World Gymnastics, World Aquatics and World Boxing have lifted restrictions on Russian athletes, World Athletics has maintained its ban.

Last month, Moscow was announced as the host of the 2030 World Weightlifting Championships, marking the first time an Olympic sports federation has awarded Russia a global event since the country invaded Ukraine.

Fani-Kayode denies recall, faults treatment of Nigerians in S’Africa

By Punch Punch 1d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria’s High Commissioner to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, has dismissed reports alleging that he was recalled or reprimanded by the Federal Government over

The envoy, however, raised concerns over unresolved deaths of Nigerians, alleged misconduct by some South African security personnel and recurring anti-Nigerian sentiments, insisting that protecting Nigerians abroad must remain a priority of his diplomatic mission and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Fani-Kayode spoke during a visit to Abuja, where he said his presence was part of routine diplomatic consultations with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and should not be interpreted as a disciplinary measure.

He said he briefed ministry officials on developments in South Africa and received policy guidance on Nigeria’s diplomatic engagements. His consultations included meetings with officials responsible for African affairs and the country’s diplomatic missions across the continent.

“There have been reports on social media suggesting that I have been recalled. Those claims are simply untrue,” he said.

The ambassador maintained that his visit to Nigeria was for official consultations, submission of reports, family engagements and preparations for the relocation of his family to South Africa.

He said the trip should not be mistaken for a recall, stressing that his engagements with the ministry had been cordial and productive. “If I had been recalled, I doubt I would be standing here smiling after a cordial and productive meeting with the minister.”

Fani-Kayode also disclosed that what was initially expected to be a short assignment in South Africa had been extended to allow for the completion of official diplomatic procedures.

Addressing speculation that he had been reprimanded over his recent engagements in the country, the envoy insisted that diplomatic relations between Abuja and Pretoria remained cordial.

“Our engagements with South African authorities have been cordial and constructive. Concerns have been raised on both sides in a professional diplomatic manner, and no reprimand of any kind occurred.”

In a statement issued on Monday, Fani-Kayode called for stronger bilateral cooperation between Nigeria and South Africa, describing relations between the two countries as critical to Africa’s economic integration, political stability and development.

He said that while the Nigerian government remained committed to strengthening diplomatic and economic relations with South Africa, it would not overlook issues affecting the safety, dignity and welfare of Nigerians living in the country.

“The future of Africa is closely tied to the relationship between Nigeria and South Africa. If these two countries fail to work together, it could have significant implications for the continent’s progress,” he said.

He noted that since assuming office, he had consistently advocated dialogue, mutual respect and stronger bilateral ties between both countries.

However, he said the cordial relationship between the two governments should not prevent Nigeria from raising concerns over the treatment of its citizens. “As ambassador, I would be failing in my responsibility if I did not raise concerns about the treatment of Nigerians in South Africa,” he said.

Among the issues he identified were unresolved cases involving the deaths of Nigerians, reports of misconduct by some security personnel and recurring anti-Nigerian rhetoric by certain groups.

Meanwhile, no fewer than 12 Nigerians have been killed in South Africa in 2026 as a result of anti-migrant protests, alleged police brutality and Afrophobia attacks.

The envoy also faulted attempts to portray Nigerians living in South Africa as criminals, arguing that such generalisations unfairly undermined the contributions of thousands of law-abiding citizens.

“Thousands of Nigerians in South Africa are law-abiding professionals, including doctors, lawyers, academics and entrepreneurs. A small minority engaged in wrongdoing should not be used to define an entire community,” he said.

Fani-Kayode maintained that the anti-Nigerian sentiments expressed by some organisations should not be interpreted as representing the position of the majority of South Africans.

He nevertheless described such rhetoric as troubling, urging both countries to address tensions through sustained engagement, dialogue and mutual understanding.

The ambassador also commended Nigerian community organisations in South Africa for their contributions to protecting the interests and welfare of citizens living in the country.

He said their engagement had helped ensure that the concerns of Nigerians received greater attention and representation.

“Many Nigerians believe that their concerns are finally being heard and represented. I remain committed to speaking on their behalf and standing up for their interests,” he said.

Fani-Kayode further encouraged Nigerian investors and business owners operating in South Africa to sustain their investments and expand their enterprises, while supporting fellow Nigerians facing difficulties.

Reaffirming his commitment to defending Nigeria’s interests, he said: “Wherever Nigeria’s dignity is challenged or undermined, I will not hesitate to speak out and defend the green-white-green.”

He criticised what he described as the growing spread of misinformation on social media, arguing that unverified claims were often amplified by individuals seeking to undermine Nigeria’s interests.

The ambassador reaffirmed his commitment to protecting Nigeria’s diplomatic interests in South Africa, adding that he remained fully aligned with the foreign policy objectives of President Bola Tinubu’s administration.

He reiterated that strengthening Nigeria-South Africa relations, promoting economic cooperation and safeguarding the welfare of Nigerians abroad would remain central to his diplomatic mission.

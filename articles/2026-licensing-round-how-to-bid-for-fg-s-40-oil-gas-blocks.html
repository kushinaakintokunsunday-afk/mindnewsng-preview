2026 licensing round: How to bid for FG’s 40 oil, gas blocks

By Punch Punch 2d ago 8 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has opened applications for its 2026 oil and gas licensing round, offering 40 petroleum blocks to companies interested in investing in Ni

The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission opened the registration portal on November 20, 2026, with interested companies given until January 15, 2027, to complete their registration and prequalification processes.

The 40 blocks are spread across three categories, comprising 17 onshore blocks, 14 shallow water blocks and nine deep offshore blocks.

The commission disclosed this in a statement issued by its Head of Corporate Communications and Media, Eniola Akinkuotu, on Saturday, urging prospective investors to visit the portal to access the full list of available blocks, bidding guidelines and frequently asked questions.

The statement read, “The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission wishes to inform the general public that the 2026 licensing round portal is now live. On offer are 40 oil and gas blocks across three terrains. The blocks are grouped as follows: Onshore (17), shallow water (14) and deep offshore (9). Interested bidders are urged to visit the site to view the full list of the 40 blocks, as well as the guidelines and frequently asked questions.”

According to the NUPRC, the licensing round will be conducted in five stages, beginning with registration and prequalification, followed by access to data, submission of technical and commercial bids, technical evaluation and a commercial conference.

According to the commission, the licensing round was first announced by its Chief Executive, Oritsemeyiwa Eyesan, on October 6, 2026, following approval by President Bola Tinubu, who is also the Minister of Petroleum Resources.

How to apply for the oil and gas blocks

According to the NUPRC’s 2026 Licensing Round Guidelines and Frequently Asked Questions, applications are to be completed electronically through the official licensing round portal at br2026.nuprc.gov.ng.

Here are the steps interested companies are expected to follow.

1. Visit the official portal

Interested companies should visit the NUPRC’s licensing round portal at br2026.nuprc.gov.ng.

The website contains the full list of available blocks, bidding guidelines, frequently asked questions, prequalification criteria, maps and other relevant documents.

Applicants are advised to study the information provided before beginning the registration process.

2. Create an account and register

Companies seeking to participate must create an account on the portal and complete the electronic registration process.

Applicants are required to provide a valid work email address for verification and submit their corporate details, including their country of incorporation, registration number and address.

The registration process also requires companies to indicate whether they are applying independently or as part of a consortium.

3. Pay the registration fee

According to the NUPRC’s guidelines, applicants are required to pay a non-refundable registration fee of $`10,000.

The payment details are contained in Schedule F of the licensing round guidelines. The commission must receive value for the payment by the application deadline.

Companies are advised to follow the payment instructions on the portal and ensure that the transaction is completed early enough to avoid missing the deadline.

4. Upload the required documents

After registration, applicants must upload the documents required for prequalification.

The NUPRC’s requirements cover legal and corporate information, technical experience, financial capacity, and health, safety and environmental compliance.

For legal and corporate requirements, companies are expected to provide documents such as their certificate of incorporation, particulars of shareholders and directors, memorandum and articles of association, organisational chart and beneficial ownership declaration.

The beneficial ownership declaration must cover persons with at least five per cent control or interest in the company. Nigerian companies are also required to provide a letter of good standing from the Corporate Affairs Commission and valid tax clearance certificates covering the preceding three years or the period since incorporation.

For technical prequalification, applicants must demonstrate experience relevant to the terrain or category of block they intend to bid for.

Companies that do not independently possess the required experience may rely on a partnership, technical services agreement or support from a parent company, provided the relevant agreements and supporting documents are submitted.

For financial prequalification, applicants are expected to provide audited financial statements for the last three years, bank letters showing available funds or credit facilities, records of capital expenditure, or binding commitments for equity or financing.

The commission will assess whether applicants have the financial capacity to meet the minimum work programme and other financial obligations associated with the blocks.

Companies must also provide their health, safety and environmental policies and management system documents, as well as records of major accidents or enforcement actions within the preceding five years.

5. Apply as a consortium where necessary

Companies that do not independently meet all the requirements may participate through a consortium, subject to the conditions set out in the licensing guidelines.

A consortium must have at least two members and submit a consortium agreement identifying the lead member, the roles of participating companies and their respective interests.

The designated operator must hold at least a 30 per cent participating interest.

Each consortium member must satisfy the applicable legal requirements, while the operator is expected to meet the technical, financial and health, safety and environmental requirements.

6. Submit the application for prequalification

After completing the registration and uploading the required documents, companies must submit their applications through the portal before the deadline.

Applicants are advised to check their submissions carefully because applications cannot be changed after submission.

The commission will review the applications to determine whether the companies meet the requirements for participation in the licensing round.

According to the published timetable, the evaluation of registration documents is scheduled to take place between January 18 and February 1, 2027, with notifications to prequalified applicants expected on February 1.

Only companies that meet the prequalification requirements will proceed to the subsequent stages of the licensing exercise.

7. Access data on the available blocks

Companies that successfully pass prequalification will proceed to the data access stage.

At this stage, applicants are expected to obtain geological, seismic, well and other technical information needed to assess the blocks they intend to bid for.

According to the guidelines, applicants are required to obtain relevant data through the National Data Repository and accredited vendors, with evidence of purchase to be submitted as required.

Applicants seeking physical access to the data room are required to execute a confidentiality agreement. The guidelines also provide for a data prying fee of `$10,000 per session per block.

8. Submit technical and commercial bids

After reviewing the available data, prequalified companies can prepare and submit their technical and commercial bids.

The technical proposal is expected to contain the company’s work programme, assessment of the block’s exploration potential and proposed development concept.

Applicants must also demonstrate their capacity to undertake the proposed exploration and development activities in line with the commission’s requirements.

The bid submission stage is scheduled to run from February 2 to April 15, 2027.

A non-refundable bid application fee of $`25,000 is required for each bid. Applicants must also provide a bid guarantee in the form of a bank guarantee equivalent to five per cent of the signature bonus offered.

The guidelines limit each applicant to a maximum of two blocks, including interests held through affiliates and consortium arrangements.

9. Undergo technical evaluation

Following the submission of bids, the commission will assess the technical proposals to determine whether applicants meet the requirements for the blocks they seek to acquire.

The technical bid evaluation is scheduled to take place between April 16 and April 30, 2027.

The commission is expected to notify qualified companies on May 1, 2027, ahead of the commercial bid conference.

10. Participate in the commercial bid conference

The final bidding stage is the commercial bid conference, scheduled for May 7, 2027.

At this stage, technically qualified bidders will participate in the commercial process for the blocks.

Successful bidders will be announced alongside reserve bidders and will proceed towards the award of the relevant Petroleum Prospecting Licences, subject to the fulfilment of post-award conditions.

According to the guidelines, signature bonuses may range from $3m to $7m per block, depending on the asset. Successful bidders are required to pay the applicable signature bonus in full in US dollars before the grant of the licence.

Important dates and fees

The NUPRC’s 2026 licensing round has the following key dates and financial requirements:

Portal opening and registration: November 20, 2026.

Pre-bid conference: December 16, 2026, in Lagos.

Registration deadline: January 15, 2027, at 4:30pm WAT.

Evaluation of registration documents: January 18 to February 1, 2027.

Notification of prequalified applicants: February 1, 2027.

Data access, evaluation and bid submission: February 2 to April 15, 2027.

Technical bid evaluation: April 16 to April 30, 2027.

Notification of qualified companies: May 1, 2027.

Commercial bid conference: May 7, 2027.

Registration fee: `$10,000, non-refundable.

Bid application fee: $`25,000 per bid, non-refundable.

Physical data room access fee: `$10,000 per session per block.

Bid guarantee: Five per cent of the offered signature bonus.

Signature bonus: 7m per block.

The commission advised prospective investors to consult the full licensing round guidelines, frequently asked questions and prequalification criteria on its official portal, noting that the documents contain the detailed requirements governing the exercise.

The licensing round comes as Nigeria seeks to increase crude oil and gas production, attract investment into its petroleum industry and improve the country’s competitiveness in the global energy market.

It also follows the 2025 oil and gas licensing exercise, which offered 50 blocks and attracted interest from 143 companies. The exercise recorded 200 bids, with 31 companies emerging as successful bidders for 37 blocks, while 13 blocks did not receive bids.

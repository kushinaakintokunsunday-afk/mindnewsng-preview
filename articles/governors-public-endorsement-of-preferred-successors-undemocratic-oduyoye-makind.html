Governors’ public endorsement of preferred successors undemocratic – Oduyoye, Makinde’s ex-aide

By Punch Punch 2d ago 14 min read
MINDNEWSNG

Babatunde Oduyoye, a former House of Representatives member and ex-Oyo State Advisory Council member, speaks on his fallout with Governor Seyi Makinde, the politics of succession in Oyo State and why he believes governors’ endorsement of preferred candidates undermines democracy. GABRIEL OSHOKHA brings excerpts

In July, you resigned from the Oyo State Advisory Council. What actually informed that decision?

The Advisory Council is expected to advise the governor on policy, politics and administration. When I resigned, I cited personal reasons, including the need to improve myself in certain areas. Beyond that, I felt the council had outlived its usefulness. When a council is no longer functioning effectively, or the governor is no longer paying attention to its contributions, there is little reason to remain a member. That was why I decided to resign honourably.

In your resignation letter, you said you had not joined any political party other than the Alliance for Democracy. We know that the AD is no longer a registered political party. Where do you stand politically, at the moment?

I was never a card-carrying member of the Peoples Democratic Party, nor did I attend any of its local government or state meetings. All my activities within the party were carried out in my official capacity, first as Special Adviser on Strategy and Politics to the governor of Oyo State and later as a member of the Advisory Council. There were occasions when I represented the governor outside the state, particularly in Edo and Kogi states. However, I was never invited to meetings of PDP leaders in the South-West. On one occasion, I even sent out an invitation on behalf of the governor for a meeting of the party’s South-West chapter in Ibadan. I recall that a commissioner from Osun State questioned why someone who was not a PDP member was inviting them to a party meeting. What happened in 2019 was that, as members of the AD, we joined hands with Seyi Makinde to support his governorship ambition. Those involved included Tunde Tijani and Pastor Akinade Akanmu, who later became Chairman of the Teaching Service Commission and also joined me in the Advisory Council.

Before I left Makinde’s government, my good friend and brother, Oloye Dotun Sanusi, popularly known as Okanlomo Oodua, and I had been very close. It was also known to the government and several other people that we were pushing for him to succeed Seyi Makinde because I believed he had all it takes to govern Oyo State. However, the idea was not welcomed in some quarters. Besides, Sanusi is a very busy man. It was surprising that an idea that had not even crystallised became toxic in some quarters before it could take off, with some people already ganging up against him. As members of the Oyo State Advisory Council, we were given the responsibility of assisting the government in choosing the preferred candidate of the governor’s party. Governor Makinde wanted the Advisory Council to make recommendations, and we submitted a list of candidates. Att the top of the list was retired Commissioner of Police Fatai Owoseni, whom the council unanimously endorsed. Segun Ogunwuyi, who was then the Chief of Staff to the governor, came second. Some of us also pushed for Nureni Adeniran to be ranked third, but the chairman, Bolaji Ayorinde, and some other members favoured Bimbo Adekanmbi for that position.

However, weeks later, we were informed that our recommendations had been altered. We never saw the original report again. The chairman submitted a report, even though it was the committee’s responsibility to submit one. Some people have raised this issue, and I have read contradictory accounts in some publications. But I am saying authoritatively, as someone who participated in the meetings, that Owoseni was unanimously endorsed by members who were physically present and those who participated virtually via Zoom. That also contributed to my resignation from the Advisory Council. If we could spend almost two days deliberating and making recommendations, only for our efforts to amount to nothing, there was little point in continuing. Of course, the final decision rested with the governor, but our recommendations were eventually disregarded. That was one of the reasons I decided to leave. Let me also add that some governors are introducing practices that are strange to democracy. As we understand and practise democracy worldwide, I do not think it is normal or consistent with democratic principles for a sitting governor to publicly announce his preferred candidate. The governors of Nasarawa and Oyo states have introduced this into our political lexicon under the current political dispensation. If we want the best in politics, we should provide a level playing field for all aspirants to test their popularity and support. At the end of the day, whoever emerges as the candidate should have the necessary followership, with everyone in the state able to rally behind that person. Unfortunately, what we are witnessing is the reverse.

You referred to Governor Makinde as your friend and brother. He is aspiring to become president of Nigeria. Will you vote for him?

No.

Why is that?

When he wanted to become governor, he came to me, as he has acknowledged on several occasions, and we discussed his ambition. I was convinced and gave him my support. He recalled this at my 60th birthday lecture at the University of Ibadan. From the moment I said yes, I never looked back. There was a time I granted an interview to some journalists when rumours emerged that he wanted to become president of Nigeria. I cautioned him then that he knew what to do. Beyond that, I believe such an ambition is something he should discuss, or perhaps has discussed, with people close to him. However, I was never part of such discussions. So, why should I key into an agenda about which I know nothing? The same thing applies to the person he is supporting as his preferred governorship candidate. I have never met him. I was not part of the government of the late Senator Abiola Ajimobi. The understanding and responsibility I had with Seyi Makinde were to help him become governor of Oyo State and ensure that he performed well in office. That was where it ended.

Your ally, Dotun Sanusi, has declared support for the ambitions of APC candidates, particularly President Bola Tinubu and Senator Sharafadeen Alli. Were you consulted on this?

Yes, very much so. We are together. When Makinde first contested the governorship election, we approached Sanusi, and he supported us without conditions. In 2023, when Makinde sought re-election, Sanusi was initially reluctant. He had good reasons to be, having given so much, as he usually does during elections. Yet, the only road leading to his resort, which the governor had personally promised to fix, remained unattended to. The road is still in a bad state. Although some people may claim that work has commenced, the pace of construction is nothing to write home about.

Meanwhile, Dotun Sanusi has personally invested millions of naira in the road through his partnership with OYSTROMA. During the governor’s 2023 re-election campaign, Sanusi also connected more than 20 communities to the national grid. That was why the first official function Governor Makinde attended after winning the 2023 election was the commissioning of transformers donated by Sanusi to the communities during the campaign. So, I am with Dotun Sanusi. We consult, collaborate and have made several things work politically. I stand with him.

You are yet to formally declare for the APC. What is happening?

Well, the APC is an offshoot of the Alliance for Democracy. At a point, we had an internal crisis that produced two factions: the Moji Akinfenwa faction and the Baba Bisi Akande faction. I was close to Abdulkadir because he ensured my re-election in 2003, so I remained with his faction. While working with Makinde, I remained a member of the AD. So, what party am I expected to defect from? The AD? Remember, I was never a card-carrying member of the PDP.

So, we can say you are a progressive, as it is fondly called?

My relationships with people are not based on political alliances. My friends and political contacts cut across the APC. The President is also someone I have known for a long time. I had the privilege of hosting him in my house in 1998, after the D’Rovas AD primaries. He returned to my house before proceeding to his hotel. What I am saying is that I do not need to make any public declaration about defection. I have remained a progressive since the beginning of the Fourth Republic.

Some people have said that Governor Makinde’s ‘good work’ is enough for him to win both the February 6 and January 16 elections.

I have made up my mind not to comment on Governor Seyi Makinde’s administration in the run-up to the coming elections. However, I make bold to say that every candidate, depending on his background and political party, will campaign and present his case to the people. Ultimately, the people’s opinion will determine who wins the election.

Your ally, Dotun Sanusi, is championing the campaign for President Tinubu’s re-election in 2027. What is your take on those who argue that President Tinubu will win re-election and secure Oyo State, in the forthcoming elections?

In 2023, President Bola Tinubu won Oyo State, marking the first time the progressives would win a presidential election in the state. In 2027, the President is looking good. He is looking good because Pa Ayo Adebanjo, my respected leader in Afenifere, supported Peter Obi in 2023 while he was alive, for reasons of equity. He believed that the South-East had been denied the opportunity to govern Nigeria because of what happened during the Civil War. That sentiment still pervades our political landscape. A lot of things also happened during the 2023 election as a result of mutual understanding. For instance, President Bola Tinubu, in no small measure, assisted Governor Makinde in securing his re-election. I remember one of the governorship candidates who claimed he had been thrown under the bus.

What I am saying is that I see the Yoruba people rallying around their own this time. I will be working for President Bola Tinubu’s success in the next election, and I believe many other people will do the same. I listened attentively to an interview on Channels Television with former Lagos State Governor, Raji Fashola, in which he spoke about flipping Lagos back. I believe that with Obafemi Hamzat contesting the governorship election in Lagos State, a lot of good things will happen. Something worked in Obi’s favour during the 2023 election. The younger generation, who were very enthusiastic at the time, have lost some of that zeal, and the noise on social media has also waned. Because of the youth movement in 2023, many of our children across Yorubaland voted for Obi. However, a lot has happened since then, and I believe they are now wiser. That is why we are no longer seeing the same momentum. It is no longer the kind of movement we witnessed in 2023. I therefore believe President Tinubu will record resounding success at the polls in the South-West. Beyond that, the good infrastructural projects he is undertaking will also earn him votes across the country.

Are you saying thatTinubu’s re-election is almost a done deal?

I think he is looking good, and at the end of the day, he will succeed. However, a lot still needs to be done between now and the election. There are many misconceptions surrounding issues such as the removal of fuel subsidy and the President’s promise to provide electricity. People often overlook the qualification attached to his statement on electricity. He said, “If I don’t provide electricity, don’t vote for me.” However, there was a proviso that allowed for circumstances in which there might be legitimate reasons for not meeting the target. I think that, considering the performance of the power sector so far, the new person at the helm of affairs and the many items that had been lying fallow at the ports for 20 years, I share Babatunde Fashola’s views on that particular statement by the President.

Do you view the 2027 governorship election in Oyo State as a two- or three-horse race?

I know there are many candidates in the race. However, my projection, as a political analyst, is that there will be a coalition, which will turn the contest into a two-horse race. Whoever the coalition backs will most likely emerge victorious. You can look at the role coalitions played in the 2019 and 2023 elections. As an advocate of coalition-building, I believe the alliances that shaped the elections in 2019 and 2023 will also play a significant role in 2027. Ultimately, that will turn the contest into a two-horse race.

In 2023, there was a group formed by you and some other stakeholders, called the Rescue Mission. Are we likely to see something similar this time?

At that time, somebody needed to be rescued, and the mission succeeded in rescuing that person. This time, however, the objective is to rescue Oyo State and ensure that it is placed on the path of progress.

You said part of the reason you resigned from Governor Makinde’s government was the way retired Commissioner of Police Fatai Owoseni was treated. Now, Owoseni has launched a group called Oyo Lagba Forum. Is this a political vehicle you are aware of?

Well, CP Fatai Owoseni is a fine gentleman and an epitome of a disciplined police officer. I know many young officers who joined the police force because of the way and manner he conducted himself while in service. Having said that, I never personally pushed his candidacy during our meetings at the Advisory Council. I remained neutral. However, several influential members of the council, for good reasons, urged other members to support and recommend Owoseni. Some members reminded us of the understanding between the two dominant faiths and the advantage he had, politically, as someone from Ibadan. However, I believe such an arrangement should not be permanent. As for the Oyo Lagba Forum, I am part of it. I am an integral member, and I believe in its ideals, ethos and values. It is a noble group that places emphasis on mentorship- something that is lacking in this part of the country. It seeks to mentor young people and organise seminars and discussions. These are issues I have consistently advocated for through my annual birthday lectures.

So, I am fully involved in Oyo Lagba. At the initial stage, we wanted Owoseni to be the face of the organisation, and we deliberately positioned him that way. Given the respect he commands across the country, the group has attracted a large membership. You cannot imagine how successful the membership drive has been. I am therefore part and parcel of the Oyo Lagba Movement and fully involved in its activities. The Oyo Lagba Movement will also play a prominent role politically. We are already playing a role behind the scenes, and when the time comes, or when the necessary resolutions have been passed in accordance with the group’s constitution, appropriate pronouncements will be made.

Is it not getting late, considering how close the elections are?

Let me say this, although I do not yet have the authority to speak on behalf of Oyo Lagba: I have an inkling that the group will gravitate towards the noble and popular choice in Oyo State. I have a good feeling about that. I may not have its mandate to speak on the matter yet, but some of the group’s subsidiaries are already putting structures in place across the state. So, time will tell.

Governor Seyi Makinde has been campaigning strongly against President Bola Tinubu in the presidential race. Again, what do you think of his presidential ambition?

Let me state clearly that Governor Seyi Makinde has a constitutional right, an inherent right enshrined in the Constitution, to contest for the highest political office in the country. We should also recall that Governor Makinde, under the G5, worked closely with others, although at the expense of his own party. The President, in turn, reciprocated by supporting his re-election. I also listened to Governor Makinde’s recent interview on Arise TV, and I think he has left the door open. He said he would be willing to discuss with the Minister of the Federal Capital Territory, Nyesom Wike.

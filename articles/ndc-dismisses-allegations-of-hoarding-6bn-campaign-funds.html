NDC dismisses allegations of hoarding ₦6bn campaign funds

By Vanguard Vanguard 2d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Vanguard

Party says money is reserved for operations, accuses critics of seeking access to development fund

By Henry Umoru

ABUJA – The national leadership of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) has dismissed allegations that it is withholding more than ₦6 billion meant for its presidential campaign, describing the claims as false and misleading.

The party said the allegations reflected a lack of understanding of party administration and the funding requirements of political campaigns.

Some members of the NDC National Campaign Council (NCC) had reportedly accused the party’s national leadership of failing to release adequate funds for campaign activities, including grassroots mobilisation, billboard advertisements and the printing of posters.

The aggrieved members alleged that more than ₦6 billion realised from the sale of nomination forms and contributions by aspirants had been kept in a fixed deposit account while the campaign council struggled to finance basic activities.

They also claimed that 75 per cent of the money came from the South-East, following the entry of aspirants who joined the party alongside its presidential candidate, Peter Obi, and expressed concern that the alleged funding constraints could affect his electoral prospects.

However, speaking with Vanguard, the party’s National Publicity Secretary, Osa Director, dismissed the allegations as rumours allegedly being promoted by individuals seeking to create divisions within the party.

He said the claims were intended to create friction between Obi and the party’s national leadership.

“These are rumours and machinations of mischief makers who don’t wish the party well, and want to create problems where there is none between our presidential candidate and the national leader of our party.

“Also, it shows acute ignorance on the part of those peddling the rumours about party administration and how presidential campaigns are funded,” he said.

Director advised those making the allegations to familiarise themselves with party administration and the provisions of the Electoral Act on campaign financing.

However, a senior party source, who spoke on condition of anonymity, confirmed that the campaign council had yet to receive substantial funding from the national leadership, despite the revenue reportedly generated from the sale of nomination forms.

The source expressed concern that the funding situation could hamper publicity, grassroots mobilisation and other campaign activities.

According to the source, the party disclosed at its third National Executive Committee (NEC) meeting that it had generated about ₦8 billion, spent approximately ₦2 billion and retained about ₦6 billion in a fixed deposit account.

The source said the funds were generated through the sale of nomination forms and development levies.

“Let me also inform you that more than 80 per cent of the candidates could not pay for the nomination and expression of interest forms. They were given forms free. Many states still do not have offices, and the party is assisting them. The national leadership recently got its office.

“The Obidient Movement is coming out crying about money. Where are the funds for senatorial, governorship and other positions?” the source queried.

The source further disclosed that the party had proposed the establishment of a National Campaign Fund involving its presidential candidate, his running mate, Senator Rabiu Kwankwaso, and the national leadership.

According to the source, the arrangement proposed the allocation of ₦1 billion to the South-South, another ₦1 billion to the South-East, while the remaining ₦3 billion would be managed by the Campaign Council.

The source claimed that Obi was asked to contribute ₦5 billion towards establishing the fund but provided only ₦220 million, out of which ₦200 million was subsequently transferred to the National Campaign Council.

“The party is not holding any campaign money,” the source insisted.

The source further alleged that those demanding access to the money were seeking the release of funds contributed for the party’s development.

“They want the development fund money people paid to be given to them. The ₦6 billion is to run the party. Only Aisha Yusuf has helped to open an account to assist the party. The party has not raised funds, Obi has not raised funds.

“The party will be going to Edo State on Wednesday. The party has no money; the people of Edo State will finance the visit.

“If we give them the money we saved to run the election, we will not have money to run the party,” the source said.

The source also lamented the absence of adequate funding for campaign publicity, including posters, billboards and youth mobilisation, as well as activities involving various support groups.

Vanguard’s efforts to obtain the reaction of the National Campaign Council spokesperson, Dr Constance Ikokwu, were unsuccessful, as she did not respond to a WhatsApp message sent to her over a period of more than five hours.

Recall that the NDC, at its third NEC meeting held on October 24 at JD Event Centre, Abuja, announced that it had generated ₦8,529,632,438.77 in revenue since its establishment seven months earlier.

The party attributed the revenue to the sale of nomination forms, contributions from supporters, membership dues and levies.

Presenting the financial statement, the National Treasurer, Dr Peter Onigah, said the party had spent approximately ₦2.40 billion and retained a balance of about ₦6.13 billion.

He said the expenditure covered party activities, including primaries, publicity, salaries and other administrative expenses.

Onigah said the party had maintained financial discipline and ensured that resources entrusted to its leadership were managed prudently.

He explained that its financial management was guided by the experience of party officials, advice from national leaders and the National Working Committee (NWC).

According to him, the party would provide a detailed breakdown of its financial transactions as it approached the end of its first accounting year.

“We have been very prudent. Our chief is made up of technocrats, people who are experienced and people who have different capacities,” he said.

Onigah also appealed to Nigerians and party members to support the party financially, noting that it was still developing its structures and had yet to control government at any level.

“As a new party, we don’t have people in government yet. All of us are outside the government. So we need to contribute together and ensure that our party will prevail and achieve the desired objectives,” he said.

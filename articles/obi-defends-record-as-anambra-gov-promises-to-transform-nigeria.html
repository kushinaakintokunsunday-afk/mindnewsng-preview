Obi defends record as Anambra gov, promises to transform Nigeria

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

Presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), Peter Obi, has defended his eight-year record as governor of Anambra State, challenging Nigeria

Presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), Peter Obi, has defended his eight-year record as governor of Anambra State, challenging Nigerians to scrutinise his achievements and insisting that the governance model he introduced could transform the country if replicated at the federal level.

His defence comes amid renewed attacks on his leadership credentials by President Bola Tinubu, who recently accused him of failing to tackle the erosion crisis in Anambra while contrasting the former governor’s record with his own efforts to address coastal challenges in Lagos.

In a statement posted on his X account yesterday, Obi launched a series titled “Nigerians, Go and Verify,” detailing the policies, institutional reforms and development initiatives undertaken by his administration between 2006 and 2014.

He maintained that his record demonstrated what could be achieved through competent leadership, prudent management of public resources, effective planning and accountability.

The former governor said his administration inherited serious institutional and developmental challenges, including the absence of a coordinated framework for setting government priorities and allocating resources.

According to him, the situation informed the establishment of the Ministry of Economic Planning and the introduction of the Anambra Integrated Development Strategy (ANIDS), which provided a framework for interventions across critical sectors.

He explained that the strategy was aligned with the Millennium Development Goals and designed to address education, healthcare, poverty reduction, infrastructure, agriculture, environmental sustainability and institutional development.

Obi said his administration deliberately avoided concentrating resources on a single sector, arguing that sustainable development required simultaneous investment in essential public services and infrastructure.

He maintained that his government strengthened public institutions and introduced measures aimed at improving accountability and ensuring that government expenditure produced measurable benefits for residents.

“Our aim was to create a state where public resources produced tangible public benefits, institutions existed to serve the people and development was assessed by the lives transformed rather than by the promises made.”

He also recalled changing Anambra’s slogan from “Home for All” to “The Light of the Nation,” describing the decision as part of efforts to strengthen the state’s identity and restore confidence in government.

He said his administration was guided by four principles: character, competence, capacity and compassion, which he described as essential requirements for effective leadership.

The NDC candidate rejected suggestions that his achievements in Anambra were peculiar to the state or could not be reproduced nationally, insisting that the same principles could guide Nigeria’s development.

“I assure Nigerians that the progress demonstrated in Anambra State was neither a one-time occurrence nor an experiment that cannot be repeated.

“It reflected a proven model of governance that we are ready to extend to the national level. What we accomplished in Anambra can also be accomplished in Nigeria.

“With capable leadership, effective planning, responsible management and accountable institutions, Nigeria, too, can work for all its citizens,” he declared.

The latest defence follows Tinubu’s criticism of Obi during an All Progressives Congress (APC) rally in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State, on November 6, where the President questioned his handling of erosion in Anambra.

“In Anambra State, he could not confront erosion. I, Bola Ahmed Tinubu, confronted the Atlantic Ocean,” Tinubu had said, while comparing their records as former state governors.

The President also questioned Obi’s leadership credentials and his ability to manage political challenges ahead of the 2027 presidential election.

Obi subsequently rejected the erosion allegation during a radio interview in Jos, Plateau State, maintaining that he worked with the Federal Government under former President Umaru Musa Yar’Adua to develop an intervention programme for Anambra.

He said he had approached Yar’Adua with proposals to address the erosion problem and that the former President directed the then Minister of Environment to work on the initiative.

The exchange has placed the respective governance records of Tinubu and Obi at the centre of their political disagreement ahead of the January 2027 presidential election.

Obi maintained that his administration’s policies and achievements were open to public scrutiny, insisting that his experience in Anambra provided the foundation for his proposed approach to national governance.

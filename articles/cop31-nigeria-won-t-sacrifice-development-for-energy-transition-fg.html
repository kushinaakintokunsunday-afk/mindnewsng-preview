COP31: Nigeria won’t sacrifice development for energy transition – FG

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has reaffirmed Nigeria’s commitment to achieving net-zero emissions, insisting that the country’s transition from fossil fuels must reflect its national development priorities and should not be dictated by international partners.

The Minister of State for Foreign Affairs, Sola Enikanolaiye, said Nigeria was making progress towards cleaner energy but would not abandon its petroleum resources without adequate alternatives to protect government revenue, industrialisation, energy security and economic growth.

He spoke in an exclusive interview with The PUNCH on Sunday, on the sidelines of the ongoing 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference in Antalya, Türkiye, after Nigeria and the United Kingdom announced a new energy transition partnership.

Enikanolaiye said the agreement allowed Nigeria to present its position on the global shift away from fossil fuels and negotiate international support to advance its development objectives.

“Our position is very clear. We need fossil fuels to even fund the transition to other alternative sources of energy. So we cannot jump into a transition plan that does not address our national development aspirations and objectives.

“This is why we are calling for a just transition plan, an equitable transition plan and a plan that is also sustainable that would not inflict any damage on our own national development aspirations as contained in our NDC 3.0,” he said.

The minister stressed that Nigeria must participate in decisions affecting its energy future, warning against transition policies that ignored the different economic realities and development stages of countries.

“We have to be part of that conversation to ensure that nothing is imposed on us. You cannot impose an energy transition plan on us without us being at the table to negotiate and agree on what we know will promote our own national interests,” he said.

He explained that the non-binding Memorandum of Understanding signed with the UK was intended to promote cooperation at different levels, including government-to-government engagements, private sector investments and partnerships with civil society organisations and host communities.

The minister said Nigeria was already diversifying its economy to reduce excessive dependence on petroleum revenues, citing developments in agriculture, technology, digital trade, financial technology and services.

He said the administration of President Bola Tinubu was pursuing a broader economic restructuring that would create additional sources of growth and revenue beyond oil and gas.

Addressing concerns that Nigeria was caught between the effects of climate change and international pressure to reduce fossil fuel production, Enikanolaiye said diplomacy remained essential to balancing competing global interests.

He cited the Russia-Ukraine war and tensions involving Iran and the United States in the Middle East as examples of geopolitical developments affecting global energy supplies and petroleum prices.

The minister also highlighted the contribution of the Dangote Petroleum Refinery to stabilising petroleum supply, saying its impact extended beyond Nigeria to the wider African market.

“So, I believe we are on course, and we are heading towards zero emissions ultimately. But it has to be gradual. It must take into consideration the different levels of development, like I stressed, development and national development aspirations,” he added.

Enikanolaiye dismissed suggestions that Nigeria was being pressured into accepting the energy transition agenda of developed countries, insisting that the country had the diplomatic capacity to defend its interests.

“I don’t think we are under any pressure. And even if we do, we have the capacity to remain focused on our national interest. The Nigerian Foreign Ministry and diplomacy have demonstrated that over the years. So pressure is not a point. We are into partnership of equals, of sovereignty. You know where our national interest, in the spirit of strategic autonomy, guides what we do,” he said.

He challenged international partners to demonstrate their commitment through climate financing, infrastructure development and technology transfer rather than merely advocating the abandonment of fossil fuels.

The minister also cited the Federal Government’s compressed natural gas bus programme as part of efforts to expand cleaner transportation options and reduce the pressure of energy costs on Nigerians following economic reforms.

Under the UK-Nigeria Energy Security and Transition Partnership, both countries are expected to strengthen cooperation on methane reduction, particularly in the oil and gas, agricultural and waste sectors.

Enikanolaiye said the partnership would support improved methane measurement, reporting and verification, leak detection and repair, as well as the reduction of gas flaring and venting.

Dangote Raises Petrol to N1,300/Litre - But Several Nigerian Depots Now Sell Cheaper, See Their Rates

By Mindnewsng Mindnewsng 10 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Mindnewsng

Just a day after Dangote Petroleum Refinery pushed its gantry price to N1,300 per litre and suspended loading, depot owners in Lagos and Port Harcourt began sel

In a market used to prices only moving one way, the curveball arrived this week: Dangote Petroleum Refinery raised its petrol gantry price to N1,300 per litre on Tuesday, November 3 - up from N1,215 - and suspended loading ahead of its new fixed-naira pricing template. Yet several Nigerian depots immediately started selling petrol cheaper than Africa's largest refinery.

The depot list that undercuts the refinery

According to the latest depot price review, Masters depot in Port Harcourt listed the lowest price at N1,283 per litre - a full N17 below Dangote's new rate - with others not far behind:

  • Bulk Strategic: N1,284/litre
  • Aiteo, Nipco and Rain Oil: N1,290/litre each
  • A. A Rano: N1,293/litre
  • African Terminal, Ascon and Integrated: N1,295/litre

The refinery also set its coastal lifting price at N1,676,250 per metric tonne for cargoes, as it shifted to the new pricing regime implemented from midnight of November 3.

Diesel operators do the same

Diesel (AGO) buyers saw the same pattern: with the refinery's new gantry rate at N1,840 per litre, Nipco listed N1,800 and Edo Refinery N1,820, cutting their prices by up to N40. The average diesel price fell from about N1,837.50 to N1,807.50 per litre across monitored depots in a week.

Why depots can sell below Dangote

Market analysts explain that import-parity economics still matter: Nigeria's petrol landing cost had dropped to about N1,240 per litre at spot level per MEMAN data, letting depot owners with imported stock or older inventory price aggressively. Nigerian oil importers have also brought in petrol worth about N436 billion during the pricing dispute, showing some marketers would rather import than patronise the Lekki refinery - which NMDPRA data shows is running above capacity but whose domestic deliveries fell about 21% in September even as output rose.

Fuel prices move daily. This article is market reporting, not price advice - always confirm the day's gantry and depot rates on petroleumprice.ng and the NMDPRA factsheets before making supply decisions.

Sources: Petroleumprice.ng depot price review (November 2-6, 2026); Legit.ng energy desk; MEMAN import parity data; NMDPRA September 2026 factsheet.

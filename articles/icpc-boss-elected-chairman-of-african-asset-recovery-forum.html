ICPC boss elected chairman of African asset recovery forum

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 3d ago 5 min read

From Adanna Nnamani, Abuja

The Chairman of the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC), Dr Musa Adamu Aliyu, has been elected Chairman of the African Asset Recovery Practitioners’ Forum (AARPF), in a move expected to strengthen continental efforts to trace, recover and repatriate stolen assets.

Aliyu was elected by members of the General Assembly at the first General Assembly of the forum, held in Abuja from November 17 to 20, 2026, under the theme “From Commitment to Action”.

The four-day meeting brought together delegates from all 55 member states of the African Union, as well as international partners, under the auspices of the African Union.

In his acceptance speech, Aliyu expressed appreciation for the confidence reposed in him, pledging to work with members of the steering council to deliver measurable results and advance institutional reforms.

He said his leadership would focus on ensuring that stolen assets belonging to African countries were traced, recovered and returned for the benefit of citizens.

Aliyu said the forum would be judged by its ability to deliver concrete results, including the enforcement of an amended charter, the presentation of a baseline compliance report, the training of practitioners, the development of model legal provisions and the establishment of an adequately equipped secretariat.

He said: “Let me be clear about how this council should be judged. Within the life of this work plan, this assembly is entitled to see an amended charter enforced, a baseline compliance report on its table, practitioners trained and placed, model provisions in the hands of members, and a secretariat equipped to sustain the work.

“I accept this promise on these terms, and I will account to this assembly against them. Africa’s stolen wealth must go home. With your support, this forum will help bring it home.”

The ICPC chairman said the operationalisation of the forum had strengthened his confidence that African countries could collectively overcome the challenges associated with tracing and repatriating illicit assets.

He recalled that efforts to establish the forum began in 2022, adding that member countries had since been working together to develop a coordinated approach to recovering stolen wealth.

Aliyu stressed that asset recovery should not be viewed merely as a punishment for corruption but as a means of restoring resources to communities deprived of basic infrastructure and social services.

“Every stolen asset belonging to African citizens represents a school not built, a clinic not equipped and a road not laid.

“Recovery is not an act of punishment alone; it is an act of restitution to our people. This forum exists so that no African practitioner has to pursue that restitution alone,” he said.

He identified institutional governance, the Kampala Voluntary Tool, capacity building, technical assistance and peer learning, the strengthening of legal and institutional frameworks, and cooperation as the strategic priorities that would guide the steering council.

On resource mobilisation, Aliyu announced that Nigeria would contribute $20,000 in 2027 under the Resource Mobilisation Initiative.

He also urged member countries to redeem at least half of their agreed annual contribution of $20,000, amounting to $10,000, on or before the end of May 2027.

The Vice Chairman of the forum, Marie-Rose Messy, expressed confidence that the new leadership would deliver on its mandate.

Messy, who is the Director of the Debt Recovery Agency in Cameroon, said her 35 years of experience would be deployed to support the committee’s work and advance the objectives of the forum.

Also speaking, the Chairperson of the African Union Advisory Board Against Corruption, Kwami Senanu, said the successful convening of the first General Assembly demonstrated what African countries could achieve through unity, shared responsibility and cooperation.

Senanu commended Nigeria for its support and consistent championing of the Common African Position on Asset Recovery, urging member states to translate existing commitments into practical actions and stronger institutional engagement.

The General Assembly, in its communiqué, called on African political leaders to demonstrate stronger political will by providing adequate funding for anti-corruption and asset recovery institutions, enacting relevant national laws and safeguarding the independence of agencies responsible for recovering stolen assets.

It said translating the Common African Position on Asset Recovery (CAPAR) from commitment to action required concrete support from political leaders across African Union member states.

The assembly also urged governments to facilitate mutual legal assistance requests, strengthen information sharing and ensure that recovered assets were managed transparently and used for the benefit of citizens.

It identified the challenges affecting asset recovery across the continent as including difficulties in tracing illicit assets, differences in national legal frameworks, delays in mutual legal assistance, and challenges in recognising and enforcing foreign confiscation orders.

The forum consequently called for stronger cross-border cooperation, harmonisation of national laws, joint investigations and faster processing of mutual legal assistance and extradition requests.

It also endorsed the CAPAR Monitoring Tool as a baseline compliance assessment mechanism and urged the African Union Advisory Board Against Corruption (AUABC) and the African Union Commission’s Political Affairs, Peace and Security Department to finalise the tool and its digital reporting platform to enable pilot implementation within six months.

The assembly further validated the forum’s 2027–2029 work plan and strategic priorities, while welcoming an agreement on an annual country contribution of $10,000, with the first payment due by May 2027.

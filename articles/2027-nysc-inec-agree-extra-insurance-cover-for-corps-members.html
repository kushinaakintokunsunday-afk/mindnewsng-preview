2027: NYSC, INEC agree extra insurance cover for corps members

By Punch Punch 1d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The National Youth Service Corps and the Independent National Electoral Commission have agreed on additional insurance coverage for corps members who will serve

The NYSC Director-General, Brigadier General Olakunle Nafiu, disclosed this while addressing corps members at the scheme’s orientation camp in Kogi State.

This was according to a Monday update shared by the NYSC on its official X account.

He said the “arrangement was part of the scheme’s efforts to strengthen the safety, protection and welfare of corps members deployed for electoral duties.”

Nafiu “encouraged the corps members to register for participation in the 2027 elections,” “assuring them that the NYSC was collaborating with relevant security agencies to ensure their safety throughout the electoral process.”

He also urged them to activate the “NYSC emergency response application,” noting that the platform had enhanced security agencies’ capacity to respond promptly to emergencies involving corps members and had helped save lives.

The director-general charged them to “demonstrate responsible citizenship by discharging their electoral duties in strict compliance with INEC’s rules and guidelines.”

He described participation in the conduct of free, fair and credible elections as one of the ways corps members could contribute to national development and reciprocate the nation’s investment in subsidising their tertiary education.

Nafiu also cautioned them against the “misuse of social media, urging them to verify information before sharing it and to contribute meaningfully to public discourse rather than spread misinformation and disinformation.”

The NYSC State Coordinator in Kogi, Mrs Chika Chris-Moneke, disclosed that the Asaya Orientation Camp hosted corps members deployed to Kogi State and the Federal Capital Territory for the 2026 Batch ‘C’ Stream II Orientation Exercise.

She said 2,540 corps members, comprising 1,166 males and 1,374 females, registered at the camp.

Chris-Moneke “commended the corps members for their discipline and cooperation, noting that the orientation activities had progressed smoothly.”

₦5bn libel suit: Court to rule on APC elders’ objection January 27

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Ondo State High Court sitting in Akure has reserved ruling until January 27, 2027, on whether a preliminary objection in a N5bn libel suit against 35 All Pr

The suit was instituted by the state Commissioner for Finance, Omowunmi Isaac, and Kunle Odide against the party elders in Owo Local Government Area over an allegedly libellous publication in a national newspaper in February.

The claimants are seeking N5bn in damages and an order directing the defendants to publish apologies to them in three national newspapers, among other reliefs.

The PUNCH had reported that the commissioner and Odide instituted the action against the 35 elders over the publication.

At the proceedings on Thursday, the first defendant, Kola Olawoye, SAN, asked the court to hear the defendants’ preliminary objection alongside the substantive suit to avoid wasting judicial time.

However, counsel for the claimants, Olaoluwa Imoru, opposed the application, arguing that the objection was a threshold issue that should be determined before the substantive trial.

Imoru said resolving the objection first would prevent the parties from spending time and resources on proceedings that could become unnecessary if the court eventually upheld it.

The presiding judge, Justice Yemi Fasanmi, adjourned the matter until January 27, 2027, for a ruling on the application.

Speaking with our correspondent after the proceedings, Olawoye said the case was scheduled for the hearing of the defendants’ preliminary objection, but they sought the court’s permission to defer it until the substantive suit so that both issues could be considered together and reflected in the final judgment.‎

‎”This is to safeguard against the wastage of precious judicial time on the preliminary objection. It is an issue of jurisdiction, which can be raised at any stage of the trial, even for the first time at the Supreme Court,” he said.

Olawoye, a former Ondo State Attorney-General and Commissioner for Justice, said the party elders remained willing to reconcile with the claimants but could not compel them to withdraw the suit.

“We, as elders and leaders of this party, are open 24 hours a day, seven days a week, to resolving issues and reconciling members of the party,” he said.

He added that the defendants had a responsibility to respond to the court action and could not avoid the proceedings because of the disagreement.

On his part, the claimants’ counsel, Imoru, maintained that the preliminary objection should be decided before the substantive case proceeds.

He said, “‎By its nature, a preliminary objection is an application through which a party argues that a case, as presently constituted, is not maintainable in law.
‎
“‎To that extent, we believe it is a threshold issue. As the name implies, a preliminary issue is something that should be addressed as quickly as possible, even before the parties begin dissipating energy on the trial and proceeding further with the case.
‎
“‎This is because, if a preliminary objection is upheld, whatever proceedings have taken place could amount to an exercise in futility. ‎That was our position. We believe it is better to address the issue now and take a decisive step on the preliminary objection before proceeding further with the substantive matter.”

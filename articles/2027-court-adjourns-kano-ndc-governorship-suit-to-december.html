2027: Court adjourns Kano NDC governorship suit to December

By Punch Punch 5h ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal High Court in Kano has adjourned until December 7 a suit challenging the Nigeria Democratic Congress’s governorship candidacy in the state ahead of

The adjournment followed a request by the plaintiffs for more time to respond to a preliminary objection filed by the party’s counsel, Barrister Magaji Mato, seeking the dismissal of the suit.

Briefing journalists after Monday’s proceedings, the NDC counsel, Mato, said the plaintiffs were not ready to proceed because they had yet to respond to the objection and other processes served on them the previous week.

“Unfortunately, the other side that brought us to court said they were not ready because we had filed some processes, including a notice of preliminary objection, asking the court to dismiss the matter at this stage because there is no case for us to answer,” he said.

He said his clients did not oppose the request for an adjournment.

“The court graciously adjourned the matter to December 7 for definite hearing. We are ready, and if they serve us with their processes, we will also respond to them. By December 7, we will be fully ready to hear the matter, by the grace of God,” Mato added.

Explaining the grounds of the preliminary objection, the lawyer argued that the suit did not qualify as a pre-election matter because the three plaintiffs were not members of the NDC.

He also contended that even if they were members, they did not participate in the party’s governorship primary organised by its National Working Committee on May 29, 2026.

“Our grounds are simple. First of all, it is not a pre-election matter because the three persons who brought us to court are not even members of our political party,” he said.

Mato further argued that the plaintiffs had not attached documents showing that they purchased nomination forms or were registered members of the party.

He described them as “mercenaries being presented for the purpose of causing confusion among the good people of Kano State”.

The lawyer also challenged the timing of the suit, arguing that it was filed outside the 14-day period stipulated under Section 285(9) of the 1999 Constitution.

He said the NDC submitted its list of candidates on June 10, while the plaintiffs instituted the suit on October 17.

“In this case, the party submitted its list of candidates on June 10, while the suit was instituted on October 17. If you calculate from June 10 to October 17, you will realise that it is far beyond the statutory 14 days,” he said.

Mato argued that the court consequently lacked jurisdiction to entertain the suit.

He said his clients were asking the court to dismiss the case and award costs against the plaintiffs and their lawyers.

“We are asking the court to dismiss their matter because it is a waste of time, energy and resources. Secondly, we are asking the court to impose costs not only on the parties who went to court but also on their counsel,” he stated.

The suit was filed by three NDC members in Kano, Garba Umar Isa, Yau Abubakar and another party member, who are seeking to stop the party from fielding a governorship candidate in the state for the 2027 election.

They are also asking the court to restrain the Independent National Electoral Commission from publishing the name of the party’s governorship candidate, citing a Supreme Court judgment in the Zenith Labour Party case.

In an earlier proceeding, the court, presided over by Justice Simon Amobeda, joined the party’s governorship candidate, Aminu Gwarzo, as a defendant following an application by his counsel, Sagir Gezawa (SAN).

Gwarzo, who secured the NDC ticket with Mustapha Rabiu Kwankwaso as his running mate, was joined as a necessary party to the suit.

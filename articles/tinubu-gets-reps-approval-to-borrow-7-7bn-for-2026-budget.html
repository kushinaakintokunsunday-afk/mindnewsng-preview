Tinubu gets Reps’ approval to borrow $7.7bn for 2026 budget

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 1h ago 2 min read

Photo: VeryNigerian

The House of Representatives has approved President Bola Tinubu’s request for a supplementary external borrowing plan worth $20.52 billion and €1.04 billion for the 2026–2027 fiscal period.

The approval was granted during Tuesday’s plenary after lawmakers considered a report presented by Sada Soli on behalf of Abubakar Nalaraba, chairman of the House Committee on Aids, Loans and Debt Management.

As part of the resolution, the lawmakers approved Tinubu’s request to borrow N10.790 trillion, equivalent to approximately $7.707 billion at an exchange rate of N1,409 to the dollar.

The proposed borrowing is expected to help finance part of the Federal Government’s 2026 budget.

The house further authorised the federal government to raise the approved funds through one or more of the financing sources outlined in the president’s request, including concessional and commercial financing windows of multilateral and bilateral institutions, the International Monetary Fund (IMF), bridge financing facilities, loan syndication, and Eurobond issuance.

The lawmakers stipulated that the government must prioritise financing arrangements with the most favourable terms and conditions.

As part of measures to ensure accountability, the house directed the federal government, through the Debt Management Office (DMO), to submit periodic reports to the national assembly detailing concluded financing arrangements.

The reports are expected to include the amounts borrowed, sources of financing, interest rates, repayment periods, associated fees and other relevant loan conditions.

The lawmakers mandated the committee on local and foreign debts to maintain continuous oversight of the borrowing programme, including monitoring financing arrangements, actual drawdowns, utilisation of proceeds and compliance with the terms and conditions approved by the national assembly.

In a letter dated November 5, 2025, and addressed to Tajudeen Abbas, speaker of the house, Tinubu said the new external borrowings are required to implement the items, programmes, and projects in the 2026 Appropriation Act, which are designed to stabilise and strengthen the macroeconomic reforms and gains recorded by the administration in the past few years.

“The key projects in priority sectors, such as defence and security, transport, agriculture, power, education and health, in the budget will receive attention,” Tinubu said.

“It is also important to add that the proceeds will increase the accretions to the external reserves since they will be received by the Central Bank of Nigeria on behalf of the FGN and thereby support the naira exchange rate’.

Tinubu has also asked the national assembly to repeal and re-enact the 2026 Appropriation Act.

The president is proposing to reduce the total 2026 aggregate budget expenditure from N68.323 trillion to N65.176 trillion.

Ex-labour leaders broker peace between Ajaero, Osifo

From Adanna Nnamani, Abuja

Presidents of the Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC), Joe Ajaero, and the Trade Union Congress of Nigeria (TUC), Festus Osifo, have resolved their differences and agreed to work together following the intervention of former leaders of the two labour centres.

The reconciliation was announced on Wednesday at a joint press conference in Abuja, after meetings convened by the former presidents ended late on Tuesday night.

The disagreement centred on the Federal Government’s N70,000 monthly transport allowance for public sector workers. While Ajaero defended the NLC’s engagement with the government, Osifo faulted the process, arguing that the TUC was not adequately carried along. The dispute subsequently degenerated into public accusations and counter-accusations.

Speaking on behalf of the former presidents, Peter Esele said they intervened because they were uncomfortable with the public exchanges and concerned about their implications for the unity of the labour movement.

He listed those involved in the intervention as Hassan Sunmonu, Peace Obiajulu, Ayuba Wabba, Bobboi Bala Kaigama, Quadri Olaleye and himself.

“Certain things happen in the labour movement; we disagree. There’s nothing wrong with that. But what played out in the open was something that the past presidents were not comfortable with.

“So, the past presidents of NLC and TUC met with the leadership. We had meetings here yesterday that finished late in the night,” Esele said.

He explained that the resolutions would be communicated to state councils of both organisations to strengthen cooperation across the labour movement, adding that Ajaero and Osifo had endorsed the agreement.

In a joint statement signed by both leaders, the NLC and TUC confirmed that the misunderstanding had been amicably resolved and pledged to conduct their affairs with mutual respect, trust and cooperation.

“It is no longer news that a family misunderstanding arose between the two Labour Centers, which unfortunately played out in the public domain. However, in the true spirit of labour solidari ty, maturity and collective responsibility, distinguished former presidents of NLC and TUC intervened and worked tirelessly to resolve the disagreements. We are pleased to announce that their intervention has yielded the desired results, and the misunderstanding has been amicably resolved,” they stated.

The two organisations said their responsibility to defend workers’ rights must take precedence over disagreements between their leaders.

“We wish to place it on record that the unity of the Nigerian working class is far greater than any disagreement between its leadership. Our common struggle, shared history and collective responsibility to defend workers’ rights must never be sacrificed on the altar of misunderstanding or institutional differences.

“Consequently, the leadership of both Labour Centers has unanimously resolved to work together in an atmosphere of mutual respect, trust and cooperation in the defense of the interests of Nigerian workers, pensioners and the downtrodden. We recognize that the challenges confronting the working people are enormous, and this is certainly not the time for division, needless rivalry or distractions.”

Both labour centres reaffirmed their commitment to the welfare, dignity and material wellbeing of their members, stressing that solidarity and collective action remained essential to protecting workers’ interests.

They warned that disagreements within organised labour must not create opportunities for forces seeking to weaken the movement or reverse gains secured through years of struggle.

“We are determined to ensure that our differences do not provide an opportunity for anti-worker forces to weaken the labour movement or undermine the gains achieved through decades of collective struggle. Our responsibility is to confront the forces of exploitation, defend decent work, demand economic justice and resist every policy that impoverishes workers and pensioners,” they said.

To sustain the reconciliation, the organisations agreed to strengthen cooperation and establish mechanisms for addressing future disagreements before they escalate.

“To consolidate this renewed commitment, both Labour Centers have agreed to institute appropriate measures that will strengthen mutual respect, promote harmonious working relations, establish clear and effective mechanisms for resolving future disagreements, and prevent a recurrence of such misunderstandings.”

They thanked the former presidents for their intervention and urged affiliates, state councils, industrial unions, workers and pensioners to remain united.

“The temporary misunderstanding between our Centers must not be allowed to weaken the confidence of Nigerian workers in their organizations.

“The NLC and TUC are united in purpose, resolute in commitment and unwavering in the struggle for social and economic justice,” they stated.

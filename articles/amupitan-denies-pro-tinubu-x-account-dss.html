INEC Boss Amupitan Denies Owning Pro-Tinubu X Account, Reports Matter To DSS, Police - Check Below For Full Details

By Mindnewsng Blueprint 13 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Blueprint

INEC Chairman Joash Amupitan denied owning the @joashamupitan X account linked to pro-APC posts, saying the matter has been reported to the DSS and the Inspecto

Chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Professor Joash Amupitan, has denied owning an X (Twitter) account that was linked to him and allegedly used to express support for President Bola Tinubu and the All Progressives Congress.

Speaking at a dialogue in London, the INEC boss said the account, which bore the handle @joashamupitan, did not belong to him, insisting that he had never operated an account on the platform in his life.

"I never had an X account. I tried to get in touch with X, but there's no way if you don't have an account," he said. "I have my Yahoo mail. I check Yahoo mail. It was never my account. And that is the truth."

He disclosed that the allegation has been formally reported to the Department of State Services (DSS) and the office of the Inspector-General of Police, and that an independent report on the matter had been obtained.

"We have an independent report and the matter was also reported to the DSS as well as to the IG," he said.

Amupitan spoke on Thursday during a dialogue organised by Chatham House in partnership with the Kimpact Development Initiative, themed 'Nigeria's 2027 Elections: Democratic Trajectory and Regional Stability Beyond the Ballot'.

The controversy dates back to allegations that the X account bearing his handle had posted messages celebrating APC victories during the 2023 general election, raising questions about the political neutrality of the man who will oversee the 2027 polls.

INEC has consistently dismissed the allegation as fabricated, and in a statement of April 10, 2026, the chairman's media adviser, Adedayo Oketola, said fraudulent accounts had been created in the chairman's name to mislead the public and damage his reputation.

The INEC chairman also suggested the timing of the renewed allegation was significant, saying it emerged around a court ruling involving the African Democratic Congress, and argued that the only way to bring down an institution was to attack the person leading it.

He added that earlier accusations, including claims that he was a member of the President's legal team and that he lied about his educational background, were investigated by the security agencies and found to be unfounded.

Amupitan said some civil society organisations have also indicated interest in investigating the matter, and expressed confidence that the commission will deliver an election Nigerians will be proud of in 2027.

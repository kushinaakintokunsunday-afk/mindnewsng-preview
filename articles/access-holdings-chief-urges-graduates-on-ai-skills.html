Access Holdings chief urges graduates on AI skills

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Group Managing Director/Chief Executive Officer of Access Holdings Plc, Innocent Ike, has urged graduates of the Federal University of Technology, Akure, On

Ike said technological advancement was transforming industries and redefining career opportunities, making continuous learning, adaptability and innovation essential for young professionals seeking to compete in the global economy.

He spoke at the combined convocation ceremony of FUTA on Friday, where he challenged the graduates to see their certificates as the beginning of their professional journeys and not the end of their pursuit of knowledge.

“Your certificate is a ticket, not a destination. Much of what you learned in your first year may already be out of date.

“Master artificial intelligence and digital tools in whatever field you choose, but also build the human skills machines cannot replace: creativity, clear communication, sound judgement, teamwork, and integrity,” he said.

The Access Holdings chief urged the graduates to build meaningful professional relationships, seize available opportunities and avoid waiting for perfect conditions before pursuing their ambitions.

He noted that their university classmates and professional contacts could become future business partners, executives, public servants and investors, stressing the importance of building reputations that would attract recommendations and career opportunities.

“Do not wait for the perfect job or the perfect moment. Seize the opportunity in front of you, make the most of it, and let it lead to the next. Start something, learn from it, and improve. Always be ready for the next opportunity,” he said.

Ike also cautioned the graduates against measuring their progress against the achievements of others, particularly through social media, urging them to prioritise their mental and physical well-being.

He further encouraged those whose careers might take them outside Nigeria to maintain connections with the country and contribute to its development through investments, ideas and expertise.

“Whether your career takes you to Lagos, London, or Lusaka, find ways to put your skills, ideas, and resources to work for Nigeria,” he said.

Earlier, the Vice-Chancellor of FUTA, Prof Adenike Oladiji, urged the graduates to become problem-solvers and contribute meaningfully to their communities and the country.

She said the university had equipped them with the knowledge and skills to become innovative thinkers, critical problem-solvers and responsible citizens capable of making meaningful contributions beyond the institution.

Oladiji noted that technological advancements, particularly artificial intelligence and digital transformation, were reshaping industries, redefining careers and creating new opportunities.

She urged the graduates to embrace continuous learning and innovation, adding that their certificates should mark the beginning of a lifelong commitment to knowledge, personal development and service to society.

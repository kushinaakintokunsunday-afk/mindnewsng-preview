Trump vows to pursue nuclear arms deal despite challenges with China, Russia

United States President Donald Trump has vowed to continue pursuing a nuclear arms control agreement involving China and Russia, despite admitting that securing the cooperation of the two countries would be difficult.

Trump spoke in a telephone interview with Reuters aboard his flight back to Washington following discussions with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin at a trilateral summit in China.

He said establishing a three-way framework for nuclear arms control remained a major priority, arguing that reducing the world’s nuclear stockpiles was necessary despite the challenges involved in reaching an agreement.

“I love the concept of it but in terms of pure negotiation, it’s a very hard thing for me as a president to do,” Trump said.

“Because it should be done, but it’s hard to do psychologically,” he added.

China has been reluctant to participate in broader nuclear arms control negotiations even as it expands its nuclear arsenal.

The Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists estimated that China had about 410 nuclear warheads in 2023, compared with approximately 3,708 held by the United States. Russia’s nuclear stockpile was estimated at about 5,420 warheads as of March 2026.

The Pentagon has projected that China’s operational nuclear warheads could exceed 1,000 by 2030.

Trump said Xi supported the principle of reducing nuclear weapons but acknowledged that convincing countries with large arsenals to surrender a substantial portion of their stockpiles would be difficult.

He illustrated the challenge with a hypothetical situation in which a country possessing 4,000 nuclear warheads was asked to give up half of them.

Despite the difficulties, Trump maintained that discussions towards an agreement should continue.

Following the summit, a joint statement by Trump, Xi and Putin reaffirmed that a nuclear war could not be won and must never be fought.

The three leaders also expressed support for continued discussions aimed at reducing nuclear risks and preventing the proliferation of nuclear weapons.

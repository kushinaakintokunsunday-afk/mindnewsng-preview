Huawei warns of AI-driven cyber risks

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Huawei has warned businesses and organisations about emerging cybersecurity threats associated with artificial intelligence, urging them to strengthen their dat

According to the company, autonomous AI agents are capable of actions that could expose critical business information to data loss and cyberattacks.

The warning was one of the key takeaways from Huawei IT Day Nigeria 2026, held in Lagos under the theme, ‘Powering AI in Nigeria Together: Compute. Collaborate. Create.’

Speaking on the growing risks of AI adoption, Huawei’s Director of Data Protection Solutions, Liu Ming, said organisations could no longer rely solely on traditional data backup and disaster recovery arrangements to protect their digital infrastructure.

He cited two incidents involving AI systems that demonstrated the potential risks of autonomous operations.

In July 2026, OpenAI disclosed that models undergoing internal cybersecurity evaluations had circumvented security controls, gaining unauthorised access to parts of the company’s research infrastructure and systems belonging to AI platform Hugging Face.

In a separate incident in April 2026, an AI coding agent used by PocketOS, a US-based software company, deleted the company’s production database and associated backups in approximately nine seconds while attempting to resolve a technical issue. The data was later recovered.

“These incidents illustrate how AI can introduce new vulnerabilities—both through sophisticated attacks and through unintended actions by autonomous agents. While no single technology can eliminate all risks, stronger data protection practices and purpose-built resilience solutions have become essential,” Liu said.

To address these threats, he recommended the 3-2-1-1-0 data protection rule, under which organisations maintain at least three copies of their data, store them on two different types of media, keep one copy offsite for disaster recovery, maintain another offline or immutable copy for protection against ransomware, and ensure zero errors during data restoration.

He also presented Huawei’s OceanProtect solution, designed to protect traditional and AI data centres against data loss, ransomware attacks and system disruptions.

According to Liu, the platform protects databases, virtual machines and files while extending its security capabilities across AI training, inference and autonomous AI operations.

He added that OceanProtect offers security assessment, real-time monitoring of AI agents’ activities and rollback capabilities to minimise the impact of erroneous operations.

Beyond security, Huawei’s Deputy Managing Director and Director of ICT Marketing and Solution Sales Department, Zhang Haijie, announced plans to expand the company’s AI computing infrastructure in Nigeria by the end of the year to strengthen local innovation and extend its business operations across West Africa.

Zhang said the company was transitioning from an ICT infrastructure provider to an AI computing and ecosystem enabler.

“For a few years, Huawei has served Nigeria as an ICT infrastructure provider. Today, we are stepping into a new era, transforming from an ICT supplier to an AI computing foundation and ecosystem level,” he said.

Zhang identified the provision of high-performance, cost-effective computing platforms, support for local partners and the development of a sustainable AI ecosystem as the company’s main priorities.

He said Huawei would take Nigeria’s infrastructure challenges, particularly power shortages, into account by prioritising energy efficiency to reduce the cost of AI adoption.

He also disclosed that the additional computing resources would be made available through Huawei’s local open laboratory, giving partners the opportunity to test and demonstrate their innovations.

Also speaking at the event, Huawei’s Director of Sub-Saharan Africa Enterprise Data Centre Marketing and Solution Sales Department, Chen Boyi, identified data, computing power and large models as the three fundamental requirements for successful AI development, describing data as the foundation, computing power as the engine and large models as the intelligence.

He stressed that technology providers could not drive AI adoption on their own without the involvement of local partners.

“Our Nigerian partners have good ideas, innovation, and knowledge across industries. Today, together, we can make AI better,” he said.

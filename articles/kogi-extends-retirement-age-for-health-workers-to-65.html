Kogi extends retirement age for health workers to 65

By Punch Punch 3d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Kogi State Government has approved an extension of the retirement age for eligible skilled clinical health professionals in state-owned health institutions

A statement issued on Saturday by the state Commissioner for Information, Kingsley Fanwo, said the approval was granted by the state Governor, Alhaji Ahmed Usman Ododo, as part of his administration’s deliberate efforts to improve the welfare of healthcare personnel, retain experienced professionals and ensure that the state continues to benefit from the expertise of skilled health workers while they are still active and capable of contributing meaningfully to healthcare delivery.

The commissioner said, “The decision reflects Governor Ododo’s recognition of the invaluable contributions of healthcare professionals to the well-being of citizens and the need to create an enabling environment that encourages them to remain in service and give their best.”

Fanwo disclosed that the governor conveyed the approval through the Office of the Head of Service for formal communication and implementation across the affected health institutions.

The State Head of Service, Hajiya Falilat Yusuf Musa, Esq., applauded the governor for the approval, describing it as a progressive workforce policy that recognises the value of experience, professional competence and the need to sustain critical manpower in the state’s healthcare system.

She said, “This is a commendable decision by His Excellency, Governor Ahmed Usman Ododo. It demonstrates his sensitivity to the needs of our healthcare professionals and his appreciation of the enormous contributions they make to the well-being of our people. Retaining experienced and competent personnel for longer years will strengthen our institutions, improve mentorship and ensure continuity in the delivery of essential services.”

According to the approved guidelines, the new retirement age applies to eligible skilled clinical health professionals in state-owned tertiary hospitals, general hospitals, cottage and comprehensive hospitals, primary healthcare centres and clinics.

The State Commissioner for Health, Dr Abdulazeez Adams Adeiza, also commended the governor for the approval, describing it as a timely intervention that would boost morale among health professionals and further strengthen the state’s capacity to deliver quality healthcare.

He said, “This approval is a major boost to the health sector in Kogi State. Experienced clinical professionals remain invaluable to our healthcare system, particularly in mentoring younger practitioners, handling complex cases and sustaining quality service. Governor Ododo has demonstrated a clear understanding of the importance of keeping our skilled health personnel in service while they remain physically fit, professionally competent and productive.”

The policy is expected to strengthen the state’s healthcare workforce by retaining experienced personnel, promoting continuity in service delivery and allowing younger health professionals to benefit from the wealth of knowledge and practical experience accumulated by senior colleagues.

The Commissioner for Health said the development was consistent with the Ododo administration’s commitment to improving healthcare infrastructure, personnel welfare and access to quality medical services across the state.

“The Ododo administration has continued to invest in healthcare development as part of its determination to build a responsive health system, improve citizens’ well-being and ensure that residents have access to quality and affordable medical services,” the commissioner noted.

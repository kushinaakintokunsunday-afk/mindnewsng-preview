Academic Activities Paralysed As MAPOLY Lecturers Embark On Indefinite Strike

By Channels TV Channels TV 6d ago 5 min read

Academic activities at the Ogun State-owned Moshood Abiola Polytechnic (MAPOLY), Abeokuta, have been paralysed following an indefinite industrial action by members of the Academic Staff Union of Polytechnics and other unions.

The lecturers say the action has become imperative over unsettled welfare packages.

According to the Chairman of the Union, Adeleke Amos-Adebayo, the demands include payment of Earned Academic Allowances, review of the monthly subvention to tertiary institutions, and implementation of the Consolidated Academic Staff Allowance, among others.

The chairman of the Mishood Abiola Polytechnic Academic Staff Union say the indefinite industrial action has become imperative in view of notices given to the state government informing them about the need to FasTrack actions on the implementation of the issues raised, “the first letter was released in August, August 5th, 5th of August, which I took to the Governor personally, by myself.”

“September 21st, another reminder was sent to the State Governor for the implementation of this CONTISS and so on.”

“The last National Executive Council Meeting we had in Abuja, that was October 22nd. The NEC reached the conclusion that there should be an ultimatum to all the State Governors to implement with immediate effect,” he said

“So the following week, which happened to be October 26th, the letter came from our national body again, to all State Governors. So we took it down, and the ultimatum took effect from October 27th, which lasted till November 3rd. That is a 15-day ultimatum, that failure to implement or accede to the union’s demand, the chapters, meaning the State Polytechnics, are being given the mandate, or the directive, or permission to embark on industrial action, to push further their demands from the State Government”

” And November 11th, that happened to be the last day, that was last week Tuesday, so it was communicated, and the mandate, the directive was given. In Ogun State, all the chapters in Ogun State met, with the approval from the Zone and the National, and we commenced the strike Wednesday, 11th, November 2026. And up till now, we are still on strike.”

The indefinite industrial action followed the expiration of a 15-day ultimatum issued by members of the Academic Staff Union of Polytechnics.

“And I want to believe, maybe Mr. Governor has not been carried along, that he didn’t have to be… because we know him to be a listening… to have listening ears to people in the state, as the father. So I don’t mind using this medium, if permitted, to call his attention to what is happening in those tertiary institutions, so that he can come intervene, find a way to resolve this matter.

“We don’t, as union leaders, we don’t expect this industrial action to take long, because we have our children here, we have relatives here, and this is our own Jerusalem that we will not want anything to happen to it. So the Governor is the person we are now looking forward to to intervene, come in, attend to all these demands, by providing the necessary”

As monitored at the Moshood Abiola Polytechnic, Abeokuta, academic activities are grounded, classrooms deserted, as lecturers stay away from classes over welfare issues.

The situation has left many students of the institution in a state of uncertainty as to when the industrial action will come to an end.

Tope Adeleke, one of the students of the institution, laments the disadvantages of the ongoing strike on students’ academic pursuits.

“There will be a small delay in a lot of activities. We have a lot of activities, classes. There will be a lot of delays.

“And the second is, students are now being demotivated, since we actually resumed very early this time, that the school is now getting back on track, and you know, getting to this point where new students are now in, is going to be a very big struggle at the moment.”

” And my appeal is, what they know they can do for us, to actually help, this strike just being called off. We don’t understand the economic challenges of the country, and also every situation that the country is facing as at now, but what they can do to provide a solution, to get us, so that we students can actually be on track to continue our studies, and also continue to do what we know is best for us in the institution,”

On his experience in the last couple of days when the industrial action commenced

“The experience has not been good. The experience among students, I think, I will feel like it’s a big shock, because I think since last year that the school has been running, from the new Rector, there have been no strikes, you know, everything was running perfectly. So it was a shock, because we knew it was the Ogun State ASUP that actually made the order. So, I will say, it was a bit of a surprise, and students didn’t believe that it would actually take a bit longer to this stage. You know, it’s making us believe that, are we still going to have classes this year again? But we do hope that it gets resolved as soon as possible.”

Others, whose fate seems to be hanging in the balance, have appealed to the authorities and the lecturers to reach an amicable resolution to the impasse and call off the strike.

“We are making a passionate appeal to all concerned that they should consider the plight of the students and come to negotiation, resolve the impasse and let us have a smooth academic session”

And while this is ongoing, the lecturers say they are not averse to returning to classes as soon as their demands are met by the management of the institution and the State Government.

Efforts to get the reaction of the school management on the development proved abortive, as the Rector of the institution, Dr. Babatunde Jolaoso, was said to be unavailable for interview.

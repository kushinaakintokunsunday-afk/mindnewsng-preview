Nigeria’s transition will fail if young businesses can’t get reliable power, Skills, trusted access to markets

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

A Nigerian Founder, Sunny Idoko has said that Nigeria’s transition will fail if young businesses cannot get reliable power, skills and trusted access to markets

A Nigerian Founder, Sunny Idoko has said that Nigeria’s transition will fail if young businesses cannot get reliable power, skills and trusted access to markets while climate negotiators at COP31 debated emissions targets.

Sunny, founder and board member of SunnySide Eco Hub, is seeking $10 million to build a youth-focused entrepreneurial hub in Lagos. He is pitching it not as a commercial workspace, but as development infrastructure for Nigeria’s youth economy.

In development terms, the problem he describes is familiar. Nigeria does not lack talent or enterprise. It lacks the systems that allow small firms to survive, grow and export.

“Africa has no shortage of young people with ideas,” Idoko said. “What is often missing is the environment that helps those ideas move from conversation to reality.”

For Idoko, sustainability starts with economics.

Unreliable electricity, he said, acts as a daily tax on productivity. Small firms spend money on fuel, generators and downtime instead of hiring, training or expanding. For freelancers and creative businesses operating on thin margins, that tax is often fatal.

“A lot of businesses run on traditional energy, fossil fuel, and rely on electricity distribution companies. But it is not reliable, and the cost of energy is going up,” he said. “How can businesses operate sustainably without these barriers?”

Inside the planned Lagos Island hub, power and connectivity will be built in. Members will not have to buy fuel, power generators, or pay separate electricity and internet bills.

The building is designed with LEED and EDGE principles for a net-zero future, with solar energy and storage, water recycling, energy-efficient systems, low-emission building materials, modular steel construction and passive design.

“Sustainability is at the core of Sunny Side,” the project states. “Use less. Waste less. Create more value.”

The model is to treat clean energy not as an add-on, but as basic business infrastructure that allows young firms to compete.

The second barrier, Idoko said, is trust.

Foreign businesses often view Nigeria as a risky venture because of a trust factor and an information gap, he said. Nigerian businesses, meanwhile, struggle to prove themselves to the world.

That gap keeps foreign capital away and keeps Nigerian talent undervalued. Foreign companies recruit Nigerian tech professionals, he said, but those professionals rarely get access to markets beyond Nigeria.

“We are selling within ourselves, consuming within ourselves, and we are locked in with the networks we know,” Idoko said.

SunnySide is therefore positioned as a market entry launchpad as well as a hub — offering market access and linkage, product demos and showrooms, pilot programmes and partnerships, business networking, community engagement and a gateway to West Africa.

It is designed as a two-way bridge: African businesses to the world, and foreign businesses to verified African partners.

“This is the time for Africa,” Idoko said. “There needs to be that synergy” between businesses coming into Africa and African businesses going out.

The Lagos hub will bring together tech entrepreneurs, photographers, designers, beauticians, makeup artists, hairdressers, freelancers and founders in one community-led ecosystem.

It will include workstations under the name The Hive, a creative hub, a beauty studio, a cafe called Wish Cafe, and learning, wellness and community spaces for mentoring, skills development and events.

The aim, according to the project, is to help young Nigerians discover opportunities, build businesses, learn new skills, meet mentors, find collaborators, access networks, experiment with ideas, showcase their work, and connect with investors and incubators.

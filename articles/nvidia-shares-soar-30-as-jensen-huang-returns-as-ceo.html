Nvidia shares soar 30% as Jensen Huang returns as CEO

By Punch Punch 8h ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Shares in American chipmaker Nvidia rose by more than 30 per cent after its board reinstated co-founder Jensen Huang as chief executive, following pressure from

CNN reported on Tuesday that the company’s shares recorded their third-best day on record on Monday after the board announced Huang’s immediate return as chief executive and president.

The decision reversed the board’s earlier position that it did not intend to reinstate Huang, who stepped down on November 12 following revelations about his relationships with two women, including one suspected by US officials of having links to Chinese intelligence.

Trump said he had spoken directly to Nvidia’s board about bringing Huang back, arguing that the technology executive had done nothing wrong.

The president also defended his intervention, saying the United States risked losing Huang’s expertise to China amid competition between the two countries in artificial intelligence development.

“The bigger threat would be losing the brain power of somebody like Jensen. That would be a threat — to me that would be a far bigger threat,” Trump told CNBC.

Trump’s financial portfolio has also had millions of dollars invested in Nvidia shares, although the White House has consistently denied that he influences investment decisions in his accounts.

Nvidia’s board said it received input from senior US government officials before reaching its decision and was confident that Huang’s reinstatement posed no national security risk.

However, the move has drawn criticism from lawmakers across party lines, with some questioning the board’s decision and the potential security implications.

The Republican chairmen of the House Intelligence and Foreign Affairs committees and the China select committee said the company’s “brief internal investigation” and Huang’s reinstatement did not remove the national security concerns surrounding the case.

Other Republican lawmakers also criticised the decision, while Democratic members of Congress raised questions about who in the government advised Nvidia’s board.

Huang is expected to testify before the House and Senate next month.

The controversy followed reports that Huang had an affair with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman whose possible links to Chinese intelligence had raised concerns among US officials. Beijing has rejected the allegation, describing it as an attempt to smear China.

Huang, who co-founded Nvidia more than three decades ago, acknowledged making serious mistakes in his personal life before stepping down. The company’s board initially ruled out bringing him back, but later reversed its position following pressure from Trump and senior administration officials.

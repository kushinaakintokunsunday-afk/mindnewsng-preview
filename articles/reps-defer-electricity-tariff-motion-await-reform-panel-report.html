Reps defer electricity tariff motion, await reform panel report

By Punch Punch 5d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The House of Representatives Ad-hoc Committee on Power Sector Reform is expected to present its findings to the Green Chamber next Wednesday, a development that

The committee’s report came up during Wednesday’s plenary as lawmakers considered a motion seeking a probe of the Nigerian Electricity Regulatory Commission’s Service-Based Tariff System and the classification of electricity consumers into Bands A to E.

Sponsored by Kwara lawmaker, Aluko Yinka, the motion raised concerns about alleged discriminatory electricity distribution practices and the failure of distribution companies (DisCos) to deliver the minimum supply hours promised to customers under the tariff arrangement.

Providing an update on the committee’s assignment, a member of the committee, David Agada, said the panel had completed consultations with regulatory agencies, electricity distribution and generation companies, as well as other stakeholders in the industry.

He disclosed that the committee had also “undertaken visits to Lagos and Rivers states to interact with stakeholders and assess the difficulties faced by electricity consumers.”

According to Agada, “the investigation covered outstanding electricity-related debts owed by government ministries, departments and agencies.”

The disclosure, however, prompted Speaker of the House, Tajudeen Abbas, to question why the committee was still functioning, given his earlier directive dissolving all ad-hoc committees and requesting the submission of their reports.

Members of the panel explained that they had concluded their assignment and were preparing to submit their findings the following week.

Abbas subsequently directed the House to defer consideration of Aluko’s motion until the committee presents its report on Wednesday. The motion was accordingly stepped down.

Earlier, Aluko told the House that NERC introduced the Service-Based Tariff System to ensure that electricity charges reflected the quality and duration of supply available to consumers.

He said the policy was designed to improve accountability in electricity distribution by making customers pay according to the level of service they received.

However, the lawmaker argued that its implementation had produced unequal access to electricity, “with affluent neighbourhoods, commercial districts and high-profile consumers enjoying relatively better supply than rural and low-income communities.”

He expressed concern that even customers classified under Band A, who were entitled to a minimum of 20 hours of electricity daily, were not consistently receiving the stipulated supply.

He added that consumers in Bands B to E continued to endure lengthy power outages despite paying electricity bills under the existing tariff structure.

Aluko also faulted the frequent increase in electricity tariffs, arguing that the higher charges had increased the financial burden on households and businesses without delivering corresponding improvements in power supply.

He accused some DisCos of resorting to practices such as “targeted load shedding against consumers in lower bands, estimated billing and arbitrary transfers of customers between tariff categories.”

The lawmaker further highlighted the metering gap in the electricity market, noting that millions of consumers remained without prepaid meters and were consequently vulnerable to inaccurate billing and possible exploitation.

He warned that the combination of high tariffs, unreliable supply and questionable billing practices was placing additional pressure on small businesses, undermining industrial activities and constraining economic growth.

According to him, access to affordable and reliable electricity was critical to economic development, employment generation and poverty reduction.

As part of the proposed intervention, Aluko urged the Federal Ministry of Power and NERC to review and suspend the existing band-based tariff arrangement pending wider consultations with stakeholders.

He also called for an investigation by the House Committee on Power into alleged discriminatory practices by electricity distribution companies, with a directive that the committee submit its findings within four weeks for further legislative action.

The motion was, however, stepped down following the Speaker’s directive that lawmakers await the findings of the Ad-hoc Committee on Power Sector Reform before proceeding with the matter.

The Service-Based Tariff System, introduced by NERC in 2020, was designed to link electricity tariffs to guaranteed minimum daily supply hours. Under the arrangement, customers in higher service bands pay higher tariffs in exchange for longer periods of electricity supply.

However, complaints over failure to meet promised supply levels, inadequate metering, estimated billing and tariff increases have continued to generate public concern and calls for regulatory intervention.

The House is expected to consider the committee’s recommendations when the report is presented on Wednesday.

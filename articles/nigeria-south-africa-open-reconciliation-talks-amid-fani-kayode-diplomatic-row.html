Nigeria, South Africa open reconciliation talks amid Fani-Kayode diplomatic row

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 1d ago 5 min read

• Top officials hold closed-door meetings to ease tensions  •Tinubu reportedly intervenes   •Compensation for victims of xenophobic attacks remains uncertain

From Fred Itua and Aidoghie Paulinus, Abuja

Nigeria and South Africa have opened reconciliatory talks to strengthen diplomatic relations following the controversy involving Nigeria’s High Commissioner to Pretoria, Femi Fani-Kayode, amid concerns over recurring xenophobic attacks on Nigerians .

Daily Sun gathered that senior diplomatic officials from both countries have begun holding closed-door consultations to resolve outstanding differences and prevent the latest disagreement from undermining relations between Africa’s two major economies.

A diplomatic source, who spoke on condition of anonymity, confirmed that the two governments were already discussing ways to move beyond the controversy and strengthen bilateral cooperation.

“Yes, there are reconciliatory moves already. Both countries are already talking on ways that we can forge ahead,” the source said.

The renewed engagement comes against the backdrop of longstanding concerns over attacks on Nigerians and other foreign nationals in South Africa, which have resulted in loss of lives and property and heightened diplomatic tensions between Abuja and Pretoria.

According to information obtained by Daily Sun, more than 2,000 Nigerians have been evacuated from South Africa since June 10, 2026, through interventions involving the Federal Government and private organisations, including the Redeemed Christian Church of God.

However, it remains unclear whether the ongoing diplomatic discussions include compensation for Nigerians who lost relatives or property during the attacks.

Asked whether the issue had featured in the talks, the source said: “I can’t say if there will be any compensation.”

The reconciliatory moves also follow reports that President Bola Tinubu has initiated consultations with his South African counterpart, Cyril Ramaphosa, to ease tensions arising from Fani-Kayode’s recent confrontation with officials of the South African government.

Sources within the Presidential Villa and the Federal Ministry of Foreign Affairs claimed that Tinubu had reached out to Ramaphosa to reassure Pretoria of Nigeria’s commitment to maintaining cordial relations, although neither government has publicly confirmed such presidential communication.

The reported intervention came as consultations continued in Abuja over Fani-Kayode’s future following his formal reprimand by South Africa’s Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) on November 17, four days after he presented his letters of credence to Ramaphosa.

Minister of Foreign Affairs was also said to have briefed the President on developments surrounding the dispute, while the Chief of Staff to the President, Femi Gbajabiamila, reportedly maintained contact with ministry officials as consultations continued.

The controversy began on November 13 when Fani-Kayode joined 14 other newly accredited ambassadors and high commissioners to present their credentials to Ramaphosa at the Sefako Makgatho Presidential Guest House in Pretoria.

Shortly after the ceremony, the Nigerian envoy alleged that his introduction and address had been deliberately excluded from the livestream of the event, unlike those of other diplomatic representatives.

He also claimed that he raised the matter with Ramaphosa during a photo session and that the South African President directed him to relevant officials.

The allegation drew a response from Ramaphosa’s spokesperson, Vincent Magwenya, who disputed Fani-Kayode’s account of the President’s involvement.

Magwenya maintained that Ramaphosa had neither directed the broadcaster nor interfered with its editorial operations but had merely referred the Nigerian envoy to an appropriate official to address his complaint.

Although the South African Presidency subsequently made footage of Fani-Kayode’s address available, the disagreement escalated into a public exchange involving the Nigerian High Commission and South African officials.

On November 17, DIRCO Director-General Zane Dangor summoned Fani-Kayode and formally conveyed Pretoria’s displeasure over his public comments and handling of the matter.

Dangor drew the envoy’s attention to Article 41 of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, which requires diplomats to respect the laws and regulations of receiving states, refrain from interfering in their internal affairs and conduct official business through established diplomatic channels.

The South African government emphasised that official communications should pass through DIRCO, while reaffirming its commitment to maintaining constructive relations with Nigeria.

South Africa’s Minister of International Relations and Cooperation, Ronald Lamola, subsequently described the reprimand as a closed matter, indicating that Pretoria was prepared to move beyond the immediate disagreement while continuing to monitor developments.

Diplomatic sources told Daily Sun that the controversy could have been addressed through established diplomatic procedures without the public exchanges that followed.

They explained that complaints involving a host government were ordinarily handled through formal representations, diplomatic notes and other recognised channels rather than public statements capable of embarrassing either country.

A source familiar with the consultations said the manner in which the dispute unfolded had placed Nigeria in an uncomfortable diplomatic position.

“Whatever the merits of his complaint, the manner in which he handled it has put Nigeria in an awkward position with one of Africa’s biggest economies. A final decision on the recall is expected soon,” the source said.

Fani-Kayode, who was initially designated for Germany before his redeployment to South Africa, is also concurrently accredited to Lesotho and Eswatini.

His appointment came at a sensitive period in bilateral relations, following recurring tensions over xenophobic attacks, the treatment of Nigerian nationals and allegations of criminal activities involving some migrants.

In July, Lamola visited Abuja as Ramaphosa’s special envoy for discussions aimed at reducing tensions and strengthening diplomatic engagement.

Both governments subsequently emphasised the need to discourage inflammatory rhetoric, protect foreign nationals and address criminal conduct through lawful processes rather than collective blame or vigilante action.

At his November 13 accreditation ceremony, Fani-Kayode recalled Nigeria’s support for South Africa during the struggle against apartheid and pledged to strengthen cooperation between the countries.

Despite the subsequent controversy, the envoy and the Nigerian High Commission defended their position, while Fani-Kayode later described his meeting with Dangor as cordial and constructive.

Spokesperson of the Federal Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Femi Adeniyi, also declined to comment directly on the matter , maintaining that Fani-Kayode had been accredited and was performing his diplomatic responsibilities.

“Ambassador Femi Fani-Kayode has been accredited as Nigeria’s High Commissioner to South Africa. Therefore, he has since commenced the discharge of his diplomatic and representational duties, engaging with relevant South African authorities and key stakeholders on matters of mutual concern and shared interest to Nigeria and South Africa,” Adeniyi said.

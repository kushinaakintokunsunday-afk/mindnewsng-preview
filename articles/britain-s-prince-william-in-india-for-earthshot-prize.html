Britain’s Prince William In India For Earthshot Prize

By Channels TV Channels TV 16 Nov 2026 3 min read

Photo: Channels TV

Britain’s Prince William played street cricket and took a metro ride in India’s financial capital, Mumbai, on Monday ahead of hosting his environmental Earthshot Prize, on a visit that will also include a meeting with Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

The future king, accompanied by his wife Princess Catherine, took part in India’s beloved game of “gully” cricket with batting legend Sachin Tendulkar in the Ballard Estate area of the city.

The princess, dressed in a lime-green blazer and white trousers, tried her hand at batting before posing for photographs with young local players.

The three-day visit is the first official overseas trip undertaken together by the couple in four years, with Catherine’s cancer diagnosis and treatment limiting international travel.

The royal couple also hopped on for a ride in the city’s metro train to highlight sustainable travel.

“Catherine and I are delighted to be in India for this year’s Earthshot Prize,” the Prince of Wales said in a social media post.

The prize, which supports innovative solutions to the world’s most pressing environmental challenges, selects five winners from 15 finalists in categories covering nature conservation, ocean protection, waste reduction, climate action and clean air.

Each winner will receive £1 million ($1.3 million) at Tuesday night’s ceremony to expand their work.

Hosted by filmmaker Karan Johar, the event is expected to draw Oscar-winning actor Rami Malek and Bollywood stars Alia Bhatt and Sonam Kapoor.

William said that India had “become one of the most exciting places in the world for environmental innovation, with people across the country developing practical solutions to some of the greatest challenges we all share”.

“I am very appreciative that the royal family has taken up such a noble cause,” Tendulkar, international cricket’s all-time leading run-scorer, told reporters.

‘Great challenges’

The clean-air category includes Indian vehicle manufacturer Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, which is expanding its electric three-wheeler business. Other finalists include Uganda’s AirQo, which uses artificial intelligence and pollution monitors to track air quality, and Argentina’s Satellites on Fire, which uses satellite data and artificial intelligence to detect wildfires.

“We know that there are still great challenges but we cannot wait for everyone to agree before we act,” William said at a ceremony where the Earthshot finalists were introduced.

“We all want clean air to breathe, a chance to build good lives… these are not ambitions that divide us, they are ambitions that unite us.”

The seaside city of Mumbai faces persistent air pollution, although it is spared the choking winter smog that engulfs northern India.

New Delhi, where the royal couple will travel on Wednesday, was shrouded in toxic smog on Monday, as vehicle emissions, farm fires and industrial pollution choke the capital.

Readings of cancer-causing PM2.5 fine particles hit 556 micrograms per cubic metre in New Delhi on Monday, according to monitoring organisation IQAir, more than 37 times the World Health Organization’s recommended daily limit.

A 2024 study in The Lancet Planetary Health estimated that fine-particle air pollution was linked to 3.8 million deaths in India between 2009 and 2019, equivalent to five percent of all deaths over that period.

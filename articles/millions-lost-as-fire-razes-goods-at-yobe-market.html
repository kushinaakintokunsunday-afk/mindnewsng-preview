Millions lost as fire razes goods at Yobe market

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 2d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: VeryNigerian

A midnight fire outbreak has destroyed several shops and goods worth millions of naira at Potiskum Central Market in Yobe State, leaving traders with huge financial losses.

The incident reportedly occurred around 2:00 a.m. on Sunday, November 22, 2026, with the fire initially affecting the section of the market occupied by traditional medicine dealers before spreading to nearby shops.

The outbreak reportedly took traders and residents around the market by surprise as the flames quickly spread across the affected area.

The intensity of the fire made it difficult for traders to rescue their goods and other valuable belongings, resulting in extensive damage to shops and merchandise.

The affected traders are now counting their losses following the destruction caused by the inferno.

Following the incident, traders and residents rushed to the scene in an attempt to rescue merchandise and prevent the fire from spreading to other parts of the market.

However, several shops and their contents had already been destroyed before effective intervention could be mounted.

The arrival of fire service personnel helped contain the inferno and prevent it from spreading to other densely populated sections of the market, thereby averting further destruction.

An eyewitness commended the firefighters for their timely intervention, saying their arrival helped prevent the fire from engulfing the entire market.

Although no casualties had been reported as of the time of filing this report, the destruction of goods and property has left affected traders facing significant financial losses.

The exact number of shops destroyed and the total value of merchandise lost were yet to be ascertained.

The Yobe State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA) is expected to assess the affected section to determine the extent of the damage, document the losses suffered by traders and establish the circumstances surrounding the outbreak.

The incident has renewed concerns over fire safety in commercial markets, particularly those with closely packed shops and large quantities of combustible materials.

For the affected traders, the immediate challenge is how to recover from the losses and rebuild their businesses following the destruction of their merchandise.

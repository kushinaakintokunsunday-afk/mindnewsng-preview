Saraki accuses Kwara gov of summoning monarchs to scuttle visit

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The Media Office of former Senate President Bukola Saraki has accused Kwara State Governor Abdulrahman Abdulrazaq of deliberately summoning traditional rulers from Kwara North Senatorial District to Ilorin, the state capital, to prevent them from being in their palaces during Saraki’s political visit to the area.

The office described the alleged action as a desperate attempt by the governor to frustrate the former Senate president’s political engagements in Baruten Local Government Area, claiming that the growing acceptance of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) across the state had unsettled the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC).

In a statement, the media office alleged that traditional rulers from the district were compelled to undertake journeys lasting up to nine hours on deplorable roads to Ilorin, where they had remained without any clearly defined official assignment.

It claimed that the timing of the invitation, coming on the eve of Saraki’s visit, suggested a deliberate attempt to keep the monarchs away from their domains while the former Senate president engaged residents and political stakeholders.

“The royal fathers who have been compelled to embark on the tedious journey of about nine hours on bad roads from their respective towns to Ilorin have been in the state capital since yesterday without any specific assignment or any reason except that the state governor does not want them to be in their palaces during Saraki’s visits to Baruten Local Government Area for political engagement with the people,” the statement alleged.

The office accused Abdulrazaq of resorting to intimidation rather than addressing what it described as growing dissatisfaction with his administration, insisting that the alleged treatment of the traditional rulers demonstrated the government’s increasing political desperation.

“We consider this action another desperate and unproductive move by the governor, who is obviously confused about the best ways to respond to the widespread acceptance that the Peoples Democratic Party and its candidates are enjoying across the state.

“For a democratically elected governor to adopt this authoritarian method of coercing royal fathers and depriving them of the opportunity to listen to all sides of the political divide, it only demonstrates how unpopular the Abdulrahman Abdulrazaq administration has become,” it stated.

The media office further criticised the condition of roads linking communities in Kwara North to the state capital, accusing the governor of neglecting critical infrastructure while allegedly subjecting traditional rulers to unnecessary hardship.

According to the statement, compelling monarchs to travel long distances on bad roads instead of addressing the developmental challenges confronting their communities amounted to an advertisement of the administration’s failures.

It also questioned the competence of the governor’s political advisers and intelligence team, arguing that they had misread the purpose of Saraki’s ongoing consultations across the state.

The office maintained that the former Senate president was engaging directly with voters and stakeholders rather than seeking political endorsements from traditional rulers.

“If those teams are truly working and giving the governor good information and advice, he should have known that Saraki’s ongoing political engagement across the state is not with traditional rulers. He is engaging directly with the people and taking his messages to the voters. He is meeting with stakeholders and having useful exchanges of ideas,” the statement added.

It stressed that Saraki and the PDP recognised the nonpartisan position of traditional institutions and had no intention of turning royal palaces into political campaign grounds.

“Saraki and the PDP, out of respect to the royal fathers, do not turn palaces into campaign grounds. The PDP does not seek to turn our respected royal fathers into politicians,” it said, adding that traditional rulers remained fathers to all residents, irrespective of political affiliation.

The media office warned that the PDP had yet to commence its full electioneering campaign, predicting that the party’s popularity would become more evident when its campaigns formally began.

It accused the ruling APC of lacking clear programmes, a manifesto and fresh ideas, alleging that the administration had resorted to replicating initiatives associated with the opposition.

The office appealed to traditional rulers across the state to remain steadfast and resist being drawn into partisan political contests, describing the alleged inconvenience imposed on them as a reflection of the governor’s frustration over the changing political situation.

“These oppressive measures of the Kwara State governor can only bring temporary discomfort. They are the last acts of a drowning governor and his confused party in Kwara State. Their days are numbered,” the statement declared.

Criminal cases suffer over court rejection of confessional statements – Nasarawa gov

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nasarawa State Governor, Abdullahi Sule, has raised concerns over the loss of criminal cases due to procedural lapses in obtaining and presenting confessional s

Sule said inadequate knowledge of emerging legal principles and failure to comply with established procedures could weaken prosecution and allow criminal cases to fail in court.

The governor spoke on Monday in Lafia through the Secretary to the State Government, Labaran Magaji (SAN), at a one-day workshop on the practice and procedure for obtaining, tendering and admissibility of confessional statements in criminal trials.

The workshop was organised by the Nasarawa State chapter of the Law Officers Association of Nigeria, with the theme, “Navigating Charles v. State of Lagos and Compliance with ACJA 2015 and ACJL of Nasarawa State, 2018 for Effective Prosecution.”

Sule described the workshop as an opportunity for prosecutors and other justice-sector stakeholders to examine emerging legal challenges and strengthen their capacity to handle criminal cases.

He lamented that some law officers concentrated more on reviewing case files and appearing in court than studying developments in the law, a situation he said could contribute to unsuccessful prosecutions.

“You are always excited going to court and reviewing your case files, but you have never had time to understudy the trending issues and emerging legal questions.

“And therefore, when you go to court, rather than winning more cases, you are always losing cases. So, opportunities like this will give us the time to look at the past, consider the present and determine what we are going to do tomorrow in our jobs,” he said.

The governor urged participants to take advantage of the workshop, engage the resource persons and ensure that the knowledge acquired translated into improved performance in their respective institutions.

He reaffirmed his administration’s commitment to the rule of law, judicial independence and strengthening institutions responsible for maintaining law and order.

Also speaking, the Attorney General and Commissioner for Justice, Isaac Danladi, said the government was committed to strengthening criminal prosecution in response to emerging security challenges.

Danladi explained that effective prosecution required coordinated efforts among investigators, prosecutors and other justice-sector stakeholders, beginning from the investigation and evidence-gathering stages to trial and post-trial proceedings.

He said the introduction of the Administration of Criminal Justice Act 2015 and its domestication in Nasarawa State through the 2018 law had fundamentally changed the procedures for obtaining and tendering confessional statements.

According to him, the police and other law enforcement agencies are now under binding legal obligations to comply with prescribed procedures before confessional statements can be admitted as evidence in court.

“Unlike the previous regime, where the process of obtaining and tendering confessional statements by the police and prosecutors was treated as merely administrative, the laws have created binding obligations on the police and other law enforcement agencies before such statements can be extracted, tendered and admitted in court,” he said.

The Attorney General stressed that compliance with the relevant provisions was essential because confessional statements could be relied upon by courts in determining criminal cases.

In her remarks, the Chief Judge of Nasarawa State, Justice Aisha Uthman, represented by Justice Abdullahi Shammah, said procedural errors in obtaining confessional statements remained a major challenge to successful criminal prosecution.

She observed that cases involving seemingly sufficient evidence could suffer setbacks where investigators failed to follow the legally prescribed procedures for obtaining confessional statements.

According to her, such lapses could render statements inadmissible and undermine the prosecution’s case, potentially resulting in the discharge of defendants.

The Chief Judge urged investigators to familiarise themselves with the Evidence Act and the state’s Administration of Criminal Justice Law, particularly provisions relating to the presence of legal representatives or video recording during the taking of confessional statements.

Earlier, the Chairman of the Law Officers Association of Nigeria, Nasarawa State chapter, Raymond Umoru, said the workshop was organised to address emerging legal developments and improve compliance with procedures governing criminal investigations.

Umoru said confessional statements constituted a significant part of evidence relied upon by prosecutors but regretted that some were rejected by courts because investigators failed to observe statutory requirements.

He explained that suspects were entitled to safeguards during the recording of their statements, including the presence of their lawyers, family members, civil society representatives or other persons of their choice.

Where such persons were unavailable, he said,  the law provided for video recording of the statement to promote transparency and protect suspects against torture, coercion and other unlawful practices.

The workshop featured judicial officers, legal practitioners and human rights advocates as facilitators. They included Justice Solomon Ayenajeh of the Nasarawa State Judiciary; Nuhu Egya, Second Vice-President of the Nigerian Bar Association; and Emmanuel Kuza, a human rights legal practitioner.

Participants included representatives of the Nigeria Police Force, Department of State Services, Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, National Drug Law Enforcement Agency, National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons, the judiciary, Magistrates Association of Nigeria and Nigerian Bar Association, among other justice-sector stakeholders

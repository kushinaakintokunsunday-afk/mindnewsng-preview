UBA, police, telcos seek stronger intelligence sharing to tackle digital fraud

Banks, telecommunications operators, regulators and law enforcement agencies have called for stronger intelligence sharing and investment in technology to tackle the growing threat of digital fraud in Nigeria.

They made the call at a panel session organised by United Bank for Africa (UBA) Plc as part of its 2026 Fraud Awareness Week, themed, “Fraud Dynamics: Staying One Step Ahead.”

The hybrid event, held at UBA’s head office in Lagos on Friday, November 20, brought together industry experts, security agencies, regulators, telecommunications operators, bank executives and other stakeholders to discuss emerging digital fraud threats.

UBA’s Group Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer, Oliver Alawuba, represented by the Group Deputy Managing Director, Chukwuma Nweke, said timely intelligence sharing among stakeholders was critical to detecting fraud and protecting customers.

He noted that the growing use of digital platforms for financial transactions had created more opportunities for criminals to exploit weaknesses in banking systems and customer information.

According to him, fraud schemes now range from phishing emails and fake text messages to account takeovers, SIM-related fraud and the abuse of digital banking channels.

“Consider a suspicious SIM change followed by an unusual transfer. The telecommunications provider will see the first warning sign while the bank sees the second. Sharing intelligence promptly can help us effectively act in time to protect customers,” he said.

UBA’s Group Chief Internal Auditor, Eugene Inotu, who hosted the event, said preventing fraud was the responsibility of everyone, urging bank employees to move beyond awareness and take active steps to stop fraudulent activities.

“The best fraud incident is the one we prevent, and not the ones we successfully investigate,” he stated.

He also warned customers against responding to suspicious calls and messages or visiting fake websites designed to impersonate banks.

“We also appeal to customers to always be circumspect when they get calls or suspicious messages from scammers or spoofed websites pretending to represent us or any bank. Kindly ignore and reach us immediately on our verified channels to report such,” he said.

Delivering the keynote address, Managing Director of WSC Technologies Limited, Wemimo Oluwalade, said digital fraud had moved beyond individual transactions to become a wider threat to the survival and operations of businesses.

He explained that a single fraud incident could involve stolen identities, tricking customers into revealing confidential information, compromised devices, accounts used to move stolen money, insider assistance and weak controls by service providers.

“The question we should ask ourselves is: how do we verify what is real? The fraudsters see the entire chain, and they build their design across the blind spots,” he said.

During the panel discussion, moderated by the Manager, Industry Fraud Desk at the Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System (NIBSS), Oluwatosin Obadimu, the experts identified human error, insider involvement and SIM swap fraud as major threats to digital banking.

The panel included ACP Ty Mahmud of the Nigeria Police Special Fraud Unit; Revenue Assurance and Fraud Management Manager at Globacom, Sunday Alani; and Chairperson of the Association of Chief Audit Executives of Banks in Nigeria, Aina Amah.

Mahmud said criminals were increasingly exploiting human trust to gain access to financial accounts, adding that internal compromise accounted for about 70 per cent of cases handled by the police’s specialised fraud units.

“In every situation, fraudsters first attack the people, not the system,” he said.

Alani identified SIM swap fraud as a major weakness in digital banking because one-time passwords (OTPs), used to approve transactions, are often sent to customers through their SIM cards.

“If I can swap your SIM, then truly I’m in charge. The OTP that the bank sends to authorise the transaction comes to me,” he explained.

Amah urged financial institutions to invest more in fraud prevention technology, stronger audit systems and skilled security teams, warning against treating such investments as unnecessary costs.

“If you think you are saving cost by not improving your technology, not enhancing your technology team, your audit team, your CISO, you are doing yourself an injustice,” she said.

The panellists commended UBA for sustaining discussions on fraud prevention and agreed that banks, telecommunications operators, regulators and security agencies must work together, share information and strengthen their technology to keep pace with increasingly sophisticated fraudsters.

UBA operates in 20 African countries and several international markets, including the United Kingdom, the United States, France and the United Arab Emirates. The bank says it serves more than 45 million customers globally.

Group urges ECOWAS court to deliver ruling on detained activist

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Take It Back Movement Nigeria, Delta State Chapter, has urged the President of the Community Court of Justice of the Economic Community of West African Stat

The group said the ruling in Suit No. ECW/CCJ/APP/28/25, instituted by Aghogho against the Federal Republic of Nigeria, had remained pending for about nine months after the application was adjourned for ruling on January 29, 2026.

In a letter dated November 16, 2026, and addressed to the ECOWAS Court President in Abuja, the organisation expressed concern over the delay, noting that Aghogho had reportedly remained in detention at the Nigerian Correctional Service’s Warri Custodial Centre, Okere-Warri, Delta State, since June 16, 2025.

The letter, signed by the group’s state coordinator, Ochuko Etu, and state secretary, Efafe Okemute, noted the prolonged detention and the outstanding application for interim protection, raising concerns about the urgency of the relief sought and the effectiveness of interim judicial protection.

It stated, “Interim measures serve an important protective purpose. They are intended to preserve the rights of parties, prevent irreparable harm and safeguard the effectiveness of judicial proceedings pending the final determination of substantive claims.”

The organisation acknowledged that filing an application for interim measures did not automatically entitle an applicant to the relief sought but maintained that such applications required timely judicial consideration, particularly where the alleged circumstances giving rise to the request for protection were continuing.

It said, “The purpose of such an application underscores the importance of timely judicial consideration, particularly where delay may affect the practical value of the protection being requested.”

The group, however, stressed that its letter was not intended to interfere with the court’s independence or impartiality or influence the outcome of the pending application.

It said it was not a party to the suit and was neither seeking to intervene in the proceedings nor introduce additional evidence or legal arguments.

“Our intervention is, therefore, strictly limited to an institutional expression of concern and a respectful request for administrative attention to the status and timely communication of the outstanding ruling,” the letter stated.

Citing Articles 79 and 80 of the ECOWAS Court’s Rules of Procedure, alongside the applicable Instructions to the Chief Registrar, the organisation said the procedural framework provided arrangements for handling applications for interim measures, including their submission for the President’s consideration and prioritisation of translation where necessary.

It urged the court president to determine whether any administrative or procedural issues caused the delay and, where appropriate, facilitate the delivery and formal communication of the ruling.

The group also requested that if the ruling had already been delivered, information on its status should be communicated through the court’s recognised channels.

It further asked that any additional procedural steps required from the parties be promptly communicated to them.

The organisation said its appeal was motivated by the need to strengthen public confidence in judicial institutions, adding that the credibility of the judiciary depended not only on the independence and fairness of its decisions but also on the timely administration of justice.

It added that it acted in good faith and in the public interest, without prejudice to the rights of either party or the merits of the substantive proceedings.

Courts dismiss suits against ADC, NDC candidates in Kano

From Desmond Mgboh, Kano

The Federal High Court in Kano has dismissed separate suits challenging the nominations of Ibrahim Ali Amin as the African Democratic Congress (ADC) governorship candidate and Tijjani Husseini Gandu as the National Democratic Congress (NDC) candidate for Kano Municipal State Constituency in the 2027 elections.

The judgments, delivered by Justices M.M. Shuaibu and Simon Amobeda, respectively, cleared the two candidates of the legal challenges to their nominations, with the court in the ADC case ruling that the action was filed outside the constitutionally prescribed period for pre-election disputes.

In the first suit, marked FHC/KN/CS/375/2026, Sheikh Ibrahim Khalil Idris had approached the court through his counsel, A.Y. Gumel, seeking a declaration that he was the duly elected governorship candidate of the ADC.

He joined the party, Amin and the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) as first, second and third defendants, respectively.

The dispute arose from the party’s governorship nomination process, during which both aspirants reportedly signed an agreement on May 27, 2026, accepting a consensus arrangement and authorising the party leadership to select its candidate.

Following consultations, the ADC leadership nominated Amin, a decision Khalil challenged in court, insisting that he was entitled to the ticket.

However, Amin’s legal team, led by N.A. Ayagi, raised a preliminary objection challenging the court’s jurisdiction to entertain the suit.

Delivering judgment, Justice Shuaibu upheld the objection, ruling that the action was statute-barred because it was instituted outside the 14-day period prescribed by the Constitution for filing pre-election matters.

The judge held that the cause of action arose during the nomination process and submission of Amin’s name to INEC between July 4 and August 5, 2026, rather than in September when Khalil commenced the action.

The court also found deficiencies in the plaintiff’s claim, particularly his reliance on an alleged party primary held on May 22, 2026.

Justice Shuaibu observed that INEC had confirmed that it neither participated in nor monitored the alleged primary, while the plaintiff failed to produce an official commission report authenticating the exercise.

The court further faulted a letter tendered by Khalil, noting that it was signed by the party’s state chairman, whereas the authority to transmit candidates’ names to INEC rested with the party’s National Working Committee or National Executive Committee.

It also held that Khalil failed to exhaust the party’s internal dispute resolution procedures as required under Section 10.9 of its guidelines.

Consequently, the court dismissed the suit, leaving Amin’s nomination as the ADC governorship candidate undisturbed.

In a separate judgment, Justice Simon Amobeda dismissed a suit challenging the nomination of Tijjani Husseini Gandu, a Kwankwasiyya political singer, as the NDC candidate for Kano Municipal State Constituency in the 2027 House of Assembly election.

The action was instituted by Aminu Isyaku Yusuf, who alleged that he purchased the party’s nomination forms to contest the election but that Gandu’s name was subsequently forwarded to INEC and published as the party’s candidate.

Yusuf contended that the process leading to Gandu’s nomination was marred by irregularities and sought judicial intervention to challenge the party’s decision.

However, counsel to Gandu, Badamasi Suleiman Gandu, opposed the suit and urged the court to uphold his client’s nomination.

In his judgment, Justice Amobeda accepted the defence submissions and dismissed the action, leaving Gandu’s candidature intact.

The two judgments resolve the challenges at the Federal High Court, subject to any appeals that may be filed by the dissatisfied parties.

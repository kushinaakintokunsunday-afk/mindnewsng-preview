Kaduna varsity unions suspend strike, insist on demands

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Joint Action Committee of Kaduna State University has suspended its one-week warning strike following discussions with key stakeholders in the Kaduna State

The JAC Chairperson, Sa’adiyya Mustapha, announced the suspension while addressing journalists on Monday, saying the decision was taken to allow ongoing negotiations over the unions’ grievances to continue.

The committee comprises the Senior Staff Association of Nigerian Universities, the Non-Academic Staff Union of Educational and Associated Institutions and the National Association of Academic Technologists.

Mustapha stressed that the suspension did not mean the unions had surrendered or abandoned their demands but demonstrated their willingness to resolve the dispute through dialogue.

“We have gone on a one-week strike and have now decided to suspend it, not because we are surrendering, but for the good of this university.

“Several key stakeholders in this administration have made efforts to dialogue with us, and we have seen reason in those discussions. We have therefore decided to suspend the strike to show that we are willing to support the government and give dialogue a chance,” she said.

The JAC chairperson appreciated members for their support and resilience during the industrial action, explaining that the strike was intended to draw the government’s attention to their unresolved grievances.

She reaffirmed the unions’ support for Governor Uba Sani, recalling previous salary-related challenges faced by university workers and the governor’s intervention in clearing outstanding payments.

Mustapha recalled that university workers received half salaries in 2017, while their May 2023 salaries were not paid. She added that the outstanding payments were presented to the governor and subsequently cleared after he assumed office.

“Because of what he has done for us, we cannot say we are not in support of him. We support the governor and are solidly behind him.

“However, what happened to JAC was what we were unhappy with, and that was why we decided to embark on the strike,” she said.

She expressed optimism that the ongoing negotiations would lead to government intervention and a resolution of the dispute, urging the university management, governing council and state government to ensure fairness and equity in their dealings with staff.

PUNCH Online reports that the industrial action followed a seven-day ultimatum issued by the three unions on November 2, 2026, to the Kaduna State Government and the university management over outstanding welfare demands and the alleged failure to implement agreements reached with non-teaching staff unions.

The unions demanded the immediate implementation of the 2026 Federal Government/non-teaching staff agreement, particularly the Consolidated Non-Teaching Tools Allowance, payment of approved wage awards and the Federal Government’s N70,000 monthly transport support for workers for six months.

Other demands included respect for university autonomy, provision of group life insurance for staff and settlement of outstanding allowances and entitlements.

The unions had warned that failure to address their grievances within seven days would compel them to embark on industrial action.

The warning strike commenced on November 10 after the unions said the ultimatum expired without a response, invitation for dialogue or concrete action from the authorities.

The development came despite an interim order issued by the National Industrial Court in Abuja on November 5 restraining the three unions from embarking on industrial action pending the determination of a suit instituted by the university, its governing council and vice-chancellor.

The court, presided over by Justice E. D. Subilim, adjourned the hearing of the application to November 18, 2026.

The dispute also followed earlier disagreements over the Kaduna State Government’s decision to implement the 2025 Federal Government agreement with university academic staff without extending similar benefits to non-academic workers.

In September, the JAC had protested what it described as discriminatory implementation of the agreement, insisting that non-teaching staff should not be excluded from benefits arising from negotiations with university-based unions.

The unions had also raised concerns over outstanding earned allowances, excess workload allowances, promotion arrears, pension arrears, group life insurance and the implementation of salary adjustments.

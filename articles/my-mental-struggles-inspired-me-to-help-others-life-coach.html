My mental struggles inspired me to help others – Life coach

Life coach and founder of Reworded, Ayomide Adedayo-Adeleke, speaks with BIODUN BUSARI about her life, passion, career and efforts to build a community that challenges mediocrity

What best captures who you are beyond your professional roles?

I like to describe myself without titles. I’m an author and public speaker. I work with the belief that we are motivated by what we believe. My conviction is that you cannot build beyond what you can see or think. Many times, we do things based on our experiences, but I’m someone who likes to challenge people’s beliefs. If I see what you’re building and know that you can achieve more, I’ll challenge you to do more. So, I’m a transformational woman.

What influenced your decision to study Business Administration?

I went to Covenant University. My mum’s fashion business inspired me because I wanted to understand the business aspect of what she did. Initially, I wanted to study Mass Communication because, from childhood, I had envisioned speaking to people in different settings. I also enjoyed exploring the business side of my mum’s enterprise, using it as a case study. I think that influenced my choice of course.

Did the course influence what you do currently?

Definitely. Business has to do with customers, and I’m someone who wants to understand my customers. I can be very inquisitive. I want to know why you are buying and why you aren’t buying. This influences everything I do because I work with people who are building their lives, brands, relationships and finances, among other things. My background in business has helped me understand that space, and it has been very useful.

You mentioned your childhood desire to study Mass Communication. What happened to that ambition?

People often talk about not abandoning their childhood passions, and I eventually found my way back to mine. After obtaining my certification in brand strategy, I wanted to improve my communication skills. I subsequently graduated from the National Broadcast Academy and was expected to undertake an internship. I went to TVC, where I worked on the breakfast show. I had to get to work very early, read the newspapers, write scripts and look for punchy headlines.

You also mentioned being certified as a brand strategist.

I would say curiosity inspired me to go into brand strategy. As I mentioned, my mum owned a fashion business, and I wanted to learn how to sew. However, despite several attempts, I couldn’t. I became the only one among my mum’s children who couldn’t learn the skill. So, I decided to focus on other areas.

I realised that I often felt bad about myself because of that, so I started concentrating on how to use my mind and intellectual abilities. My brother told me about a particular academy, and I enrolled in a brand strategy programme. There, I learnt to think, work and relate differently so I could become better.

I initially thought I would be given software that would transform the way I think, but I realised that achieving what I wanted still depended on how I used my brain and mind. That experience has contributed significantly to my personal growth.

How have these experiences shaped your personality?

It has been a tremendous journey. I can say that I’m a woman who can do well at anything I decide to pursue. However, that can also be a weakness because it means having many things to do.

I have worked in the fashion industry and sold food, among other things. I’ve done quite a lot, but I’m currently building Reworded, which demands a significant part of my time and energy.

What is Reworded all about?

First, I would say that Reworded was born out of a personal experience. There was a period in my life when I was struggling mentally and needed people around me to encourage me, but I wasn’t getting that support. I had believed many things about myself that were not true.

At a point, I left a place, and someone spread rumours about me. I started explaining to people that the allegations were false. Whenever anyone approached me to ask what had happened, I would spend time explaining my side of the story. I did that for a long time until the Holy Spirit warned me that by constantly meeting people to correct the falsehoods, I was helping to spread the very lies someone had told about me. The person involved was influential.

The Holy Spirit directed me to return to my Bible, write down everything God had said about me and begin to affirm those things. I realised that the answer was not to keep proving that I was not the person someone had portrayed me to be, but to affirm who God says I am.

That was the idea behind Reworded. I returned to the Word of God to remake, remould and reinvent myself. I began putting the idea into practice when I met several young girls in a particular community who were involved in teenage pregnancies. I reoriented them and gave them reasons to believe they could build better lives. Some embraced the message and became testimonies to others.

Are you organising a gathering to advance this vision?

Yes. The first Reworded Conference will be held on November 21, 2026. However, it is not just a conference; it is an ecosystem. We also have a programme called Inner Architect, which we plan to launch early next year. It focuses on the inner mind.

I often describe the mind as a step-down transformer when it comes to building something. No matter what happens, it will always bring you to the level at which you can see. The conference theme is See Differently, Build Differently. It is intended to help people understand that their experiences do not have to define the limits of what they can achieve.

Two people can go through the same situation but emerge with different perspectives. I know the story of a man who died as an alcoholic. He had two sons. One stayed away from alcohol, while the other became an alcoholic. Their different responses were influenced by how they perceived their father’s lifestyle. Those perceptions eventually shaped how they built their lives.

Similarly, when I experienced someone spreading falsehoods about me, I eventually realised that the experience was something I could use to build my life rather than allow it to define me negatively.

Who are the guest speakers?

The speakers and panellists are experts in their respective fields. They are people I have seen making meaningful contributions to the ecosystem.

The theme of the panel session is Who Told You That? It is the second chapter of my book and seeks to address false beliefs that society has conditioned people to accept, which often become limitations.

We have five panellists who will discuss different subtopics. Essentially, the questions are designed to challenge conventional thinking about why we are where we are.

For instance, we will ask: Who told you that becoming a mother means putting your life on hold? Many women have paused their personal ambitions because they became mothers.

Another question is: Who told you that leaving means you have to lose the relationship? Some people believe that walking away from someone necessarily means ending the relationship, but it does not always have to be so.

We will also ask: Who told you that starting again means you have failed? Some people can pursue different interests and thrive in all of them, but society has made us believe that pursuing multiple interests is impracticable.

We need to challenge such beliefs. We are multi-hyphenated individuals, and the conference is not exclusively for women or any particular audience. It is for anyone building something. If God created you to build, then it is for you. Whether you are going through a season of transition in your relationship, business, career, ministry or academics, you are welcome.

You mentioned that Who Told You That? is the second chapter of your book. What is the book about?

The title is Before You Become Someone Else. I cannot recall exactly how I arrived at the title, but I know the inspiration was spiritual.

The book explores who God designed us to be. I believe we all have a design. When you buy a product, for instance, it comes with a manual. However, what most people do is ignore the manual until something goes wrong. That is when they return to it.

The same applies to our lives. God created us and gave us a manual, but we are often quick to say that we live in a modern world where everybody has their own truth. People are in a hurry to do whatever they want without necessarily considering whether they are doing the right thing. However, I care about the original design and what God has said.

Another part of the book discusses the idea of being Too Early, Too Late. Sometimes, people tell you that you are too early, while at other times, they say you are too late. But who has the authority to determine where you should be at a particular point in your life?

I agree that some opportunities have time limitations. For instance, you cannot become a professional footballer at 70, but there are other ways to pursue aspects of that dream.

Many successful business owners did not start early. I once came across an eight-year-old who wrote a book. She did not write it based on experience but as an expression of herself. So, what is wrong with me expressing myself as an author? Nobody owes you permission to pursue your dreams because you are supposedly too early or too late. That is the mindset I’m trying to build through this community.

What does the community stand to gain?

Reworded is an ecosystem with different components. We have the conference and the Reworded Room, which is our community.

The idea behind the Reworded Room is to bring women together in a private development space where we challenge existing beliefs, grow together and take action. It is a place where something must change within you.

Remember I mentioned being a brand strategist and how people approach me for different things? In the Reworded Room, I can respond to people’s questions based on their individual needs. If someone needs a therapist, we refer them to one. If the issue concerns branding, we can provide the necessary guidance.

Members will also have their thinking challenged every month. The Bible talks about renewing our minds; it does not say we should renew them just once.

I once saw a post that asked whether, when a fish is sick, you give the fish medication or put the medication in the water. The point is that your environment matters.

Membership of the Reworded Room runs for one year, with payment options available annually, quarterly or monthly to accommodate different financial capacities. Members can ask questions and participate in the community.

We also have Inner Architect, a six-week programme designed to challenge people’s beliefs and reshape their thinking.

What advice would you give people at different stages of their personal or professional journeys about overcoming challenges?

There are different stages in the process of building something. There are people at the foundational stage, those in the middle and those who have reached the accomplished stage.

Some people support your vision from the beginning. Those at the middle stage begin to support you when they see evidence of what you are building, while others only show up after you have achieved success.

Some people may see you at the beginning of a project but refuse to contribute or support you. They may even pretend not to notice your efforts. However, don’t write them off because they may eventually become the people who say, ‘We knew her when she started.’ They can testify to how your efforts paid off.

Above all, you must believe in your dreams. People must believe in their dreams. Otherwise, you risk becoming like the third servant in the biblical Parable of the Talents, whom his master called wicked for failing to use the talent entrusted to him.

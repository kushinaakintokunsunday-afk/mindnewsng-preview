2027: Federal polytechnic rectors pledge staff, students’ support for Tinubu’s re-election

From Juliana Taiwo-Obalonye, Abuja

Federal polytechnic rectors across Nigeria have pledged to mobilise staff and students of their institutions to support President Bola Tinubu’s re-election bid in 2027, citing reforms and interventions by his administration in the polytechnic sector.

The rectors, under the umbrella of the Committee of Federal Polytechnic Rectors (COFER), made the pledge on Wednesday when they visited the Secretary to the Government of the Federation (SGF), George Akume, in Abuja.

The committee, led by its Chairman and Rector of Auchi Polytechnic, Dr Salisu Shehu Umar, said its decision to support Tinubu’s re-election was in recognition of what it described as unprecedented government interventions aimed at repositioning polytechnics and addressing longstanding challenges in the sector.

Umar said the rectors had resolved to deploy their collective influence and institutional networks to mobilise support for the President ahead of the 2027 general elections.

He urged the SGF to convey their appreciation and pledge of solidarity to Tinubu, assuring him that the committee would mobilise its members, staff and students towards the success of the administration.

Responding, Akume, who was represented by the Permanent Secretary, General Services Office, Dr Ibrahim Abubakar Kana, commended the rectors for their support, describing technical and vocational education as indispensable to national development.

He said no country could achieve meaningful development without prioritising skills-based education, adding that the Federal Government remained committed to repositioning polytechnics to address Nigeria’s skills gap and strengthen its productive capacity.

Akume said the interventions through the Federal Ministry of Education and the National Board for Technical Education (NBTE) represented significant steps towards restoring the relevance of technical and vocational education.

He noted that Nigeria’s previous emphasis on certificate acquisition at the expense of practical and technical skills had contributed to some of the challenges confronting the education sector.

The SGF said the Tinubu administration was determined to build an inclusive society in which every institution and individual had equal opportunities to contribute to national development.

On the longstanding discrimination between Higher National Diploma (HND) and university degree holders, Akume assured the rectors that the Federal Government was committed to equity and fairness.

He also disclosed that the bill seeking to address the disparity, which is before the President, would receive due attention to facilitate its eventual assent.

Earlier, Umar said polytechnics had for years been treated as inferior to universities, but expressed optimism that the current administration’s policies and interventions would address the imbalance.

He identified the removal of the HND/BSc dichotomy as a major priority for the sector, noting that successive administrations since 1999 had made efforts to resolve the issue without a permanent solution.

The rector expressed hope that the ongoing legislative process would finally end the discrimination and enable polytechnics to fulfil their mandate of producing skilled manpower for national development.

He said the committee remained committed to advancing the interests of polytechnic education while supporting government policies aimed at strengthening the sector.

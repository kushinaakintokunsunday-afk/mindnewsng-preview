Xi, Trump, Putin Exchange Greetings Before Historic China Summit

The leaders of China, the United States and Russia exchanged greetings as they met in Shenzhen on Wednesday, a day before their history-making first formal trilateral summit in the Chinese city.

The agenda for Thursday’s meeting between presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin — which has raised fears of a carve-up into new “spheres of influence” — was unclear, however.

And the three-way meeting was set to last “no more than an hour”, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told Russian state media Wednesday.

When Trump touched down in Shenzhen, Xi became the first Chinese paramount leader in decades to welcome a foreign leader as he arrived, mirroring the US president’s gesture at Joint Base Andrews in September.

Trump descended the stairs from his plane to be met by Xi, as well as renditions of both countries’ national anthems and fighter jets that flew overhead and left colourful vapour trails.

The pair strolled down a red carpet lined with people waving pom-poms, brandishing Chinese and US national flags, and shouting: “A warm welcome to you!”

A motorcade of dark limousines later carried Trump into the city, where the streets had been cleared of traffic, an AFP reporter observed.

Xi, Trump, Putin and other leaders attending the broader Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit then gathered for a welcome dinner, and the three briefly exchanged greetings before a group photo.

“I look forward to an in-depth exchange of views with you on major issues concerning China-US relations, Asia-Pacific cooperation, and the global landscape, with the aim of advancing a constructive, strategically stable relationship between our two nations,” Xi told Trump, according to Chinese state news agency Xinhua.

Sanctions ‘Failed’: Putin

Washington had signalled that the Ukraine war — something Xi vehemently sees as not China’s problem — was not expected to be addressed in the trilateral meeting, before Trump said that it would.

The summit is expected to include discussions of the Iran war and nuclear disarmament, according to Washington, after a US-Russia bilateral treaty on atomic weapons expired in February.

Beijing has long maintained that the US and Russia should reduce their giant nuclear arsenals before it agrees to talks on its smaller stockpile.

Earlier on Wednesday, Putin struck a defiant tone on his war in Ukraine, saying Western sanctions provoked by the 2022 invasion had “failed”.

Addressing the APEC gathering, the Russian leader said he expected the trilateral summit to be “highly useful”.

“At a time when military-political risks are on the rise, what is desperately needed is unity rather than division… and dialogue rather than ultimatums,” Putin said.

The Russian leader has been largely shunned by the West since his 2022 offensive in Ukraine began, but has found an audience with Trump.

However, “full-scale” talks solely between Putin and Trump are unlikely to take place during their visits to China, Moscow said, according to Russian state media.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, also in Shenzhen, said sanctions would “continue to increase as long as this war goes on”.

Ukraine’s Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiga, speaking to reporters in Kyiv, said he hoped Xi and Trump would put “pressure on Russia to end the war”.

Kim Incoming?

Putin and Xi met, their fifth tete-a-tete this year, including one by video link. The two men have long touted their strong personal friendship.

Xi urged closer ties with Moscow, saying the nations should “leverage the mechanisms for exchanges between the two countries’ governments, legislative bodies, militaries and political parties”.

Trump came to China from the Philippines, where a day earlier he met fellow leaders of the Quad — Australia, Japan and India — for the first time in his second term.

The grouping is broadly aimed at counterbalancing China’s regional influence.

Trump has also floated the possibility of meeting Kim Jong Un in China, saying the North Korean leader “would like” to meet, although there has been no confirmation from Pyongyang.

Trump was in the Philippines for a summit of the 11-member Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) where there were protests against a US supply chain initiative.

The spectre of Trump’s ongoing tariff blitz has loomed large over his recent travels.

Chinese Premier Li Qiang on Monday slammed what he called a “countercurrent of unilateralism and protectionism” while touting Beijing’s own expanding trade with ASEAN.

On Tuesday, a combative Trump doubled down, saying the United States would catch China in rare-earths production “within 10 months”.

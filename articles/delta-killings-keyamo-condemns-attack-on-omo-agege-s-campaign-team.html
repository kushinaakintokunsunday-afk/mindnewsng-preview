Delta killings: Keyamo condemns attack on Omo-Agege’s campaign team

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 11h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, has condemned the attack on the campaign team of former Deputy Senate President, Ovie Omo-Age

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, has condemned the attack on the campaign team of former Deputy Senate President, Ovie Omo-Agege, in Delta State, which reportedly left four people dead and four others critically injured.

Keyamo described the incident as disturbing and called on security agencies to identify and arrest those responsible for the killings.

The attack occurred on Monday in the Uduere area of Ughelli North Local Government Area while Omo-Agege, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) candidate for Delta Central, was on a campaign tour of the community.

Sunny Areh, the former senator’s media adviser, said armed men opened fire on Omo-Agege’s entourage at close range as he addressed supporters. He added that Omo-Agege escaped unhurt.

Those reportedly killed in the attack were identified as Moses Anaughe and Akima Efemena Umukoro, while the injured included Evance Ivwurie, Chuks Erhire, Tobore Agba and Ayodo Wanogho.

In a statement issued on Monday, Keyamo linked the incident to what he described as an increasingly hostile political atmosphere in the state.

He also recalled the killing of a tricycle operator in a daylight attack in Asaba on November 7, urging law enforcement agencies to investigate both incidents.

“I call on the law enforcement agencies to fish out the perpetrators of these killings, both in Asaba and Ughelli, and bring them to book,” he said.

The minister said he had been engaging senior security officials over the rising incidents of political violence and inflammatory statements in Delta State.

He noted that political disagreements in the state had largely remained peaceful since 1999, expressing concern that political competition was now being accompanied by threats and violence.

“We are all brothers and sisters who went to the same primary, secondary and tertiary institutions, and we all know ourselves and our families so well. There is absolutely no need for a descent into violence,” Keyamo said.

He specifically criticised the use of the phrase “Rig and Die”, which he said had contributed to tensions in the state and elsewhere.

“Those who have promoted the mantra of ‘Rig and Die’ in Delta State and elsewhere should have a rethink, publicly withdraw it, and apologise for overheating the political temperature in the State and country,” he said.

Keyamo also warned political leaders against encouraging supporters to attack opponents or other citizens, insisting that the responsibility for dealing with offenders rested with law enforcement agencies.

“It is wrong to tell your supporters to kill other citizens under whatever guise. It smacks of desperation,” he added.

The minister appealed to Delta State Governor Sheriff Oborevwori to address residents and caution individuals making threats of violence.

He said the governor, as the state’s chief security officer, should urgently speak to the people and warn those threatening to cause violence.

The statement comes weeks after Omo-Agege made remarks about the 2027 general election, warning that anyone caught rigging the poll could be killed.

Speaking in October, the former deputy senate president said, “Anybody who rigs the election on that day, we will kill him! If you don’t value your life, come and rig the election.”

He also declared, “It is rig and die. We’re going to give them aura for aura. There’s no court this time. We’re going to fight.”

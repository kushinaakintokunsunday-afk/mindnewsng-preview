UK expands six-month paid jobs scheme for 90,000 youths

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A young person in the United Kingdom has said a government-backed employment scheme helped rebuild their confidence after a prolonged period of unemployment lef

The participant, who secured a placement at Kenny Waste through the Jobs Guarantee scheme, said the experience had improved their confidence in handling workplace responsibilities and preparing for career progression.

“Taking part in the Jobs Guarantee scheme has had a really positive impact on my confidence with finding and being in work,” the participant said.

“Before starting my placement at Kenny Waste, being out of work made me feel uncertain about my abilities in settling into a workplace and being able to handle the job role after being out of one for so long.

“Working in a supportive team and having reassurance from my brilliant co-workers helped me overcome this. I now feel much more confident in my abilities to handle the workload and progress more in the workplace.

“Even though I have only been in the role for 6 weeks, I feel like it was the perfect placement for me and have gained so much from it already.

“I have the Jobs Guarantee scheme to thank for this, as the support I had there was amazing and they really do find the right role for each person,” he added.

The account comes as the UK Government announced plans to expand the Jobs Guarantee scheme nationwide, offering fully funded six-month paid employment to more than 90,000 young people aged 18 to 24 who are claiming Universal Credit and looking for work.

Our correspondent on Monday retrieved this from the Department for Work and Pensions in a statement published on its website on November 14, saying the programme would be extended across England, Scotland and Wales from the end of November.

Under the scheme, the government will fund up to 25 hours of paid work weekly for six months at the applicable minimum wage. Jobs will be matched to participants’ skills, with tailored support provided before and during their employment.

The department said the expansion followed a successful rollout in six areas with some of the highest levels of youth unemployment and was expected to help more than 90,000 young people into work by March 2029.

It also announced that from April 2027, eligible young people who have been claiming Universal Credit and looking for work will be able to access the scheme after 12 months rather than the current 18 months.

Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden said the expansion was intended to ensure young people did not remain unemployed for extended periods without opportunities.

“The longer someone is out of work, the harder it is to get back in, and no young person should be left waiting on a scrapheap for an opportunity.

“That is why we are expanding our guaranteed jobs scheme across Great Britain and bringing it forward, so that help arrives when it matters most.

“Employers are crying out for talent, and there is a generation of talent ready to be unleashed. We are backing them with the support they need to build a fulfilling future,” he said.

More than 400 employers have supported the scheme, including Lidl, Leonardo Hotels and Boots.

Lidl GB’s Chief People Officer, Stephanie Rogers, said the company had already welcomed its first employees through the programme and planned to expand recruitment.

“Young people have huge ambition to offer, but too often struggle to find the right door to open – which is why initiatives such as the Youth Guarantee Scheme are so important.

“At Lidl GB, young people are a key part of our workforce and we believe in giving our colleagues a fair chance to build meaningful careers. We have already welcomed our first colleagues through the scheme, with plans already to expand recruitment further.

“Through this scheme, we are not just offering a placement, but industry-leading training and a supportive environment where young people develop transferable skills to secure their future. Alongside our fast-track interview pathways for the long-term unemployed and our apprenticeship programmes, we’re proud to offer a strong launching pad into stable, long-term careers in retail,” she said.

The government also said young people receiving the health element of Universal Credit would be able to participate in the scheme voluntarily from April 2027.

The Jobs Guarantee is part of a wider £2.5bn government investment aimed at supporting almost one million young people and creating 500,000 opportunities.

The expansion comes ahead of a review by Alan Milburn into the causes of rising youth inactivity, with the final report expected the following week.

The government said the scheme was part of its wider effort to ensure young people had access to employment, education and training opportunities.

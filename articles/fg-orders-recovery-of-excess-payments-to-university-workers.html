FG orders recovery of excess payments to university workers

By Punch Punch 6d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has directed federal universities, polytechnics and colleges of education to recover all overpayments arising from the implementation of

The government also capped the payment of excess workload allowance for academic and non-academic staff in federal universities at a maximum of 52 hours per month.

The directives were contained in a November 11, 2026, circular issued by the National Salaries, Incomes and Wages Commission and signed by its Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, Maj. Gen. Junaid S. Bindawa.

The circular, with reference number SWC/S/04/S.149/T/187, was addressed to the Chief of Staff to the President, Secretary to the Government of the Federation, Head of the Civil Service of the Federation, ministers, federal permanent secretaries and heads of government agencies, among others.

The commission said the clarification became necessary following several requests from universities, colleges of education and polytechnics seeking guidance on the implementation of remuneration packages contained in the 2025/2026 agreements between the Federal Government and tertiary education-based unions.

It stated, “Following several requests from Universities, Colleges of Education and Polytechnics on the urgent need for clarification on the implementation of remuneration packages contained in the 2025/2026 Agreements between the Federal Government of Nigeria and the Tertiary Education Institutions Based-Unions, the Commission wishes to clarify as follows.”

On the Consolidated Academic Tools Allowance, the commission said the payment was restricted to academic staff on the Consolidated University Academic Salary Structure and the Consolidated Polytechnics and Colleges of Education Salary Structure.

It explained that vice-chancellors, rectors and provosts were not entitled to the allowance because they were not on the specified salary structures.

The circular stated that the allowance did not apply to the heads of the institutions because they were covered by the Certain Political, Public and Judicial Officer Salaries (Salaries, Allowance, etc.) (Amendment) Act 2008.

It further excluded staff on the Consolidated Medical Salary Structure and associated medical allowances, citing ongoing negotiations between the Federal Government and health-sector unions.

The commission said, “This does not apply to staff on Consolidated Medical Salary Structures and associated medical allowances, as collective bargaining is ongoing between the FGN and Health Sector-Based Associations and Unions on the review of CONMESS and associated medical allowances.”

Similarly, it restricted the Consolidated Non-Teaching Tools Allowance to non-academic staff on the Consolidated Tertiary Institutions Salary Structure and Consolidated Tertiary Education Institutions Salary Structure.

The commission also excluded workers on the Consolidated Health Salary Structure and associated medical allowances.

It said, “This does not apply to staff on the Consolidated Health Salary Structure and associated medical allowances, as collective bargaining is ongoing between the FGN and Health Sector-Based Associations and Unions on the review of CONHESS and associated medical allowances.”

On the Excess Workload Allowance, the commission specifically directed that payments to members of ASUU and NASU in federal universities must not exceed 52 hours monthly.

It stated, “A maximum of 52 hours per month applies to the payment of Excess Workload Allowance for the Academic Staff Union of Universities and the Non-Academic Union of Educational and Associated Institutions in Federal Universities.”

The circular also addressed the 10 per cent Earned Academic Allowance, explaining that the provision in the revised salary structure of university academic staff was designed for mainstreaming and budgeting, with five per cent allocated to employees funded by the Federal Government and the remaining five per cent to those funded by university councils through internally generated revenue.

According to the commission, the provision was captured on page 12 of the agreement between the Federal Government and ASUU.

It stated, “The provision of 10% academic earned allowance (EAA) in the table of revised total salary of universities’ academic staff as approved on page 12 of the agreement between FGN and ASUU was for mainstreaming and budgeting – 5% for those funded by FGN and 5% for those funded by Councils through Internally-Generated Revenue.”

However, the commission warned that the implementation must follow its earlier circular of December 30, 2025, which outlined the categories of beneficiaries funded by the Federal Government and university councils.

It said, “However, the implementation of EAA should be in line with the Commission’s Circular No. SWC/S/04/149/IV/924 dated 30th December 2025, which clearly identified those funded by FGN and Councils, respectively.”

The commission subsequently directed all federal universities, polytechnics and colleges of education to comply with the clarifications and recover excess payments within the stipulated period.

It stated, “All Federal Universities, Colleges of Education and Polytechnics are to comply with these clarifications, and all overpayments should be recovered within three months.”

It added, “All enquiries relating to this circular should be directed to the Commission.”

The directive comes amid the Federal Government’s efforts to implement renegotiated remuneration agreements with academic and non-academic unions in the tertiary education sector after years of disputes over workers’ welfare, salaries, funding and conditions of service.

The Federal Government and ASUU signed their renegotiated 2025 agreement on December 23, 2025, following prolonged negotiations over the 2009 agreement. The deal introduced a 40 per cent upward review of university academic staff emoluments, effective January 1, 2026, alongside improved allowances and pension benefits.

In February 2026, the Minister of Education, Dr Tunji Alausa, announced the commencement of implementation of the 40 per cent increase in the Consolidated Academic Allowance, directing federal universities to reflect the approved adjustments in their payroll systems.

The Federal Government subsequently unveiled the 2026 agreements with academic and non-academic staff unions in October, announcing the payment of approved allowances to eligible personnel across universities, polytechnics and colleges of education, effective January 1, 2026.

The agreements cover unions including ASUU, the Academic Staff Union of Polytechnics, the Colleges of Education Academic Staff Union, the Senior Staff Association of Nigerian Universities and NASU. The government said the agreements were designed to improve staff welfare, strengthen industrial harmony and reduce disruptions to academic calendars.

Meanwhile, healthcare professionals in federal universities have threatened legal action against the National Salaries, Incomes and Wages Commission over the contents of the circular.

The affected medical doctors, nurses, pharmacists, medical laboratory scientists and allied health professionals, under the Coalition of Healthcare Professionals in Tertiary Education Institutions of Nigeria, described the circular as discriminatory and contrary to earlier government directives on the allowance.

The National Chairman and Coordinator of CHPTEIN, Musa Shehu, said in his reaction that the disputed circular, with reference number SWC/S/04/S.149/T/187, restricted CONTTA payments to employees on the Consolidated Tertiary Institutions Salary Structure, excluding those on the Consolidated Medical Salary Structure and Consolidated Health Salary Structure.

He noted that the original circulars, “referenced SWC/S/04/S.149/V/1067 for SSANU and SWC/S/04/S.149/IV/1071 for NASU, did not expressly exclude healthcare professionals earning salaries under CONMESS and CONHESS.”

He also maintained that the affected workers were non-teaching employees of federal universities and legitimate members of the two unions, with their check-off dues regularly deducted from their salaries.

“The Budget Office of the Federation calculated the financial implications of CONTTA based on the entire workforce of the Non-teaching staff on the payroll.”

CHPTEIN added that the ministry warned vice-chancellors in paragraph five of the circular that any deviation from the approved scope of funds released by the Federal Government would attract appropriate sanctions.

Questioning the basis for excluding healthcare professionals, the coalition asked why workers classified as non-teaching staff should be denied the allowance when the funds were reportedly calculated based on the entire non-teaching workforce.

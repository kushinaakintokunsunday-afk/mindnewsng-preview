Tinubu: Water stopped flowing while I was shaving in Villa

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 1d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

• Admits electricity, water supply challenges, promises solutions • Lagos must deliver maximum votes in 2027, says 200,000 out of 7m unacceptable From Juliana T

• Admits electricity, water supply challenges, promises solutions

• Lagos must deliver maximum votes in 2027, says 200,000 out of 7m unacceptable

From Juliana Taiwo-Obalonye, Abuja

President Bola Tinubu has revealed that water supply was interrupted at the Presidential Villa in Abuja while he was shaving last Wednesday, acknowledging that the country’s electricity and water supply challenges persist even within the seat of power.

The President, who promised to address the infrastructure deficiencies, said his administration remained determined to improve essential services across the country, insisting that Nigeria had the capacity to overcome its development challenges.

Tinubu made the disclosure on Sunday at the Tinubu Family Special Prayer for his second-term re-election bid, held at the Yoruba Tennis Club, Onikan, Lagos, where he also challenged leaders of the All Progressives Congress (APC) to intensify voter mobilisation ahead of the January 16, 2027 presidential election.

Recalling his experience at the Villa, the President said the interruption demonstrated the inconvenience Nigerians faced because of inadequate public utilities.

“Last Wednesday, I was shaving when the water supply went off in the Villa o. You would not know the inconvenience that caused there, too.

“We will correct all these problems. No light, no water, no electricity, no development. We have only just started. We can do it, and we will do it for Nigeria,” he said.

Tinubu maintained that the difficulties confronting the country were surmountable, citing ongoing infrastructure development in Abuja and Lagos as evidence of what could be achieved through determination and sustained investment.

“There is nothing we cannot achieve. Look at Abuja; we have moved mountains. In Lagos, we have tamed the Atlantic. In Abuja, we have moved mountains to make way for development.

“There is nothing we cannot achieve if we put our hearts into it,” he declared.

Turning to preparations for the 2027 general election, the President expressed dissatisfaction with the level of voter mobilisation in Lagos, warning that the APC could not afford to record low turnout in a state with millions of registered voters.

He challenged party leaders and supporters to work towards securing the maximum possible votes for his re-election, arguing that winning with only a fraction of the state’s registered voters would fall short of expectations.

“To me, Lagos is the big deal. How can you have seven million registered voters and produce only 100,000 or 200,000? That is failure.

“Even if you win and say a win is a win, no. You have to generate the maximum number of votes,” Tinubu said.

He urged party members to set aside personal grievances, reconcile with aggrieved supporters and encourage eligible voters to obtain their Permanent Voter Cards (PVCs), stressing that internal disagreements should not undermine the party’s electoral preparations.

“It is not about anger. Anger will not do it. You may be angry with Silva or Mulika. Put that anger aside and call Mulika to go and vote.

“Call Mulika and let her go and vote. We finish the anger that day, and you can go back to it the next day. Do you get my message? We must generate enough votes to overwhelm them,” he said.

The President also directed his Special Adviser on Political Matters, Ibrahim Masari, to begin reconciliation efforts among aggrieved APC members, starting from Katsina State before extending the exercise to Jigawa and other parts of the country.

Describing Masari as his political confidant and bulldozer, Tinubu said the party needed to resolve internal differences and restore unity ahead of the election.

“To my confidant and bulldozer, Alhaji Masari, go back home and reconcile with everybody. That is the order of the President, and when you finish, move to Jigawa and other places and reconcile them, beg them. Badaru, you get what I said. We have to reconcile. Every anger must evaporate,” he declared.

Tinubu recalled his political disagreements in 2021 and 2022, saying he had to put aside personal grievances when former President Muhammadu Buhari invited him to serve as coordinator and co-chairman of the campaign.

He said political differences should not prevent party members from working together towards common objectives, urging them to embrace forgiveness and reconciliation.

“The first thing we ask from Allah is forgiveness. If you cannot give it, why are you asking Allah to forgive you? What you want from Him, you must be able to give to humanity,” he said.

The President also thanked members of the Tinubu family for their prayers and support throughout his political career, recalling their involvement during his governorship contests in Lagos and subsequent presidential ambition.

He said their continued encouragement had accompanied him through successive political challenges and campaigns.

Reflecting on the economic difficulties experienced during the 2022 and 2023 political transition, Tinubu recalled the cash scarcity and fuel shortages that affected Nigerians, maintaining that the challenges confronting the country today were less severe than those of the previous period.

“The challenges we face today are much easier than those of yesterday, but we must not take that for granted,” he said.

He also urged young Nigerians to prepare for leadership responsibilities, stressing that the task of building and sustaining the country would increasingly rest on their shoulders.

The event was attended by Lagos State Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu; Kwara State Governor AbdulRahman AbdulRazaq; Lateef Femi Okunnu; Chief of Staff to the President, Femi Gbajabiamila; and members of the Tinubu family, among other dignitaries.

Germany Lists 31 Countries Whose Citizens Can Work Without A Visa - But Nigeria Is Not On The List - Check Below For Full Details

Germany has confirmed that citizens of 31 countries can move to the country and take up work without first obtaining a visa or a residence permit - a list that has sent Nigerian job seekers searching for their passports. But there is a catch: Nigeria is not one of the 31.

Who the 31 countries are

The 31 are made up of all 27 European Union member states, plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland.

  • EU (27): Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden
  • EEA, outside the EU: Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway
  • Plus: Switzerland

What this actually means

This is not a new programme and not a visa lottery. It is the European Union's long-standing free movement of persons rule: citizens of those countries can live, work, study and retire in Germany using only a valid national ID card or passport.

German authorities have simply restated the rule in updated official guidance, which is why it is being reported as a fresh announcement.

What it means for Nigerians

Nigeria is a third country under EU rules. That means a Nigerian cannot travel to Germany and start working on arrival. You need a residence title, and in most cases you must obtain a visa before you enter Germany and take up employment.

That does not close the door. Nigerians with the right qualifications still have several legal routes:

  • Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte): a points-based job-search permit that lets qualified non-EU nationals enter Germany for up to a year to look for work, based on qualifications, experience, language skills and age.
  • EU Blue Card: for graduates with a recognised degree and a qualifying job offer in Germany.
  • Skilled worker visa: for people with a recognised vocational qualification or degree and a concrete job offer.
  • Job seeker visa: for degree holders who want to travel to Germany to search for a job within a limited period.
  • Study or training visa: study, then use the post-study route to stay and work.

Beware of agents

The German mission in Nigeria does not require payment to any agent for visa processing, and job offers that ask for money upfront are a red flag. Applications go through the German embassy or consulate, and official information is published on make-it-in-germany.com and the mission's own website.

Bottom line: the 31-country list is real, but it is a European free-movement list - not a new open door for Nigeria. If Germany is your target, the Opportunity Card, the Blue Card and the skilled worker visa remain the routes worth preparing for, and they reward qualifications, language skills and genuine job offers.

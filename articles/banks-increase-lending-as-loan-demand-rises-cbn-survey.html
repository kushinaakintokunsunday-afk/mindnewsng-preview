Banks increase lending as loan demand rises – CBN survey

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigerian banks raised credit supply to households and businesses in the third quarter of 2026, as demand for secured, unsecured and corporate loans strengthened

The improvement was accompanied by higher loan approval rates and lower default rates across major lending categories, although borrowing costs remained elevated in some segments.

The findings are contained in the CBN’s Q3 2026 Credit Conditions Survey, which examined changes in banks’ lending behaviour, loan demand, approval rates and repayment performance.

For secured lending, the demand index stood at 25.6, driven largely by consumer loans to households, which recorded 42.9, and credit for house purchases at 28.0. Mortgage and remortgage lending recorded 12.0, while lending to small businesses by households declined to -19.7.

In the unsecured lending segment, the demand index stood at 7.2. Overdrafts and personal loans to households recorded an index of 24.7, while credit card lending contracted to -20.0.

Corporate lending recorded a demand index of 18.9, reflecting increased demand from small businesses, medium-sized private non-financial corporations and large companies, which recorded indices of 22.6, 18.3 and 20.7, respectively.

However, demand from other financial corporations weakened, with the index falling to -16.2.

Banks attributed increased demand for corporate loans mainly to inventory financing, balance sheet restructuring and capital investments, which recorded indices of 18.6, 2.5 and 0.7, respectively.

The survey also identified changing economic outlook, liquidity positions and market share objectives as factors influencing banks’ decisions to expand secured lending. For unsecured credit, market share objectives, economic outlook and the cost and availability of funds were among the major drivers.

The proportion of loan applications approved increased across secured, unsecured and corporate lending categories compared with the previous quarter.

However, the spread between lending rates and the Monetary Policy Rate widened in several categories, suggesting that improved credit availability has not necessarily translated into lower borrowing costs.

The spread for secured lending widened to -14.5 index points.

For corporate loans, the spreads widened to -13.6 index points for medium-sized private non-financial corporations, -12.2 for other financial corporations, -11.0 for small businesses and -10.3 for large private non-financial corporations.

The spread for unsecured lending remained unchanged during the quarter.

Banks also reported declining default rates across secured, unsecured and corporate lending, including loans to small businesses and medium-sized and large companies.

The improvement in banks’ lending conditions comes against the backdrop of declining outstanding credit to Nigeria’s private sector.

Credit to the private sector fell by N537.6bn to N83.93tn in September 2026 from N84.47tn in August, despite annual growth.

The decline followed the CBN’s September decision to cut the MPR by 350 basis points to 23 per cent, its largest reduction since 2006.

The Monetary Policy Committee also adjusted the interest rate corridor to +50 and -300 basis points around the benchmark rate, while retaining the Cash Reserve Requirement for commercial banks at 45 per cent.

Cost of funds remains a major challenge facing businesses. Nigerian manufacturers faced a 53 per cent increase in the average cost of bank credit between 2020 and 2025, underscoring the growing financial pressure on businesses seeking funds for production and expansion.

Data from the Manufacturers Association of Nigeria showed that manufacturers paid an average interest rate of 32.2 per cent on borrowed funds in 2025, compared with 21 per cent in 2020.

The difference represents an 11.2 percentage-point increase in five years, despite a moderation in borrowing costs last year.

Manufacturers’ average lending rate stood at 32.5 per cent in the first half of 2025 before declining to 31.8 per cent in the second half, bringing the full-year average to 32.2 per cent.

MAN said that commercial bank credit allocation to manufacturing contracted by N1.92tn, from N8.53tn in December 2024 to N6.61tn in December 2025.

This represents a significant year-on-year contraction of -22.5 per cent, which is particularly disturbing, given that manufacturing recorded one of the largest credit contractions among the top sectors, surpassed only by the General Services sector at -25 per cent.

Director-General of MAN, Segun Ajayi-Kadir, said this steep decline leaves manufacturing lagging far behind the extractive oil & gas industry’s N10.59tn and a booming finance sector’s N9.24tn, demonstrating a systemic preference for speculative and rent-seeking activities over tangible productivity.

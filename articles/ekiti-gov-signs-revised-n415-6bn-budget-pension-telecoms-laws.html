Ekiti gov signs revised N415.6bn budget, pension, telecoms laws

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The three laws presented to the governor for assent in his office in Ado-Ekiti by the Speaker, Ekiti State House of Assembly, Adeoye Aribasoye, are the 2026 Revised Appropriation Law; Ekiti State Pension Reform (Second Amendment) Law, 2026 (No. 4 of 2026); and Ekiti State Right of Way Law, 2026 (No. 5 of 2026).

The Special Adviser to the Governor on Media, Yinka Oyebode, said in a statement in Ado Ekiti, “The signing of the three laws underscores the Oyebanji administration’s commitment to strengthening public institutions, improving the regulatory environment for infrastructure development and ensuring effective management of public resources in the interest of the people”.

According to the statement, the legislation on the 2026 Revised Appropriation Law retains the state’s total budget size at ₦415,572,070,139.44, while adjusting the allocation between recurrent and capital expenditure. Under the revised budget, recurrent expenditure increases from ₦221,872,839,071.84 to ₦268,765,546,864.66, while capital expenditure decreases from ₦193,699,231,067.60 to ₦146,806,523,274.78.

It stated, “The adjustment represents a reallocation of resources from capital expenditure to recurrent expenditure to accommodate the state’s financial and operational obligations without increasing the overall budget size originally approved in December 2025.

“The revised appropriation is expected to sustain government operations while maintaining funding for infrastructure, social development, economic growth and institutional efficiency,” Oyebode stated.

He added that the Ekiti State Pension Reform (Second Amendment) Law, 2026 “is an amendment of the existing Pension Reform Law No. 3 of 2022 to formally accommodate the designation of a Director-General alongside the Permanent Secretary in the administrative and governance structure of the Commission responsible for the Contributory Pension Scheme in the state.

“The amendment also makes consequential adjustments to relevant provisions of the principal law, including the composition of the Commission’s Board, staff administration, remuneration and allowances among others,” he said.

He added that the Ekiti State Right of Way Law, 2026, established a regulatory framework for the approval and administration of permits for the installation and maintenance of telecommunications infrastructure in the state.

Oyebode stated, “The legislation fixes the maximum Right of Way fee at ₦145 per linear metre for ducts and cables and ₦700,000 per site for a Base Transceiver Station.

“It also requires telecommunications operators to comply with approved engineering standards, protect public safety and the environment, minimise damage to roads and public utilities, and restore affected infrastructure after installation.”

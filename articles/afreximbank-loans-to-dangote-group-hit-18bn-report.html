Afreximbank loans to Dangote Group hit $18bn – Report

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The African Export-Import Bank has disclosed that it has disbursed more than $18bn to the Dangote Group since 2015, making it one of the conglomerate’s largest

The financing covers different businesses within the Dangote Group, including support for the refinery’s construction, commencement of operations, crude oil procurement and debt refinancing.

According to documents obtained by The PUNCH from sources within Afreximbank, the institution has maintained a financing relationship with the conglomerate for more than a decade, providing funding across different stages of its industrial expansion.

One of the documents read, “Afreximbank is one of the largest providers of financing to the Dangote Group and has disbursed in excess of $18bn in the Group since 2015.”

The bank clarified that the $18bn represented financing disbursed to the entire Dangote Group rather than the petroleum refinery alone.

The documents showed that Afreximbank’s involvement in the refinery began during construction and subsequently expanded to working capital, crude oil procurement, financial advisory services and debt restructuring.

In 2018 and 2019, the bank provided $900m in construction financing, comprising a seven-year $650m term loan to Dangote Industries Limited and an additional $250m facility.

It subsequently extended other forms of financial support, including a $675m five-year corporate finance term loan to Dangote Cement Plc in 2024 and a $650m currency swap facility.

The document explained that the additional facilities were provided to support the wider conglomerate and accelerate the completion of the refinery.

Following the commencement of refining operations in February 2024, Afreximbank provided a $1bn working-capital facility to finance crude oil purchases, refined petroleum product offtake and operational continuity.

The bank also disclosed that it had issued letters of credit exceeding $6.8bn for crude oil procurement, demonstrating the substantial financing requirements associated with maintaining refinery operations.

The document read, “Following the commencement of refining operations, Afreximbank provided a $1bn working-capital facility to support crude-oil procurement, refined-product offtake and continuity of operations.”

Afreximbank also served as financial adviser on Nigeria’s naira-for-crude initiative, which was designed to facilitate crude oil purchases and refined petroleum product transactions in local currency.

The arrangement was intended to reduce dependence on foreign exchange in domestic petroleum transactions.

In March 2026, the bank further strengthened its financial involvement by underwriting $2.5bn of a $4bn five-year senior syndicated term loan for the refinery.

Afreximbank and Access Bank acted as co-mandated lead arrangers, with the continental trade finance institution accounting for the largest underwriting participation.

According to the documents, the refinancing arrangement was designed to consolidate existing obligations, improve the refinery’s capital structure and align its financing with operational requirements and expansion plans.

The bank explained that the transaction “consolidated existing financing, optimised the refinery’s capital structure and aligned its funding with its operational status and long-term growth plans.”

Beyond financing construction and operations, Afreximbank has developed trade finance arrangements to help African petroleum traders and buyers purchase refined products from the Dangote Refinery and other African producers.

The institution considers the refinery a strategic investment capable of reducing Africa’s dependence on imported petroleum products while promoting industrialisation and regional energy security.

The documents also disclosed that the refinery, originally designed to process 650,000 barrels of crude oil daily, had increased its operating capacity to approximately 700,000 barrels per day following operational improvements.

This expansion has strengthened its ability to supply Nigeria’s domestic market while exporting refined products to other countries.

Another document obtained from sources within the bank revealed that the refinery supplied more than 400,000 tonnes of aviation fuel to Europe in July 2026, representing approximately 20 per cent of the region’s jet fuel imports that month, according to market intelligence firm Kpler.

The shipments occurred amid disruptions to international petroleum supply routes associated with geopolitical tensions, particularly conflicts affecting Russia, Ukraine and the Middle East.

The bank noted that the refinery’s Atlantic location and ability to process predominantly Nigerian crude, alongside other Atlantic Basin supplies, provided an alternative source of refined products for international buyers.

It added that the refinery had helped Nigeria move beyond exporting crude oil and importing finished petroleum products towards domestic processing and exports of higher-value products.

The document explained, “The refinery has transformed Nigeria from a predominantly crude-exporting and refined-product-importing economy into a producer capable of exporting higher-value refined petroleum products.”

However, Afreximbank identified African markets as an important long-term commercial opportunity for the refinery, particularly countries that continue to import substantial quantities of petroleum products from Europe, Asia and the Middle East.

UniAbuja land cut approved by Wike’s predecessor – FCTA

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Capital Territory Administration has defended its decision to reduce the land allocated to the University of Abuja, saying the action was based on r

The FCTA said the committee, comprising representatives of the administration and the university, had recommended that the institution’s landholding of over 11,000 hectares be reduced to 100 hectares in line with the National Universities Commission’s recommended size for universities.

The administration’s position followed a protest by the university’s academic and non-academic staff, students and alumni on Monday over the reduction of its original 11,824.04 hectares of land to 4,685 hectares by the administration of the incumbent FCT Minister, Nyesom Wike.

In a statement issued on Monday by the minister’s Senior Special Assistant on Public Communications and Social Media, Lere Olayinka, the FCTA described the protest as misplaced, insisting that Wike merely implemented a decision taken by his predecessor.

According to the statement, the joint committee was inaugurated on November 10, 2016, to examine the university’s land size, encroachment by indigenous settlers, illegal developments around its Gwagwalada mini-campus and the issuance of title documents for the institution’s two campuses.

Olayinka disclosed that five of the committee’s 12 members were representatives of the university, namely Prof Otaki Alanana, Prof A. Mallam, Mohamed Kassim, A.E. Ripite and Sadiq O. Shuaibu.

He said the committee subsequently recommended that the university’s landholding of over 11,000 hectares was far beyond the size recommended by the NUC, adding that the 100-hectare recommendation was approved by Bello.

“It is on record that on November 10, 2016, the then FCT Minister inaugurated a Joint Committee of FCTA and UNIABUJA on Land and Sundry Issues. The committee was to look into the UNIABUJA land size vis encroachment by indigenous settlers, encroachment on the Gwagwalada Mini Campus land by illegal developers and issuance of titles for the two campuses of the university with their actual land size.

“The committee recommended, amongst others, that the land area of over 11,000 hectares of the main campus was far beyond the recommended size by the National University Commission (NUC), and that the recommended size of 100 hectares by the NUC be given to the university.

“Report of the committee was approved by the immediate past FCT Minister, Mohammed Musa Bello, and going by the committee’s recommendation, only 100 hectares of land ought to be given to UNIABUJA,” the statement partly read.

Olayinka, however, said Wike approved 4,519.80 hectares for the university, rather than the 100 hectares recommended by the committee.

It added that the retained land could accommodate more than 80,000 buildings, using a plot size of 500 square metres.

The FCTA also accused the university of allocating portions of its land to individuals and corporate organisations without consulting the administration, citing the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission as one of the beneficiaries.

The statement questioned the rationale behind the university’s insistence on retaining more than 11,000 hectares, asking what significant expansion the institution had undertaken in its 38 years of existence to justify such a land requirement.

“Questions to ask the protesters, particularly the Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU), Senior Staff Association of Nigerian Universities (SSANU), Non-Academic Staff Union of Educational and Associated Institutions (NASU), Students’ Union Government (SUG) and the university’s alumni association are; were they unaware that the university had representatives in the committee that recommended the reduction of the UNIABUJA land to 100 hectares in line with the NUC recommendation?

“Where in the civilised world is a university built on over 11,000 hectares of land, on which over 220,000 houses can be built at 500 square meters per plot?

“If the government that owns a university chooses to reduce the size of its land on which no structure has been built in the school’s 38 years of existence, what effect will that have on ASUU, SSANU and NASU? Will that affect the payment of their salaries? Will it affect the students in terms of their studies?,” Olayinka asked.

The media aide urged the academic unions to be honest and clarify their interest in a land dispute between the administration and the management.

The FCTA’s defence comes amid an escalating dispute between the administration and the university community over the ownership, control and proposed redistribution of portions of the institution’s land in Giri District, Abuja.

The controversy dates back to June 2025, when Wike announced that the administration had reclaimed about 7,000 hectares of land from the university, accusing the institution of laying claim to an extensive portion of government land without adequate documentation.

At the inauguration of access roads in Giri District, the minister directed relevant agencies to carve out about 4,000 hectares for the university, while the remaining portions would be made available for other developments.

Wike had argued that the university could not retain such an extensive landholding without developing it, particularly as the FCTA sought to open up the district for infrastructure and institutional development.

However, the university’s management and ASUU have maintained that the original land allocation was legitimate and necessary for the institution’s long-term development, including academic expansion, agricultural research and other infrastructure.

The university’s Pro-Chancellor, Senator Olanrewaju Tejuoso, had also argued that much of the land left for the institution comprised rocky hills, raising questions about the actual amount of land available for development.

The dispute has also taken a legal dimension, with the university instituting Suit No. FCT/HC/CV/3500/2026 before the High Court of the Federal Capital Territory, Maitama.

In the suit, Justice F.E. Messiri, on August 18, 2026, reportedly ordered parties to maintain the status quo ante bellum concerning Plot No. 1, Giri District, pending the determination of the substantive matter.

ASUU has subsequently accused the FCTA of violating the court order through alleged allocations of portions of the disputed land to private individuals and companies on September 26, 2026.

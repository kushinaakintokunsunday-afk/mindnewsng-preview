Nigeria’s mutual fund assets fall to N10.06tn

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria’s mutual fund assets fell by N36.48bn in the week ended 30 October, 2026, as losses in dollar funds outweighed gains in money market funds and other inv

Data from the Securities and Exchange Commission showed that the net asset value of collective investment schemes declined by 0.36 per cent to N10.06tn from N10.10tn recorded on 23 October.

The decline was mainly driven by dollar funds, which lost N77.59bn, representing a 4.26 per cent drop from N1.82tn to N1.74tn.

The loss in dollar funds was greater than the combined gains recorded by several other investment categories, pushing down the industry’s overall assets.

Dollar funds accounted for 17.34 per cent of total mutual fund assets during the period, making them the second-largest category after money market funds.

Money market funds, which continued to dominate the industry, gained N31.95bn, or 0.47 per cent, to N6.90tn from N6.86tn.

The category accounted for 68.55 per cent of total industry assets, showing that a large share of investors’ money remained concentrated in money market investments.

Together, money market and dollar funds controlled 85.89 per cent of Nigeria’s mutual fund industry.

The figures show that the performance of these two categories has a major influence on the overall direction of the market, as the decline in dollar funds outweighed the growth in money market funds.

Several other investment categories recorded increases during the week, although their gains were not enough to prevent an overall decline.

Shariah-compliant funds recorded the highest percentage growth among the major categories, rising by 1.57 per cent to N154.24bn.

Balanced funds gained 1.44 per cent to N196.57bn, while equity-based funds increased by 1.25 per cent to N239.76bn.

Bonds and fixed-income funds also recorded growth, rising by 0.44 per cent to N234.16bn.

Real estate investment trusts increased marginally by 0.03 per cent to N520.98bn, while specialised funds remained almost unchanged at N54.40bn after declining by 0.01 per cent.

Ethical funds fell by 0.78 per cent to N19.74bn, making them one of the few categories to record a decline.

Exchange-traded funds rose by 2.51 per cent to N34.26bn from N33.42bn.

Despite the weekly decline, Nigeria’s mutual fund industry recorded growth over the eight weeks to 30 October.

SEC data showed that total fund assets increased from N9.59tn on 4 September to N10.06tn on 30 October, representing an increase of N466.20bn, or 4.86 per cent.

Money market funds recorded strong growth over the period, while balanced funds also contributed to the increase in industry assets.

Dollar funds also remained above their September level despite the sharp decline recorded in the latest week.

However, the latest SEC figures do not show whether the fall in dollar funds resulted from investor withdrawals or changes in the value of their underlying investments.

This distinction is important because a mutual fund’s net asset value changes with new investments, withdrawals, investment income and movements in the prices of the assets it holds.

Dollar funds are also reported in naira, meaning exchange rate movements can affect their reported value even when the dollar value of their investments remains unchanged.

The weekly decline therefore does not necessarily mean investors withdrew N77.59bn from dollar funds.

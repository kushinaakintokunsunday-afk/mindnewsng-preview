Business leaders promote resilience, transformative leadership

By Punch Punch 4d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Top business leaders have urged Nigerians to embrace transformational and resilient leadership, stressing the need for individuals to make a positive difference

They said effective leadership goes beyond holding positions of authority, noting that leaders must demonstrate integrity, inspire others, build capable teams and remain focused on creating lasting impact.

They spoke at the Business Leaders Live2Lead Conference, themed ‘Vision to Execution’, hosted by L’ecole Consulting in partnership with Maxwell Leadership in Lagos on Tuesday.

The Managing Partner of L’ecole Consulting, Mrs Evbusogie Osojie, said leadership required individuals to identify ways to improve the lives of people around them and build a legacy through service and positive transformation.

Osojie explained that she conceived the conference to expose Nigerians to global leadership insights, following her participation in the John Maxwell Leadership Conference held in Orlando, Florida, in September, which motivated her to create the platform.

She noted that the celebration of Maxwell Leadership’s 50 years of global influence had strengthened the commitment of leadership coaches to promote values centred on service, personal growth and transformation.

“His DNA includes things like serving people, making people better versions of themselves, equipping people, giving them tools to be better leaders, living by example, having a positive attitude as a leader, and finally, becoming a catalyst for change and transformation,” Osojie said.

She challenged Nigerians in positions of responsibility to assess the impact of their leadership on their communities, workplaces and society.

“Our duty as a leader is to know how to make a difference in the lives of people. My thought was, what am I doing today to make a difference in the lives of people in my world? What is my legacy?” she asked.

Osojie stressed that leadership development must begin with personal discipline and the ability to lead oneself before attempting to lead others.

She said, “A leader must be self-led. We talk about being a great leader, but that great leadership starts from you being a better version of yourself. People don’t follow leaders who don’t know where they’re going.”

The L’ecole Consulting managing partner added that leaders needed clear visions, strategies and the competence to manage people and resources to translate their ideas into tangible results.

Similarly, the President of the Nigeria-British Chamber of Commerce and Industry, Abimbola Olashore, said business leaders needed integrity, a clear vision and the ability to build cohesive teams to navigate disruptions.

He noted that businesses must understand their purpose and remain committed to solving customers’ problems, adding that strong leadership and team cohesion would help organisations withstand internal and external challenges.

“Leadership is a journey, and you must have very strong leadership traits for you to succeed in the long run. Leadership must be based on integrity, must be based on the vision, and your ability to share that vision with a team that will execute what the business is set up to do,” Olashore said.

He urged emerging business leaders to prepare for disruptions, stressing that organisations with strong teams and a clear understanding of their customers’ needs would remain resilient.

The Managing Director of Optimus Bank, Ademola Odeyemi, also emphasised authenticity, competence and accountability as essential qualities of effective leadership.

He said leaders must earn people’s trust by demonstrating integrity and surrounding themselves with individuals who possess the right knowledge, temperament and experience.

“The most important thing for a leader is to be authentic and to be real. If you’re authentic, you give the best, and you give what you have. You cannot give what you don’t have,” Odeyemi said.

He warned against appointing people to leadership positions based on political considerations rather than competence, arguing that organisations needed capable individuals to deliver results.

Odeyemi also called for greater accountability in the public sector, urging Nigerians to become more knowledgeable about economic issues to make informed electoral choices.

“Don’t teach politicians economics, teach the electorate economics. When you teach the electorate what they need to know, when there’s a lot of political education, people understand what they should ask for, what they should expect, then they will vote the right person in,” he said.

Speaking on the banking sector, Odeyemi commended the Central Bank of Nigeria’s reforms, particularly the recapitalisation exercise, saying the measures had helped restore public confidence and strengthen the industry’s financial position.

He said sustained reforms and stronger regulatory supervision would support the banking sector’s capacity to finance economic growth.

Meanwhile, the Founder and Chief Executive Officer of Sochitel Group, Jerry Ejikeme, described resilience as the ability of business leaders to remain focused and committed to their goals despite challenges.

He said his company’s nearly two decades of operations in Africa’s financial technology sector had reinforced the importance of consistency and innovation in business leadership.

“Resilient leadership means staying the course, staying focused. Sochitel will be 20 years next year, during which we’ve been innovating and providing services to banks around Africa,” Ejikeme said.

He expressed optimism about the future of business leadership in Nigeria, particularly the growing contribution of young entrepreneurs and technology-driven businesses.

Ejikeme said Nigerian youths had demonstrated remarkable capacity to innovate and compete globally despite the country’s infrastructure challenges.

“I’m very excited about the young leaders in Nigeria. I’m very excited about innovation in Nigeria because, despite the infrastructural deficit challenges, the young people have gone digital,” he said.

He added that the widespread adoption of mobile technology had created new opportunities for businesses, allowing Nigerians to transact and connect with the rest of the world more easily.

The Sochitel chief executive described Nigerian youths’ entrepreneurial drive as one of the strongest across Africa, adding that their growing participation in technology and innovation offered significant prospects for the country’s business future.

NLC, TUC end public disagreement over N70,000 workers’ allowance

By Punch Punch 6d ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Nigeria Labour Congress and the Trade Union Congress of Nigeria have resolved their disagreement over the Federal Government’s N70,000 six-month transport a

The unions announced the resolution on Wednesday during a joint press conference in Abuja, saying they had agreed to put their differences behind them in the interest of Nigerian workers.

In a joint statement signed by NLC President, Joe Ajaero, and TUC President-General, Festus Osifo, the organisations acknowledged that their disagreement had played out publicly but stressed that their shared history and responsibility to workers were more important than institutional differences.

They described the dispute as a family misunderstanding, which was resolved amicably through the intervention of former leaders of both labour centres.

The statement read, “The unity of the Nigerian working class is far greater than any disagreement between leaderships. Our common struggle, shared history, and collective responsibility to defend workers’ rights must never be sacrificed on the altar of misunderstanding or institutional differences.”

The disagreement centred on the N70,000 transport allowance approved by President Bola Tinubu for workers across different levels of government for six months.

The dispute became public after Ajaero and Osifo separately appeared on Channels Television’s The Morning Brief, where they disagreed over which labour centre had the authority to negotiate with the Federal Government on behalf of workers.

Ajaero argued that the NLC could negotiate independently, relying on the principle of representation under the International Labour Organisation.

“TUC can negotiate along with NLC, but NLC can negotiate alone based on the principle of most workers’ representatives of the ILO”, the NLC President noted.

Osifo, however, described the N70,000 agreement announced by Ajaero as an aberration, arguing that the TUC was not involved in the negotiations.

He challenged the NLC president to produce the ILO regulation that permitted one labour centre to negotiate on behalf of the other.

He stated, “He should show me in the ILO Regulations where it says one party is going to negotiate without the other one being there or consulted. He should show me! He should show Nigerians!

“ILO doctrine is that when you have more affiliates, and you are going for one of their events, it is not correct that the most represented will go and negotiate on behalf of the other one when the unions have their own umbrella.

“There is nowhere in ILO regulations that says a trade centre cannot negotiate for its members. It is not there! It is just that if you are going to the ILO, it is tripartite- the labour, government and the employers.”

Osifo also argued that the NLC could not negotiate on behalf of workers represented by the TUC simply because it had more affiliated unions.

He added, “But what we are saying is that you have about 60 affiliates while we have about 40. You cannot! You cannot negotiate for a body that is represented, for a body that has a trade union.

“You cannot negotiate on their behalf! It is not there anywhere!

“Yes, if we go to negotiations- I sit down, you sit down, and we agree”.

Ajaero, in his response, argued that the Trade Union Amendment Act 2005 made union membership voluntary and allowed workers to choose the organisations they wished to join.

“The 2005 Trade Union Amendment Act made membership of trade unions voluntary.

“It is the worker who determines the union he belongs to.

“Section 40 of the Nigerian 1999 Constitution, as amended, was clear that every Nigerian worker has the right to belong to any trade union, political or religious institution of his choice, which gives every worker the latitude to belong to any union of their choice,” Ajaero stated.

He also questioned Osifo’s claim of having a substantial number of workers in the federal civil service, arguing that only one TUC-affiliated union belonged to the Joint National Public Service Negotiating Council.

According to Ajaero, the council comprises eight union bodies, seven of which are affiliated with the NLC. He further argued that the TUC-affiliated union had subjected its negotiating authority to the joint body.

Osifo maintained that the two labour centres needed to participate in negotiations where their respective members were concerned.

He stated, “In that case, the two bodies must come together. We are not contesting the fact that NLC has more affiliates.

“But what we are saying is that you have about 60 affiliates while we have about 40. You cannot! You cannot negotiate for a body that is represented, for a body that has a trade union.

“You cannot negotiate on their behalf! It is not there anywhere!

“Yes, if we go to negotiations- I sit down, you sit down, and we agree.”

The TUC leader also questioned the legitimacy of the meeting at which the transport allowance was agreed, saying it was not a meeting of the Joint National Public Service Negotiating Council.

“But that meeting that took place was not the Council meeting. The Minister is not a member of the Council. Ajaero is not a member of the Council. The Council even has a quorum and the number of persons that must be seated to form a quorum. But at the end of the day, that meeting was not a JNC meeting. The members of JNC were not there!

“Again, if you see the release that was sent out by those three bodies, you will discover that after the last minimum wage, we got about a 33 per cent increment, and how does that work? Because from N30,000, we got N70,000.

“What you are supposed to do is that JNC will have to sit down and apply consequential adjustments,” he asked.

Ajaero, on his part, said the NLC had engaged the Federal Government after issuing an ultimatum over the workers’ demands.

“We gave the government an ultimatum; we were supposed to start an action. The government asked for four days to meet with us, and we met, and they resumed sitting after four days.

“After the meeting, the 70k allowance came up.”

He added that demands for a new minimum wage could not be negotiated without the establishment of a formal negotiation committee and wider consultations involving labour, government and employers.

The resolution of the disagreement comes amid renewed discussions over workers’ pay, following the Federal Government’s approval of a committee to examine concerns raised by the TUC over a new national minimum wage.

The TUC Secretary-General, Comrade General N.A. Toro, disclosed the development in a statement on Tuesday after a meeting between the union and the Federal Government.

The meeting followed a letter dated October 8, 2026, titled “Request for an Urgent Meeting to Address Critical National Economic, Security and Labour Challenges,” submitted by the TUC.

The Secretary to the Government of the Federation, Senator George Akume, chaired the meeting, while the TUC delegation was led by Osifo.

Other members of the delegation included Toro, Deputy President Shehu Mohammed, Trustee Akintola Benjamin and Auditor II Kamal Ibrahim.

The Head of the Civil Service of the Federation and the Permanent Secretary in the Office of the Secretary to the Government of the Federation, Ibrahim Kana, also attended.

The TUC said it had rejected the proposed six-month transport allowance, insisting that workers should receive a salary increase while discussions on a new national minimum wage were being concluded.

“In the course of the deliberations, the TUC made its position unequivocally clear on the proposed ₦70,000 transport intervention for a period of six months, which it rejected, preferring instead a salary increase pending the commencement and conclusion of discussions on a new National Minimum Wage.

“This position, alongside the other demands and concerns raised by Congress, will form part of the issues to be thoroughly interrogated by the High-Powered Committee,” the statement read in part.

The union said the committee would assess the issues raised, examine steps already taken by the government and make recommendations on outstanding matters.

“The Committee will therefore undertake a comprehensive review and reconciliation of the issues, establish the extent of actions already taken by the Government, identify outstanding matters and make appropriate findings and recommendations,” the statement said in part.

The committee comprises three TUC representatives, alongside officials from the Office of the Secretary to the Government of the Federation, the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation, the National Salaries, Incomes and Wages Commission, and the Federal Ministry of Labour and Employment.

It is expected to begin work immediately and submit its report to the Secretary to the Government of the Federation within two weeks.

Unlicensed teachers face ban from classrooms by December 2027 —TRCN

From Fred Ezeh, Abuja

The Teachers Registration Council of Nigeria (TRCN) has issued a December 2027 deadline for all teachers across the country to obtain professional certification and practising licences, warning that those who fail to meet the requirements will no longer be permitted to teach in Nigerian schools.

The council also uncovered 250 forged professional certificates during a teacher recruitment exercise in Nasarawa State and more than 47 cases of impersonation during its recent qualifying examination in Taraba State, raising fresh concerns over the infiltration of the teaching profession by unqualified personnel and examination fraudsters.

Registrar and Chief Executive of TRCN, Ronke Soyombo, who disclosed this during an interaction with journalists in Abuja, said the council was intensifying its professionalisation programme to ensure that every teacher, whether in public or private schools, possessed the qualifications and credentials required to practise.

She said the certification requirement would apply to everyone seeking recognition as a professional teacher, irrespective of social or political status, revealing that the Deputy Governor of Adamawa State participated in the council’s recent Professional Qualifying Examination (PQE).

“In Adamawa State, for the first time, we actually saw the deputy governor of Adamawa State coming to sit for our exam. She sat for the exam last Friday.” Soyombo described the development as evidence of growing acceptance of professional certification and the need for everyone involved in teaching to meet the same standards.

“Every teacher must be certified, every teacher must be licensed before they can actually teach in our schools,” she declared.

Soyombo explained that while professional certification would be obtained once in a teacher’s lifetime, practising licences would be renewed every three years upon presentation of evidence of participation in Mandatory Continuous Professional Development (MCPD).

According to her, graduates of education programmes were now required to sit for the TRCN qualifying examination before completing their training, while teachers already in service must also undergo the examination to obtain professional recognition.

She acknowledged that compliance with licensing requirements remained low but said the council had introduced an accelerated professionalisation pathway to enable teachers without education degrees to acquire the necessary qualifications.

The registrar disclosed that participation in the qualifying examinations had increased considerably following the introduction of online registration and improvements in the council’s technological infrastructure.

She recalled that approximately 13,000 teachers participated in the examination when she assumed office in May 2025, but the figure rose to about 26,000 by November of the same year.

She added that more than two million teachers were currently registered on the council’s online platform, although many had yet to complete the professional certification and licensing processes.

As part of efforts to enforce compliance, Soyombo disclosed that TRCN was working towards making professional certification a major requirement for schools seeking to register candidates for National Examinations Council (NECO) examinations.

Under the proposed arrangement, schools would be expected to have at least 75 per cent of their teachers professionally certified before qualifying for examination registration.

She said the council had interfaced its website with those of the West African Examinations Council (WAEC) and NECO to facilitate verification of teachers’ credentials and strengthen enforcement.

On examination malpractice, Soyombo revealed that an invigilator in Taraba State detected more than 47 impersonators during the council’s recent Professional Qualifying Examination.

She said the discovery prompted additional measures to protect the integrity of the examination, including the use of candidates’ photographs, surveillance cameras and improved identity-verification technology.

According to her, the council had also upgraded its examination platform from a capacity of 120 candidates to 5,000 simultaneous users, although internet connectivity challenges affected the exercise in two states.

The registrar further disclosed that TRCN uncovered 250 fake professional certificates during a teacher recruitment exercise in Nasarawa State, describing the discovery as evidence of the activities of individuals producing and circulating forged teaching credentials.

She said a suspect identified as Godfrey was in custody over alleged involvement in the production of fake certificates, while the council was collaborating with the police to apprehend other suspects.

To curb certificate forgery and impersonation, Soyombo said TRCN had introduced Quick Response (QR) codes on professional certificates, enabling employers, school proprietors and other stakeholders to verify teachers’ credentials directly through the council’s website.

She explained that the measure would make it easier to identify forged documents and prevent unqualified individuals from gaining employment through fraudulent certification.

Soyombo also disclosed that a proposed amendment to the TRCN Act was before the National Assembly, with provisions intended to strengthen enforcement powers and review penalties considered inadequate for unlicensed teaching.

She, however, maintained that the ongoing legislative process would not derail the council’s professionalisation timetable.

“By 2027, end of the year, we expect everybody to be fully certified as teachers in Nigeria,” she declared.

The registrar said the reforms would also strengthen teacher induction, child protection and professional accountability, while ensuring that teachers continued to update their knowledge and skills throughout their careers.

She maintained that professional certification, periodic licence renewal and continuous training were essential to improving teaching standards, protecting pupils and restoring public confidence in the quality of education delivered in Nigerian schools.

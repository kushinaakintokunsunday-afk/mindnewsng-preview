Fani-Kayode’s ego trip to South Africa

By Punch Punch 5d ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The choleric man Nigeria appointed as its ambassador to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, had not even spent a full day at his duty post when he started breaking things apart. On Saturday, after the occasion when Fani-Kayode and 14 other ambassador-designates presented their letters of credence to President Cyril Ramaphosa, our man jumped on Twitter to rant about how the South African Presidency deliberately cut off the Nigerian delegation (him, actually) from the broadcast during the ceremony (it turns out that he got that detail wrong; it was the SABC that supposedly did). Before long, he had dragged the Nigerian High Commission in South Africa, the South African presidency, and the South African Broadcasting Corporation into an impolite exchange online and elsewhere. In his Twitter post, Fani-Kayode moaned that ‘some elements in South Africa do not want to hear what we have to say or see our faces, but we are here as long as we are welcome and have the confidence of President Ramaphosa, which I am sure we do.’ In that sentence alone, Fani-Kayode proved himself unfit for the exalted position to which Bola Tinubu, the renowned headhunter, promoted him.

First, you must be the most unthinking man in the world to imagine your actions endeared you to President Ramaphosa to the point that you would pit him against his people. Second, if they truly do not want to see or hear you, have you not justified their prejudice? If you want to act like an agbero, you could have done yourself a favour and waited until you returned to Lagos. Nigeria is not small among countries where our national representative will have a full-blown public meltdown simply because a South African media channel refused to platform him. Nigeria has many challenges, but what we do not lack by any measure are electronic media platforms to platform ourselves. If the situation were reversed, would a South African envoy to Nigeria have gone crazy because a Nigerian television station did not cover his speech? What is so earth-shaking about what you have to say that the world must stop to acknowledge you? By Fani-Kayode’s own admission, two Nigerian television stations were present at the event and recorded the ceremony. You could simply have shared their recording of the event instead of disgracing yourself. Also, if you truly suspect you were deliberately slighted, you could have raised the issue through official channels or back channels. Why yell like a college fresher who just discovered aluta? He has barely started at the job, and they have already censured him. That is not to Nigeria’s credit.

All this drama was Tinubu’s fault because, if he were the sort of person who recruits people based on merit and not because of how much they can worship his majestic ego, he should never have made a temperamental fiend like Fani-Kayode an ambassador. Yorubas popularly analogise character with smoke. They say you can try hard to bottle character, but it will seep out somehow and express its nature. That is why Fani-Kayode could not even wait to learn if his suspicions about SABC were valid or not before he started smoking out and polluting the air. He is irascible, the type of person who does not care for decorum and takes a fight public easily. Meanwhile, he does not go public to engage in a battle of ideas or take a principled stance, but merely to yell and insult. His domestic affairs are public entertainment because he is constantly getting into spats and shouting matches. Not too long ago, he and one of his wives took their domestic issues public, and they did not stop talking until they had shredded their mutual dignity. His wife blames his problems on his erectile dysfunction, and he calls her “crazy.”

The same Fani-Kayode once got into a Twitter spat with Jimoh Ibrahim, and running out of insults, began to spew homophobic insults. That alone should ordinarily disqualify him from public office if Tinubu took standards and accountability seriously. Who can forget how Fani-Kayode tore into a Daily Trust journalist, Eyo Charles, during a press briefing in Calabar, Cross River State? The journalist had simply done his job of asking him a pertinent question, only for Fani-Kayode to explode in rage. His mercurial nature extends to his political habits. He has a history of spouting tribalistic nonsense depending on which part of the political divide he finds himself. The same Fani-Kayode who labelled the APC with ethnic and religious slurs (he called them the party of the “janjaweed” and “the Taliban”) defected to join them once they won the 2015 election. Unstable as water, he will swing in any and every direction.

For someone emotionally dysregulated, lacking self-mastery, and too uncouth for civilised company like Fani-Kayode, a diplomatic assignment was tantamount to casting pearls before a swine. Even if he had to be compensated with an ambassadorial position, he should not have been assigned to South Africa, whose xenophobic citizens frequently threaten the welfare of Nigerians. The ambassador you send to a country like South Africa must be a person of great wisdom, who understands the art of diplomacy, is assertive, and level-headed without being a pushover. S/he must understand that it is not cowardice to approach an issue with diplomatic tact. Even if you are a registered aluta warrior, in that role you must set your insurgent tendencies aside; you do not alienate the people with whom you are supposed to work.

The Nigerian High Commission said Fani-Kayode was treated with ‘contempt and disdain, and by extension so was Nigeria.’ I disagree. Nigeria was disdained by its own thin-skinned representative. Nobody in Nigeria would have lost anything if Fani-Kayode had not dragged us into his ego fights. The shame is on him for using the Nigerian High Commission in South Africa to spill out his unedited guts online. Some characters, like Former Kaduna Central Senator Shehu Sani, applauded Fani-Kayode for reacting hormonally when he should have let reason prevail. They seem to have confused the nature of international diplomacy with the gbas gbos language of the internet in which they are well-versed. You break the walls of hostilities by building good relations, not by acting before thinking. Even if you must break protocols and take an issue public, it should be on substantial issues, not petty matters like whether they put you on their television or not. No ambassador who goes around howling for “respect” will be taken seriously; if he does not learn to discipline himself, he will simply be ignored.

Now that the news report of Fani-Kayode’s past confession of having been previously treated for mental illness is circulating around South Africa, I am reminded of Nwibe, a character from Chinua Achebe’s short story, The Madman. A prosperous man, Nwibe was about to take the prestigious Ozo title when he let his emotions get the better of him. He chases a madman into the marketplace, gets mistaken for a madman, and subsequently loses his social prestige. The story concludes that anytime Nwibe asks the elders about his pending Ozo title, they change the subject. Just like no one would take Nwibe seriously again, South Africans are reading the report, looking at an ambassador who typed furious things on Twitter, and thinking, “no wonder.” Sincerely, I am not so bothered that Fani-Kayode will be looked on by South Africans as an aláwòkù—a sick person who has been treated for their madness, but whose actions still contain the residues of lunacy. My rage is that their thoughts about his state of mental health will affect the thousands of Nigerians who live, study, and work in that country, and on whose behalf he is supposed to advocate.

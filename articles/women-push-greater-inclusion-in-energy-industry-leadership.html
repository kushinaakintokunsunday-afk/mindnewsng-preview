Women push greater inclusion in energy industry leadership

By Punch Punch 18 Nov 2026 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Women in Nigeria’s energy industry are demanding greater access to executive positions and business opportunities, as industry leaders face renewed calls to tra

The Women in Energy Network made the call at its just-concluded 2026 Gala and Awards in Lagos, where it launched a Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Benchmark Report in collaboration with KPMG to assess workplace inclusion and track companies’ progress through an annual scorecard.

The initiative is intended to move the industry beyond policy statements and public commitments by providing a framework for measuring how organisations create opportunities for women, particularly in senior leadership and across the energy value chain.

WIEN President, Eyono Fatayi-Williams, said the industry could no longer afford to repeat conversations about gender inclusion without demonstrating what had changed, arguing that qualified women needed fair opportunities to compete for leadership positions.

“As we celebrate six years, we also have to ask ourselves an important question: What does the next six years look like? Because we cannot spend the next six years having the same conversations we have had in the last six years,” Fatayi-Williams noted. “We cannot continue to talk about inclusion without asking what has changed.”

She said the network was not seeking preferential appointments but wanted companies to identify, develop and give qualified women opportunities to demonstrate their capabilities and compete for executive roles.

“For us at WIEN, diversity, equity and inclusion must mean something beyond a statement in a company policy or a paragraph in an annual sustainability report. It must be visible in the workplace. It must be reflected in the opportunities available to women. And it must be seen in the decisions organisations make every day,” she noted.

The push comes amid persistent gaps in women’s participation in the energy industry, particularly in technical positions, corporate leadership and access to contracts.

Figures cited by WIEN from the International Energy Agency indicate that women account for about 20 per cent of the global energy workforce, with their representation in senior executive positions remaining limited. In Nigeria, audits by the Nigeria Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative put women’s share of the extractive sector workforce at 18 per cent.

Industry surveys cited by the network also indicate that 64 per cent of women working in Nigeria’s renewable energy sector occupy non-STEM and administrative roles, while women-owned businesses account for fewer than two per cent of companies registered on the national Joint Qualification System for oil and gas contracting.

The figures highlight the gap between the industry’s growing emphasis on diversity and the participation of women in the technical, commercial and leadership roles that influence investment and business decisions.

Chairperson of WIEN’s Board of Trustees, Cecilia Umoren, said the network had expanded opportunities for women since its establishment six years ago, contributing to more appointments and a broader pipeline of women entering science, technology, engineering and mathematics fields.

However, she said the progress had not translated into parity, noting that men continued to occupy most leadership positions in the industry.

“In the six years that WIEN has been in existence, we have created more opportunities for women, resulting in numerous appointments and an expanded STEM pipeline,” Umoren noted.

“However, progress is not parity; we are ultimately aiming for true parity. We must acknowledge that most leadership positions today are occupied by men, meaning we cannot achieve our objectives without partnering with them to create access,” she added.

Umoren also urged women to create opportunities where existing structures did not provide them, saying, “If the room is not created for you, create your own room, set your own table, and be at your own table.”

The network’s Executive Secretary, Asanimo Omezi, said WIEN had grown from an initial group of 30 women to more than 1,000 members, with 30 corporate partners supporting its activities.

She said the DEI benchmark would provide a basis for assessing corporate commitments and making inclusion more visible and accountable.

“The newly unveiled DEI benchmark will move us from mere conversation to visible accountability,” Omezi noted.

She added that the growing membership demonstrated the presence of women with the knowledge and professional experience to take on more influential positions in the industry.

Delivering the keynote address, Chairman of the Sage Centre for Leadership Excellence, Mutiu Sunmonu, urged the network to sustain its advocacy for fair and equitable standards across the energy industry.

He said companies should maintain standards that allow qualified women to compete on a level playing field, without compromising the requirements for professional and executive positions.

The benchmark was unveiled following remarks by KPMG Partner, Bimpe Afolabi, as part of the network’s efforts to establish a framework for assessing workplace practices and measuring corporate inclusion progress.

WIEN said the initiative would help determine whether companies’ commitments were translating into tangible opportunities for women, including greater participation in executive roles and women-owned businesses’ access to opportunities across the energy value chain.

The event also featured an executive leadership fireside chat moderated by Rosario Osobase, which examined the role of corporate leadership and male allyship in addressing gender disparities.

The panel included Executive Director of Human Resources at ExxonMobil Affiliates in Nigeria, Emem Abraham; Managing Director of Oando Energy Resources, Alex Irune; Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of Seplat Energy, Effiong Okon; and Managing Director of Nigeria LNG Limited, Adeleye Falade.

Falade disclosed that women accounted for more than half of the applicants in NLNG’s recent recruitment exercise, while 50 per cent of the candidates selected for the positions were women.

The disclosure highlighted women’s willingness to participate in recruitment processes when opportunities arise, but the network is seeking broader inclusion beyond employment to cover promotions, technical positions and representation at senior decision-making levels.

The event also featured remarks from the Minister of Women Affairs, delivered by the Director-General and Chief Executive Officer of the Maryam Babangida National Centre for Women Development, Adedayo Benjamins-Laniyi.

The gala marked WIEN’s sixth anniversary and brought together policymakers, energy executives and professionals at the Metropolitan Club in Lagos.

Omezi said the organisation’s expansion from 30 founding members to more than 1,000 members and 30 corporate partners reflected the growing network of women working across the energy industry.

The ceremony also recognised individuals and organisations for their contributions to the sector through the Winning With Women in Energy Awards.

Folake Soetan received the WIEN Woman of the Year and Outstanding Woman in Power awards, while Moroti Adedoyin was recognised with the Lifetime Achievement Award.

Bassey Adie received the Female Energy Entrepreneur of the Year award and was also named Outstanding Woman in Midstream, Gas and Energy Logistics. Caroline Eboumbou was recognised for her contribution to renewable energy, while Funmi Ogbue was honoured for her advocacy for girls in science, technology, engineering and mathematics and the energy sector.

Maryamu Idris received recognition for leadership in the downstream sector, while Judith Ikwuwunna was named Emerging Woman in Energy, also called as the Rising Star award.

On the corporate side, ExxonMobil and Seplat Energy jointly received the Corporate Champion for Women-Owned Energy Businesses award. Seplat Energy also won the Excellence in Diverse Leadership award, while the Nigerian Content Development and Monitoring Board was named Public Sector Champion for Women in Energy.

Telmek Global Resources received the Employer of Choice award.

Five other industry professionals received recognition as Outstanding Women and Special WIEN Honourees. They included Audrey Joe-Ezigbo, Ibilola Amao, Elohor Aiboni, Olu Verheijen and Oritsemeyiwa Eyesan.

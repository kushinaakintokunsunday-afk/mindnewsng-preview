Oborevwori, Diri, governors attend Orodje’s 20th coronation anniversary

By Punch Punch 2d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Delta State Governor, Sheriff Oborevwori, on Saturday joined his Bayelsa State counterpart, Douye Diri, and other prominent Nigerians at the 20th coronation anniversary of the Orodje of Okpe Kingdom, Major General Felix Mujakperuo (retd.).

The ceremony, held at Udogun Hall in the monarch’s palace in Orerokpe, headquarters of Okpe Local Government Area of Delta State, attracted political leaders, traditional rulers, government officials and other dignitaries from within and outside the state.

The anniversary, themed “Two Decades of Peace and Progress in Okpe Kingdom,” marked 20 years of Mujakperuo’s reign, which began on July 29, 2006.

Oborevwori was accompanied by his wife, Deaconess Tobore Oborevwori, while Diri chaired the grand finale of the celebration. Also in attendance were Taraba State Governor, Agbu Kefas, and his Kogi State counterpart, Ahmed Usman Ododo.

Former Delta State governors, James Ibori and Senator Ifeanyi Okowa, were among the dignitaries, alongside the state’s Deputy Governor, Sir Monday Onyeme; the Olu of Warri, Ogiame Atuwatse III; and other first-class traditional rulers and monarchs from across Delta State and beyond.

Other government officials and political figures at the event included Senator Ede Dafinone; Speaker of the Delta State House of Assembly, Emomotimi Guwor; Deputy Speaker, Arthur Akpowowo; Secretary to the State Government, Dr Kingsley Emu; and Delta State All Progressives Congress Secretary, Chief Efe Uko.

Also present were Matthew Tonlagha and Mideno Bayagbon, among other prominent personalities.

Speaking at the event, Diri described traditional rulers as important partners in governance, noting that their roles were crucial to maintaining peace and stability in their communities.

Oborevwori, in his remarks, commended the monarch for his contributions to peace and development in Okpe Kingdom, while the Orodje expressed appreciation to the people and the state government for their support over the years.

The monarch also called on the people of Okpe Kingdom to preserve their cultural heritage, protect ancestral lands and encourage the younger generation to sustain the use of the Okpe language.

The ceremony also featured cultural dances and other side attractions.

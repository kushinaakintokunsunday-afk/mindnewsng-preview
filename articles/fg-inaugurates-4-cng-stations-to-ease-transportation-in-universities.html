FG inaugurates 4 CNG stations to ease transportation in universities

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The Federal Government has commissioned Compressed Natural Gas stations in four universities to ease transportation burden of students, staff and other members

The Federal Government has commissioned Compressed Natural Gas stations in four universities to ease transportation burden of students, staff and other members of the host communities.

The instutions include University of Uyo, Akwa Ibom state, University of Calabar, Cross River state , University of Nigeria, Enugu campus and Federal University of Petroleum Resources, Efurun in Delta state respectively.

‎Speaking during the commissioning ceremony at the University of Uyo , Minister of State, Petroleum Resources (Gas), Hon Ekperikpe Ekpo, said the initiative was a source of special pride to him as a project in his home state.
‎
‎Ekpo who commissioned other 3 CNG stations virtually , said the stations will give students, staff and surrounding communities access to cleaner and more economical mobility, while offering the institutions opportunity to build research, technical skills and local expertise in CNG technologies.
‎
‎“The benefits of adopting CNG for mobility are practical and immediate. CNG is generally cheaper than petrol and diesel, so vehicle owners, commercial drivers and transport operators spend less on fuel
‎
‎“For students, young people who earn a living from commercial transport, and vulnerable members of society who depend on public transport, these savings mean lower transport costs and more money kept in their pockets for education, enterprise, food and other household needs.”
‎
‎He commended president Bola Tinubu, whose Renewed Hope Agenda has placed natural gas at the heart of Nigeria’s economic renewal.
‎
‎“Under his watch, gas is no longer a resource we merely flare or export; it is a resource we are using to power our vehicles, create jobs and put money back in the pockets of our people,” he said.
‎
‎He said the President also approved the Special Palliative Relief on University Transportation Programme, in line with the 20 Universities CNG Ecosystem Initiative, to cushion the effect of petroleum subsidy reform on students and staff.
‎
‎He also commended the governors of Akwa Ibom, Cross River, Enugu and Delta states for deepening the penetration of natural gas in their states, and acknowledged the Minister of Education for supporting initiatives that connect educational institutions with national energy priorities.
‎
‎He appreciated the vice-chancellors and councils of the four universities, as well as host states and communities, for providing the enabling environment for the projects
‎
‎The Executive Director of Midstream and Downstream Gas Infrastructure Fund, Oluwole Adama, said the four newly commissioned Compressed Natural Gas stations in Uyo, Calabar, Enugu and Warri can fill about 540 vehicles every day.
‎
‎He said the figures are not just technical, but mean cheaper rides for students, lower costs for transport operators and a living classroom for young engineers.
‎
‎“For students, affordable and dependable transport means more than a cheaper ride. It can mean getting to lectures on time, spending less on daily travel and having more time and energy for learning,” he said.
‎
‎He added that more facilities will be executed, stressing that the facilities can also become living classrooms, offering young Nigerians practical exposure to gas operations, engineering, safety and maintenance.
‎
‎”About 50 more projects will be nearing commissioning by the end of this year. I urge transport operators and businesses to use the facilities judiciously. Our young Nigerians should embrace this opportunities in technical services, safety and maintenance”, he stated.
‎
‎On his part, the Chief Executive Officer of FEMADEC Energy Limited; Fola Akinnola, said the four newly commissioned Compressed Natural Gas daughter stations in universities will drastically cut daily fuel expenditure and bring clean, affordable fuel directly into the heart of tertiary institutions.
‎
‎Akinnola said establishing the daughter station within the University of Uyo alongside the three sister campuses going live across the South-South and South-East, was directly addressing the commuting challenges faced by students, lecturers, campus shuttle operators and surrounding host communities.
‎
‎He described the commissioning of four strategic university stations in a single day as a milestone made possible through deliberate, and high-impact collaboration.
‎
‎”On behalf of the board and management of FEMADEC Energy Limited, he thanked the Midstream and Downstream Gas Infrastructure Fund and the Presidential Initiative on Compressed Natural Gas and Electric Vehicles for their vision, catalytic funding and commitment to President Bola Tinubu’s Decade of Gas and clean mobility agenda”, Akinnola said.
‎

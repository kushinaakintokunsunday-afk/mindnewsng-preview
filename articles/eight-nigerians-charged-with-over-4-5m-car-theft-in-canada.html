Eight Nigerians charged with over $4.5m car theft in Canada

By Punch Punch 18 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Eight Nigerians have been charged alongside four other people in Canada over an alleged organised vehicle theft network linked to the recovery of 96 stolen vehi

Peel Regional Police in Ontario disclosed this in a statement published on its website on Monday, saying the 12 suspects were facing a total of 231 charges following an investigation into the theft and exportation of luxury sport utility vehicles, pickup trucks and other high-value vehicles.

The investigation, code-named Project Cadetta, began in March 2026 in partnership with the Canada Border Services Agency and initially focused on a warehouse in Brampton, Ontario, allegedly used to store stolen vehicles before they were shipped to West Africa through Canadian ports.

Police said the investigation later uncovered a wider criminal network operating across the Greater Toronto Area and neighbouring jurisdictions.

According to the police, five key suspects allegedly coordinated the theft, movement and exportation of the vehicles, while others supported the operation through loading sites, container movements and transportation of vehicles with altered identification numbers.

The 96 recovered vehicles were discovered concealed in shipping containers, the police said.

Those listed as Nigerian include Stanley Udeh, 46, of Brampton; Odewale Abraham, 57; Oluyemi Sodade, 51; Tosin Damilola Oyebade, 45; Oluwaseyi Olawale Bameke, 39; and Olushola Oke, 42, all of Toronto.

Others are Gbolahan Qudus Babatunde, 29, of Vaughan, and Kenneth Okafor, 58, listed as having no fixed address.

The other accused are Karamveer Singh, 26, of Brampton; Henrik Gancz, 57; Jaspreet Singh, 25, both listed as having no fixed address; and Darryl Davance, 25, of Saint-Jerome, Quebec.

The accused face various charges, including possession of property obtained by crime, trafficking in stolen goods valued at more than $5,000, forgery and uttering forged documents. One of them also faces a charge of dangerous operation of a motor vehicle.

Peel Regional Police said nine of the accused were charged and released with future court dates, while three were held for bail hearings and subsequently released.

The police added that some of the accused could face immigration enforcement action, including possible removal from Canada, after the conclusion of their criminal proceedings.

Commenting on the investigation, the Deputy Chief of Peel Regional Police, Nick Milinovich, said organised vehicle theft was connected to wider criminal activities.

“Organised auto theft is a predatory business model run by transnational syndicates that terrorise families and fund broader criminal enterprises. Through Project Cadetta, our Commercial Auto Crime Bureau and partners dismantled this supply chain from street-level thieves to international export brokers,” he said.

Ontario’s Solicitor General, Michael Kerzner, said the operation demonstrated the government’s commitment to tackling organised criminal networks.

He said, “Organised auto theft is a serious crime that threatens the safety and security of our communities, and our government will never back down from taking on the criminal networks responsible.”

The Canada Border Services Agency’s Acting Regional Director General for the Greater Toronto Area, Trishann Pascal, said the agency helped prevent stolen vehicles from reaching international markets through targeted container examinations and intelligence-sharing with law enforcement partners.

Peel Regional Police said the investigation remained ongoing, adding that further charges were anticipated and arrest warrants had been issued for additional individuals.

The police also reported that auto thefts across Peel Region had declined by more than 50 per cent since reaching a peak in 2023, with a further 10 per cent reduction recorded year-to-date in 2026.

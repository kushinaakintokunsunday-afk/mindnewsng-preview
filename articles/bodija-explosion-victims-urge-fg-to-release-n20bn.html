Bodija explosion: Victims urge FG to release N20bn

By Punch Punch 6d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG

The residents, under the aegis of the Bodija Residents Association, also called for a review of the compensation process, alleging that the amount paid by the state government was inadequate and that several victims were excluded from the exercise.

The Chairman of BERA, Muyiwa Bamgbose, made the appeal in an interview with The PUNCH on Tuesday, saying the N4.085bn disbursed to victims represented only about 40 per cent of the association’s assessment of their losses.

He said the association’s assessment, conducted with the assistance of professionals, put the total cost of restoring affected properties and replacing destroyed vehicles at more than N10bn.

Bamgbose explained that the government reduced the assessment after citing financial constraints, adding that some claims were excluded because the affected properties were considered to be outside the designated impact area.

He said, “Our total recommended sum was over N10bn, but when they came with the N4bn argument, it amounted to about N4bn. N4bn is 40 per cent of our estimate.”

He said the association was appealing to the Federal Government to release the outstanding N20bn from the N50bn intervention fund reportedly promised to Oyo State, following Makinde’s disclosure that the state had spent the N30bn already received.

“The governor has said that N30bn has been expended. We are appealing to the Federal Government to release the remaining N20bn and look at the records we drew up,” Bamgbose said.

He, however, stressed that BERA did not want the money paid into its account, proposing that the Federal Government could verify the association’s records and pay the affected residents directly.

“We don’t want the money to come to BERA. We can provide the Federal Government of the records of victims, and the money can be paid directly to the affected victims,” he said.

Bamgbose said the association was particularly concerned about residents whose properties were damaged but whose claims were either reduced or excluded during the compensation exercise.

He explained that the association had engaged professionals to assess the cost of restoring the affected buildings to their pre-explosion condition, including the replacement of vehicles destroyed in the incident.

According to him, the government subsequently applied a formula that reduced the assessed claims to about 40 per cent, while some categories of victims were removed from the final list.

He said, “What was estimated was 40 per cent of people’s requests. The government said it did not have money, that it was not compensation but support.”

The BERA chairman said the association wanted the government to revisit the initial assessment and apply the same formula in reverse to restore the amounts deducted from the claims of affected residents.

He added that some victims had already rebuilt their homes with personal resources and were hoping to receive additional financial support to offset their expenditure.

“Some who can afford to rebuild their houses have returned; they are hoping to get more money to offset some of the money that they spent,” he said.

Bamgbose also said the association had reported cases of residents who claimed not to have received the financial support to the state government for further consideration.

He maintained that although the state government’s road rehabilitation projects were commendable, the immediate priority should be the welfare and accommodation of those whose homes were affected by the explosion.

“Doing the road is good, but the priority should be for people to have habitable abodes,” he said.

The appeal comes after Makinde’s latest disclosure that his administration had spent the N30bn released to the state.

The governor made the disclosure during an interview on Arise Television on Friday, explaining that the state engaged the Nigerian Society of Engineers and professional valuers to assess properties affected by the explosion before making payments to their owners.

He said the money had initially remained in a bank account while his administration anticipated what he described as attempts to use the Federal Government’s intervention to blackmail him politically.

Makinde said, “We’ve spent the money. We got the Nigerian Society of Engineers, we got valuers, you know, they went out there, had the valuation of the properties, and then we gave money back to those people.”

He added that he subsequently directed the Commissioner for Finance to deploy the funds to projects after presenting his administration’s account of the transaction.

“I instructed the Commissioner for Finance, please put this money now on project because they’ve done what we thought they were going to do,” he said.

The fund generated controversy after former Ekiti State Governor Ayodele Fayose accused Makinde of failing to account for the Federal Government’s intervention money, alleging that the state received N50bn but disbursed only about N4.5bn to victims.

The Oyo State Government had previously maintained that the Federal Government’s intervention money remained untouched in a dedicated bank account.

However, it had disclosed that it spent N24.6bn from its own resources on infrastructure and other interventions following the explosion, in addition to the N4.085bn disbursed to affected residents.

Speaking with The PUNCH on Tuesday on Makinde’s latest disclosure, some of the Bodija victims expressed dissatisfaction with the amount received, describing the payments as inadequate in relation to the losses they suffered.

One of the victims, Mohammed Danesi, who said his residence was close to the epicentre of the explosion, described the state government’s payment scheme as inadequate.

He said he received about N1.4m despite losing virtually everything in the explosion, adding that he and his two children were admitted to a private hospital after the incident.

Danesi added that the family incurred medical expenses following the incident and was subsequently instructed to forward the bills to the state Ministry of Health for possible reimbursement.

“We complied, and nothing has been received to date,” he said.

Another victim, Dr Akinyele Coker, said the amount he received was about 40 per cent of the loss assessed by BERA.

Coker, 88, who lives on Aderinola Street, described himself as one of the fortunate victims because he had personal savings with which he could rebuild his property.

He said only two residents on his street had so far been able to rebuild their homes, adding that both had relied on personal resources to supplement the financial support received from the government.

Another victim, Toyin Olujumi, said she had received payment about two to three weeks earlier, although she stated that it was N15m less than the amount quoted by BERA as her losses.

She also claimed that the distribution was faulty, as some people whose houses were not affected received substantial amounts, while some tenants who suffered significant losses received relatively little.

Olujumi, however, said she appreciated the state government for providing some support to victims, adding that residents needed to move on with their lives after the tragedy.

The January 16, 2024, explosion in the Bodija area of Ibadan caused extensive damage to residential buildings and other properties, leaving several people dead and many others injured.

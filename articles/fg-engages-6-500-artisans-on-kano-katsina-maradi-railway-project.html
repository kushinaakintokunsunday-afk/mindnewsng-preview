FG engages 6,500 artisans on Kano-Katsina-Maradi railway project

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 3d ago 3 min read

The Federal Government has revealed that it has directly recruited, trained, and engaged more than 6,500 Nigerians in various construction and production activities on the ongoing Kano-Katsina-Maradi railway project.

The Permanent Secretary of the Federal Ministry of Transportation, Olufunso Adebiyi, announced this on Friday, during an inspection tour of the project at the Kazaure construction site in Jigawa State.

Adebiyi highlighted that approximately 6,500 workers were currently engaged on the project, with only about 200 of them being expatriates. He explained that the locally recruited workers would be retained as staff after the completion of the project to ensure that Nigerians continue to operate and manage the railway infrastructure.

He also highlighted the contributions of the Federal University of Transportation, Daura, Katsina State, in providing specialised training and developing local manpower to strengthen the country’s railway sector.

The Permanent Secretary announced that the first phase of the project, covering the Kano-Dawanau to Katsina section, was scheduled for commissioning in December 2026, while construction would continue on the remaining sections linking Katsina to Jibia and Maradi in the Niger Republic.

Providing an update on the level of progress, Adebiyi disclosed that approximately 650,000 of the 800,000 concrete railway sleepers required for the project had already been manufactured at the construction yard.

He added that most of the materials needed to complete the project, including rail tracks and reinforcement materials, were already available at the site, while the formwork required for construction activities had also been completed.

According to him, workers were currently engaged in assembling and coupling railway tracks towards Maradi, adding that the new executive rail line was expected to be completed within 10 days.

Adebiyi described the Kano-Maradi railway project, which spans approximately 400 kilometres, as significantly larger than the Abuja-Kaduna and Kaduna-Kano railway corridors, which cover 189 kilometres and 203 kilometres respectively.

On cross-border operations, he disclosed that the Federal Government had resumed discussions with the Niger Republic’s Ministry of Transportation to address security, immigration and customs requirements.

According to the Permanent Secretary, the discussions were aimed at facilitating the movement of construction equipment, machinery and personnel between the two countries to ensure the timely completion of the remaining sections of the railway project.

Meanwhile, the Vice-Chancellor of the Federal University of Transportation, Daura, Professor Umar Adam Katsayal, commended the Permanent Secretary and his entourage for the inspection visit.

Katsayal expressed optimism that the visit would further strengthen the Ministry of Transportation’s support for the university.

He added that the engagement would also promote stronger collaboration between the university and companies involved in railway construction and the provision of critical railway infrastructure, particularly signalling and communication systems.

Boat conveying mourners capsizes in Bayelsa, many feared missing

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

What was meant to be a gathering of family members, friends and sympathisers to bid a loved one farewell has turned into a tragedy in Bayelsa State, following t

The boat, popularly known as a “Love Boat”, reportedly sank near Oweikorogha Community on Friday while conveying passengers to Ukubie Community for the burial ceremony.

More than 30 persons were feared dead, while about 300 others were reportedly missing, according to a community source who witnessed the loading of passengers at the departure jetty.

The incident has plunged families and residents of the affected communities into anxiety, with relatives waiting for information about the whereabouts of their loved ones.

The community source said the vessel was carrying a large number of passengers when it left the jetty and capsized shortly after passing Oweikorogha Community.

He disclosed that some passengers had reportedly expressed concern over the number of people aboard the vessel and suggested that it should stop at Oweikorogha to reduce the passenger load.

But the suggestion was allegedly not taken, and the boat continued its journey before the tragedy occurred.

The source said local divers, volunteers and other public-spirited individuals had joined efforts to search for survivors and recover missing passengers.

The scale of the reported casualties has also raised concerns about the challenges of emergency response in the riverine communities, where access to affected locations can be difficult and rescue operations depend heavily on available boats, divers and local volunteers.

For residents of Southern Ijaw, waterways are essential transportation routes connecting communities separated by rivers and creeks.

Consequently, boats are routinely used to transport residents attending burials, weddings, religious activities, political gatherings and other social events.

Burial ceremonies in Ijaw communities often attract relatives and sympathisers from different settlements, increasing the demand for water transportation.

The latest incident has therefore renewed concerns about the safety of passengers travelling on commercial and privately operated boats, particularly when large numbers of people are travelling for communal events.

Former Chairman of the Maritime Union Workers in Bayelsa State, Mr Ipigansi Ogoniba, confirmed receiving information about the accident but said he was yet to ascertain the actual number of casualties.

The Police Public Relations Officer in the state, DSP Musa Mohammed, also confirmed the incident, saying the Commissioner of Police had directed the Marine Police Unit and the Divisional Police Officer covering the area under Oporoma Division to join the rescue operation.

“The incident actually happened. The Commissioner of Police has detailed the Marine Police Unit as well as the Divisional Police Officer covering that area, in Oporoma Division, so they are on ground for the rescue operations,” he said.

On the reported figures of 30 deaths and 300 missing persons, Mohammed said he was still making efforts to establish the actual number of casualties.

As families continue to await information, the tragedy has raised fresh questions about passenger safety, the enforcement of boat capacity limits and the availability of life-saving equipment on vessels operating across Bayelsa waterways.

The actual number of passengers aboard the boat, the number rescued and the confirmed casualty figures were yet to be established as of the time of filing this report.

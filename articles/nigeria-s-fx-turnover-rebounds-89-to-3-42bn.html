Nigeria’s FX turnover rebounds 89% to $3.42bn

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Trading activity in Nigeria’s foreign exchange market rebounded sharply in the week ended 13 November, 2026, as dollar transactions rose by 89.17 per cent to $3

The increase represents an additional $1.61bn in transactions, reversing much of the 51.29 per cent decline recorded in the preceding week, according to the latest data from FMDQ.

The report, which tracks transactions between authorised dealers and their clients, showed that average daily FX turnover increased to $684.18m from $361.67m.

Despite the recovery, weekly turnover remained below the $3.71bn recorded in the week ended 30 October, indicating continued fluctuations in activity at the official FX market.

Spot transactions accounted for most of the increase, rising by 81.73 per cent to $3.25bn during the review week from $1.79bn in the preceding week.

The segment contributed about $1.46bn, representing approximately 90.5 per cent of the total increase in weekly turnover.

Spot transactions accounted for 94.89 per cent of total FX trading, compared with 98.77 per cent in the previous week, while average daily turnover rose to $649.19m from $357.23m.

However, spot trading remained about 11 per cent below the $3.64bn recorded in the week ended 30 October.

FX forward transactions recorded a much faster percentage increase, although from a significantly smaller base.

Turnover in the derivatives segment jumped by 687.97 per cent to $174.93m from $22.20m, representing an increase of $152.73m.

Forward transactions accounted for 5.11 per cent of total turnover, up from 1.23 per cent in the preceding week.

Average daily derivatives turnover also climbed to $34.99m from $4.44m.

The latest figure was more than twice the $73.83m recorded in the week ended 30 October.

The reported figure represents forward contracts executed during the week, rather than the value of outstanding forward positions.

The recovery in FX turnover coincided with pressure on the naira at both the official and parallel markets.

At the official window, the naira weakened to N1,364 per dollar on 11 November from N1,349 recorded at the close of the previous week, before recovering slightly to N1,358.50 on 12 November, according to Central Bank of Nigeria data.

This represented a depreciation of about 0.7 per cent from the previous Friday’s closing rate.

In the parallel market, the local currency weakened to N1,445 per dollar on 12 November from N1,430 the previous day, widening the gap with the official rate to N86.50.

Meanwhile, interbank transactions at the Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market stood at about $644.85m on 12 November, while total NFEM turnover was approximately $397.54m on 11 November.

The latest figures highlight the volatility in Nigeria’s FX trading activity, with weekly turnover moving from $3.71bn to $1.81bn before recovering to $3.42bn over three weeks.

Scrap metal regulation won’t hurt legitimate traders – FG

By Punch Punch 48m ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has said its proposed regulation of the scrap metal industry is aimed at curbing the theft and vandalism of public infrastructure without

The Minister of Steel Development, Shuaibu Audu, gave the assurance on Tuesday in Kano during a stakeholders’ engagement on the proposed scrap metal regulations.

Represented by the Director of Steel and Non-ferrous Metals Department, Umar Tsoho, the minister, according to a statement issued by the Head, Press and Public Relations Department, Tine-Iulun, M.A., said the government wanted to establish an orderly business environment where legitimate scrap metal collectors, traders and recyclers could operate without providing cover for criminal activities.

He said, “The Federal Government’s intention is not to suppress legitimate commercial activities or undermine the livelihoods of Nigerians engaged in scrap metal collection and trading, but to create an orderly business environment where lawful operators can thrive while criminal activities are effectively addressed.”

The government’s position comes amid persistent concerns over the theft of metallic components from electricity installations, railway facilities, pipelines, manhole covers and other public assets, with the stolen materials often finding their way into the scrap metal market.

The proposed regulations are expected to introduce licensing and supervision of operators, improve the tracking of scrap metal transactions and establish standards for collection centres, aggregation facilities and recycling plants.

Audu described scrap metal as an important resource for Nigeria’s industrial development, particularly the steel, foundry and manufacturing sectors, noting that effective recovery and recycling could create jobs and support a circular economy.

He said Nigeria’s ambition to develop a competitive steel industry depended partly on an organised scrap metal value chain capable of providing reliable supplies of raw materials to legitimate industrial users.

According to him, materials must be lawfully sourced, safely handled, properly documented and supplied through transparent channels to support sustainable industrial production.

The minister, however, warned that an inadequately regulated industry could encourage infrastructure vandalism, unsafe dismantling practices, environmental degradation and significant economic losses.

He said the theft of metallic components from public infrastructure went beyond the loss of materials, as it disrupted essential services, threatened public safety, increased maintenance costs and undermined national development.

“The theft of electrical cables, railway components, manhole covers and other metallic installations goes beyond the loss of materials,” he said, stressing the need to prevent the illicit acquisition and trading of metals belonging to public institutions and protected industrial facilities.

Audu said the proposed framework would also accommodate small-scale operators through practical formalisation arrangements, with requirements proportionate to the size and risks of their businesses.

He explained that while small collectors should not be subjected to unnecessarily burdensome requirements, large-scale aggregation, processing and recycling facilities would be expected to meet appropriate regulatory and operational standards.

The Kano meeting followed the first phase of consultations held in Lagos between November 9 and 10, 2026, where stakeholders examined provisions covering licensing, classification of operators, traceability, collection and sorting facilities, environmental compliance, occupational safety, security and enforcement.

Audu said the Kano engagement was designed to expand consultations and incorporate regional and operational perspectives, particularly the experiences of industry stakeholders in northern Nigeria.

He urged participants to offer practical recommendations that would make the proposed regulations effective and implementable, assuring them that submissions from both meetings would be documented, reviewed and harmonised in line with the applicable legal framework.

The minister reaffirmed the ministry’s readiness to work with government agencies, industry operators, environmental authorities and security organisations to ensure effective implementation of the regulations.

In his remarks, the Permanent Secretary of the ministry, Amb. Nura Abba Rimi, represented by the Director of Metallurgical Inspectorate and Environmental Protection Department, Christopher Wanje, said the consultations were intended to ensure that the proposed framework reflected the realities and legitimate concerns of operators across the country.

He said the regulations would address weaknesses in the scrap metal value chain through licensing, compliance requirements, improved traceability, environmental safeguards and stronger monitoring and enforcement.

Rimi urged stakeholders to make further submissions on licensing arrangements, operator classification, security, environmental protection and inter-agency collaboration.

He assured them that their contributions would be carefully considered to produce a legally sound and practical regulatory framework.

Also speaking, the Acting Executive Secretary of the National Steel Council, Aminu Shehu, represented by the Head of Planning, Research and Statistics, Nana Firdausi Muhammad, expressed the council’s support for the initiative.

He said effective regulation of the industry could promote transparency, create employment and contribute to national economic growth.

Similarly, the Kano State Commandant of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps, Mohammed Hassan Agalama, who spoke on behalf of the Commandant-General, Prof. Ahmed Abubakar Audi, supported the proposed regulations.

He said proper management of the scrap metal industry was necessary to curb the theft and vandalism of critical national assets, including electricity installations, pipelines, railway facilities and government properties.

Agalama reaffirmed the corps’ readiness to collaborate with the Ministry of Steel Development and other relevant agencies to enforce the proposed framework.

2027: Lagos APC declares ‘Awa lo kan’ movement for Tinubu

The Lagos State chapter of the All Progressives Congress (APC) has declared that President Bola Tinubu’s re-election bid in 2027 has evolved from a personal political ambition into a collective national movement, describing the emerging slogan as “Awa lo kan” (It is our turn).

The party said the political movement that began with Tinubu’s “Emi lo kan” declaration ahead of the 2023 presidential election had grown into a shared determination among Nigerians to consolidate the administration’s reforms and sustain the country’s progress.

Lagos APC spokesman, Seye Oladejo, stated this on Monday, saying the shift reflected a defining moment in the country’s political evolution, where an individual aspiration had become a collective conviction.

According to him, the emerging “Awa lo kan” sentiment represents Nigerians’ desire to protect the gains of the Tinubu administration, expand opportunities and sustain the country’s journey towards prosperity.

The party said the movement was about ordinary Nigerians, including traders seeking a more predictable business environment, farmers demanding improved productivity, graduates looking for jobs, entrepreneurs seeking affordable credit and families hoping for a better standard of living.

“It is about a nation that cannot afford the luxury of abandoning its reform trajectory halfway through the journey,” the party stated.

The Lagos APC maintained that Tinubu had demonstrated the political will to confront structural challenges that previous administrations either postponed or approached with hesitation.

It cited the removal of the fuel subsidy, unification of the foreign exchange markets and investments in infrastructure, education, healthcare, agriculture and economic diversification as some of the administration’s major interventions.

However, the party acknowledged the difficulties associated with the reforms, saying responsible leadership required recognising both the present challenges and the opportunities being created for the future.

“The answer to transitional difficulties cannot be a reckless return to the policies and practices that contributed to the problems being collectively overcome,” it said.

On national unity, the APC said Tinubu’s administration had demonstrated commitment to inclusivity and representation across geopolitical zones, ethnic groups, religious affiliations, gender and social backgrounds.

It argued that the administration’s appointments, policies and development priorities reflected an understanding that Nigeria’s strength lay in its diversity and that national progress must benefit all sections of the country.

The party also pointed to efforts to strengthen the nation’s security architecture, saying interventions, operational improvements and increased investments in security capabilities represented steps in the right direction, despite continuing challenges in some parts of the country.

It accused the opposition of dismissing every improvement and amplifying setbacks for political purposes, insisting that national security should not be reduced to partisan propaganda.

The Lagos APC said the growing “Awa lo kan” campaign was not a call for complacency but an appeal for collective responsibility and commitment to sustaining the administration’s policies.

It added that the benefits of governance should be consolidated and expanded rather than sacrificed through what it described as political experimentation.

The party also criticised opposition groups, accusing them of being preoccupied with internal disagreements, ideological differences, leadership disputes and struggles for political relevance.

“Their inability to present a coherent, credible and sustainable alternative to the Renewed Hope Agenda makes their promises of an imaginary national rescue increasingly unconvincing,” it said.

The APC argued that Nigerians deserved more than political rhetoric and criticism, insisting that opposition parties should explain what they intended to do differently, how they planned to achieve it and why their proposals should be trusted over the current administration’s reforms.

It described the 2027 presidential election as a contest that would go beyond political parties and personalities, saying it would be a choice between continuity and uncertainty, consolidation and disruption, as well as sustained reforms and a return to what it called unsustainable policies.

“For the Lagos APC, the message is unmistakable: the Tinubu presidency has laid important foundations, and the responsibility before Nigerians is to ensure that these foundations translate into enduring national prosperity,” the statement said.

The party said the transition from “Emi lo kan” to “Awa lo kan” captured the evolution of Tinubu’s political project from an individual declaration into a collective aspiration centred on continuity, opportunity and shared destiny.

“In 2027, it is not simply about giving President Bola Ahmed Tinubu another term. It is about giving Nigeria the opportunity to complete the transformation that has begun.

“It is about our collective future. It is about our shared prosperity. It is about our common destiny. Awa lo kan! Nigeria’s turn to consolidate, progress and prosper,” the Lagos APC stated.

Messi Makes MLS History No Player Has Ever Achieved - Then Miami Nearly Threw It All Away

By Mindnewsng Mindnewsng 9 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Mindnewsng

Lionel Messi became the first man to win back-to-back MLS Golden Boots after 26 goals this season - yet his Inter Miami side blew a 3-0 lead at home on Decision

Lionel Messi has done something no player in MLS history has ever done - and, in the strangest twist, it happened on the very same afternoon his Inter Miami side nearly accomplished one of the ugliest collapses imaginable.

The Argentine superstar was confirmed as the winner of the 2026 MLS Golden Boot presented by Audi after finishing the regular season with a league-high 26 goals in 31 matches. Nobody in the 31-year history of the league has ever lifted the top-scorer award in consecutive seasons - until Messi did it.

The numbers that broke the record book

  • 26 goals - a league-high, with 25 from open play, plus 17 assists for a jaw-dropping 43 goal contributions, by far the most in MLS.
  • 0.84 goals per game - again the best in the league.
  • He finished clear of FC Dallas' Petar Musa (22 goals) and Nashville's Sam Surridge (21).
  • He is now only the sixth player in MLS history to win the Golden Boot more than once.
  • Across the 2025 and 2026 seasons combined, Messi scored a staggering 55 regular-season goals - second only to Josef Martínez's all-time two-year mark of 58.

And yet the day almost ended in disaster

Messi himself did not score on Decision Day, but he set up Miami's first two goals from corner kicks as Inter Miami raced into a 3-0 halftime lead over Charlotte FC at Nu Stadium: Ian Fray (14') pounced on Messi's swirling delivery, Luis Suárez (33') tapped in from close range after Germán Berterame flicked on another Messi corner - Suárez's 17th of the season, and Messi's 17th assist - and Rodrigo De Paul (42') hammered home a long-range rocket.

Then came the meltdown. Liel Abada scored twice (50', 55') in five crazy second-half minutes for Charlotte, and in the 90th minute Rodolfo Aloko slammed home the rebound after Tyger Smalls' shot came back off the post: 3-3. Messi even struck the post in stoppage time - while Aloko nearly won it at the other end seconds later.

Miami fans will remember the last time this happened: on May 2, Orlando City rallied from 3-0 down to win 4-3 at the same stadium.

Why Miami can still smile

The single point was enough to lock up second place in the Eastern Conference on 60 points after third-place New England fell 1-0 at Orlando. That means Inter Miami - and Messi - now have home-field advantage for the first two rounds of the playoffs, starting with a best-of-three first-round showdown against the Columbus Crew, with games running between November 20 and December 2.

One more twist from Decision Day: runaway leaders Nashville SC beat Cincinnati 2-0 to finish on a record 77 points, breaking the all-time MLS single-season record of 74 - which, remarkably, was set by Messi's Miami back in 2024.

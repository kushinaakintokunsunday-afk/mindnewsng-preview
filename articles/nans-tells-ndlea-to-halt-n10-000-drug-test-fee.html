NANS tells NDLEA to halt N10,000 drug test fee

…Warns against turning students into revenue sources

By Adeola Badru

The National Association of Nigerian Students (NANS) has urged the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) to immediately suspend the alleged N10,000 drug testing fee imposed on students, describing the charge as exploitative and an additional burden on families struggling with economic hardship.

The association specifically called on the NDLEA Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, Brigadier-General Mohamed Buba Marwa (retd.), to stop the enforcement and collection of the fee, arguing that students should not bear the financial cost of an initiative introduced by a government agency.

NANS, in a statement issued on Monday and signed by its President, Akinteye Babatunde Afeez, said it was unacceptable for a government agency to turn students into revenue sources through a programme purportedly designed to promote drug-free educational institutions.

While expressing support for genuine efforts to combat drug abuse and promote safer campuses, the association insisted that such initiatives must not come at the expense of students’ welfare.

“While we are not opposed to drug testing, the promotion of drug-free institutions or any genuine effort aimed at addressing drug abuse among students, we stand fiercely opposed to any attempt to transfer the financial burden of such exercises to Nigerian students, particularly when the initiative is being driven by the Agency itself and students have not independently requested these tests,” the statement said.

The student body expressed concern that the proposed charge would worsen the financial difficulties confronting students, who are already grappling with rising tuition fees, increasing living costs and other economic pressures.

“It is disheartening to see government agencies turn students into business ventures, exploit them and impose additional financial burdens on Nigerian students despite the government’s various interventions and policies aimed at making education accessible and improving students’ welfare,” it added.

NANS maintained that government agencies should fund programmes initiated in the discharge of their statutory responsibilities rather than transfer the financial burden to students.

It urged Marwa to intervene without delay and direct an immediate halt to the collection of the N10,000 fee, warning that it would resist any policy capable of subjecting students to avoidable financial exploitation.

“If the Agency must carry out its official responsibilities, it must do so without imposing any financial burden on Nigerian students.

“Charging students for an exercise initiated by the Agency itself is utterly unacceptable, and we therefore demand that the Chairman and Chief Executive Officer immediately halt this practice,” the association declared.

NANS further argued that students’ welfare should not become a revenue-generating opportunity for government institutions, particularly when many Nigerian families are struggling to meet the cost of education.

Reaffirming its support for initiatives aimed at creating safer learning environments and discouraging substance abuse among young people, the association said its objection was to the financial implications of the alleged policy and not the broader objective of tackling drug abuse.

It vowed to continue defending students’ interests and opposing measures that could impose additional financial pressure on them.

“As the apex student representative body, we stand firmly for affordable education and the protection of students’ welfare. We shall not fold our arms and watch students being subjected to avoidable financial exploitation,” it said.

The association urged the NDLEA leadership to prioritise students’ welfare and ensure that efforts to promote drug-free educational institutions did not become another source of financial hardship for Nigerian students.

14 arrested over $22.5m train cargo theft in US

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

No fewer than 14 people have been arrested in the United States on federal charges over an alleged $22.5 million cargo theft conspiracy. The defendants are accu

The defendants are accused of belonging to a theft ring that burgled at least 36 freight trains across Southern California, stealing merchandise including Nike sneakers, Milwaukee tools and electronics.

The stolen merchandise was allegedly sold through black-market dealers and social media platforms.

According to a tweet on Thursday by the First Assistant US Attorney for the Central District of California, Bill Essayli, the syndicate is led by 21-year-old Nathan Angel, of East Los Angeles.

Essayli said the suspects allegedly stopped trains, removed cargo and transported the stolen goods to storage locations before selling them.

“This morning we arrested a train robbery crew. Nathan Daniel Angel, 21, of East Los Angeles, and 13 co-defendants have been federally charged with orchestrating a $`22.5 million cargo theft conspiracy.

“The indictment alleges the crew burglarised at least 36 freight trains across Southern California, stealing Nike sneakers, Milwaukee tools, electronics, and other merchandise for black-market resale.

“The defendants allegedly stopped trains by cutting air hoses, boarded rail cars, moved the goods to stash houses, and sold them via fence networks and social media,” his tweet partly read.

The official lamented the volume of damage caused by train robberies as they involve valuable goods.

He also stated that if convicted, the syndicate could face long sentences.

“Train robberies cause enormous economic damage because of the valuable cargo involved.

“These defendants face long federal prison sentences if convicted. All are presumed innocent until proven guilty,” he wrote.

According to a report by NBC Los Angeles on Thursday, the case was investigated by operatives of the Homeland Security Investigations and the Los Angeles Police Department Commercial Crimes Bureau, among other agencies.

Other members of the syndicate include Angel Sanchez, 24; Ruben Banuelos, 26; Fredy Marquina, 25; Carlos Martinez, 23; and Jorge Espinoza, 36, among others.

The report noted that Angel and the other defendants coordinated the thefts through Instagram.

“The defendants allegedly used trucks and vans to take burglars to rail lines in Pico Rivera, Fullerton and elsewhere in Southern California, where they boarded freight trains.

“Prosecutors allege some conspirators cut train air hoses to force emergency stops, then loaded stolen cargo into vehicles and transported it to stash houses, including residences in Los Angeles.

“The indictment alleges that some of the stolen merchandise was sold to an unnamed Los Angeles-based sneaker and streetwear resale company that was not an authorised Nike reseller,” it stated.

Prosecutors said, according to the report, that between May and October 2025, Marquina and Martinez allegedly sold thousands of pairs of stolen Nike sneakers and other goods to the company, receiving about $1.08m.

The charges carry maximum penalties ranging from five years in federal prison for conspiracy to life imprisonment for violence against railroad carriers, the report stated.

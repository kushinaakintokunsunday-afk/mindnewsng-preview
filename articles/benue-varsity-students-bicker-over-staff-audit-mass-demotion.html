Benue varsity, students bicker over staff audit, mass demotion

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The university’s response followed a petition by the Benue Students Network to President Bola Tinubu on Monday, seeking an independent investigation into the audit exercise and its implications for employees.

In an open letter to the President on Monday, the group called for a review of the administration of the Vice-Chancellor, Prof. Francis Aba Uba, following concerns over the audit exercise.

The petition, signed by the network’s Coordinator, Yusuf Adakole, and Secretary, Tersoo Igbor, was copied to the Minister of Education, Dr Tunji Alausa; the Executive Secretary of the National Universities Commission, Professor Abdullahi Ribadu; and the National Security Adviser, Nuhu Ribadu.

The students said some employees who were recruited, confirmed and promoted through the university’s established procedures had, years later, been informed that their original appointments did not meet the requirements for their respective grades.

The group said, “Some senior professionals received letters in October 2026 stating that they lacked the requisite cognate experience for the grades at which they were initially employed, despite years of service and subsequent promotions.

“Some academic staff were also demoted or redeployed to non-teaching positions despite possessing advanced qualifications in related fields.

“A staff audit should strengthen an institution, not destroy the confidence of its workforce. It should correct established irregularities, not create new grievances.”

The students urged Tinubu to direct the NUC to investigate the audit, review affected appointments, promotions and reclassifications, ensure fair hearings for affected employees and examine the university’s administrative procedures.

“FUHSO belongs to the Nigerian people. Its future must not be jeopardised by administrative decisions that are perceived to be arbitrary, discriminatory or inconsistent with established procedures,” the group added.

The network also called for the protection of staff members who might cooperate with the proposed investigation against intimidation or victimisation.

However, in a statement issued on Monday by its Head of Information, Public Relations and Protocol Unit, Sunday Adakole Igah, the university said the exercise was initiated following complaints from members of its community about appointments and staff placements.

It said the Governing Council ordered a comprehensive audit to address concerns about inappropriate placement, unequal treatment and inconsistencies in career progression.

According to the management, the committee that conducted the exercise comprised experts from the NUC, the Office of the Head of Service, public stakeholders, legal practitioners and representatives of staff unions.

The statement said the committee examined employees’ records, including qualifications, credentials, cognate experience and existing placements, before submitting its findings and recommendations to the Governing Council.

It added that the Council subsequently referred the report to a subcommittee for further consideration, with emphasis on fairness, staff welfare and a humane approach to implementation.

The university stated, “No member of staff was dismissed as a result of the exercise. Employees found to have been placed above the levels justified by their qualifications and experience were retained at their existing grades without further progression pending regularisation, while those placed below their appropriate levels were upgraded.”

It said the measures were intended to correct disparities while protecting employment and ensuring fairness in staff placement and career progression.

“Management therefore rejects the characterisation of the exercise as a mass demotion of staff. It was a Council-directed process undertaken in response to concerns raised within the University community, with safeguards to ensure careful scrutiny, institutional oversight and opportunities for redress,” the statement added.

The university also said affected employees could submit additional documents, explanations and objections through its Staff Appeals and Regularisation Committee.

It said some employees had already submitted complaints for review and assured staff that each case would be considered on its merits.

“Where an error or inconsistency is established, the necessary correction will be made. The review process remains open to legitimate representations and is intended to ensure that no employee suffers an injustice,” it stated.

The management further said records relating to the audit were available for scrutiny through appropriate university channels, subject to confidentiality requirements and the protection of personal staff information.

It maintained that the exercise was guided by Federal Government regulations, approved Schemes of Service, NUC guidelines and the university’s Conditions of Service.

One hospitalised, 12,000 evacuated from Manchester concert

By Punch Punch 1d ago 3 min read

More than 12, 000 people were evacuated during US rapper Don Toliver’s concert at Co-op Live Arena in Manchester, United Kingdom, on Sunday, following reports of people in the crowd suffering effects from an unknown substance.

The incident prompted a major emergency response, with some concertgoers medically assessed at the scene.

Greater Manchester Police said in an updated statement on Monday, the officers were first notified of the incident by venue staff shortly after 10 pm on Sunday, November 22, 2026.

Assistant Chief Constable Steph Parker, who was Gold Commander for the incident, said one person had been taken to hospital, adding that emergency services worked with partner agencies to coordinate the response.

He noted that investigations have begun to unravel the nature of the substance.

The police chief also advised other attendees to seek medical assistance if they felt unwell.

Parker said, “Our priority was to ensure the safety of every person who was attending the concert. Several people were treated on site, and one has been taken to hospital at this time. Anyone who has left the event and since become unwell is advised to attend their local hospital.

“We understand the concern that this incident will have raised – particularly those with loved ones at the venue last night – and we’d like to thank everyone for their patience as we dealt with it.

“There were over 12,000 people in attendance during this concert who were safely escorted from the arena so they could make their way home.

“Our declaration of a major incident has allowed us to ensure emergency services and partner agencies can work together quickly and effectively to coordinate a safe response. We will now begin to fully understand what this substance was and how this incident came about.”

North West Ambulance Service on Sunday stated that the incident occurred at 10.25 pm, saying it deployed several emergency crews, including the Hazardous Area Response Team, to support the safe assessment and treatment of people at the scene.

The ambulance service said more than 50 people were assessed by clinicians, with some “reporting eye and respiratory symptoms.”

It noted, however, that decontamination is being carried out as a precaution and one patient has been taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment.

“The incident remains ongoing, and our teams remain at the scene assessing and caring for patients. We continue to work closely with emergency service partners while enquiries are carried out to establish what the substance was.

“Anyone at tonight’s event who has since noticed side effects or become ill should seek medical advice,” it advised.

The Standard quoted Co-op Live as saying, “Following an incident in the crowd at last night’s show, we are working closely with Greater Manchester Police to identify the cause and act appropriately.

“Our priority remains the safety and security of all visitors to Co-op Live, and we will provide more information once we have an update from GMP.”

Toliver, whose real name is Caleb Zackery Toliver, was performing in Manchester as part of his Nitrous tour. He is scheduled to perform at London’s O2 Arena on Monday and OVO Arena Wembley on Tuesday.

The Co-op Live Arena is scheduled to host US singer Phoebe Bridgers on Thursday.

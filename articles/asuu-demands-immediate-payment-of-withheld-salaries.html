ASUU demands immediate payment of withheld salaries

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Academic Staff Union of Universities has demanded the immediate payment of the remaining three-and-a-half months’ withheld salaries of its members, warning

The demand was contained in a press release signed by the ASUU President, Prof. Chris Piwuna, titled “Why Our Withheld Salaries Must Be Paid Now” and issued in Jos on Thursday night.

According to the union, it has continually demanded payment of the withheld salaries since the twilight of the Buhari-led government, following the 2022 nationwide strike occasioned by the government’s failure to address issues arising from the poor implementation and non-renegotiation of the 2009 FGN-ASUU Agreement.

“Agitation in this respect has witnessed some twists and turns under the current administration led by President Bola Ahmed Tinubu,” the ASUU president said.

The union recalled President Tinubu’s involvement in the matter before his emergence as president on May 29, 2023.

“He, like some other friends of the Buhari government, had intervened in the matter. Candidate Tinubu agreed with ASUU that the crisis could have been averted if steps had been taken early enough to address the union’s grievances and pledged his commitment to preventing future occurrence if he emerged victorious in the then-upcoming presidential election. He alluded to the principle of ‘CLEMENCY’ as a solution to the problem of withheld salaries in critical labour matters such as the case of ASUU and the Federal Government,” the statement said.

ASUU said its members heaved a sigh of relief when Tinubu won the presidential election, with the expectation of a full release of the seven-and-a-half months’ salaries withheld under President Muhammadu Buhari.

“However, the hope raised through his pre-election intervention now seems to have been dashed. Three-and-a-half years down the line, only the arrears of four months have been paid!” the union said.

The union said its engagement with the President’s Chief of Staff, Rt. Hon. Femi Gbajabiamila, earlier in the administration, met with a brick wall.

“He categorically declared that government was not ready to pay the withheld salaries in full. Surprisingly, the Hon. Minister of Education, Dr. Tunji Alausa, has continued to echo the Chief of Staff’s position on the matter, and all ASUU’s entreaties to have a direct audience with President Tinubu have been rebuffed,” ASUU said.

The union categorically restated its position that non-payment of the withheld salaries is an ill wind that blows nobody any good.

“Non-payment of the withheld salaries is an ill wind that blows nobody any good; it is an undeserved punishment for academics which could snowball into a bigger crisis for the Nigerian university system,” it said.

“Under both Presidents Buhari and Tinubu, our members’ rightful entitlements (salary shortfall, third-party deductions, promotion arrears, etc.) were delayed for months and years, for which no compensation for depreciated value and sundry losses were paid. When government ‘went on strike’ by withholding what is due to our members, we took our losses with equanimity and did not declare No Pay, No Work. Why then should government criminalise strike action through slavish enforcement of the so-called No Work, No Pay rule?”

ASUU said its members made enormous sacrifices to cover the lost grounds during the strike period to enable affected students to graduate and move on with their lives.

“At huge risks to their health, career progression and families, ASUU members forfeited their annual and research leaves, and doubled their efforts to regularise the academic calendar and stabilize the public university system. If the Tinubu government would not commend Nigerian academics for moderating their demands to guarantee industrial harmony in the universities, it should at least release what they are owed,” the statement said.

The union stressed that its members were not beggars.

“ASUU members are no beggars. Neither are they a conquered people or slaves. As free citizens, asking for what is due to us is not too much in a country that prides itself on a democratic pedestal. Our members cannot be cowed on account of platitudes. We reject the ‘No more ASUU strikes’ sloganeering and ‘Let’s keep them talking’ strategy. As scholars, we know our rights and we shall continue to demand for our dues – come rain, come shine. History teaches us that no government – no matter how powerful – has ever conquered a people of ideas for too long. The intellect prevails in the long run,” ASUU said.

The union disclosed that its National Executive Council (NEC), at its meeting of November 13 and 14, 2026, at the University of Abuja, called for the payment of the three-and-a-half months’ salaries without further delay in the spirit of fairness, equity, justice and respect for the worth of Nigerian academics.

“More specifically, NEC appealed to President Bola Ahmed Tinubu to resist the temptation of punishing ASUU members for honoring him with the suspension of the 2022 nationwide strike over which about half of the unpaid salaries are still withheld under his watch,” the statement said.

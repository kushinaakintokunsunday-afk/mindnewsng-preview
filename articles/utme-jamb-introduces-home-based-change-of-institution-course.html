UTME: JAMB introduces home-based change of institution, course

From Fred Ezeh, Abuja

Candidates seeking to change their preferred institutions or courses in the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME) can now do so from the comfort of their homes without visiting offices of the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) or its accredited centres.

The Board, which announced the introduction of the new service in Abuja, said the initiative would eliminate the stress, transportation costs and long waiting periods associated with physical visits to its offices, while making its services more accessible and convenient for candidates across the country.

It explained that candidates could now effect changes to their institutions and courses from any location with internet access through their registered JAMB profiles, describing the development as another milestone in its ongoing digital transformation.

In a statement issued by its Acting Director, Public Affairs and Protocol, Fabian Benjamin, JAMB said the initiative was part of efforts by its Registrar, Segun Aina, to reposition the Board as a service-oriented and digitally driven institution.

The Board said the arrangement aligned with the Federal Government’s Ease of Doing Business policy and global best practices in digital public service delivery, adding that it would significantly reduce congestion at JAMB offices and accredited facilities nationwide.

Explaining the procedure, JAMB said candidates were required to log into its e-Facility portal using their registered email addresses and passwords, select the Corrections and Uploads option on the sidebar menu and click on Change of Programme/Institution.

“Under the arrangement, candidates can process changes to their institutions and/or programmes directly through the JAMB e-Facility portal using their registered email addresses and passwords.

“To access the service, candidates are required to log into the portal, select the Corrections and Uploads option on the sidebar menu and click on Change of Programme/Institution.

“They are also required to select UTME, choose the relevant examination year and initiate payment for the service,” the statement explained.

JAMB said candidates would thereafter be redirected to the CREDO payment platform to complete their transactions using debit cards or bank transfers.

It advised candidates to return to their payment pages after completing the transactions to ensure that their payment status reflected “PAID (Not Used)” before proceeding with the changes.

“Thereafter, candidates can return to the Change of Institution/Course page, enter their preferred institution and/or programme details and validate the transaction using a One-Time Password (OTP) sent to their registered email addresses. Upon successful OTP validation, candidates can submit their changes by clicking the SAVE button,” the Board stated.

It explained that a confirmation message would appear once the change of institution or course had been successfully processed, after which candidates were expected to print their Change of Programme/Institution slips.

The Board also directed candidates to log into the Central Admission Processing System (CAPS) to confirm that their new institution and course selections had been updated.

JAMB, however, cautioned candidates against making errors in their selections, advising them to carefully review their preferred institutions and courses before submitting their requests.

It stressed the need for candidates to ensure that their payments had been confirmed and that their registered email accounts remained accessible for OTP authentication.

The Board also warned candidates against engaging intermediaries or paying individuals to undertake the exercise on their behalf, insisting that the process was designed to be completed personally through their individual profiles.

It urged candidates to protect their login credentials and OTPs against unauthorised access to prevent fraud and manipulation of their admission records.

Aina reiterated the Board’s commitment to deploying technology to simplify administrative procedures and ensure that candidates, irrespective of their locations, could access essential services without unnecessary difficulties.

He said the ultimate objective was to build a more responsive and efficient institution where candidates would spend less time seeking administrative services and devote more attention to pursuing their educational aspirations.

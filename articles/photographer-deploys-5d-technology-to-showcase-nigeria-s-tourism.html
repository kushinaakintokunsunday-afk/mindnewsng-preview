Photographer deploys 5D technology to showcase Nigeria’s tourism

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigerian documentary photographer, Dayo Adedayo, is deploying immersive 5D technology at the DAP Experience Centre in Lagos to showcase the country’s tourism as

The technology will enable visitors to take a virtual journey through different parts of Nigeria, including the Atlantic Ocean, the Niger Delta creeks, Onitsha and Lokoja, before returning to Lagos. The experience is designed to simulate environmental conditions such as rainfall, turbulence, sunrise and sunset without requiring visitors to wear virtual reality goggles.

Adedayo disclosed this during a media breakfast at the DAP Experience Centre in Lekki, Lagos, recently, where he explained that the facility would combine digital experiences with traditional photography to tell stories about Nigeria’s history, landscapes and cultural identity.

“Virtually, 5D. Not wearing goggles. That’s an old technology of 3D. This will be looking at everything live,” he said, describing the centre’s virtual experience.

The facility will also feature a large exhibition room where visitors can view different parts of Nigeria, as well as a documentary room for films relating to the exhibitions. Adedayo said the digital content would be regularly updated, allowing visitors to encounter different experiences on subsequent visits.

Beyond the digital installations, the centre will preserve the history of photography through an analogue section featuring a darkroom where visitors can observe how photographs are developed and printed.

Visitors will be photographed with both analogue and digital cameras. While digital images will be available immediately, the analogue photographs will be processed in their presence, giving them an opportunity to understand the traditional photographic process.

Adedayo said the facility was also intended to support education, with plans to attract school visits from across the country and expose younger generations to the evolution of photography.

The project reflects his broader ambition to use photography as a tool for documenting Nigeria’s heritage and promoting its tourism potential. He argued that the country’s visual stories could improve its international image and draw more visitors.

“If we tell our story, a very interesting visual story with our pictures, with our images, it will increase not just our GDP but also our image globally,” he said.

He also linked tourism to employment and spending across several sectors, including transportation, hospitality, food services and logistics, arguing that visitors generate economic activity beyond the destinations they visit.

“Tourism is the largest employer of labour,” Adedayo said, pointing to the range of businesses that benefit from the movement of visitors from airports to tourist destinations.

The centre is also expected to provide a commercial platform for photographers to exhibit and sell their work. Adedayo said he hoped to begin selling photographs before the end of 2026, with the centre earning a percentage from transactions.

He said the platform would give photographers an opportunity to generate income from their work and support further documentation of the country.

Adedayo has built an archive of more than four million photographs documenting Nigeria, which he said informed his decision to establish the centre as a place where his collection and other photographic works could be preserved and displayed.

The DAP Experience Centre combines the digital exhibition spaces with traditional photographic displays, positioning the project as both a tourism attraction and a platform for preserving the country’s visual history.

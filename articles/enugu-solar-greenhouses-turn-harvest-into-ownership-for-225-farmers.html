Enugu solar greenhouses turn harvest into ownership for 225 farmers

By Punch Punch 18 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Fifteen solar-powered greenhouses in Ugwuomu-Nike, Enugu East Local Government Area, are turning harvest income into ownership for 225 smallholder farmers under

Each greenhouse costs N10.5m, but farmers paid nothing upfront. The 225 members of the 15 cooperatives—including women, young people and persons with disabilities—will own their greenhouse once harvest income covers the cost.

The cluster was built and is run by Manamuz Electric, led by Chief Executive and Co-founder Uzochukwu Mbamalu, with partner firms.

It is backed by the Energising Women and Youth in Agri-Food Systems programme, a three-year partnership between the Global Energy Alliance for People and Planet and the Mastercard Foundation running in Nigeria and Ethiopia from 2024 to 2027, with RMI and All On as technical partners in Nigeria.

EWAS aims to create 17,000 jobs and support 136,000 livelihoods by 2027, with seven in 10 job openings intended for women. Manamuz has created more than 570 jobs so far.

Under the model, the state provides land, development partners fund construction, and Manamuz supplies technology and buyer links.

Each cooperative’s 15 members farm sell as a group, keep most of the income and use a smaller share to repay the greenhouse cost. Once paid off, the greenhouse belongs to the cooperative, and repayments fund the next group.

Mbamalu said, “Our agreement with the government is a state pilot of 150 greenhouses with two cold box stores. The government has already passed a policy to take this project to its 260 farm estates. Each estate covers 200 hectares, and 1 per cent of every estate will go to clean energy in agriculture. That means cold rooms linked to solar mini-grids and greenhouses. It will be youth-focused.”

The initiative comes as the Federal Government declared a state of emergency on food security and recapitalised the Bank of Agriculture with N1.5tn, with loans of up to N1m for young farmers.

The World Bank says Mission 300, a 2024 World Bank-African Development Bank initiative supported by the Rockefeller Foundation and Global Energy Alliance, will bring electricity to 17.5 million Nigerians by 2030.

On Monday, Chianda Njogu, who is Director for Powering Opportunity in Africa at the Global Energy Alliance, visited the site. He said, “The community is off-grid, but nothing is lacking here. There is power, there are sustainably run greenhouses, and there is water.

“The most important element is the people. You see it in how the greenhouses have been set up and in the participants’ attention to detail.”

Njogu said ending energy poverty means reaching unelectrified communities with new, better and affordable renewable energy. “The outcome that matters most to us is jobs and livelihoods, and the economic prosperity that follows for countries and communities,” he said, adding that scaling up will need private sector funding and investment, along with government funding.

Mrs Nkolika Ugokwe, President of the Donum De Caelo Cooperative, said the programme had made a difference to her group, whose first bell peppers should be ready by the end of November. Beekeeper Mrs Onyia Salome said, “As a beekeeper, I need a greenhouse where my bees can find nectar and give us honey. This is a big opportunity for me.”

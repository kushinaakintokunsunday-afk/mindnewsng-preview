Complainant seeks AGF takeover of fraud, assault suit as court fixes December 10 for hearing

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 20h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

Justice Hauwa Joseph Yilwa of the Federal High Court in Abuja on Monday adjourned the trial of Mrs Paula Mary Nnoruka and Muntari Yusuf over alleged fraud, assa

Justice Hauwa Joseph Yilwa of the Federal High Court in Abuja on Monday adjourned the trial of Mrs Paula Mary Nnoruka and Muntari Yusuf over alleged fraud, assault and property damage to December 10, after the prosecution indicated they wanted the charge withdrawn.

The development prompted the complainant, Chief Chuma Nnoruka, to seek the court’s attention, while his solicitors have separately petitioned the Attorney-General of the Federation (AGF) to take over the prosecution.

At Monday’s proceedings, prosecution counsel, J. I. Kpandegh, told the court that he had been instructed to withdraw the charge.

He explained that he could not file a formal notice of withdrawal because the instruction came during the weekend.

The judge questioned why the notice had not been filed and said any oral application for withdrawal would require the other parties to respond.

Kpandegh opted to make a formal application and requested a short adjournment.

Chief Nnoruka subsequently sought to address the court, but the judge explained that the criminal case was instituted by the Inspector-General of Police and that the complainant was not the prosecuting authority.

The judge advised him to liaise with the police over his concerns, adding that counsel appearing for him was only holding a watching brief and could not make submissions on the prosecution.

The matter was adjourned to December 10 for to enable the prosecuting counsel bring a formal application for withdrawal

Meanwhile, solicitors to two of the complainant’s clients have asked the AGF to assume responsibility for the prosecution, expressing concern that the case could be discontinued for reasons unrelated to the allegations before the court.

In a petition dated November 22 and received by the Federal Ministry of Justice on November 23, Law Corridor said its clients, Kamsiyonna Samuel Nnoruka and Tobenna Kerry Nnoruka, were apprehensive about the possible termination of the proceedings.

The firm alleged that a police officer in the Department of Legal Services, had urged the parties to pursue alternative dispute resolution over the disputed property.

According to the petition, the officer allegedly warned that refusal to participate in settlement could lead to a recommendation that the prosecution be classified as malicious and discontinued.

The solicitors asked the AGF to investigate the allegations, protect the integrity of the proceedings and take over the prosecution to its conclusion.

The claims concerning the police officer are contained in the petition and have not been independently established.

The case, Charge No. FHC/ABJ/CR/272/2025, was filed by the Inspector-General of Police against Nnoruka, Yusuf and others said to be at large.

The amended charge contains 13 counts, including allegations of fraudulent alteration of company records, forgery, conspiracy, assault, intimidation and damage to property.

The prosecution alleges that Nnoruka unlawfully altered the directorship and shareholding structure of West Wood International Services Ltd in 2019 and submitted disputed documents to the Corporate Affairs Commission.

Other counts concern alleged assaults and threats against Mrs Racheal Futuk and her children, as well as the alleged demolition of part of a property at No. 11 Rudolf Street, Maitama, Abuja.

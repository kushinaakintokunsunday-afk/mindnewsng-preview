2027: IPC urges journalists to shun sensational reporting

By Punch Punch 5d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Barely two months before the 2027 general elections, the International Press Centre has cautioned journalists against sensational reporting, warning that such p

Speaking in Yola on Wednesday during a training session for over 30 journalists in Adamawa State on election coverage, the Executive Director of IPC, Lanre Arogundade, urged Nigerian journalists to uphold professionalism, fairness and balanced reporting before, during and after the 2027 general elections.

Arogundade, who was represented by the Programme Manager, Mrs Stella Nwofia, said the training was coming at a crucial time as the general elections approached.

He said, “The media had a crucial role to play in providing credible, inclusive and public-interest information before, during and after elections.”

He called on media practitioners to change their approach to reporting political campaigns and party activities by focusing attention on issues that directly affected women, citizens and their expectations of their leaders.

He said, “The media played a critical role in shaping citizens’ opinions on elections. Most people vote based on the media reports they read or listen to before elections.”

He also emphasised the need to ensure that women, youths, persons with disabilities and other underrepresented groups were prominently featured in media reports.

Delivering a paper titled, “2027 General Election in the Context of Democratic and Accountability Reporting: Scanning the Electoral Environment and Reporting Citizens’ Priority Concerns,” Dr Shadrach Idi of the Department of Communication, Taraba State University, said journalists should focus on politicians’ campaign promises to hold them accountable.

He said reporting citizens’ priority concerns should be the hallmark of journalism, adding that such an approach would bring to the fore the challenges facing people in rural communities.

Also speaking, Dr Olusola Oludira, in a presentation titled, “Integrating Fact-Checking for Democratic and Accountability Reporting,” urged journalists to verify information presented to them before publishing reports.

He said misinformation, disinformation and malinformation were among the tools politicians used during election campaigns.

Oludira described data as a vital tool for journalists to report facts rather than fiction.

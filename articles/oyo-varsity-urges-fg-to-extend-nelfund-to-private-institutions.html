Oyo varsity urges FG to extend NELFUND to private institutions

By Punch Punch 7h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Vice-Chancellor of Ajayi Crowther University in Oyo State, Prof Ebunoluwa Oduwole, has urged the Federal Government to extend the Nigerian Education Loan Fu

She made the appeal during a press conference to herald the institution’s 18th Convocation Ceremony, held on the school premises in Oyo town on Monday.

Oduwole argued that the exclusion of students in private higher institutions from the scheme defeats its fundamental purpose, which is to make higher education accessible to qualified Nigerians irrespective of their financial circumstances.

The VC said, “Federal Government support for education should be about the Nigerian student, not about the ownership of institutions. If a student is a Nigerian, is admitted through the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board, and is studying in an accredited university in Nigeria, that student deserves support.

“I want to urge the Federal Government and NELFUND management to review the existing legal framework and establish a mechanism through which accredited private universities could participate in the student loan scheme.

“Students in private institutions in Nigeria are still waiting for the full outcome of this initiative,” she added.

PUNCH Online reports that NELFUND, established under the Student Loans (Access to Higher Education) Act, 2024, currently covers students in public universities, polytechnics and colleges of education, leaving out thousands of Nigerian students enrolled in private institutions.

Oduwole explained that the university’s contribution to the nation’s higher education system, as reflected in the number of graduands it would produce this year, demonstrated why private institutions should not be ignored in national funding policies.

Speaking on the convocation, the varsity don added, “A total of 4,104 graduands would be awarded degrees and other qualifications at the university’s 18th convocation. About 116 graduands would receive First Class degrees, 1,207 Second Class Upper degrees, 693 Second Class Lower degrees and 45 Third Class degrees at undergraduate level.

“At postgraduate level, 158 graduands would be awarded postgraduate diplomas, 1,798 master’s degrees and 87 doctoral degrees.”

Oduwole added that the figures demonstrated that private universities were shouldering a significant proportion of Nigeria’s higher education responsibilities and deserved corresponding recognition in the country’s educational funding framework.

She dismissed concerns about academic standards in private universities as an insufficient justification for excluding their students from NELFUND, insisting that institutions such as ACU were subject to the same regulatory standards as their public counterparts.

“If there are objections, if there are issues, there is a formal process we follow. When we talk about quality assurance, it means there is something you are assuring. We ensure that quality is not compromised. So when we say First Class, we mean First Class,” she said.

She stressed that the institution was fully accredited by the JAMB and deployed information and communications technology not only in teaching and learning but also in conducting credible examinations.

According to her, the university had adopted a blend of face-to-face and virtual learning to enhance its academic delivery and improve access to educational resources.

The VC said the National Universities Commission had approved new academic programmes for the university for the 2026/2027 academic session.

“The programmes include Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS), Pharmacy, B.Sc. Anatomy, B.Sc. Physiology, B.Sc. Pharmacology, B.Sc. Public Health, and B.Sc. Artificial Intelligence.

“These are not just numbers. They are a resounding vote of confidence in our academic and infrastructural standards. At Ajayi Crowther University, we don’t just raise scholars; we raise Godly Intellectuals,” Oduwole said.

Atiku’s candidacy: ADC files 17-ground appeal

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The African Democratic Congress has asked the Court of Appeal to restrain the Independent National Electoral Commission from altering its leadership records and

The party said it had filed a 17-ground appeal challenging the November 5, 2026 judgment, which it described as fundamentally flawed, arguing that the trial court lacked jurisdiction to entertain the leadership dispute.

In a statement issued on Tuesday by the Director of Strategic Communication, ADC Campaign Council, Phrank Shaibu, the party said it was also challenging an earlier ruling that granted Bala Gombe leave to amend his originating summons despite an unresolved objection to the court’s jurisdiction.

The ADC maintained that allowing INEC to implement the November 5 judgment before the Court of Appeal determines the issues raised could undermine its appeal and expose its candidates to uncertainty.

It therefore urged the appellate court to preserve the party’s existing leadership and candidate records until the legal dispute was resolved.

Shaibu said the 17 grounds of appeal questioned the jurisdiction of the Federal High Court, Gombe’s standing to institute the action, the assessment of disputed evidence and the orders made against the party.

“The 17 grounds challenge the foundation, findings and orders of Justice Lifu’s judgment. The ADC has asked the Court of Appeal to examine whether the trial court had jurisdiction, whether the claimant had standing, whether disputed evidence was properly assessed, and whether the sweeping orders made against the party can stand in law,” he said.

The party is also challenging the July 24, 2026 ruling in a separate appeal, arguing that the trial court granted Gombe permission to amend his originating summons while deferring its decision on the ADC’s preliminary objection to jurisdiction.

According to the statement, the party contends that the proposed amendment altered the description of parties and the capacity in which a defendant was sued without prior leave of court.

The appellant’s brief challenging that ruling was settled by Chief J.S. Okutepa (SAN), who leads the ADC’s legal team in the appeal.

The party is asking the Court of Appeal to set aside the amendment order and determine the outstanding objection to the trial court’s jurisdiction.

The ADC further argued that Justice Lifu proceeded to deliver judgment on a court process whose validity was already being challenged before the appellate court.

It expressed concern that INEC could act on the judgment before the appeal is determined, potentially creating a situation that would be difficult to reverse even if the appellate court eventually rules in its favour.

“INEC must not make changes that could preempt the court’s decision or put the party’s candidates in jeopardy while our applications are pending. We are asking the court to protect the subject of the appeal so that its eventual decision has practical effect,” Shaibu added.

The party said its decision to make the legal challenge public was intended to clarify the issues before the appellate court and assure its members and supporters that it would pursue the matter through lawful judicial processes.

The dispute centres on the leadership of the ADC and the legal validity of the processes through which its leadership records and related party affairs are being contested.

Justice Lifu’s November 5 judgment in the suit instituted by Gombe has thrown the party’s leadership structure and its preparations for the 2027 general elections into fresh legal uncertainty.

The latest appeal comes amid continuing litigation over the ADC’s leadership, with the party insisting that earlier court decisions recognising its leadership transition remain valid and have not been set aside.

The ADC said it would defend the rights of its members, its leadership and the decisions taken by the party, urging that no administrative action be taken on the basis of a contested judgment before the Court of Appeal has considered the issues raised.

The party’s applications and appeals are now before the appellate court for determination.

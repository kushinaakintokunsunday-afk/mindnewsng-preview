AI-generated fake content threatens national security – Ex-FPRO

By Punch Punch 3d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A former Force Public Relations Officer, Olumuyiwa Adejobi, has warned that the misuse of artificial intelligence and social media to spread fake news poses a g

Adejobi, a Deputy Commissioner of Police in charge of Operations at the Delta State Police Command, said fabricated images, videos, audio recordings and narratives could mislead the public, provoke hostility, incite violence and undermine confidence in public institutions.

He gave the warning in a statement titled, “The Palongo Dance of Social Media Palava,” issued in Lagos on Saturday, urging Nigerians to exercise caution and communicate responsibly on digital platforms.

The former police spokesperson expressed concern about the growing ability of artificial intelligence to make people appear to have said or done things they never did.

According to him, such fabricated content could have serious consequences for public safety and national security.

“Fabricated content can provoke hostility, incite violence, create panic and undermine public confidence in institutions,” he said.

Adejobi also blamed the monetisation of social media for encouraging some content creators to prioritise views, engagement and financial rewards over accuracy and responsibility.

He said some influencers resorted to sensational headlines, unverified allegations and provocative publications without considering the consequences for individuals, institutions and the country.

“Freedom of expression must be exercised responsibly and should not be used to fabricate information, destroy reputations, incite violence or deliberately mislead the public,” he said.

He urged Nigerians to verify information before believing, sharing or reposting it, describing fact-checking as a civic responsibility in the digital age.

Adejobi commended the Inspector-General of Police, Olatunji Disu, for recently urging the public to remain vigilant against fake news and irresponsible information dissemination.

He also acknowledged the efforts of former Minister of Information and National Orientation, Lai Mohammed, and the incumbent minister, Mohammed Idris, in drawing attention to the dangers of misinformation and disinformation.

The former police spokesman called for sustained public enlightenment, strategic communication, institutional collaboration and appropriate regulatory measures to address the problem.

He, however, warned against allowing regulation to undermine constitutionally guaranteed freedoms, stressing that legitimate criticism and dissent must be distinguished from deliberate fabrication, impersonation and incitement.

Adejobi advocated a whole-of-society approach involving government institutions, security agencies, media organisations, technology companies, professional bodies, traditional and religious leaders, civil society groups and citizens.

Recalling his personal experience with misinformation and social media manipulation, he said false narratives could cause emotional and reputational damage that might take years to repair.

He warned that fabricated stories could be created within seconds, while the damage they caused could persist for years.

Adejobi urged Nigerians to promote digital literacy, ethical journalism, responsible content creation, fact-checking and constructive public engagement.

He said social media should serve as a platform for enlightenment, innovation, accountability, economic advancement and national development rather than hatred, hostility and destruction.

He also appealed to Nigerians not to “dance Palongo”, a Yoruba expression he said his grandmother used to describe an irregular and disorderly dance.

He urged citizens to reject digital recklessness and embrace truth, responsibility, civility and patriotism.

“Protect the country’s collective destiny from misinformation and manipulation. Nigeria is bigger than our individual interests, political affiliations, personal grievances and social media engagements,” he said.

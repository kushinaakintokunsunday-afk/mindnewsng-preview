World Children’s Day: UNICEF, Lagos pledge action on children’s rights, welfare

By Doris Obinna

The United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) and the Lagos State Government have pledged stronger action to protect children’s rights, improve their welfare and secure their future, with a commitment to translate their demands and aspirations into concrete policies and interventions.

The commitment was made on Friday in Lagos to mark the 2026 World Children’s Day organised by UNICEF in collaboration with the Lagos State Ministry of Youth and Social Development, with the theme “My Day. My Rights. My Future.”

The Commissioner for Youth and Social Development, Lagos State, Mr Mobolaji Ogunlende, reiterated that the government remained committed to the welfare, protection and development of every child but acknowledged that achieving this required stronger collaboration with parents, schools, communities and development partners.

Speaking on the theme “My Day, My Rights, My Future,” Ogunlende said children were not merely leaders of tomorrow but important voices in shaping society today, stressing that their opinions, rights and aspirations must be reflected in government policies.

He said the state government remained committed to promoting the welfare, protection and development of every child but acknowledged that government could not achieve this responsibility alone.

According to him, parents, schools, communities, development partners and other stakeholders must work together to create an environment where children feel safe, valued and heard.

Ogunlende said the children’s activities, particularly their artworks and conversations, provided an opportunity for government to understand their expectations and the kind of future they wanted.

He assured that the issues raised by the children would not end with the celebration but must translate into meaningful commitments and interventions.

“The future of every child in the state is important to the government. That is why we have the three pluses in the THEMES Plus Agenda, particularly the pillar on children’s rights and children’s future,” he said.

The commissioner described UNICEF as a critical development partner, noting that the ultimate beneficiaries of the collaboration were the children whose lives government and its partners were working to improve.

Earlier, the UNICEF Chief of Field Office, Lagos, Celine Lafoucriere, urged the Lagos State Government to ensure that every child, particularly the most vulnerable, enjoyed their fundamental rights, warning that children’s rights were increasingly under threat globally.

She identified education, healthcare, good nutrition, clean water, sanitation and social protection as essential rights that must be accessible to every child.

Lafoucriere said UNICEF was working closely with the Lagos State Government to ensure that no child was left behind, particularly those whose families lacked the resources to provide basic necessities.

She stressed that social protection was critical to bridging the gap between having rights and actually enjoying them, explaining that vulnerable children needed government support to access education, healthcare and adequate nutrition.

“Having a right is one thing, but it’s not going to put food on the table,” she said.

The UNICEF official challenged government and other adult stakeholders to move beyond listening to children’s demands and demonstrate their commitment through practical action.

“When our children paint the Nigeria they dream of, our job as adults is not to admire the picture. Our job is to build it,” she said.

She disclosed that commitments made by adults during the children’s gallery walk would be documented and followed up, insisting that promises to children must be measurable and kept.

The celebration featured 80 children divided into eight groups, who used paintings and other creative expressions to communicate their vision of the Nigeria they wanted.

The eight panels were subsequently brought together into a collective installation titled “80 Children, One Future.”

A member of the Lagos State Children’s Parliament and Young People’s Action Team, Rt. Hon. Abdulsalam Anjola, used the occasion to call on government to invest more in children’s education and involve them in decisions affecting their lives.

He said children who experienced abuse should be encouraged to speak for themselves, noting that their accounts could better convey the pain and trauma they endured.

Anjola also urged the government to provide educational opportunities for vulnerable children, including those living and working on the streets, stressing that investment in education would help build a safer Lagos and a better Nigeria.

Similarly, a 14-year-old student of Ikeja Senior High School, Ishola Moyinoluwa, urged government to create platforms where children could express their views and discuss issues affecting their rights and future.

She said every child deserved free basic education and the opportunity to pursue their dreams, explaining that her artwork, titled “My Dream, My Possibilities,” portrayed a young boy aspiring to become an astronaut.

In her closing remarks, the Permanent Secretary, Ministry of Youth and Social Development, Mrs Toyin Oke-Osanyintolu, called on children to speak up against violations of their rights and remain confident in pursuing their aspirations.

She described children as the pride of Lagos State and urged them to take responsibility for contributing to the future they desired.

Oke-Osanyintolu appreciated UNICEF for its continued support to the state, expressing optimism about stronger collaboration to advance children’s welfare and protection.

She urged the children to believe in their abilities, declaring: “If you can dream it, you can do it.”

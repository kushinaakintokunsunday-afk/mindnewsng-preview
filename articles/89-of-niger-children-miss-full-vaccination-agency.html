89% of Niger children miss full vaccination – Agency

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Niger State Primary Healthcare Development Agency has disclosed that only 11 per cent of children in the state are fully immunised, leaving about 89 per cen

The Executive Director of the agency, Dr Junaidu Inuwa, raised the concern during an interview with AREWA PUNCH on Monday, saying the situation had compelled the agency to adopt new strategies to improve immunisation coverage across the state.

Inuwa said the agency had entered into partnerships to provide incentives to parents and caregivers who brought their children for immunisation, particularly to encourage uptake among families facing transportation and other challenges.

He also disclosed that additional measures had been introduced to reach children in hard-to-reach communities and areas affected by insecurity.

According to him, the agency remains concerned about inadequate awareness of the importance of routine immunisation, despite efforts by the state government and development partners to improve access to healthcare.

“The Primary Healthcare Development Agency, one of our key strategies is immunisation. The last time I checked my data, when you look at the figures, you find out that 11 per cent of children in Niger State are fully immunised.

“That tells you that out of 100 children, only 11 are fully immunised in Niger State, meaning I am still battling with about 89. To us, that calls for a lot of concern,” he said.

Inuwa explained that the agency had introduced several interventions to address the challenge, including partnerships aimed at encouraging parents to utilise routine immunisation services.

He said although he was initially not convinced about the idea of offering incentives to parents, the agency eventually recognised it as an opportunity to engage caregivers and educate them on the importance of vaccination.

“There are lots of strategies we have put in place. One of the things we have done is that we found out there is still a lack of awareness about immunisation, so we partnered with a particular group. We call them New Incentives.

“They were piloting some of these services, even though, for me, I did not come from that school of thought that incentives should be given to people to bring their children.

“But what we looked at was the opportunity to interact with the caregivers. So, every time a parent knows that he will get something to support his transportation, he now brings his child to the facilities,” he said.

The agency’s executive director added that the intervention provided an opportunity for health workers to educate parents about their responsibility to ensure that their children received routine vaccinations.

“When they do that, we use the opportunity to let them know that it is their right, privilege and duty that their children get immunised. We also try to tell them some of the things we have done and let them know that across all our facilities, routine immunisation is free,” Inuwa stated.

He commended the Niger State Government and development partners, including the World Health Organisation and the United Nations Children’s Fund, for supporting efforts to improve immunisation coverage and strengthen primary healthcare delivery.

Meanwhile, the Executive Director of Global Promoters for Community Initiatives, Mr Olasukanmi Kalejaiye, said the Media Chat was a quarterly engagement organised by the Niger State Primary Healthcare Development Agency.

He said the engagement, themed, “Strengthening Primary Health Care Delivery and Improving Access to Quality Healthcare 1.0,” provided a platform for the agency’s executive director to interact with journalists on its achievements, challenges and strategies for improving healthcare delivery.

Kalejaiye, who spoke to ArewaPunch on a sideline of the Media Chat,  explained that the initiative, powered by Global Promoters for Community Initiatives, was designed to strengthen media engagement, address public concerns and promote awareness of primary healthcare services across the state.

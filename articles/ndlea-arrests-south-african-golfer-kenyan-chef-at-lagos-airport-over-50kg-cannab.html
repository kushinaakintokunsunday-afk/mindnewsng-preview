NDLEA arrests South African golfer, Kenyan chef at Lagos airport over 50kg cannabis shipment

From Godwin Tsa, Abuja

A 37-year-old South African professional golfer, Grobler Henlo, and a 25-year-old Kenyan chef, Abubakar Faiza Njambi, have been arrested by the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) at the Murtala Muhammed International Airport (MMIA), Ikeja, Lagos, for their alleged roles in a syndicate’s attempt to smuggle 50.40 kilogrammes of Loud, a potent strain of cannabis, into Nigeria.

The agency’s Director of Media and Advocacy, Femi Babafemi, disclosed this in a statement on Sunday.

According to Babafemi, Grobler was arrested on Sunday, October 11, 2026, exactly one month before an American professional basketballer, Greene Da’monte Khiree, was intercepted at the same airport on Wednesday, November 11, with 33.95kg of the same substance.

Grobler arrived from South Africa on a South African Airways flight and was intercepted at the Terminal 2 arrival hall.

A search of the two boxes in his possession revealed 46 parcels of Loud with a gross weight of 50.40kg.

Investigators found that although the boxes were with him, they bore the luggage tags of his fellow courier, Njambi, who had collected two other boxes bearing his tags from the conveyor belt and left the airport.

The statement described the tag swap as a deliberate ploy by the syndicate to frustrate identification of ownership, allowing each courier to deny boxes bearing their name if intercepted.

In a preliminary interview, the golfer, who travelled in business class while Njambi flew economy, said he was recruited by a South African associate, Shanel, who linked him with other syndicate members he never met.

He said a driver picked him up from his home, handed him four boxes and took him to the airport, where he was told to meet the Kenyan woman and swap luggage tags. He claimed he was promised 5,000 South African rand on successful delivery.

Babafemi said tracking devices were found on the boxes and on the golfer’s mobile phone. Similar devices were found in the bags abandoned by the American basketballer a month later, which he said showed how closely the cartels monitor their couriers and consignments.

Njambi’s passport biodata page, recovered from the golfer’s phone, was profiled and she was placed on the NDLEA watchlist.

She was arrested at the departure boarding gate of the Lagos airport on Thursday, October 15, as she tried to board a South African Airways flight back to Kenya.

The private chef told operatives she was recruited by a man, and that a friend in Kenya persuaded her to accept an offer of US$8,000 on successful delivery.

She said she left Kenya for South Africa on October 4, stayed in a hotel for about a week, and met the golfer at the airport on October 11, where he handed her two of the four boxes before they checked them in under their respective tags.

She claimed that on arrival in Lagos she collected the two boxes bearing the golfer’s tags, after which a driver took her to a hotel in the Lekki area.

The claim tag for the two boxes bearing her name, which she abandoned at the airport, was recovered from her hand luggage. Both suspects identified each other during an identification parade.

At the import shed of the Lagos airport on Friday, November 13, operatives intercepted a consolidated consignment from the United Kingdom aboard an Allied Air flight. A freight agent, Sobowale Oladayo, was arrested when he presented himself for clearance.

Examination of the shipment led to the recovery of 10 parcels of synthetic cannabinoid (10.10kg); 27 packs of marshmallow (318.80kg); and precursor chemicals comprising 11 containers of N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) (18.90kg), 10 containers of 5-Bromo-1-Pentane (8.05kg) and seven containers of Potassium Carbonate (7.60kg). The total gross weight was 363.45kg.

In a follow-up operation on Saturday, November 14, the consignee, Kareem Issa, a.k.a. Ola, 35, was arrested when he came to take delivery at the premises of a logistics company in a grey Lexus 460 SUV, accompanied by two others.

A plumber by profession who lives on Lagos Island, he allegedly posed as a senior security officer, routinely blaring a siren fitted to his vehicle on visits to the logistics company to avoid attention.

On Tuesday, November 17, operatives on patrol along the Abuja-Kaduna expressway at Jere arrested a 35-year-old man, Lawali Muhammad, with 160 pieces of explosives bound for Zamfara State. The intended recipient, Aliyu Mohammed, 30, was arrested in a follow-up operation in Gusau the same day.

On the same axis, two suspects, Yusuf Magaji and Buhari Yusuf, 32, were arrested with 6,900 tablets of tramadol 225mg, 20,000 tablets of diazepam and 25,000 tablets of Exol-5.

Along the Zaria-Kano expressway at Gwargwaje, Zaria, operatives recovered 627,500 capsules of tramadol, weighing 21.50kg, from a Hummer bus whose driver abandoned it and fled on sighting the officers.

In Ogun State, a woman, Esther Ogundipe, was arrested on Thursday, November 19, with two locally made firearms and five live cartridges during a raid on a drug joint at Eleweron, Abeokuta.

Two days earlier, on Tuesday, November 17, two women, Balikis Ogunbiyi and Omobola Ajani, were arrested in a raid in Ilaro with 7.4 grams of cocaine, 638 grams of cannabis and 5.9 grams of methamphetamine.

Tonnes of cannabis in Osun, Ondo

In Osun State, a 43-year-old man, Francis Oduke, was arrested along the Ikire/Ibadan expressway, Ikire, with 359 bags of skunk weighing 4,627.7kg, concealed under a load of granite in a truck.

In neighbouring Ondo State, a Kano-bound truck carrying 93 bags of cannabis weighing 1,045kg was intercepted on Monday, November 16, along the Zango/Ado expressway, Akure.

The vehicle, loaded at Ogbese town, had been monitored following sustained intelligence gathering. Four suspects, Adamu Yerima, 19; Mohammed Mohammed, 19; Nura Mohammed, 25; and Usman Mohammed, were arrested.

Edo: Emmanuel Eze, 48, was arrested on Friday, November 20, with 11½ bags of cannabis weighing 156kg at Aduwawa community, Benin City.

Katsina: Abbas Bello, 39, was arrested on Tuesday, November 17, in the Gafai area with 148 blocks of cannabis weighing 72kg, hidden in the boot of his white Honda Accord.

Abia: On Wednesday, November 18, operatives raided the shop of Oluebube Innocent, 25, at Amankwa Street, Obingwa LGA, and recovered 2kg of cannabis from a hole dug into the floor and covered with ceramic tiles.

Rivers: Peter Sunday, 46, who is physically challenged, was arrested on Tuesday, November 17, with 300 grams of cannabis in a raid at Eneka.

Marwa: Cartels changing faces, not tactics

Commending officers of the MMIA, Kaduna, Ogun, Osun, Ondo, Edo, Katsina, Abia and Rivers commands, the NDLEA Chairman/Chief Executive Officer, Brig. Gen. Mohamed Buba Marwa (Rtd), said the arrests of the golfer and the Kenyan woman, coming in the same period as that of the American basketballer, showed that “the cartels are changing faces but not their tactics”, according to Babafemi.

“The two cases show a trend in which international drug cartels now recruit sportsmen and other professionals as couriers, in the hope that their profiles will shield them from scrutiny,” Marwa said. “The NDLEA has a clear message for such syndicates: no profession, passport or travel class will provide cover for drug trafficking through Nigeria’s ports of entry.”

He urged officers at all ports of entry to remain vigilant and professional, and directed commands to balance drug supply reduction with demand reduction, especially War Against Drug Abuse (WADA) sensitisation campaigns in schools, workplaces, worship centres and communities.

Over the past week, commands continued WADA sensitisation activities. These included lectures to students and staff of Local Government Primary School, Ebute Ikorodu, Lagos; Government Girls Unity College, Birnin Kebbi; and Community Secondary School, Usa, Enugu Ezike. The Ondo State Command also paid an advocacy visit to the Deji of Akure, Oba Aladetoyinbo Ogunlade Adelusi, Odundun II.

New Enugu CP pledges people-centred policing, warns against extortion

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The newly appointed Commissioner of Police in Enugu State, CP Fidelis Ogarabe, has given officers a three-hour ultimatum to report political and election-relate

Ogarabe issued the directive while unveiling his policing priorities during his maiden address to officers at the State Command Headquarters, GRA, Enugu, on November 17, 2026.

The new CP also pledged to strengthen professional, intelligence-led and people-centred policing in the state.

He took over from CP Mamman Bitrus Giwa, who has been redeployed to the Force Criminal Investigation Department, Abuja, to head the Special Enquiry Bureau.

According to a statement issued by the Police Public Relations Officer, SP Daniel Ndukwe, Ogarabe pledged “to build a professional, disciplined, intelligence-led, technology-driven and people-oriented Command focused on crime prevention, protection of lives and property, and effective response to security threats.”

Outlining his priorities, the commissioner listed intelligence-led policing, decisive action against violent crimes, police visibility, professional investigation, community partnership, respect for human rights, personnel welfare and accountability as key focus areas.

He warned officers against “partisanship, corruption, extortion, abuse of power and other forms of unprofessional conduct,” insisting that personnel must “respect citizens’ rights, constituted authorities and traditional institutions, remain politically neutral and strictly comply with the law and court orders.”

Ogarabe charged officers to take responsibility for security within their respective areas of jurisdiction and adopt proactive policing. He also directed them to “promptly report political and election-related incidents to him within three hours of occurrence.”

He further directed the reinvigoration of the command’s X-Squad and other discipline-enforcing units to strengthen oversight and address misconduct, declaring zero tolerance for extortion and all forms of corruption.

A native of Ohaji/Egbema Local Government Area of Imo State, Ogarabe holds a Bachelor of Science degree in Political Science from the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, and a Master of Science degree in International Law and Diplomacy from Enugu State University of Science and Technology.

The new CP joined the Nigeria Police Force as a Cadet Assistant Superintendent of Police and was commissioned in 1994, beginning his career in Enugu State Command, where he served as Divisional Police Officer of the then Trade Fair Police Station.

He subsequently served as Officer in Charge of General Investigation, State CID, Abia State Command, before returning to Enugu, where he served as Chief Security Officer to two successive governors of the state.

He also served as Assistant Commissioner of Police and Deputy Commissioner of Police in charge of the command’s Criminal Investigation Department, as well as Deputy Commissioner of Police, Force CID Annex, Enugu, and Deputy Commissioner of Police in charge of Finance and Administration, Anambra State Command.

Following his promotion to the rank of Commissioner of Police, he served at the Office of the National Security Adviser, Abuja, and subsequently as Commissioner of Police, Independent National Electoral Commission, Abuja. Before his present posting, he was Commissioner of Police, INTERPOL Annex, Lagos.

Ogarabe is a professional member of the National Institute of Police Studies, where he attended the Institute Strategic Leadership and Command Course. He has also attended other professional courses, including the Chief Security Officers’ Course, Junior Command Course and Advanced Detective Course.

With over 30 years of policing experience spanning operations, criminal investigation and administration, the new CP assured residents of his commitment to “professional, disciplined, intelligence-led, preventive and people-centred policing, with emphasis on community partnership.”

He pledged to align the command’s policing strategies with the vision and mission of the Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, and “solicits the cooperation and support of residents and other stakeholders in maintaining peace, security and public order across the State.”

Ogarabe assured residents of “professional, impartial and people-oriented policing,” while calling on officers to embrace “discipline, integrity, courage and teamwork in the collective pursuit of a safer Enugu State and a Police Command worthy of public trust.”

He is married with children. His interests include football, physical exercise and philanthropy.

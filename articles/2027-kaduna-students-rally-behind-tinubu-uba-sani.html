2027: Kaduna students rally behind Tinubu, Uba Sani

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

From Charity Nwakaudu, Abuja

The Kaduna State chapter of JAGABA/UBA Students’ Mandate 2027 has unveiled campus ambassadors to mobilise students across tertiary institutions for the re-election of President Bola Tinubu and Governor Uba Sani in 2027.

The group said its support for the two leaders was driven by their investments in education, skills acquisition and youth empowerment.

Speaking at the inauguration, the convener of the group, Comrade Munzali Abubakar, described the Campus Ambassadors initiative as a strategic platform to mobilise students and promote awareness of government policies and interventions in the education sector.

He said the ambassadors would establish a strong network across tertiary institutions to educate students on the achievements of the Tinubu and Uba Sani administrations.

Abubakar said the Federal Government’s Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND) scheme had expanded access to tertiary education, benefiting over 1.6 million students, with more than ₦322 billion disbursed.

He also cited the government’s Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) programme, which targets over one million youths across 1,600 centres, offering free tuition, monthly stipends and starter packs.

The convener further highlighted the Federal Government’s agreement with the Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU), which he said had improved university operations, salaries, research funding and staff welfare.

On Kaduna State, Abubakar commended Uba Sani for allocating 25 per cent of the state’s budget to education in 2024 and increasing it to 26.14 per cent in 2025.

He said the governor had constructed 62 secondary schools, comprising 39 junior and 23 senior schools, while approving 50 additional schools.

According to him, the administration has also delivered 2,326 classrooms and renovated 707 others across the state.

He listed the $62.8 million Reaching Out-of-School Children (ROOSC) project, being implemented with the Kuwait Fund and other development partners, as another major intervention.

The project is expected to provide 102 climate-resilient schools and rehabilitate 170 learning centres across the state’s 23 local government areas.

Abubakar also disclosed that over ₦1 billion had been invested in scholarships, student loans and other educational support programmes.

On teachers’ development, he said 33,137 teachers, head teachers and school support officers had benefited from professional development programmes covering modern teaching methods, computer literacy and classroom management.

He further highlighted the establishment of three Vocational and Technology Skills Acquisition Cities across the state’s three senatorial zones, targeting 12,000 trainees in the first year and 24,000 in the second.

The centres will provide training in 13 vocational areas, including welding, solar panel installation, fashion design and aluminium fabrication.

The group also cited the transformation of Panteka Market into an industrial and vocational skills development hub, noting that the market accommodates more than 38,000 artisans.

Other initiatives highlighted included the Kaduna Youth Economic Empowerment Programme (K-YEEP), which has supported over 6,000 startups, and the Kaduna TechHUBs, where young people receive training in artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and robotics.

Abubakar also commended the AREWA LADIES-4-TECH initiative for equipping 5,100 women with ICT skills, as well as career fairs that reportedly facilitated over 300 job placements.

He said the inauguration of the Kaduna State Skills Development Council had further strengthened the state’s framework for coordinating skills development programmes.

The newly inaugurated ambassadors were charged with taking the mobilisation campaign to campuses and engaging students on government policies and programmes.

Abubakar said: “We cannot afford to return to a past where young people were denied opportunities, schools lacked basic facilities and students struggled to access quality education.”

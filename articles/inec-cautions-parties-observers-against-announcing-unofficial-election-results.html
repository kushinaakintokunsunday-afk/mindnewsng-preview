INEC cautions parties, observers against announcing unofficial election results

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Independent National Electoral Commission has warned political parties, election observers and other stakeholders against presenting collated election resul

The commission said independent verification of election results should complement, rather than undermine, its statutory responsibility for the collation and declaration of election results.

INEC Chairman, Prof Joash Amupitan, stated this on Tuesday at a stakeholder roundtable on Independent Results Tabulation in Abuja, where he was represented by the commission’s Director of Information and Communication Technology, Dr Lawrence Bayode.

Amupitan said the proposed voluntary code of conduct for independent election results tabulation presented an opportunity to strengthen electoral transparency, protect individual rights and reinforce public confidence in election outcomes.

He, however, warned that incomplete, inaccurate or misleading independently collated results could create confusion, heighten political tension and undermine confidence in the electoral process, particularly in an era of instant digital communication.

He said, “Indeed, transparency should not be regarded as a threat to electoral administration; rather, credible independent scrutiny will serve as an additional layer of accountability. However, transparency must be accompanied by responsibility.”

The INEC chairman stressed the need to distinguish between independent verification of election results and the official collation and declaration of results, which he said remained the statutory responsibility of the commission.

He said election observers, political parties, media organisations and other stakeholders were free to collect, analyse and independently verify election information in accordance with the law.

However, he cautioned that independent tabulation must not be presented as a substitute for the commission’s statutory responsibilities, while unofficial projections and privately generated figures must not be communicated as officially declared results.

“The Commission’s position is clear. Independent verification should complement electoral accountability, not create competing centres for statutory authority,” he said.

Amupitan said INEC was strengthening safeguards within its election results management architecture ahead of the 2027 elections.

He listed improvements in the use of the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System, the commission’s results-viewing platform and additional consistency and validation checks to improve the accuracy of polling unit results.

According to him, the measures are designed to ensure that results originate from the appropriate polling units, are accurately documented and remain verifiable throughout the legally prescribed collation process.

He, however, acknowledged that technology alone could not guarantee electoral integrity, stressing the need for sound procedures, trained personnel, effective oversight and informed public participation.

The INEC chairman outlined six principles that should guide the proposed code of conduct, including methodological integrity, verification of election-related information, safeguards against electoral misinformation and adequate cybersecurity measures.

Others are mechanisms for identifying discrepancies and correcting inaccurate publications, as well as broad consultations to ensure that the framework does not compromise the independence of election observers, editorial freedom of media organisations or the lawful participation of political parties.

In his keynote address, the Chairman of the Editorial Board of THISDAY Newspapers, Olusegun Adeniyi, said the period between the conclusion of voting and the official declaration of results was particularly vulnerable to rumours, competing claims and misinformation.

He argued that independent results verification already existed and that the important question was whether it would help narrow the information gap during that period or worsen it.

Adeniyi identified premature declarations, inadequate verification methodologies, artificial intelligence-generated misinformation and the inability of citizens to distinguish between verified results and unofficial projections as major challenges facing independent results tabulation.

He warned that premature victory declarations could be deliberately deployed to mobilise supporters and portray competing claims as evidence of electoral manipulation.

Adeniyi welcomed the voluntary nature of the proposed code, arguing that credible self-regulation by election observers, political parties and media organisations would protect against excessive government regulation.

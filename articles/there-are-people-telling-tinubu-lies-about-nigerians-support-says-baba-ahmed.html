‘There Are People Telling Tinubu Lies About Nigerians’ Support,’ Says Baba-Ahmed

The National Chairman of the Peoples Redemption Party (PRP), Hakeem Baba-Ahmed, has accused some political loyalists of misleading President Bola Tinubu about the level of public support for his administration.

Baba-Ahmed, who once served under the Tinubu administration as Special Adviser on Political Matters in the Office of the Vice President, said the president’s associates could be giving him a false impression of his popularity by relying on assurances from governors and party loyalists rather than listening to ordinary Nigerians.

He made the remarks on Friday during an appearance on Channels Television’s The Morning Brief programme.

The PRP chairman said his assessment of public sentiment was based on his grassroots political connections and familiarity with the administration, which he initially supported.

“I’m a politician. I lead a political party that is entirely grassroots-based. I have very strong organic links with the people. I followed the Tinubu administration from inception. Initially, I was even part of it,” he said.

Baba-Ahmed said he was familiar with the feelings of Nigerians and was not exaggerating the widespread dissatisfaction with the government.

He identified poverty, economic mismanagement, corruption and insecurity as some of the issues contributing to the growing distance between the administration and the people.

He also suggested that some political associates were giving Tinubu assurances about his chances of securing re-election in 2027 without adequately reflecting the mood of Nigerians.

“There are a lot of people who are telling President Tinubu lies if he thinks himself, and they think that the people are with them. Just look at wherever APC goes. People either chase them away or they trigger violence,” he said.

‘Bamayi’

Baba-Ahmed also described the ‘Bamayi’ chants directed at the president as an expression of widespread dissatisfaction, insisting that the sentiment was not restricted to young people or northern Nigerians.

“This Bamayi thing, it’s not just a slogan that young people throw at APC. It’s genuinely an organic element of the way Nigerians, not just northerners, Nigerians feel,” he added.

The ‘Bamayi’ chants, which translate to ‘we don’t want’ in Hausa, were heard on November 13 after Tinubu attended Friday prayers at the Shehu Shagari Mosque in the Garki area of Abuja.

Some protesters reportedly threw stones at the president’s convoy and destroyed APC campaign posters, prompting security operatives to intervene.

The incident attracted reactions from political figures, including Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) chieftain Segun Sowunmi, who condemned the protesters’ actions.

Sowunmi, who is also a member of the APC campaign council, criticised the destruction of campaign posters and the use of violence during an appearance on Channels Television’s *Politics Today* on Monday.

He argued that Nigerians dissatisfied with a political candidate should express their opposition through the ballot box rather than resorting to violence.

Similarly, African Democratic Congress (ADC) chieftain Dele Momodu said Nigerians were rejecting Tinubu because they were experiencing the effects of his administration’s economic policies.

Momodu, the Deputy Director-General of Media and Communications of the ADC presidential campaign council, made the remarks on Thursday during an appearance on *Politics Today*.

He linked the growing public discontent to the hardship Nigerians had experienced since the removal of the petrol subsidy.

Tinubu, who assumed office in May 2023, is seeking re-election in 2027. He is expected to face opposition from Peter Obi of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) and Atiku Abubakar of the African Democratic Congress (ADC).

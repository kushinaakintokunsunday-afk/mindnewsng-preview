Oyo, police warn NURTW against violence

By Punch Punch 2d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Oyo State Government and the state police command, on Sunday, warned the National Union of Road Transport Workers against violence over the Court of Appeal

While assuring all residents, commuters, visitors, investors and travellers that the state remains peaceful and secure, the government and the police further warned against any act of violence, intimidation, self-help or unlawful takeover of public facilities by any individual or group, regardless of affiliation.

This warning followed a possible clash between the NURTW and park managers over the control of motor parks in parts of the state, particularly Ibadan.

The PUNCH reported that the union had threatened to take over motor parks across the state in seven days, accusing Governor Seyi Makinde’s administration of repeatedly disobeying court judgments recognising its right to manage the parks.

The union said it would begin enforcing the judgments if the state government failed to withdraw its Park Management System from motor parks in the state’s 33 Local Government Areas by November 23.

The warnings were contained in separate statements issued by the governor’s Special Adviser on media, Sulaimon Olarenwaju, and the command’s Police Public Relations Officer, Olayinka Ayanlade, in Ibadan, the state capital.

The government said it had taken note of speculations regarding a possible breakdown of law and order arising from issues surrounding the management of motor parks in parts of the state.

The statement read, “The state government wishes to categorically assure all residents, commuters, visitors, investors, and travellers that Oyo State remains peaceful and secure. There is no cause for alarm. Governor Seyi Makinde has directed all relevant security agencies to maintain vigilance and ensure the protection of lives and property across the state.

“Security personnel have been strategically deployed where necessary, and appropriate measures have been put in place to prevent any breach of the peace.

“The Makinde administration remains firmly committed to preserving the peace, stability and economic progress that Oyo State has enjoyed over the years. Consequently, the government will not tolerate any act of violence, intimidation, self-help or unlawful takeover of public facilities by any individual or group, regardless of affiliation.

“The government urges all transport stakeholders, including members of the NURTW, to conduct themselves peacefully and in accordance with the law. No group or individual will be permitted to take actions capable of disrupting public order or threatening the peace of the state.”

The government also reiterated its earlier position on the judgment of the Court of Appeal, saying the judgment merely reaffirmed the constitutional right of citizens to freedom of association.

The statement explained that the judgment neither conferred ownership, control, nor management of public motor parks on the NURTW, nor did it authorise the union to take over facilities built, maintained, and regulated by the government.

It added, “Accordingly, any attempt to rely on the judgment as a basis for demanding, threatening, or effecting the takeover of public motor parks represents a clear misinterpretation of the court’s decision and has no basis in law.

“The government remains committed to upholding the rule of law and ensuring that all disputes, regardless of their nature, are resolved through lawful and peaceful means.

“Security agencies have been directed to act decisively against anyone found threatening public peace or disrupting economic and social activities in any part of the state.”

The government, therefore, advised the residents to disregard rumours, alarmist messages, and unverified information circulating on social media, urging them to rely only on official channels of communication for accurate and timely information.

It insisted that Oyo remained safe, secure, and open for business, urging all the residents to continue their lawful activities without fear, confident in the knowledge that the government has taken all necessary steps to guarantee their safety and security.

In a statement issued by the PPRO, the state police command said, “The attention of the Oyo State Police command has been drawn to a purported security advisory allegedly issued by some members of the NURTW, warning of a possible clash between NURTW members and Park Managers over the control of motor parks in parts of Oyo, particularly Ibadan, on Monday, November 23, 2026.

“The notice, which has since been circulating across various social media platforms, advises members of the public to avoid certain locations, including Iwo Road, Ojoo and Challenge areas, while urging residents to remain indoors throughout the day.

“Given the apprehension such information may generate, the command considers it necessary to address the situation and reassure the public. In this regard, the command wishes to assure the good people of Oyo State, particularly residents of Ibadan and its environs, that there is no cause for panic.

“Residents are advised to go about their lawful businesses and daily activities without fear or intimidation, as the police have taken proactive steps to prevent any threat to public peace and security. Consequently, the command has strengthened its security architecture through the deployment of adequate personnel, enhanced surveillance and intelligence-gathering mechanisms across strategic locations in the state.”

The police added that the measures are “aimed at identifying and promptly addressing any potential security threat, while ensuring that no individual or group is allowed to disrupt the peace and safety of residents.

“While the command remains committed to preventing any breakdown of law and order, it also recognises the importance of peaceful dispute resolution.”

The police, therefore, urged all parties involved to embrace dialogue and explore legitimate and appropriate legal channels for resolving their differences, saying that “no individual or group should resort to self-help, violence, intimidation or any action capable of threatening public peace.”

The command further warned that it would not allow any individual or group to undermine the security gains recorded in the state or take the state backwards through acts of lawlessness.

It insisted that all necessary measures would be taken to deal decisively with anyone found engaging in conduct capable of disturbing public peace.

The state Commissioner of Police, Olugbenga Abimbola, reassured residents of the command’s unwavering commitment to the protection of lives and property and the maintenance of law and order across the state.

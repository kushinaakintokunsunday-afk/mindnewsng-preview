Lekki Tech Academy targets next generation of African tech professionals

By Punch Punch 16h ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

As demand for technology professionals continues to grow globally, Lekki Tech Academy has announced the commencement of its technology training programmes, offe

The academy, established to contribute to technology skills development and workforce capacity building, is providing training opportunities in artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, software engineering, web development, data science, cloud computing, robotics, digital product management and other emerging technology disciplines.

The institution is targeting young graduates, students, working professionals, entrepreneurs and individuals seeking to acquire relevant technology skills or transition into the rapidly expanding technology industry.

Speaking on the vision behind the academy, Head of Corporate Communications, Jessica Izuora, said management established the Academy to close the gap between classroom learning and industry needs.

“Technology is reshaping every sector. Our goal is to equip learners with skills they can actually use in the workplace,” she said.

According to the academy, its programmes are designed to combine structured learning with practical assignments, industry-relevant projects and professional development.

In a move aimed at strengthening the connection between classroom learning and workplace experience, Lekki Tech Academy is also introducing a six-month internship placement opportunity for every professional certificate enrolment.

Miss Izuora said the internship is central to the academy’s approach.

“We don’t want our students to graduate with theory alone. The internship gives them real exposure, mentorship and confidence,” she said.

The initiative is expected to provide learners with opportunities for practical exposure, professional mentorship and portfolio development.

The academy further explained that its programmes accommodate both beginners and individuals with existing technology knowledge who wish to upgrade their professional competencies.

With Africa’s youthful population and growing digital economy, the institution believes that developing a skilled technology workforce will remain essential to economic competitiveness, innovation and employment generation.

“Africa has the talent. What we are building is the pathway for that talent to compete globally,” Mrs Izuora added.

Lekki Tech Academy says it intends to grow into a recognised centre for technology education, innovation and professional development, contributing to the development of globally competitive African technology talent.

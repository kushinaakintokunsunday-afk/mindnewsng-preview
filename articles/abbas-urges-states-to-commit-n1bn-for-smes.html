Abbas urges states to commit N1bn for SMEs

By Punch Punch 18h ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Speaker of the House of Representatives, Tajudeen Abbas, has challenged state governments to set aside at least N1bn monthly for small and medium-scale ente

Abbas, who spoke through the House Majority Leader, Prof Julius Ihonvbere, at a stakeholders’ engagement organised by the House Committee on Small and Medium Enterprises in Abuja on Monday, said state governments had the financial capacity to establish dedicated intervention funds for entrepreneurs.

He also criticised local governments for failing to provide the institutional and financial support needed to stimulate grassroots businesses, insisting that the responsibility for improving the economy should not be left to the Federal Government alone.

“There is no governor in this country that cannot put one billion naira a month in a special fund for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises in his state. There is no state that cannot do it,” he said.

The Speaker’s position came amid growing concerns over the difficulties confronting MSMEs, including high lending rates, cumbersome access to intervention funds, regulatory bottlenecks and inadequate government support.

Abbas lamented that despite the establishment of several government agencies and the injection of substantial funds into the sector, many small businesses continued to struggle to survive, largely through the determination of their owners rather than institutional assistance.

“Those that are surviving are surviving not because of government and agencies, but in spite of government and agencies,” he said.

He particularly faulted the bureaucratic procedures associated with accessing government-backed intervention funds, arguing that excessive documentation and administrative requirements had undermined the purpose of such programmes.

“I’m a systems man, but I know this system is not working. And it will not work when you bureaucratise a procedure. A lot of red tape,” Abbas said.

He also cautioned small business owners against borrowing at double-digit interest rates, noting that the cost of credit could cripple enterprises before they became sufficiently established to meet repayment obligations.

According to him, government interventions must focus not only on making funds available but also on ensuring that financing is affordable and accessible under conditions that reflect the realities of small businesses.

The Speaker further faulted state and local governments for failing to translate increased federal allocations into tangible economic benefits for their residents.

“All the complaints you are hearing in this country today is not because of Tinubu. Not because of the federal government. It’s because of state and local governments that are not performing,” he added.

He urged Nigerians to hold elected officials at all levels accountable, stressing that subnational governments had important responsibilities in promoting employment, entrepreneurship and economic development.

Earlier, the Chairman of the House Committee on Small and Medium Enterprises, Pascal Agbodike, said the engagement was organised to identify practical solutions to the challenges confronting MSMEs and examine why existing government interventions had failed to produce the desired results.

Agbodike said the committee had invited relevant government agencies and financial institutions to explain their intervention programmes, identify implementation gaps and account for the extent to which funds appropriated by the National Assembly had reached intended beneficiaries.

“We are lawmakers. Ours is simple: to appropriate the necessary funds. But when we appropriate those funds, how is it being trickled down to the common man in the street?” he asked.

He disclosed that the committee would examine the activities of institutions, including the Central Bank of Nigeria and the Bank of Industry, to establish the gaps between policy formulation, funding approvals and actual disbursement to entrepreneurs.

The lawmaker expressed optimism that the engagement would produce concrete recommendations and measurable improvements in the delivery of MSME support programmes within 100 days.

Also speaking, the Director-General of the Presidential Enabling Business Environment Council, Princess Zahrah Audu, identified affordable financing, regulatory reforms and stronger collaboration with state governments as critical to the growth of small businesses.

Audu said PEBEC monitored 69 ministries, departments and agencies, adding that the council had introduced the ReportGov digital platform to enable businesses to report difficulties encountered in their dealings with government institutions.

She explained that complaints submitted through the platform were automatically referred to the relevant agencies, which were expected to address them within 72 hours, with PEBEC intervening where necessary.

However, she stressed that improving the business environment required coordinated action beyond the Federal Government.

Audu advocated partnerships between state governments and development finance institutions, including the Bank of Industry, Bank of Agriculture and Nigerian Export-Import Bank, to expand access to credit.

She explained that state governments could provide counterpart funding to reduce lending risks, ease collateral requirements and enable entrepreneurs with viable business proposals to secure loans.

According to her, PEBEC had facilitated similar arrangements in about 15 states, providing opportunities for business associations to participate in financing partnerships with development finance institutions.

She also called for patient capital, arguing that small businesses needed financing arrangements that allowed sufficient time for their operations to stabilise and generate returns before repayment commenced.

“For me, the solution for SMEs is patient capital,” she said, urging the Central Bank of Nigeria to review prevailing interest rates to make borrowing more affordable.

In his contribution, a former member of the Anambra State House of Assembly and consultant, Charles Ezeani, identified excessive documentation and cumbersome lending procedures as major obstacles preventing rural entrepreneurs from accessing government-backed credit facilities.

Ezeani said many small business owners lacked the technical capacity and resources to satisfy the requirements imposed by commercial banks, even after obtaining preliminary approvals from development finance institutions.

He particularly criticised the lengthy process of obtaining letters of intent from commercial banks under intervention financing arrangements, arguing that the additional requirements often discouraged applicants.

He called for legislation establishing government-backed credit guarantee institutions to reduce the risks faced by banks lending to small businesses, particularly those without conventional collateral.

“Government institutions should have the capacity to give guarantees of almost zero collateral to the downtrodden,” he said.

Ezeani maintained that effective credit guarantee mechanisms, alongside simplified lending procedures, would encourage financial institutions to extend more credit to micro and small enterprises rather than concentrate largely on established businesses and corporate organisations.

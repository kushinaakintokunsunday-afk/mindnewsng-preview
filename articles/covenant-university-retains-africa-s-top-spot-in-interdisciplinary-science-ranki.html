Covenant University retains Africa’s top spot in interdisciplinary science ranking

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria has recorded its highest representation in the Times Higher Education Interdisciplinary Science Rankings, with 27 universities featuring in the 2027 edi

The Chairman of the Nigerian Tertiary Education Ranking Advisory Committee, Prof Emeritus Peter Okebukola, disclosed this in a statement issued on Wednesday, noting that Nigeria also improved its global position from 16th to 11th based on the number of universities ranked.

The 2027 ranking, released on November 18, assessed 1,058 universities across 102 countries and territories, with Nigeria retaining its second position in Africa behind Egypt, which recorded 37 ranked institutions.

Covenant University, Ota, Ogun State, retained its position as Africa’s leading university for interdisciplinary science, placing joint 49th globally, ahead of Egypt’s Cairo University, which came joint 52nd.

Covenant University was ranked 73rd globally in the 2025 edition before rising to joint 49th in 2026, maintaining its position in the global top 50 for the second consecutive year.

Landmark University, Omu-Aran, emerged as Nigeria’s second-best institution in the ranking, placing in the 251–300 band.

Ahmadu Bello University, Zaria, and the University of Ilorin followed in the 351–400 band, while the University of Calabar, the University of Lagos and the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, occupied the 401–500 band.

Ladoke Akintola University of Technology, Obafemi Awolowo University and the University of Benin completed Nigeria’s top 10, placing in the 501–600 band.

Okebukola said the latest performance reflected growing participation by Nigerian universities in international assessments but warned that increased representation had not translated into stronger overall performance across the ranking bands.

“We have good reason to be encouraged. More Nigerian universities are stepping forward to be measured against the best in the world, and one of our own has kept the first position in Africa,” he said.

He, however, noted that the number of Nigerian universities in the global top 300 declined from five in the previous edition to two, while those in the top 500 fell from eight to seven.

“The results also carry a lesson. As the field has grown from 749 universities in 2025 to 1,058 this year, competition has become keener and several of our established participants have slipped.

“Our task is to match wider participation with stronger performance,” he added.

The NiTERAC chairman identified inadequate funding for interdisciplinary research and weak institutional support structures as major factors affecting the performance of Nigerian universities.

He explained that many institutions recorded low scores in the inputs and process categories, which measure funding for interdisciplinary science, dedicated research facilities, administrative support and institutional policies that encourage collaboration across academic disciplines.

“These are matters within the reach of every university management. Full reporting of research grants and income from industry, and clear institutional policies on interdisciplinary research, can lift a university by one or two bands within a single edition, as this year’s results have shown,” he said.

Ahmadu Bello University and the University of Calabar recorded notable improvements, moving from the 601–800 band in 2026 to the 351–400 and 401–500 bands, respectively.

Eight universities made their debut in the ranking. They are the Federal University of Technology, Minna; Michael Okpara University of Agriculture, Umudike; Joseph Sarwuan Tarka University, Makurdi; the University of Jos; Rivers State University; Babcock University; Bells University of Technology; and Nile University of Nigeria.

Osun State University also returned to the ranking after a year’s absence.

Of the 27 Nigerian universities ranked, 14 are federally owned, seven are state-owned and six are privately owned. Seven other Nigerian institutions participated but were given Reporter status after submitting data.

The interdisciplinary science ranking, produced by Times Higher Education in association with Schmidt Science Fellows, measures universities’ capacity to support and deliver research that cuts across traditional academic disciplines.

It uses 11 performance indicators grouped into three broad areas: inputs, accounting for 19 per cent; processes, 16 per cent; and research outputs, 65 per cent.

The Massachusetts Institute of Technology, United States, retained the global first position for the third consecutive year.

Okebukola commended the Minister of Education, Dr Tunji Alausa, for prioritising the global competitiveness of Nigerian tertiary institutions and establishing NiTERAC to coordinate efforts towards improving their performance.

He disclosed that the committee had submitted a comprehensive report on the latest ranking, including recommendations, to the minister.

He added that NiTERAC, working with the League of Directors of Academic Planning and the Virtual Institute for Capacity Building in Higher Education, would support universities in preparing stronger submissions for the 2028 edition.

The committee also disclosed that the 2026 national ranking of universities, polytechnics and colleges of education was nearing completion, with the results scheduled for unveiling at a national conference on December 15, 2026.

Okebukola said the exercise was expected to strengthen data collection and healthy competition among institutions, adding that its impact could reflect in future international rankings.

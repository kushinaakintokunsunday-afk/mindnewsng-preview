Official denies MTN customers’ data theft

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Telecommunications Company, MTN Nigeria has insisted that stealing customers’ data is against its values, saying data usage depends on what individual users do

The company also disclosed that peoples’ lifestyle including appetite for various applications and how they are used may determine how fast their data runs, clarifying that the company does not compromise what it gives to subscribers.

Chief Customer Relations and Experience Officer, MTN Nigeria, Ugonwa Nwoye made the clarification while fielding questions from newsmen during an event to address complaints from the network subscribers tagged ‘Data On Trial’ held in Port Harcourt.

Nwoye also said with the advent of sophisticated phones, people are communicating with various equipment across the world to entertain themselves, to become more productive in their work, among others sometimes unaware that data is running out.

She stated: “Data on Trial” is something that we have put together so that we have a one-on-one, face-to-face interaction with our customers to discuss a burning topic. So a topic that our customers have been talking about to us in different formats is about how their data is used.

“They want to understand what uses data? Where did my data go? Why am I charged the way I’m being charged?

“They say that sometimes they sleep and they wake up and they don’t have data. This is what we’re here to discuss today. So, we’re doing it in a courtroom format.

“We have asked our customers to choose who is going to prosecute for them. Who is going to be the judge and we (MTN) are going to be on the defense. I am going to be on the defense, working with my colleagues to answer the questions that our customers have so that we have a fruitful dialogue.”

Nwoye said that through forum customers would learn more about how their data is used and how they can track the use of their data on their phone or on platforms the network has built them.

“So, we have tools that we have built for our customers so that they can check both on their phones and on the tools that we have to solve this burning problem of what is my data being used for? Those tools will help them know how to manage their data.

“One of the things we find is that all of us have a very high appetite for various kinds of applications. And we’re going to show you how those applications work and the tools that are within your hands already to reduce if you want the use of your data on those applications.

“We don’t steal data. MTN does not steal. We’re aside from the fact that we’re a public company it is not within any of our values to steal. So, when we say something is monthly, what we mean is that it is valid for one month,” she assured.

“Not that you can use it anyhow and it will last for one month. So, what is important to us is a common understanding.

“What we know is that ever since networks got faster and people started using the kinds of phones that you’re showing here, at the very beginning of GSM, you were using what we now call Balasa phones.

“Those phones did not even have the ability to take photographs,” Nwoye added: “Now, your phones not only have the ability to take photographs, the photographs are very high definition.

“Some people are already complaining that you guys are using your phones to steal the jobs of cameramen because the phones are now really sophisticated.

“The kinds of things that you like to do first thing in the morning when you join your prayer sessions or when you join school are things that actually use data.

“So your phone is actually communicating with various equipment all across the world to either entertain you, to bring you closer to the world, to make you more productive, to entertain you.

“Those things use data and some people are not always fully aware how much data they’re using. We’re here to dispel any issues today.”

Nwoye further said in the last two years MTN with the industry regulator has been exploring questions and carrying out campaigns on the internet educating customers how to control their WhatsApp and YouTube applications, adding, “We already know the kinds of applications that use a lot of data.

“What MTN decided to do was to take it to another notch, to decide we were going to have one-on-one conversations. One-on-one conversations sometimes are better than just talking, talking to you without you having the chance to actually interact. This is why you’re here today.”

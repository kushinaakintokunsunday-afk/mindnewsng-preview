Diaspora doctors seek medical missions to strengthen healthcare in Nigeria

By Punch Punch 5d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The President of ANPA, Dr Gertie Chimeka-Anyanwoke, said medical missions should be coordinated with state governments, ministries of health and healthcare facilities to enable visiting professionals to work with local medical teams and build their capacity to provide continuous care.

She made the call on Wednesday at the Redeemer’s Health Village in Mowe, Ogun State, during a global health roundtable involving healthcare leaders, health commissioners and other stakeholders.

Participants at the meeting discussed ways to improve healthcare delivery in Nigeria through diaspora engagement, emergency preparedness and technology.

Chimeka-Anyanwoke acknowledged the contributions of diaspora medical interventions to expanding access to healthcare services but stressed the need for structured partnerships that would enable local health workers to sustain the interventions after visiting professionals returned to their countries of residence.

She stated, “How can we in the diaspora contribute in ways that local partners value, that fit the communities you serve, and that continue after a mission ends? Chronic disease makes that question especially important. A screening event can identify high blood pressure, diabetes, or a possible cancer but what matters next is whether the person can access testing, treatment, medicines, and follow-up.

“The people and systems that make that continuity possible are already here. Our role should be to learn where support is most useful and work alongside those leading the care.

“We should measure success not only by how many people we reach during an outreach, but by whether patients remain connected to care and whether local teams have gained capacity they can continue to use.”

Also speaking, a former ANPA president, Dr Chinyere Anyaogu, said medical missions needed to move beyond occasional interventions and adopt a coordinated approach that would provide sustained support to healthcare facilities.

Anyaogu said, “What is crystallising is that we need to have a way whereby medical missions or diaspora interventions have a standardised process through the states to interface with healthcare facilities, so that it’s not incidental care.

“It is care that is interfaced with the on-ground teams so that they can sustain it after we’ve gone. They can have continuity.”

Anyaogu said working with local healthcare professionals would help visiting medical teams understand the country’s healthcare environment, operate within existing structures and identify areas requiring additional support.

She added that involving state ministries of health and healthcare commissions would improve coordination and ensure that knowledge acquired during medical missions translated into stronger local capacity.

According to her, diaspora doctors could contribute not only through direct patient care but also through training, skills transfer and other initiatives to improve healthcare delivery.

Anyaogu, an obstetrician and gynaecologist, said ANPA had sustained its commitment to healthcare collaboration, capacity building and advocacy through annual global outreach programmes and partnerships with other organisations.

She disclosed that the association, which had existed for more than 30 years, collaborated with organisations, including Rotary, to organise medical outreach programmes in Nigeria.

She said its relationships with state health authorities and other stakeholders had encouraged more diaspora medical professionals to participate in strengthening the country’s healthcare system.

“By doing that and building those relationships, we have more people who are able and willing to come back and participate in building a stronger healthcare system,” she stated.

Anyaogu said ANPA also prioritised capacity building through specialised training programmes aimed at improving the ability of Nigerian healthcare workers to manage emergencies.

She disclosed that the association conducted a Stop the Bleed training programme during its current mission to equip responders with skills to control severe bleeding in accident victims and other trauma patients before they reached hospitals.

According to her, about 12 trainers had been trained to subsequently train 200 participants in emergency bleeding controll, adding that approximately 15 healthcare workers received training in neonatal resuscitation to improve their ability to respond to newborns experiencing breathing difficulties or requiring respiratory support.

Anyaogu further disclosed that the association organised a menstrual hygiene skills camp where 40 girls were taught how to make reusable menstrual packs.

She said the initiatives highlighted the importance of combining medical outreach with practical training that could continue benefiting communities after visiting teams had left.

On emergency medical services, the former ANPA president called for stronger response infrastructure supported by technology.

She said technology could help identify hospitals equipped to receive emergency patients, warning that transferring critically ill people to facilities unable to manage their conditions could lead to dangerous delays.

She said, “There’s no point in having an emergency and then moving the patient to the hospital, only for the hospital to be unable to absorb the patient, and you go from hospital to hospital.

“Now we have technology. Perhaps there’s a way we can be smarter with that and even find a way to help relieve the hospitals of the turnover, thereby creating more capacity to absorb these emergencies.”

Also speaking, the immediate past National President of the Association of Nigerian Private Medical Practitioners, Dr Kayode Adesola, said Nigeria’s emergency medical response system was still developing and required sustained support to achieve its objectives.

Adesola called for increased investment, trained personnel and properly equipped emergency facilities, while urging greater public and private sector participation in strengthening the system.

Federal Government has existing policies aimed at making diaspora medical missions more coordinated and sustainable.

The National Diaspora Policy mandates the Federal Ministry of Health to implement national standards and guidelines for medical missions, identify healthcare needs and facilitate skills and knowledge transfer from Nigerian professionals abroad.

In July 2026, the ministry also launched the Nigerians in Diaspora Advanced Health Programme, a platform designed to connect Nigerian health professionals abroad with local healthcare institutions for specialist care, training, mentorship, and capacity building.

State police: Senate demands safeguards against abuse

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 5d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

• Presses Tinubu to submit operational bill defining powers of federal, state police  • President receives constitutional amendment endorsed by 36 states • AGF to review bill before presidential assent

By Juliana Taiwo-Obalonye and Adesuwa Tsan, Abuja

Amid longstanding concerns over the possible abuse of state police by governors and the danger of conflicting authority between federal and state security agencies, the Senate has called for urgent legislation to clearly define the powers, responsibilities and operational boundaries of the proposed decentralised policing system.

The upper chamber warned that the constitutional amendment establishing state police must be complemented by a comprehensive operational law to prevent jurisdictional disputes and ensure that the new security arrangement does not create conflicts between the Nigeria Police Force and police services to be established by the 36 states.

The demand came as President Bola Tinubu received the Constitution (Sixth Alteration) Bill, 2026, following its approval by all 36 state Houses of Assembly, clearing a major constitutional hurdle in the proposed restructuring of Nigeria’s policing architecture.

Although the amendment has secured the required legislative endorsements, concerns have persisted over the possibility of state governors deploying police formations against political opponents, intimidating dissenting voices or interfering in electoral processes, particularly in states where political competition is intense.

The fears have featured prominently in the national debate over state police, with opponents of decentralised policing warning that placing armed security personnel under the control of state authorities without adequate safeguards could expose citizens to political intimidation and abuse of power.

Supporters, however, have maintained that state police would improve intelligence gathering, strengthen community policing and enable faster responses to security threats, particularly in rural communities where federal police presence is inadequate.

Against this background, the Senate insisted that the proposed National Policing Bill must establish clear lines of authority, operational responsibilities and mechanisms for cooperation between the two policing structures before the new arrangement becomes operational.

Leading debate on a motion of urgent public importance, Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele urged Tinubu to forward the operational legislation to the National Assembly for scrutiny and passage, stressing that constitutional recognition of state police alone would not resolve the practical challenges of implementing the system.

Bamidele said the legislation was necessary to define the powers and responsibilities of state police services, establish their relationship with the Nigeria Police Force and provide safeguards against jurisdictional conflicts.

He urged the chamber to mandate the Clerk to the National Assembly, Kamoru Ogunlana, to transmit the constitutional amendment to the President in accordance with the Acts Authentication Act, 2004, while formally requesting the submission of the National Policing Bill for expeditious consideration.

“Thank Mr. President for taking ownership and boldly supporting the Bill throughout the process; and also urge him to forward the National Policing Bill that will enable the operationalisation of subnational policing, for legislative scrutiny and expeditious passage by the National Assembly,” Bamidele said.

Senate President Godswill Akpabio reinforced the demand, warning that the next critical stage of the reform must address the relationship between federal and state police authorities to avoid overlapping powers and operational disagreements.

“The next step is to address the operationalisation of the system and ensure that there is no conflict between the State Police and the Federal Police.” Akpabio described the constitutional amendment as a major achievement of the 10th National Assembly, expressing confidence that state police could contribute significantly to tackling insecurity across the country.

“This is a legislation that will not be forgotten. It is a major achievement of the 10th National Assembly.”

Akpabio also expressed optimism that the establishment of state police could address about 70 per cent of Nigeria’s security challenges, although he acknowledged that the success of the initiative would depend on an effective operational framework.

Deputy Senate President, Barau Jibrin, said the proposed state police system was intended to complement, rather than replace, the Nigeria Police Force, arguing that cooperation between both institutions would strengthen the country’s response to security threats.

“This is a collective achievement that deserves to be celebrated. The proposed State Police system is designed to complement the operations of the Nigeria Police Force, strengthening our overall policing structure and enhancing our capacity to address security challenges across the country,” Jibrin said.

Meanwhile, the Presidency has confirmed that Tinubu had formally received the constitutional amendment after its endorsement by all 36 state legislatures, surpassing the minimum approval of 24 states required under Section 9(2) of the Constitution.

Chief of Staff to the President, Femi Gbajabiamila, disclosed in a statement on Wednesday that the bill was transmitted to the President by his Special Advisers on Senate and House of Representatives Matters, Basheer Lado and Wasiu Olanrewaju-Smart.

Gbajabiamila said the President would review the amendment in consultation with the Attorney-General of the Federation before giving his assent at a formal signing ceremony.

He explained that the proposed National Policing Bill, which would establish the legal and administrative framework for the new security structure, was still undergoing executive consideration.

As Chairman of the Presidential Working Group on State Police, Gbajabiamila disclosed that the group had completed extensive stakeholder engagements and nationwide consultations towards developing the operational legislation.

He said the draft had been submitted to the Federal Ministry of Justice for legislative drafting validation, after which it would be reviewed by the working group and presented to the President.

“Upon receipt of the validated version, the Working Group will review it and present it, alongside its report, to Mr President for consideration,” he said.

Gbajabiamila commended the leadership and members of the National Assembly, the 36 state governors, state legislatures and other stakeholders for their contributions to the constitutional amendment process.

The renewed push for an operational law comes against the background of decades of controversy over decentralised policing in Nigeria, with successive administrations confronting demands for state-controlled security formations as insecurity spreads across the country.

While advocates have consistently argued that policing should be brought closer to communities to improve intelligence gathering and response times, critics have pointed to the country’s political history and the potential for governors to use state-controlled security agencies to suppress opposition activities, influence elections and settle political disputes.

The controversy has also raised questions about the independence of state police leadership, recruitment standards, funding, disciplinary procedures, accountability and mechanisms for resolving disputes between federal and state authorities.

These concerns have made the proposed National Policing Bill a critical component of the reform, as lawmakers seek to establish a framework that would strengthen local security without creating competing centres of coercive authority.

With the constitutional amendment now before Tinubu, attention is shifting to the proposed operational legislation and the safeguards it will provide against political interference, abuse of authority and jurisdictional conflicts between the two policing structures.

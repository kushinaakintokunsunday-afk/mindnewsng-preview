18 countries visit Nigeria to study data protection framework – Olatunji

From Adanna Nnamani, Abuja

The National Data Protection Commission (NDPC) says at least 18 countries have visited Nigeria in the last two years to study its data protection framework and learn from the country’s experience in regulating the sector.

The National Commissioner of the NDPC, Dr Vincent Olatunji, disclosed this on Friday in Abuja during a training programme organised for journalists by the commission.

Olatunji said Nigeria’s approach to data protection regulation had attracted international attention, particularly its Public-Private Partnership (PPP) model, which allows licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisations (DPCOs) to carry out compliance activities on behalf of the government.

He said the model had been recognised globally as unique and impactful, contributing significantly to the progress recorded in Nigeria’s data protection ecosystem.

According to him, Nigeria has also recorded more than 27,000 jobs in the data privacy ecosystem in less than three years, with the commission targeting the creation of about 500,000 jobs in the sector.

He said: “For this ecosystem, which is a sub-sector of the economic sector, we are saying that over 500,000 jobs are available in the data privacy ecosystem. And we are already pushing towards achieving that.

“If within the ecosystem we are doing over 27,000 jobs in less than three years, that is massive. That is very huge. But the target is to get to about 500,000 and even more.”

Olatunji also disclosed that Nigeria currently has more than 10,000 certified Data Protection Officers (DPOs), compared with fewer than 1,000 when the commission began its operations.

He said the growing demand for data protection professionals presented an opportunity for journalists to acquire additional skills and explore career opportunities without necessarily abandoning their profession.

The NDPC boss explained that journalists could become certified DPOs, conduct research and provide data protection services to organisations, including media establishments.

He urged participants to take the training seriously, noting that data protection was becoming an increasingly important global skill, with organisations across different sectors seeking qualified professionals.

Olatunji further said the commission was extending data privacy awareness and training to tertiary institutions to educate students about their rights and introduce them to emerging career opportunities.

He noted that about 94.7 per cent of data breaches in the United Kingdom were linked to a lack of qualified personnel in data privacy and protection, underscoring the global demand for professionals in the field.

On the commission’s regulatory approach, Olatunji said the NDPC prioritised compliance and remediation rather than imposing fines as a first response to violations.

He explained that the commission regarded organisations and other stakeholders as partners, adding that sanctions would be applied when operators crossed established legal boundaries.

According to him, the objective is to build a culture of compliance while ensuring that regulation supports economic growth and development rather than driving businesses out of operation.

He also said Nigeria’s data protection framework was attracting international partnerships, with countries including Egypt and Qatar expressing interest in collaborating with the commission.

Olatunji urged journalists to play a strategic role in promoting awareness of data protection rights and responsibilities, stressing that the effective protection of personal data was essential to Nigeria’s digital economy.

Earlier, the President of the Nigeria Information Technology Reporters Association (NITRA), Mr Blessing Olaifa, commended the commission for its partnership with the media and called for more opportunities for journalists to participate in its capacity-building programmes.

Olaifa said the training had exposed participants to data protection issues that journalists might previously have overlooked, particularly in the publication and republication of reports.

He also expressed interest in the opportunity for journalists to become certified Data Protection Officers, urging the commission to extend the initiative to more members of the association.

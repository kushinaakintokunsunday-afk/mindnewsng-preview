‘Ban Nigerians from our country’: South African protesters issue fresh demand

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 5d ago 3 min read

A group of anti-immigrant protesters in South Africa has called on the government to ban Nigerians from entering the country, accusing them of contributing to drug-related problems.

The demonstrators, led by the March and March group, staged a protest in Pretoria on Wednesday, where they demanded that Nigerian immigrants be barred from the country.

The group’s leader, Jacinta Ngobese-Zuma, also questioned the growing Nigerian presence in Sunnyside, a suburb of Pretoria often referred to as “Little Lagos” because of its Nigerian community.

She challenged Nigerians living in the area to explain why it had acquired the nickname.

“I’m being told here that we’ve entered Sunnyside, the home of Nigerians. I’m inviting Nigerians to come out let’s talk to each other.

“We want to know who gave you this place and called it Nigeria. Who gave you permission to call this place Lagos or whatever you call it?” she said.

The group’s legal representative, Xolani Zuma, called on the South African government to stop Nigerians from entering the country.

“The state must ban Nigerians from South Africa. They must ban Nigerians until they stop selling drugs to our people.

“The rest of Africa we are fine, but we do not want Nigerians in South Africa anymore, that is what we are saying,” he said.

The protesters also want South Africa to withdraw from the 1951 United Nations Convention relating to the Status of Refugees.

Ngobese-Zuma described the agreement as “not sustainable and not feasible”, arguing that migrants put pressure on public resources and contribute to frustration among South African citizens.

She said the protesters were heading to the Home Office to present their demands.

The demonstration comes amid fresh diplomatic tension between Nigeria and South Africa following a disagreement over the exclusion of a speech by Nigeria’s High Commissioner, Femi Fani-Kayode, during his presentation of credentials.

Fani-Kayode had described the omission as disrespectful to Nigeria. However, he later said the issue had been resolved after the intervention of South African President Cyril Ramaphosa and an invitation from the Department of International Relations and Cooperation.

Since assuming office, the Nigerian envoy has said he has been looking into issues affecting Nigerians living in South Africa.

He has also described those responsible for xenophobic attacks as a small minority, insisting that they do not represent the majority of South Africans.

More than 1,500 Nigerians have reportedly been repatriated from South Africa this year following xenophobic attacks that left at least four Nigerians dead.

Although Fani-Kayode has ruled out Nigeria severing diplomatic relations with South Africa, he warned that patience within the Nigerian government was running thin.

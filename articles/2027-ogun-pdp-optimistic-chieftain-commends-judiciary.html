2027: Ogun PDP optimistic, chieftain commends judiciary

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Peoples Democratic Party in Ogun State has expressed confidence in its chances in the 2027 governorship election, with its campaign spokesperson, Wale Adeda

Similarly, a party chieftain in the state, Alhaji Fasiu Bakene, commended the Federal High Court judgment directing the Independent National Electoral Commission to restore Adebutu’s candidacy, describing it as a boost to confidence in the judiciary and an opportunity for the PDP to participate in the 2027 election.

The Federal High Court in Abuja had, on Friday, ordered INEC to restore Adebutu’s name as the PDP governorship candidate in Ogun State, paving the way for his participation in the 2027 election.

Speaking during an appearance on Arise Television, monitored on Sunday, Adedayo dismissed the influence of federal might in the election, insisting that it would not prevent the PDP from securing victory.

He said the APC’s federal might would be defeated hands down in Ogun, the same way it happened in Osun State.

“The federal might was there in 2023, it didn’t work. The federal might was there in Osun State, and it didn’t work, and it won’t work again this time,” he declared.

According to Adedayo, the party had the support of voters across the state, including some people currently associated with the All Progressives Congress.

He said that “The PDP actually won in 2023, but the poll was manipulated. The PDP will clear between 18 and 19 local government areas in Ogun. I can assure you of that. Adebutu will win Ogun East, Adebutu will win the six LGAs of Ogun Central and a minimum of two in Ogun West.

“It is not the noise that makes things happen. When it comes to the real show, those who are following them know the party they will vote for.”

He added that the hardship across the country had been rejected by all, saying most of those being paid to attend APC campaigns would eventually cast their ballots for Adebutu.

Adedayo equally hailed the Judiciary for allowing democracy to thrive by returning Adebutu to INEC’s list of candidates.

He said the APC was jittery and already realising the landslide defeat it would suffer in the poll next year, which was why the party was plotting to edge out Adebutu in the contest.

Faulting Adebutu’s initial removal from the list, he said, “You can’t mention PDP in Ogun without talking about Ladi Adebutu. The electoral umpire made itself a laughing stock by coming out to say his name was not on the list. INEC brought a hard copy to court while the list of names submitted was in soft copy.

“The PDP submitted the register and not INEC. It is the party that can prove who is a member and who is not a member. It is not INEC.

“The judgment of the court was very sound. INEC should have written to the party that submitted the list rather than removing his name from the list.

“INEC monitored the primary that the same Ladi Adebutu won, and INEC updated Adebutu’s name as the winner of the primary.”

He said the removal of Adebutu’s name did not stop the campaign activities of the party, as Adebutu and his running mate, Hajia Lateefat Sowunmi-Kolapo, continued to tour villages and towns, canvassing for votes and selling their manifesto.

“Those who orchestrated the thing wanted to demoralise members, but Ladi Adebutu continued with his campaign, and that is how he has been going round all villages and towns in Ogun State, meeting with the people.

“It didn’t stop our programme; we have been moving around. Seeing the leaders coming out to campaign boosted the morale of PDP members and supporters. There won’t be any battle in Ogun State,” he said.

Adedayo said the people were craving for grassroots development, which Adebutu had promised, including local government autonomy.

“Ogun State is on a true path for real development because all our health centres must work. The farm settlement must be revived by all means; their output in terms of food production must increase, and there will be potable water in all villages.

“This will be easily achieved through the local government autonomy. Once the local government and councillors do their work, the people won’t have any problem,” he said.

On the ancestry issue raised against the APC candidate, Solomon Adeola, popularly known as Yayi, Adedayo said the people of the state were still expecting the APC candidate to clear himself and tell them his real identity and origin.

In a separate statement, Alahaji Bakene commended the Federal High Court judgment, describing it as a development that had restored confidence in the judiciary.

Bakene, therefore, urged the INEC to uphold the rule of law and ensure fairness in its preparations and conduct of the 2027 elections.

“We thank God for the landmark judgment, and we use this occasion to appreciate all citizens of Ogun State and lovers of true democracy who showed concern in the matter before the judgment,” he said.

He added that the restoration of Adebutu’s name had renewed confidence in the judiciary, which he described as a source of hope for ordinary citizens.

He also urged the APC-led government in Ogun State to allow opposition parties to campaign freely without intimidation, attacks or denial of access to public facilities.

Bakene said residents should be allowed to exercise their democratic rights and vote for candidates of their choice.

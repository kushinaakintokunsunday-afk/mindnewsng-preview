Lagos APC, ADC trade words over alleged unpaid legal fees

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Lagos State chapters of the All Progressives Congress and the African Democratic Congress have traded words over allegations that lawyers handling more than

While the APC questioned the ADC’s financial management and accountability over the alleged outstanding fees, the opposition party accused the ruling party of meddling in its internal affairs and expressed concerns about its interest in cases involving the ADC.

The Lagos APC spokesman, Mogaji Seye Oladejo, raised the concerns in a statement on Saturday while reacting to reports that members of the Coalition of West African Journalists had expressed worries over the alleged unpaid legal fees.

Oladejo described the allegations as embarrassing, arguing that a political party aspiring to govern Nigeria should demonstrate financial discipline and the capacity to meet its organisational obligations.

He said the situation was particularly troubling because some of the lawyers reportedly affected were members of the ADC’s leadership structure.

According to him, the allegations followed earlier reports of unpaid salaries involving workers at the party’s national secretariat.

He said, “If the ADC cannot honour its financial commitments to lawyers engaged to defend its own political interests, what exactly is the basis of its confidence that it can responsibly manage the vastly more complex financial obligations of the Federal Republic of Nigeria?”

The APC spokesman also questioned how the opposition party could inspire public confidence while allegedly struggling to settle its internal financial obligations and manage its leadership disputes and legal battles.

He challenged the ADC National Chairman, Senator David Mark, and National Secretary, Rauf Aregbesola, to clarify whether the legal fees were outstanding, disclose the total amount involved and explain the arrangements for settling the affected lawyers.

“Nigerians deserve clear answers. Are the reported legal fees outstanding? What is the total liability? What arrangements have been made to settle the affected lawyers? And what safeguards exist to prevent a recurrence of these troubling allegations?” he asked.

However, responding to the allegations, the Lagos ADC Chairman, George Ashiru, accused the APC of concerning itself with the internal affairs of opposition parties instead of addressing its own challenges.

Speaking with PUNCH Online, Ashiru said the ruling party’s interest in the ADC’s affairs suggested an attempt to plant moles within the opposition party to obtain information for the APC.

He said, “APC should face its woes and not mind other party’s internal affairs. This interest in other party’s affairs suggests an interest in inserting moles within the ADC in order to feed the APC back.”

Ashiru also described the relationship between the ADC and its lawyers as a legal and business arrangement, questioning why the APC was speaking on behalf of unnamed lawyers.

“The business relationship between lawyers and the Party is purely a legal and business affair and the APC becoming a spokesperson for unnamed lawyers is in itself a worrying admission of their interest in the judicial processes,” he said.

The ADC chairman further expressed concern that the APC’s comments could suggest that it had some form of communication with the party’s legal representatives.

“Our hope is that the APC is not teleguiding the outcome of court cases by its own admission of having enough interest with court cases involving our party and therefore demonstrating they have some kind of communication with our legal representation,” he added.

He maintained that the ADC was not involved in any court dispute with the APC and questioned the ruling party’s interest in its ongoing cases.

“As far as I know the ADC is not in courts with the APC, so why exactly is the APC pretending to have interest in court cases involving the ADC, unless they have an insidious interest in subverting the judicial processes,” he said.

However, Ashiru later contacted PUNCH Online to clarify the party’s position, saying the ADC owed no lawyer for services rendered.

He said the party had confirmed that it had no outstanding legal fees, adding that its National Publicity Secretary, Bolaji Abdullahi, had also stated the position.

Oladejo had urged the ADC to demonstrate transparency and accountability before seeking Nigerians’ mandate in the 2027 general elections, adding that the national treasury should not become a financial lifeline for a political organisation struggling to manage its affairs.

The APC statement did not provide documentary evidence of the alleged outstanding legal fees.

CIPM urges HR professionals to drive public service reform

By Punch Punch 3d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Chartered Institute of Personnel Management of Nigeria has urged human resource professionals in the public service to move beyond routine personnel adminis

The institute said traditional personnel management practices were no longer sufficient to address the challenges facing public institutions, calling for greater emphasis on workforce productivity, employee development, ethical leadership and institutional efficiency.

CIPM disclosed this in a news release on Saturday, following its 74th Induction Ceremony in Lagos, where 134 professionals were admitted through its Executive and Practitioners’ routes.

The President and Chairman of the Governing Council of CIPM, Mallam Ahmed Gobir, said professional HR practice should translate into better decisions, stronger workplace values and improved contributions to organisations and society.

“The letters after your name, either ACIPM or MCIPM, must be supported by the values behind your decisions,” Gobir said.

He urged the inductees to challenge outdated administrative practices and promote employee development, performance management and institutional effectiveness, stressing that the public service should be driven by the needs of citizens.

“A government office can be fully staffed and still leave citizens underserved,” he said, adding that people seeking government services were the reason public institutions existed and should not be treated as interruptions to public servants’ work.

Gobir also described professional membership as a responsibility to uphold standards and strengthen the institute’s reputation through ethical conduct and sound decision-making.

Delivering the guest presentation, the Council Manager and Head of Administration at Agege Local Government, Mr Osikoya Adesina Sogo, called for a shift from routine personnel administration to strategic workforce management, identifying changes needed to improve public sector performance.

He advocated replacing reactive staff deployment with data-driven workforce planning, generic training with targeted skills development, and reactive disciplinary measures with stronger employee engagement and organisational culture.

Sogo also called for a shift from procedural compliance to stronger governance and ethical responsibility, noting that personnel decisions at the local government level had direct implications for service delivery.

He said staff deployment, training, personnel records and industrial relations influenced the quality of services provided by local authorities, urging HR professionals to balance performance standards with employee dignity, fairness and respect for due process.

The Vice President of CIPM, Mrs Chidinma Obiejesi, said human capital strategy was critical to public service transformation and called for stronger collaboration with the Lagos State Government to advance reforms in the state’s public sector.

The institute’s National Treasurer, Mr Adeyemi Ajayi, also urged the new members to contribute to its development through mentoring, coaching, continuous learning and participation in its professional activities.

A total of 134 professionals were inducted at the ceremony, comprising eight admitted through the Executive Route and 126 through the Practitioners’ Route.

CIPM said stronger public institutions depended on competent, ethical and professionally managed workforces, adding that the value of professional recognition should be reflected in practitioners’ contributions to their workplaces and society.

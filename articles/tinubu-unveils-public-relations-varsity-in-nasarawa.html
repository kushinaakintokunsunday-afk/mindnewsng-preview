Tinubu unveils public relations varsity in Nasarawa

By Punch Punch 6d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

President Bola Tinubu on Wednesday formally unveiled the University of Public Relations and Leadership in Nasarawa State, as Nigeria seeks to strengthen its inf

Tinubu was represented by Vice-President Kashim Shettima at the ceremony organised by Global Alliance World Public Relations Forum in Abuja.

The event attracted public relations practitioners, communication experts, government officials and business leaders from different parts of the world.

Shettima, speaking on behalf of the President, said, “It’s my singular honour and privilege to, on behalf of my boss, Bola Ahmed Tinubu, President and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, unveil and launch the University of Public Relations and Leadership in Nasarawa State.”

In his address, Tinubu described the proposed institution as a lasting legacy of Nigeria’s successful bid to host the global forum, saying it would advance specialised education, research, professional development and leadership training in public relations and strategic communication.

“In this regard, I am pleased to formally approve and support the establishment of the University of Public Relations and Leadership, a first-of-its-kind initiative designed to advance specialised education, research, professional development and leadership training in public relations, strategic communications and related disciplines,” he said.

The President said the university would provide opportunities to develop a new generation of communication leaders equipped to respond to the demands of a rapidly changing global environment.

He commended the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations, the Federal Ministry of Information and National Orientation and the Nasarawa State Government for their collaboration towards making the project a reality.

Tinubu also urged stakeholders to ensure that the institution develops into a centre of excellence with national, continental and international recognition.

Beyond the university announcement, the President challenged public relations professionals to uphold truth, transparency and accountability in an era when digital technologies and artificial intelligence are transforming how information is created, distributed and consumed.

Tinubu also used the international gathering to project Nigeria’s economic potential, urging foreign delegates to look beyond the conference venue and explore investment opportunities in the country’s entrepreneurial ecosystem, creative industries, tourism and other sectors.

The President urged the delegates to establish relationships with Nigerian institutions and businesses, explore partnerships and become ambassadors of the country’s positive image.

“We want you to leave Nigeria, Prof Justin Green, not only with new professional knowledge and friendships, but also with a deeper appreciation of our country and a renewed interest in its prospects.

“We hope that your experience here will encourage you to return, invest, collaborate, and become ambassadors of Nigeria’s positive story,” he said.

Earlier, the President of the Global Alliance for Public Relations and Communication Management, Prof Justin Green, disclosed how Nigerian officials mounted an intense push to secure the hosting rights of the 2026 forum.

Green said the President of the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations, Dr Ike Neliaku, the Minister of Information and National Orientation and the Nigerian Ambassador to Ireland repeatedly called him during the final stage of the bidding process.

He said, “As President of Global Alliance, I must be impartial to all country member bids. I’m not allowed a vote. But during the final scoring process, the adjudicating panel had only a few meters to go. Africa and Nigeria were doing well. But so too were other continents.”

According to Green, he initially declined Neliaku’s calls to avoid any suggestion of influencing the adjudication process.

The situation became more delicate when the Minister of Information also called him, prompting him to cancel the calls three times.

He said, “I didn’t know what to do. So I cancelled his call, not once, not twice, but three times. I’m sorry, Minister.”

Green said the Nigerian Ambassador to Ireland subsequently called him as the country intensified its efforts to secure the hosting rights.

He added, “This is how determined Nigeria was to secure the hosting rights for the whole of Africa. It is this African and Nigerian fighting spirit to win that has the world in Abuja today.”

Welcoming the delegates, Green said Nigeria had defeated competing countries to secure the hosting rights, adding that the event had brought the world to Abuja.

In a light-hearted moment, Green declared himself Nigerian for the duration of the event and produced a Nigerian passport, saying he is now a citizen of both Ireland and Nigeria.

“Today, today we are no longer visitors from around the world. We are all Nigerians. So, Your Excellency, I surrender my Irish passport to the Minister of Foreign Affairs to collect my new Nigerian passport,” he said.

Also speaking, the President of the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations, Dr Ike Neliaku, urged Africans to be more assertive in projecting the continent’s contributions to global civilisation.

Neliaku said Africa must stop allowing others to define its history and identity, insisting that the continent had a responsibility to confidently tell its own story.

“As you are aware, though they don’t want to agree, civilisation started from Africa. And we must be bold in saying so,” he said.

He explained that the decision to host the forum on a grand scale reflected the organisers’ determination to demonstrate Africa’s capacity to stage major international events.

Neliaku also acknowledged the contributions of international delegates who travelled from countries including France, Australia, Turkey, Malaysia, Kenya, Peru, Bangladesh, the United States and South Africa.

He said the organisers had adopted ‘No Wahala’ as their rallying expression, adding that they wanted the gathering to be remembered as the biggest, brightest and boldest.

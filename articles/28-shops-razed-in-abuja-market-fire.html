28 shops razed in Abuja market fire

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

About 45 traders have been affected by a fire outbreak at the Gwagwalada International Market in the Federal Capital Territory, with 28 open stalls and two lock

The FCT Emergency Management Department disclosed this in a statement on Monday, following a post-incident assessment conducted by its Director, Muhammad Dikko, alongside officials of the National Emergency Management Agency.

The fire occurred last Saturday, with an eyewitness video showing flames engulfing parts of the market at about 7pm. The footage, shared on Facebook, showed the fire still burning as the eyewitness called on emergency responders to intervene.

PUNCH had reported on Sunday that the fire ravaged the market, with the cause yet to be established at the time.

However, during the post-incident assessment, the Chairman of the Market Association, Aminu Lawal Bakinruwa, told the delegation that security personnel on duty noticed smoke coming from one of the shops about 20 minutes after electricity supply was restored to the market by the Abuja Electricity Distribution Company.

Some of the affected traders expressed distress over their losses, describing the incident as a major setback to their businesses and livelihoods.

One of the victims, Salome Israel, said, “She lost goods worth about N400,000, including yams, tomatoes and peppers,” the statement said.

Other items destroyed in the fire included clothing, herbal medicines and beverages.

Addressing the traders, Dikko “urged market operators to prioritise fire safety by ensuring that all electrical appliances were switched off before leaving their shops”, the statement said.

He also stressed the importance of insurance coverage for businesses, noting that traders with insurance policies would be better positioned to recover from losses arising from disasters.

The FEMD director further called for the provision of adequate firefighting equipment in the market to ensure prompt intervention during emergencies.

He appealed to the Market Board of Trustees and the leadership of the traders’ union to explore ways of supporting the victims and alleviating their hardship.

Speaking on behalf of NEMA, Mariam Dantanko explained that the agency’s disaster response assistance was primarily provided through relief materials rather than direct cash payments.

She said such interventions could include food and building materials, but would depend on the outcome of the assessment and approval by the agency’s headquarters.

The statement added that FEMD’s Relief and Rehabilitation Division was also at the scene to conduct a needs assessment to determine the immediate requirements of the affected traders.

The department reiterated the need for regular inspection of electrical installations, fire safety awareness and functional firefighting equipment in markets and other public facilities to prevent avoidable fire outbreaks.

FG considers electricity tariff model review, rules out hike

By Punch Punch 1d ago 2 min read

The Federal Government is reviewing assumptions underpinning Nigeria’s electricity tariff model and considering changes to the framework, while ruling out an increase in electricity tariffs in 2027.

The Minister of Power, Joseph Tegbe, disclosed this during an interview on ARISE News on Monday, amid concerns over mounting debts in the electricity sector and the government’s plans to address the financial challenges affecting power generation and distribution.

Tegbe said the government was exploring alternative measures to address the sector’s financial difficulties without placing additional financial burdens on electricity consumers.

“Let me make it categorically clear to Nigerians. And I’ll say it again. There’s no plan to increase tariff,” the minister said.

He explained that his priority was to improve electricity access, adding that the government was considering a concessionary gas pricing arrangement for power generation companies.

“I even have, to be honest with you, the most important thing for me, is how do I get electricity to Nigeria? And I’ll tell you, Mr President is saying, we own the gas. People need to give Jenko’s gas at a particular concessionary rate that is not going to make gas suppliers go out of business. Let’s look at the model,” he said.

The minister also disclosed that the government was reviewing assumptions built into the electricity tariff model, suggesting that some could be revised.

“Also, we’re looking at assumptions that have been built into the tariff model. There’s a possibility that some of those assumptions may need to be revised. But rule out tariff increase,” Tegbe said.

He maintained that the government would reconsider existing policies and arrangements in the power sector to improve electricity supply.

“I’m re-evaluating many things. And I have no boundary. The only boundary and guiding principle I have is re-evaluate everything, re-jig whatever you can re-jig, as long as you can give Nigeria electricity access.

“I will tell you, tariff increase is not on the plates. Tell Nigerians for me,” he added.

The assurance comes as the Federal Government continues to grapple with outstanding obligations to power generation companies and other financial challenges across the electricity value chain.

Recall that in September, the minister ruled out any increase in electricity tariffs, noting that the government was focused on improving electricity supply and strengthening the financial and physical foundations of the power sector rather than imposing additional costs on consumers.

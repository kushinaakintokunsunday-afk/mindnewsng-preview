MSMEs face $4tn financing gap in developing countries – World Bank

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Micro, small and medium-sized enterprises in developing economies face a $4tn financing gap, limiting their ability to expand operations and create jobs, the Wo

The funding shortfall, estimated over the 2019 to 2023 period, highlights the difficulty smaller businesses face in securing credit despite their importance to employment and economic activity in emerging markets and developing economies.

According to the lender, MSMEs account for about 90 per cent of businesses and 75 per cent of workers in these economies, making access to finance a key factor in efforts to support private-sector growth and absorb millions of young people entering the labour market.

The bank said about 1.2 billion young people would reach working age in the next 10 to 15 years, increasing pressure on developing economies to create employment opportunities. Improving access to finance for smaller businesses could help them expand and take on more workers.

The financier’s findings show that formal loans can have a significant effect on business performance. Firms that obtain such credit record an average 12 per cent increase in employment, while sales and profits rise by about 18 per cent.

“MSMEs face four major constraints to growth: they are invisible to financial systems because they lack a digital footprint of their transactions, disconnected from markets and services, burdened by regulatory complexity, and chronically underfinanced,” the World Bank Group’s Vice President for Prosperity, Pablo Saavedra said.

He said the institution’s new MSME strategy would use its financing alongside private lenders and investors to increase credit access for businesses with the greatest potential to grow.

The strategy, launched in October 2026, seeks to address barriers that prevent smaller firms from accessing capital and connecting with markets. It includes the use of data and digital tools to improve businesses’ visibility to lenders, as well as support for banks, fintech companies and governments.

The World Bank plans to deploy risk-sharing arrangements and equity investments to encourage lending and investment in smaller businesses. It also intends to work with governments to reduce regulatory obstacles, with particular attention to fragile and conflict-affected countries where access to finance is often more constrained.

The financing challenge is part of a wider set of concerns about the resilience of financial systems in developing economies, according to the World Bank’s Finance and Prosperity 2026 report.

The report examines how financial sectors are performing amid the effects of conflict in the Middle East on commodity prices, financial conditions and market volatility. It points to uneven resilience across markets and highlights risks associated with sovereign debt exposure among banks.

The bank said stronger prudential supervision and effective safety nets were important to maintaining financial stability as economies navigate external shocks and changing market conditions.

The report also draws attention to the limited availability of insurance protection in developing regions. In parts of Africa, Asia and Latin America, more than 80 per cent of financial losses recorded over the past decade were uninsured, leaving households, businesses and governments exposed to the cost of disasters.

It recommends measures to reduce risks and expand access to affordable disaster insurance, arguing that stronger protection could help limit the economic damage caused by climate-related and other shocks.

The World Bank’s focus on MSME financing reflects the wider challenge facing developing economies: turning the large share of economic activity accounted for by smaller businesses into sustainable employment and growth. Without better access to capital, many firms may struggle to invest, expand, and take advantage of opportunities in their markets.

NDC defends Enugu governorship canddate’s manifesto

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Nigeria Democratic Congress in Enugu State has dismissed criticism of its governorship manifesto, stating that no law requires cost estimates to be included

Responding to an online report questioning the feasibility of Chinyeaka Ohaa’s “The Rescue Mission” manifesto, Ohaa’s spokesperson, Steve Eboh, said in a rejoinder issued on Saturday that the 1999 Constitution, Electoral Act 2026 and INEC guidelines did not mandate cost breakdowns.

Ohaa is the governorship candidate of the NDC in the 2027 general election.

“It is generally not mandatory for a political party manifesto to include cost estimates,” Eboh said, adding: “Manifestos are primarily pre-election documents outlining a party’s ideology, goals, and proposed policies instead of formal budgets.”

He described the document as a directional blueprint, saying, “The manifesto establishes political priorities, governance philosophy, and measurable service targets, while exact Naira figures belong to the statutory annual budgeting process after assuming office.”

He noted: “Publishing fixed project costs without access to official state books… risks misleading voters with arbitrary numbers.”

Eboh outlined a Market Traders Recovery Fund for affected traders at Ogige, Garriki, Holy Ghost and Abakpa markets, as well as the Nnamdi Azikiwe Stadium.

He said resource mobilisation would include blocking revenue leakages through digitised land records, reprioritising capital expenditure away from “white elephant projects in urban centres”, leveraging private investment in coal, natural gas and agro-industrial hubs, and transparent borrowing.

In a separate statement, the NDC accused the ruling All Progressives Congress and Governor Peter Mbah of impoverishing residents through “mandatory heavy financial pressure arising from multiple tax collections and high daily ticketing perpetrated by the Peter Mbah’s administration.”

“Enugu State residents have never had it so tough as we are experiencing it today. The pain is so excruciating,” Eboh said, noting increased costs despite higher federal allocations.

“One would have expected that more revenues from the federal government ought to have translated to lesser tax burden on the average Enugu person, but the reverse seems to be the case,” he added.

The party criticised what it described as “elitist” projects, including Hotel Presidential, Enugu International Hospital and Enugu Air, as well as Mbah’s unfulfilled 180-day potable water promise.

“Ndi Enugu, the die is cast. Our dear state is indeed in troubled times, and it will take a level-headed gentleman in the mould of Sir Chinyeaka Ohaa (ODALIJE)… to positively turn things around,” Eboh said.

The NDC candidate promised to abolish unjustified tolls, multiple taxation and arbitrary assessments, and implement a 12-month tax relief programme for small businesses through a Single Unified Business Assessment System.

He also promised to establish a Market Traders Recovery Committee within seven days of assuming office to facilitate compensation and provide alternative trading spaces for affected traders.

“A vote for Sir Chinyeaka Ohaa of the NDC as our governor on February 6, 2027 is a vote for visible economic emancipation of the good people of Enugu State,” Eboh concluded.

2027: Kalu dismisses opposition’s planned alliance against Tinubu

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former Abia State Governor and Senator representing Abia North, Orji Uzor Kalu, has dismissed the planned November 23 meeting of opposition parties to agree on

Kalu said the opposition parties, including the African Democratic Congress and the Peoples Democratic Party, had lost the opportunity to build a united front capable of mounting a formidable challenge to the ruling All Progressives Congress.

He argued that the divisions and disagreements among opposition figures had made it difficult for them to agree on a common candidate, adding that an earlier and sincere alliance could have caused concern within the APC.

The former governor spoke on Tuesday in Abuja after receiving the Best Impactful Representation Award from the Senate Press Corps.

“The meeting has no meaning to people like me. If people cannot sit and agree to work together. Is it in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean which everybody is fighting to escape that they will agree on who should be the presidential candidate? The game is too late for them,” he said.

Kalu, who was the presidential candidate of the Progressive Peoples Alliance in the 2007 election, said a united opposition would have at least made the ruling party more concerned about the political contest.

“I thought they could have sincerely come together and presented one presidential candidate. That would have made us start having headaches. Not that they would win,” Kalu said.

He, however, insisted that the political situation currently favoured Tinubu, describing the 2027 presidential election as a contest without a credible opposition challenge.

“I have told them many times. The election is Tinubu versus Tinubu. That is the truth. I ran for president in 2007. So I am more experienced than all of them. The election is Tinubu versus Tinubu. That is what it is on the ground today, if you understand this politics,” he said.

Kalu added, “I know people might laugh, people might start making jokes. I really have no opposition. Let me leave it there. Let them finish the meeting. Let us see the outcome of the meeting.”

The senator also dismissed reports that the APC was becoming apprehensive over the activities of opposition parties, insisting that the ruling party was not intimidated by their planned alliance.

“One of the newspapers reported that APC people are jittery. We are not jittery. Rather, we are playing the game according to the rules. We cannot go out and start fighting. It is people fighting us. We have never fought anybody,” he said.

He challenged opposition parties to move beyond political confrontations and present Nigerians with alternative policies and programmes capable of addressing the country’s challenges.

According to him, opposition politicians should focus on explaining what they would do differently if elected rather than relying on attacks against the ruling party.

“Fighting is not in the name of the game. They should go and tell the people what they will do for them. If they can do anything better, let them say it. If I am in opposition and running for president, I will set an alternative agenda for Nigeria to see what I will do.

“None of these presidential candidates have been able to do that. We want to see an alternative agenda. You don’t pay people to go and say the APC is jittery. Jittery for whom? How can a lion fear a lamb?” Kalu asked.

The senator maintained that Tinubu and the APC were interested in a fair political contest and national unity, warning against actions capable of escalating political tensions.

“What the APC is looking for and what the president has been preaching is fair play. Let us not look for anybody’s trouble. But we cannot be in our room, and you come and pour us hot water. We must defend ourselves. So, let us not cause trouble. I need the unity of this country. It is very important.

“People want this country to disintegrate. But that is not what we are talking about now. They should allow the lion to lie with the lamb. Can a lion lie with a lamb? Yes. A lion and a lamb can lie together if they are civilised enough,” he said.

The planned opposition meeting comes amid ongoing discussions among opposition leaders over possible cooperation and the emergence of a common presidential candidate to challenge the APC in 2027.

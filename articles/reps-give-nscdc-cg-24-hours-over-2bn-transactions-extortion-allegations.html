Reps give NSCDC CG 24 hours over ₦2bn transactions, extortion allegations

By Vanguard Vanguard 16 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Vanguard

By Gift ChapiOdekina, Abuja The House of Representatives Committee on Solid Minerals has given the Commandant-General of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence

By Gift ChapiOdekina, Abuja

The House of Representatives Committee on Solid Minerals has given the Commandant-General of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) 24 hours to appear before it over allegations of financial impropriety, extortion, unlawful arrests and human rights violations involving the Mining Marshals.

The committee is also seeking explanations over the alleged killing of a serving NSCDC officer, Deputy Superintendent Agada Levi Agada, reportedly by fellow officers while on duty at a mining site.

The ultimatum followed a motion moved by Hon. Ali Amburura, representing Sokoto State, and seconded by Hon. Majidi from Katsina State during an investigative hearing into the activities, funding and operations of the Mining Marshals.

The committee’s Chairman, Hon. Gaza Gbefwi, said the investigation was prompted by allegations of abuse of power, extortion, unlawful detention of licensed mining operators and financial irregularities within the special security unit established to combat illegal mining.

Gbefwi said the lawmakers were particularly concerned about an alleged transaction of over ₦2bn linked to the bank account of a former state commander, suspected compromise among Mining Marshals personnel across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory, as well as questions surrounding vehicle procurement and the unit’s sources of funding.

He said the investigation was being conducted in accordance with the National Assembly’s constitutional oversight powers under Sections 88 and 89 of the 1999 Constitution, as amended.

The lawmaker recalled that more than 2,200 Mining Marshals were inaugurated in March 2024 to strengthen the fight against illegal mining, protect the country’s mineral resources and improve security at mining locations.

He also called for the protection of the family of the deceased officer and other witnesses, alleging that some had been subjected to threats and intimidation.

According to him, Agada left behind a widow and two children aged 10 and seven.

Gbefwi expressed condolences to the deceased officer’s family and assured them that their petition would be forwarded to the appropriate authorities for necessary action.

He also requested an executive summary of the family’s submissions to assist the committee in documenting the allegations and determining appropriate interventions.

Meanwhile, the Director of Communications at Stone Rockers Nigeria Limited, Rachel Ogbonna, accused the Mining Marshals of subjecting licensed mining operators to arbitrary arrests, prolonged detention and harassment.

Ogbonna told the committee that the company’s quarry site had remained sealed for over one year despite its possession of valid mining licences and other relevant operational documents.

She alleged that 12 employees of the company were arrested and taken to an NSCDC detention facility along Airport Road.

According to her, the company’s chairman and director were also detained after visiting the facility with relevant documents to establish the reasons for their employees’ arrest and secure their release.

She further alleged that the detainees were denied access to their lawyers, while the company’s director was prevented from receiving essential medication during detention.

Describing the alleged treatment as a violation of fundamental human rights, Ogbonna said the Mining Marshals should not use their enforcement responsibilities as justification for disregarding the law.

She urged the committee to investigate the unit’s budgetary allocations, special intervention funds and other sources of financing, including expenditure on publications, publicity materials and operational activities.

In a separate submission, a security operative, Odey Cosmas Polycarp, accused the Mining Marshals, allegedly led by one Onoja Attah, of unlawfully arresting and detaining members of his workforce while demanding ₦100m for their release.

Polycarp alleged that the workers were arrested in a residential neighbourhood where no quarrying or mining activities were taking place, questioning the justification for their arrest and detention.

He further claimed that the detainees were repeatedly beaten while in custody, leaving them traumatised and fearful for their safety.

He urged the lawmakers to investigate the allegations of extortion, unlawful detention and assault, and ensure that any officers found culpable were held accountable.

The allegations have raised concerns about the operational conduct, funding and accountability mechanisms of the Mining Marshals, particularly their enforcement activities involving licensed mining operators.

The committee is expected to hear from the NSCDC leadership and other relevant stakeholders as it examines the unit’s financial and operational framework and determines whether its personnel have acted within their statutory mandate.

The investigation is also expected to address the protection of legitimate mining businesses, respect for fundamental rights and measures to prevent the enforcement of laws against illegal mining from being abused.

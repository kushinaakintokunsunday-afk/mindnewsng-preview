Polaris Bank deepens commitment to girl-child education

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Polaris Bank, in partnership with Evolve Charity Trust, has provided essential learning materials to 640 students across 10 public secondary schools in Nigeria,

Now in its sixth year, the intervention forms part of the bank’s Corporate Social Responsibility commitment to reducing the financial barriers that limit access to education, particularly for indigent students. By providing essential school supplies, the initiative seeks to ease the burden on families, curb the out-of-school menace, improve students’ readiness to learn, and encourage school attendance, especially among girls.

The distribution, according to the bank’s statement on Monday, covered public schools across Kano, Kogi, Imo, and Lagos states, as well as the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja. Beneficiaries included 100 students at Government Girls Secondary School, Kundila, Kano, and 60 students each at Government Junior Secondary School, Garki, and Model Junior Secondary School, Maitama, Abuja.

In Lagos, Opebi Junior Grammar School, Gbaja Girls Junior Secondary School, and Gbaja Girls Senior Secondary School each received materials for 60 students. Fortune Secondary School, Lokoja; National High School, Arondizuogu; Iheme Memorial Secondary School, Arondizuogu; and Akokwa High School, Akokwa, also benefited, with 60 students from each of the schools receiving the learning materials.

Beyond the provision of school supplies, the programme incorporates developmental interventions focused on brain training, emotional intelligence, and life skills to help students build confidence, strengthen their decision-making abilities, and prepare for opportunities beyond the classroom. Teachers in the affected schools also benefited from extended capacity building during the long holidays.

Speaking on the initiative, the Managing Director/Chief Executive Officer of Polaris Bank, Mr Kayode Lawal, said the programme reflected the bank’s commitment to creating meaningful social impact through education.

“The initiative is designed to support 50,000 students by 2028, building on its current reach of 24,000 students across 49 locations. Our goal, working with our CSR partners, is to help more children, particularly girls, access education and develop the required academic and functional skills and confidence to realise their potential,” he said.

Lawal said the initiative formed part of wider efforts to tackle Nigeria’s out-of-school problem and expand access to quality education. He stressed that the learning materials went beyond providing immediate academic assistance, reassuring students that their dreams were valid and that they could grow into accomplished scholars, innovators, and future leaders.

Speaking during the intervention at Gbaja Girls Junior and Senior Secondary Schools in Lagos, the Group Head, Customer Experience and Value Management, Polaris Bank, Mrs Bukola Oluyadi, said making basic learning resources available remained essential to helping students participate fully in their education.

“Every child deserves the opportunity to learn, grow and pursue their ambitions, regardless of their family’s financial circumstances. By providing this intervention, we are hopeful that the outcome will be to help ease some of the challenges students face and encourage them to remain focused on their education and future,” she said.

On his part, the Head, Brand Management and Corporate Communications, Polaris Bank, Rasheed Bolarinwa, said the initiative demonstrated the value of sustained partnerships in addressing community needs.

“Effective CSR goes beyond making commitments; it requires interventions that respond to real needs and deliver practical benefits. Through this partnership, we are helping students access essential learning resources while reinforcing the importance of education in building stronger communities,” he said.

Polaris Bank’s representatives across the participating states also encouraged beneficiaries to make the most of the intervention. In Kano, the Branch Head, Madiebo Godwin, reaffirmed the bank’s commitment to expanding access to educational opportunities, while in Lokoja, the Business Development Manager, Lokoja Branch, Shola Ogundele, urged students to use the materials responsibly and remain committed to their academic goals.

School leaders also welcomed the intervention, noting the importance of providing students with the resources required to participate effectively in classroom activities.

The Principal of Government Girls Secondary School, Kundila, Kano, Hajiya Aisha Yakasai, highlighted the importance of educational support for students whose families struggle to meet the cost of basic school supplies, while the Principal of Government Junior Secondary School, Garki, Abuja, Mr Rayyanu Ogande, welcomed the intervention and its contribution to supporting students’ educational needs.

The Principal of Fortune Secondary School, Lokoja, Mrs Abu Esther, noted that access to essential learning materials could improve students’ morale, encourage classroom participation, and help them concentrate on their studies.

At Model Junior Secondary School, Maitama, Abuja, the Director/Principal, Dr Mary Ebong, acknowledged the financial pressures many families face in providing for their children’s educational needs, describing interventions that address these gaps as valuable support for students and their parents.

Similarly, the Principal of Gbaja Girls Junior Secondary School, Surulere, Lagos, Mrs Dabiri Adetoun Iyabo, and the Principal of Opebi Junior Grammar School, M. O. Owolabi, highlighted the importance of sustained educational support in enabling students to make the most of available learning opportunities.

In Imo State, the principals of the beneficiary schools, Mrs Oraneto F. C. of National High School, Arondizuogu; Mrs Nwosu Adanna of Iheme Memorial Secondary School, Arondizuogu; and Mrs Nwokeji C. A. of Akokwa High School, Akokwa, welcomed the intervention and commended its contribution to supporting students’ educational needs.

The initiative builds on Polaris Bank’s partnership with Evolve Charity Trust, which commenced in 2021, and contributes to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, particularly SDG 4 (Quality Education) and SDG 5 (Gender Equality).A

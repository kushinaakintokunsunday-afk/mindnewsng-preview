Tinubu unveils first varsity of public relations, leadership at WPRF 2026

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 5d ago 8 min read

_______________________________________________________________________

From Juliana Taiwo-Obalonye, Abuja

President Bola Tinubu on Wednesday said he had approved the establishment of Nigeria’s first University of Public Relations and Leadership, a major move to advance professional communication, research and leadership development in the country.

Tinubu made the disclosure while unveiling the university through Vice President Kashim Shettima, who represented him at the opening of the 38th World Public Relations Forum (WPRF) and 5th African Public Relations Association Conference in Abuja.

He said the specialised university would provide advanced education, research, professional development and leadership training in public relations, strategic communications and related disciplines.

“I am pleased to formally approve and support the establishment of the University of Public Relations and Media, a pioneering initiative designed to advance specialised education, research, professional development and leadership training in public relations, strategic communications and related disciplines,” he said.

The President described the institution as a lasting legacy of Nigeria’s hosting of the international forum, saying it represented a forward-looking investment in human capital and the future of the communication profession.

He said the university would help develop a new generation of communication leaders equipped to respond to the demands of a rapidly changing global environment.

Tinubu commended the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations (NIPR), the Federal Ministry of Information and National Orientation and the Nasarawa State Government for collaborating to actualise the initiative.

He particularly acknowledged Nasarawa State Governor Abdullahi Sule for his commitment to the project, urging relevant stakeholders to work together to develop the university into a centre of excellence with national, continental and international recognition.

The President’s approval comes against the backdrop of growing concerns over the impact of misinformation, disinformation, hate speech and declining public trust in an era increasingly shaped by digital technologies and artificial intelligence.

Speaking on the forum’s theme, “Responsible Communication: The Voice of the World,” Tinubu said public relations and communication professionals must move beyond managing images and reputations to promoting truth, accountability, transparency, mutual understanding and constructive engagement.

He said communication professionals have a responsibility to strengthen relations between governments and citizens and to contribute to national development, democratic governance, economic growth and strong institutions.

The President urged practitioners to uphold the highest standards of ethics, professionalism and responsibility in discharging their duties.

Shettima also used the occasion to project Nigeria’s economic and demographic potential, describing the country as a nation of enormous possibilities, with diverse cultures, a dynamic private sector, a youthful population and a strong entrepreneurial spirit.

He said six of Africa’s 10 unicorn companies were Nigerian, citing Flutterwave, OPay, Interswitch, Andela, Moniepoint and Moove as examples of the country’s growing technology and business ecosystem.

The President further projected that Nigeria would become the world’s third most populous country by 2050, with an estimated population of 440 million, before emerging as the most populous nation by the end of the century.

“One out of every four black men is a Nigerian. By 2050, one out of every three black men will be a Nigerian. We will surpass the United States. We will be the third most populous nation on earth with a population of 440 million people,” he said.

He urged international delegates to look beyond the conference and explore Nigeria’s investment opportunities, creative industries, tourism assets, cultural heritage and entrepreneurial ecosystem.

President Tinubu called on participants to establish professional relationships, explore partnerships and investments, and become ambassadors of Nigeria’s economic and development prospects.

“Nigeria welcomes you. Nigeria is open for partnership, and Nigeria is ready to engage with the world,” he said.

He expressed the Federal Government’s appreciation to the Global Alliance for Public Relations and Communication Management, the African Public Relations Association and NIPR for bringing the international gathering to Nigeria.

Speaking earlier, Kenyan President William Ruto, addressing the forum through a representative, urged African governments to make communication central to policymaking and governance. He warned that public trust cannot be manufactured through publicity but must be earned through honesty, transparency, consistency and accountability.

He said Kenya adopted a National Communication Policy in August 2026, requiring ministries, departments and agencies to provide citizens with timely, accurate and accessible information, while ensuring that communication strategies are integrated into major government projects from inception.

Information Minister Mohammed Idris, for his part, called on governments, media organisations and public relations practitioners to uphold truth, accuracy, transparency and professional ethics, warning that the misuse of information and digital platforms could fuel hatred, deepen divisions and threaten global security.

Speaking at the World Public Relations Forum in Abuja, Idris said responsible communication is essential for building public trust, promoting peaceful coexistence and strengthening democratic governance. “Our communication should build bridges, not walls,” he said, urging stakeholders to ensure that information empowers citizens rather than misleads them.

Idris also stressed the need to defend freedom of expression while promoting accountability and responsible advocacy, noting that journalism remains central to nation-building and democratic governance. He urged governments to communicate openly, media organisations to uphold professional standards and public relations practitioners to embrace honesty and transparency.

He further called for stronger international cooperation, investment in media literacy and collaboration with technology companies to address misinformation and disinformation, while urging Africa to promote fairer international narratives that reflect the continent beyond familiar accounts of conflict and challenges.

The President of the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations, Ike Neliaku, in his opening remarks, declared the 2026 World Public Relations Forum in Abuja the biggest in the event’s history, with 2,837 delegates from across the world attending as of the opening day.

He said Nigeria deliberately designed the forum to showcase its cultural diversity, professional capacity and leadership in public relations while hosting the event on behalf of Africa. He said the gathering brought together international public relations professionals, government officials, traditional rulers, young people, the private sector and civil society organisations.

He said the forum was conceived to go beyond conventional professional discussions, with programmes including a global youth public relations gathering, a women in public relations conference, a knowledge conference, a royal fathers’ session on community relations and peacebuilding, and engagements with governors and other sub-national leaders.

Neliaku said the participation of traditional rulers was particularly important because peacebuilding and community relations are central to effective public relations practice in Africa, adding that the forum would provide an opportunity for the continent to share lessons with the rest of the world.

He stressed that public relations should not be mistaken for propaganda, describing it as a profession built on trust, reputation management and responsibility.

“Public relations is not propaganda. Public relations is a business of trust,” he said, adding that practitioners must be prepared to take responsibility for the information they communicate and the impact of their work.

The NIPR president also disclosed that the organisers had incorporated a cultural festival and a corporate social responsibility initiative, including plans to feed 10,000 people, as part of efforts to make the forum impactful beyond the conference venue.

He said Nigeria had made a deliberate decision to host the event on a grand scale, insisting that the country and Africa have the history, capacity and cultural heritage to command a stronger place in global conversations.

Neliaku recalled the efforts that went into securing the hosting rights, including Nigeria’s presentation at the global level and the support of the Federal Government, particularly the Minister of Information and National Orientation, Mohammed Idris.

He thanked President Tinubu, represented at the event by the Vice President; the Nasarawa State Governor, Abdullahi Sule; and other partners for supporting the hosting of the international gathering.

The President of the Global Alliance for Public Relations and Communication Management, Justin Green, praised Nigeria’s determination and diplomatic efforts in securing the hosting rights for the 2026 World Public Relations Forum, describing the country’s successful bid as a victory for Africa.

Recounting how Nigerian officials, including the Minister of Information and National Orientation and Nigeria’s ambassador to Ireland, repeatedly contacted him during the final stages of the selection process, he said the country had demonstrated an exceptional commitment to bringing the global gathering to Abuja. He said Nigeria’s “fighting spirit to win” brought delegates from 126 countries to the Nigerian capital.

Green also hailed the establishment of the University of Public Relations and Leadership in Nasarawa State, describing it as the world’s first university dedicated to the profession and a major investment in education and professional excellence. He commended Governor Abdullahi Sule for making the project a reality, saying it represented a commitment to developing the next generation of communication professionals.

Illustrating the pride Nigerians have in their country, he recounted how a Nigerian taxi driver in Dublin offered to take him to the airport without charge after learning he was travelling to Nigeria, describing the gesture as “Nigerian pride at its greatest”.

Also speaking, Arik Karani, President of the African Public Relations Association (APRA), described Nigeria as a leading force in shaping public relations practice in Africa, noting that the country provided a model for other nations through its early adoption of legislation regulating the profession.

The APRA president said Nigeria was the only African country at the time to have enacted a public relations law, more than 20 years ago, providing a benchmark for other countries seeking to strengthen professional standards. “Nigeria is the table when we are discussing public relations,” the president said, stressing that Africa must move beyond being a passive participant in global conversations to taking a leading role in shaping them.

S’Africa now super racist against Whites, Asians – Elon Musk replies Ramaphosa over appeal to remember origin

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 17 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

World’s richest man, Elon Musk has hit back at South African President, Cyril Ramaphosa after the president appealed to him to remember his origin and give back

World’s richest man, Elon Musk has hit back at South African President, Cyril Ramaphosa after the president appealed to him to remember his origin and give back to his home country.

Ramaphosa spoke about Musk during an interview in Silicon Valley after leading a South African delegation focused on artificial intelligence to California on November 14.

“I have met Elon Musk in the past, and I’ve been awed by his brilliance. But I’d also like to chat to him about remembering his country and being loyal to his home country,” Ramaphosa said.

The president also faulted South Africa-born Musk’s description of the country’s empowerment policies as “Apartheid 2.0.”

“He doesn’t understand the harm of apartheid policies and how the apartheid policies chose white people only for advancement, and the education he received was much better than what I received.

“Where the apartheid government spent R12 on his education, it spent R1 on my education. So if one does not understand that, one will never be able to understand precisely what needs to be done to remedy the harm that was done,” the president said.

Ramaphosa argued that Musk’s success was shaped by the knowledge and education made available to him in South Africa, and urged him contribute to the nation’s development.

He said, “I would say to Elon, what you’ve become today is by dint of your own origin. You started dabbling with the game machines. It is because of the knowledge and the education that was made available to you.

“And it is this endowment that you now have that was formed in South Africa. And he should be one of those who would say, I would like to contribute to the development of South Africa and give back to this country that made me who I am today.”

Musk was quick to respond on Tuesday after a clip of the interview circulated on X.

“South Africa is now super racist against Whites and Asians,” the Space X and Tesla CEO said.

Musk, who was born in Pretoria, has repeatedly criticised South Africa’s Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment (B-BBEE) framework, which mandates telecommunications companies to have at least 30% equity owned by historically disadvantaged groups.

He has also previously claimed that Starlink was denied a licence in South Africa simply because he is not Black, a claim the South African government has rejected, insisting the rules apply to all operators.

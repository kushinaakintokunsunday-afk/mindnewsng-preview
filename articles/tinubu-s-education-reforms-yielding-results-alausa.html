Tinubu’s education reforms yielding results — Alausa

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Alausa said the opposition, particularly the National Democratic Congress presidential candidate, Peter Obi, had “failed to acknowledge the benefits of the government’s interventions in education, accusing him of misleading Nigerians over some of the policies.”

The minister spoke while defending the administration’s education reforms, including the Nigerian Education Loan Fund, infrastructure development in tertiary institutions, improved funding and efforts to address staffing shortages during a briefing at the All Progressives Congress Presidential Campaign Council headquarters in Abuja.

Responding to the criticisms, Alausa said, “What Mr Obi is saying is borne out of ignorance. He needs to read the law. This is the law of Nigeria.”
He also accused the former presidential candidate of misleading the public, saying, “If Mr Obi wants to blame me and mislead the public and Nigerians, that tells you the kind of person he is.”

Alausa said the four per cent development levy introduced under the Nigeria Tax Act was designed to provide sustainable funding for the student loan scheme, adding that 15 per cent of the proceeds was allocated to NELFUND.

He explained that the provision was captured in the law and challenged Obi to verify it before criticising the government.

The minister further argued that the administration’s interventions had improved access to tertiary education through student loans and infrastructure upgrades, while efforts were being made to address the shortage of academic staff in universities and other higher institutions.

“We have almost three million Nigerian students in tertiary education and another 200,000 in secondary education. Today, students in every single section of our country have benefited the most from President Tinubu’s transformative agenda,” he said.

Alausa also dismissed what he described as the opposition’s preoccupation with policy promises, insisting that the administration was focused on delivering practical results.

“We have enough policies in Nigeria. We want tangible benefits for Nigerians. So, every single thing about today is not about policies. What I am trying to tell you is how Nigerians are benefiting,” he said.

He also disclosed that the Federal Government had developed guidelines to improve university governance, particularly in the appointment of vice-chancellors, while committees had been established to monitor compliance and address issues such as bullying in schools.

The minister added that the government was monitoring the implementation of recommendations made by previous education panels, insisting that another cycle of reviews would not begin until outstanding recommendations had been implemented.

His comments come amid sustained criticism of the government’s handling of the education sector by opposition figures, including Obi, who has repeatedly questioned the level of public investment in education and the effectiveness of government policies.

In July, Obi faulted the Federal Government’s decision to phase out the policy separating junior and senior secondary schools, arguing that the country’s education crisis was rooted in inadequate funding, poor management and weak leadership rather than the structure of the school system.

He also criticised the 2026 education budget, which he said allocated only 6.17 per cent of total expenditure to the sector, below the 15 to 20 per cent benchmark recommended by UNESCO.

Meanwhile, the Tinubu administration has made education reforms a major part of its policy agenda, with interventions spanning basic education, technical and vocational training, digital learning, teacher development and tertiary education financing.

According to figures released by the Federal Government, NELFUND had disbursed N372.62bn to students across 319 tertiary institutions, while the administration has also reported initiatives aimed at returning out-of-school children to classrooms and improving access to technical education.

The minister’s defence also comes as the ruling All Progressives Congress intensifies its campaign to secure a second term for Tinubu ahead of the 2027 general elections. Alausa had earlier argued that the President’s performance across sectors, including education, justified his re-election.

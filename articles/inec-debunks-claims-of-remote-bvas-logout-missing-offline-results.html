INEC debunks claims of remote BVAS logout, missing offline results

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Independent National Electoral Commission has dismissed claims that officials can remotely log out of the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System before the end

The commission also clarified that election results uploaded offline are securely stored on the device and automatically transmitted to its Result Viewing Portal once internet connectivity is restored.

INEC was reacting to a viral Instagram video in which a man claiming to have worked as a presiding officer alleged that officials could log voters out of BVAS before the close of polls and that results uploaded offline could be lost.

In the video, the man warned prospective poll workers ahead of the 2027 elections against uploading results offline, advising them to take screenshots of the results and wait for network connectivity before uploading them.

However, INEC, in a statement on Monday shared on its official X account, described the claims as “false, fabricated and misleading”, urging Nigerians to disregard the video.

The commission explained that each polling unit was assigned unique login credentials, which were issued to the presiding officer on the eve of an election.

It said the credentials enabled the presiding officer to log into BVAS for voter accreditation on election day and subsequently upload the polling unit result sheet after the close of polls.

“The claim that the BVAS can be remotely logged out before the conclusion of accreditation and voting is false. Only the Poll Official operating the device has the authority to log in or log out of the BVAS, whether during voter accreditation or the result upload process,” the commission stated.

It added that there was no provision for any external individual or authority to remotely log out the device.

On the allegation that results uploaded offline could disappear, INEC said the offline upload feature was introduced following challenges experienced during the 2023 general election.

According to the commission, some presiding officers were held hostage at polling units because they could not upload results due to poor or non-existent network connectivity.

It said the IReV application was subsequently upgraded to allow presiding officers in areas with poor or no network coverage to upload polling unit result sheets offline in the presence of stakeholders.

“The uploaded result sheet remains securely stored on the BVAS pending successful transmission,” INEC stated.

The commission explained that as presiding officers moved from their polling units to ward collation centres and their devices reconnected to the internet, the results were automatically transmitted to the IReV portal without further manual intervention.

INEC assured Nigerians that the BVAS and IReV systems operated in line with established procedures designed to protect the integrity, transparency and credibility of the electoral process.

It urged members of the public to refrain from creating or sharing unverified information and rely on its official communication channels for accurate information.

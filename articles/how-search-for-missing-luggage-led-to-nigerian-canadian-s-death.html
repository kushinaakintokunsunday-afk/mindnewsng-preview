How search for missing luggage led to Nigerian-Canadian’s death

By Punch Punch 2d ago 8 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

In this report, VICTOR AYENI examines how the recent death of a Nigerian-Canadian while attempting to cross the busy Airport Road in Lagos has reignited concern

In this report, VICTOR AYENI examines how the recent death of a Nigerian-Canadian while attempting to cross the busy Airport Road in Lagos has reignited concerns over the persistent disregard for pedestrian bridges

When the late Abraham Osakpolor arrived from Canada at the Murtala Muhammed International Airport, Ikeja, Lagos, on November 13, his immediate concern was retrieving his luggage.

On that sunny Friday afternoon, the 58-year-old was said to have made inquiries about where he could find and pick up his luggage.

Sunday PUNCH gathered that the Nigerian-Canadian citizen was directed to the Nigerian Aviation Handling Company, which is located near the MMIA along the Airport Road.

Osakpolor went to the place, hoping to find his luggage there, but was directed to return to the airport and make inquiries from the airlines.

Sadly, according to eyewitnesses, the middle-aged man was killed by a car while crossing the road.

In a statement signed by the Police Relations Officer of the Airport Police Command, Mohammed Adeola, on Wednesday, an appeal was made to the public for assistance in identifying Osakpolor’s family, relatives and friends.

Sharing photographs of his Nigerian and Canadian passports, the police said he died following a fatal motor accident that occurred along the expressway leading away from the MMIA towards the Oshodi axis.

Born in Benin City, Edo State, on September 1, 1968, Osakpolor was said to have been “hit by a moving vehicle and subsequently rushed to a hospital, where he was pronounced dead.”

“His remains have been deposited in the hospital morgue. The motorist involved in the accident was immediately apprehended by the Airport Police Command and will be charged to court,” the statement said.

The police said that during an initial search of the deceased’s belongings, his Nigerian passport and a mobile device without a SIM card were recovered.

A further search by mortuary attendants revealed that his Canadian passport had also been discovered among his belongings.

Even though the incident had occurred about five days earlier, the police said efforts to establish contact with Osakpolor’s family or persons who might know him had been unsuccessful.

‘Not the first time’

As the statement and accompanying photographs went viral on social media, several individuals expressed sadness and shared additional details about the circumstances surrounding the incident.

A private jet charter operator, who goes by the name Jet Daniel (@LaceVine) on X, shared a photograph of the scene of the incident taken from his car.

In a photograph he took at about 2:19 pm on the fateful day, a crowd of people could be seen surrounding the victim, while a Toyota Camry, said to have been driven by the motorist, was parked on the other side of the road.

“The lawlessness that endangered the life of the deceased and the motorist happens right in front of the Airport Police Command on a daily basis, with a pedestrian bridge in place. On what grounds are you detaining and charging the motorist to court?” Daniel queried.

He urged the authorities to intervene, adding that accidents happened on the road frequently and that this was the second death he had heard of this year.

In another tweet posted at about 4:39 pm after the accident, an X user, Lexybabs (@Lexybabs), wrote in Pidgin English, “Omo, car jam person wey just land from abroad along the airport road.”

Another X user, Cherish Olufunke, said, “According to an eyewitness, they said he came to NAHCO gate to ask where he could pick up his luggage and was told to go to the International Airport. On his way crossing, he was knocked down by a car, just last week Friday. I’m so sure he just arrived in the country. What a sad loss.”

Other netizens shared their experiences along the road, alleging that many citizens abandoned the pedestrian bridges and chose to cross the busy airport highway instead.

“This reminds me of 2019, when a friend asked me to deliver some luggage to her colleagues at the MMIA, Ikeja. I almost got run over by a car that day. I’m still anxious about that area till date,” Remi Omotola said.

On Facebook, Dozie Noble commented, “People don’t use the footbridge on that road. There is no week that an accident doesn’t happen on that road. The road is very wide, yet people prefer to run instead of using the footbridge, especially at 7 and 8 Bus Stop.”

Also recounting her experience, Adichie Uche said, “I almost hit one woman on that road one day. She just entered my front absent-mindedly, and come and see the noise my brakes made that day. Our saving grace was that cars behind me were far; if not, it would have been multiple collisions. People should follow instructions on that road.”

In January, the Airport Police Command said it had arrested a driver responsible for a fatal hit-and-run accident that led to the death of a 19-year-old female pedestrian at Airport Bus Stop, Ikeja.

Preliminary findings revealed that the bus hit the victim shortly after she alighted at the Airport Bus Stop and was proceeding towards the airport.

“Following the occurrence of the fatal accident, concerned passers-by at the scene immediately rushed the victim to the Air Force Base Hospital for urgent medical attention.

“Upon receipt of information regarding the accident, police operatives of the Airport Police Command proceeded to the hospital, where medical personnel confirmed that the victim was dead on arrival due to the severe injuries sustained from the hit,” the statement said.

There have also been reports of vehicles hitting pedestrians in other areas of Lagos where pedestrian bridges are available.

Last month, the Lagos State Traffic Management Authority, on its X page, disclosed that a hit-and-run accident had claimed the life of a pedestrian crossing the main carriageway at Ojota inward Ketu.

“Pedestrians are advised to always make use of the pedestrian bridge to cross the expressway, as it is not meant for decoration,” the post said.

Family identified, driver in court

On Thursday, the Airport Police Command quoted its earlier statement on its X account and said contact had now been successfully established with Osakpolor’s family.

The statement quoted the Commissioner of Police, Airport Police Command, Dr Olufunke Ogunbode, who noted that “while effective policing thrives on mutual trust, cooperation and shared responsibility, [the command] reiterates its commitment to sustaining a cordial and productive relationship with the public.”

When Sunday PUNCH reached out to the Airport Police Command PRO, Adeola, he confirmed that the suspect had been arrested and declined further comments.

“The motorist who drove the car that hit him has been arrested, and the case is still in court,” he said.

However, a police source within the command said that, according to the information gathered by the authorities, the deceased was indeed looking for his missing luggage.

“The late Mr Osakpolor was just arriving in Nigeria and was coming to claim his luggage. He had luggage he hadn’t got, so he went to the NAHCO axis, which is the cargo area, hoping to get his luggage, but he was redirected, since that is where people who ship cargo materials go.

“He was told to go back to the airport and meet with the airline so that he could get his luggage. So, it was while trying to get to the other side to find his way into the airport that he was hit by a vehicle,” the source disclosed.

The source further stated that Osakpolor’s family members were currently meeting to finalise how the deceased’s body would be released to them for burial.

“He has some relatives here and others abroad, but we are trying to ensure that all parties involved have a unified front in terms of how they want the body to be released and how that is done. Once that is decided by the family, then the body will be released to them so that they can give him a befitting burial,” the source added.

An officer of the Lagos State Environmental Sanitation Corps who pleaded anonymity complained that, despite efforts to enforce the use of pedestrian bridges across roads in the state, many citizens still saw direct crossing as a waste of time.

“Under the Lagos State Environmental Management and Protection Law (2017), our operatives have the mandate to prevent pedestrians from crossing expressways and major highways, particularly in areas where pedestrian bridges are provided.

“But our people continue to shun these bridges, and that is why there have been people hit by cars along routes like Fadeyi, Onipanu, Ojota and the Airport Road,” the officer said.

Also commenting on the incident, a crime and forensic analyst, Damilola Benjamin, pointed out that, under the Criminal Law of Lagos State, “fatal gross negligence behind the wheel is not just a traffic slip. It can land a driver in court for involuntary manslaughter, subsections 224 and 229, carrying up to life imprisonment.”

Benjamin added that, “Reckless and Negligent Acts, Section 251, applies if rash driving endangered life. On federal highways, the FRSC Act 2007 steps in with Causing Death by Dangerous Driving, Section 20, and panicking and fleeing the scene only adds ‘Hit and Run/Failure to Report,’ Section 25, to the charge sheet.

“Even ignoring a pedestrian’s ‘right of way,’ under Regulations 161 and 173 of the NRTR 2012, is another evidence that ties into negligence. No one leaves home planning to take a life with a steering wheel, but drivers must drive like someone’s entire world is crossing a road.”

CITN urges Nigerians to report unprofessional conduct of members

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 3d ago 4 min read

…Inducts 2326 Associates

The Chartered Institute of Taxation of Nigeria (CITN) has urged Nigerians to report any member found engaging in unprofessional conduct, assuring that commensurate disciplinary measures would be taken against those found culpable to protect the integrity, credibility and standards of the tax profession.

Speaking at the 55th induction ceremony of the Institute which saw the admission of 2326 Associate members, the 17th President & Chairman in Council of the Institute, Mr. Innocent Ohagwa, while congratulating the new members, warned that a tax professional who is not tax compliant has no moral standing to teach compliance.

Ohagwa reminded the inductees that the Institute has raised the bar of competence and ethical standards and every member, particularly the practitioners must rise to meet it.

The tax professional explained that technical competence is now non-negotiable for recertification, saying, “ethical conduct has become the bedrock of trust. Professionalism is the new benchmark for relevance. And each of you, by choosing this path, has committed to uphold these values at the highest standards”

CITN Chief reminded tax professionals that Nigeria is at the implementation phase of far-reaching tax reforms and the role of the tax professional has never carried more weight than today. “It is imperative to note that our engagements with members and stakeholders, concerns have been raised about implementation challenges. I therefore urge all members, businesses and stakeholders to support the implementation and to stay constructive on the tax reform laws”

He advised members keep reading and learning, advise with integrity, and where necessary, collaborate with other professionals to exchange knowledge, share experiences and strengthen their skills.

The CITN Act, among other things, established an Investigating Panel and a Disciplinary Tribunal to deal with members, whether practitioners, administrators or others, who cross the red line by violating our code of ethics or acting contrary to professional standards.

“The general public, institutions, clients and members of the Institute are encouraged to report any instance of unprofessional conduct or behaviour unbecoming of a tax professional to the Institute. CITN will not waver or fail to investigate and deal with any matter that comes to its attention through public knowledge or observation, whether or not a formal report has been submitted”

He informed that being a member of CITN is a call to higher service. “You must uphold integrity, pursue excellence and ensure that you are always part of the solution and never otherwise” he added.

In welcome address, the Chairman, Membership and Professional Conduct Committee, Dr. Yemi Sanni, charged the tax professionals to uphold the highest level of professionalism, coupled with honesty and integrity.

“You are the first set of inductees to be admitted after a full year of the new tax regime. Since January, 2026, the comprehensive reforms —the consolidation and harmonisation of our tax laws, the new tax administration framework and the restructuring of our revenue institutions — have moved from paper into practice.

“Taxpayers are adjusting to new rules, tax authorities are building new systems, and many questions of interpretation are still being settled. This is precisely where the profession is most needed, and it is where you now have a seat at the table”

Sanni added that their success depends on competent and ethical professionals who would advise taxpayers correctly, help businesses comply, assist the authorities in administering the law fairly, and speak up constructively where refinements are needed.

“Your contributions will support government’s revenue mobilisation efforts, protect businesses, promote investment and strengthen public trust in the tax system. I must remind you that the practice of taxation is now increasingly digital. Electronic filing, e-invoicing, data matching and analytics are changing how tax is administered and how compliance is tested”

He advised that professional must be as comfortable with data and technology as with the provisions of the law, saying, “I urge you to embrace these tools early and to use them responsibly. You must adhere strictly to the institute’s Code of Conduct and continuously improve your skills through Mandatory Continuing Professional Development in order to remain relevant in today’s globalised, technologically driven and rapidly evolving business environment”

He emphasized the need to demonstrate competence, discipline and a high level of ethical conduct in the discharge of their professional responsibilities.

“Your certificate is a licence of trust. One unethical act can undo years of hard work, and it reflects not only on you but on every member of this Institute. The current tax regime demands a great deal from professionals in this field, and we must not be found wanting” he warned.

Germany Releases List of Citizens Who Can Enter the Country First and Apply for Opportunity Card Inside - Nigerians Are Not Included

By Mindnewsng Mindnewsng 10 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Mindnewsng

From October 1, 2026, Germany's Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) applications are online-only via the Consular Services Portal - and only 8 sets of citizens can

Germany has confirmed the final list of citizens who can travel to the country first and apply for the Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) after arrival - and Nigerian applicants are not on that list.

Since 1 October 2026, the German Federal Foreign Office requires Opportunity Card applications to be submitted only online through the Consular Services Portal (digital.diplo.de) before any travel. The visa is applied for at the German mission responsible for your home country - for Nigerians, that is mainly the Consulate General in Lagos.

The citizens who can still apply inside Germany

Citizens of the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Israel, Japan, South Korea and the United States enjoy a visa-free privilege: they can normally enter Germany and apply for the Opportunity Card at the local immigration authority afterwards.

But even that list comes with a warning: German missions report difficulties on the ground - in Berlin, for example, the Opportunity Card cannot be applied for locally unless you previously held a German visa for employment, study or vocational training.

The two ways Nigerians can qualify

  1. Skilled-worker route (no points needed): you hold a university degree or vocational qualification that is fully recognised or obtained in Germany. A Nigerian B.Sc./B.Eng. must be verifiably comparable through the official anabin database. Note: an HND alone does not meet the bar - the route requires an ISCED level 6 qualification.
  2. Points route (at least 6 of the shown points): partial recognition of your qualification earns up to 4 points; German language skills up to 3 points (English at C1 earns 1); professional experience up to 3 points; age under 35 earns 2 points; others go for a shortage occupation, a previous legal stay in Germany, or an eligible partner applying with you.

The reality check for Nigerian applicants

The numbers show how competitive this route really is: in its first year, Germany issued 3,721 Opportunity Card visas to Indians, 116 to Ghanaians, 99 to Kenyans - and only 39 to Nigerians.

  • Proof of funds: you must show at least €1,091 per month (2026 minimum) - about €13,000 for the full 12 months, usually through a blocked account.
  • Costs: the visa fee is €75, plus a service fee for the external service provider handling appointments.
  • Work while searching: holders may work part-time up to 20 hours a week and do short trial employment while job-hunting, and the card lasts a maximum of one year.
  • Forged documents: the Consulate warns that any false documents trigger immediate rejection, a warning-database entry and a Schengen-wide entry ban.

This article is for general information only and is not legal or immigration advice. Application rules, points and financial requirements are set by the German Federal Foreign Office - always verify the current details on the official Opportunity Card pages (germany.diplo.de, digital.diplo.de and make-it-in-germany.com) before applying.

Sources: Federal Foreign Office/German Missions in Nigeria Opportunity Card pages; Consular Services Portal (digital.diplo.de/Chancenkarte); Make it in Germany (federal portal); Auswärtiges Amt visa statistics June 2024-June 2025.

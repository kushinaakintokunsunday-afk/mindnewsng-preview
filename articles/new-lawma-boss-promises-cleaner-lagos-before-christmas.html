New LAWMA boss promises cleaner Lagos before Christmas

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Lagos Waste Management Authority has set a 60-day deadline to clear illegal dumpsites and refuse heaps across the state, pledging to rid the metropolis of i

The Acting Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of LAWMA, Olajuwon Akinyemi, gave the assurance during a media briefing and stakeholders’ engagement on Friday.

During the meeting, Akinyemi unveiled plans to reposition the authority and address persistent waste management challenges in the state.

He said the agency would embark on an intensive clean-up operation within the next 30 to 60 days, adding that the exercise would involve the removal of refuse from illegal dumpsites and identified black spots across Lagos.

“In the next 30 to 60 days, we are going to ensure that all our staff cooperate with us to clean up the city and sustain it. We will ensure the removal of waste from every illegal dumpsite and black spot around the city.

“I can assure Lagosians that before Christmas, they will see that Lagos has found a new leaf. A new sheriff is in town to do wonders for Lagos waste management,” he said.

He said Lagos generated between 13,000 and 14,000 metric tonnes of waste daily, describing the volume as a major challenge for the densely populated state.

Akinyemi also called on residents to take responsibility for the waste they generated, stressing the need for household waste sorting and separation to reduce the volume of refuse taken to landfill sites.

He explained that recycling, reuse and reduction of waste were essential to addressing the state’s limited landfill capacity, adding that the authority could not achieve its objectives without the cooperation of residents.

On plans to decentralise waste management operations, Akinyemi said LAWMA would establish about 10 regional hubs across the state to bring its services closer to residents.

He said the proposed hubs would have offices headed by regional coordinators responsible for addressing waste management challenges in their respective areas.

According to him, the arrangement would address the challenges associated with managing distant communities from a central location.

“Take, for example, Ojo and Amuwo-Odofin down to Badagry; there is no office there. We are looking at creating regional hubs, about 10 of them, with offices and regional heads who understand what to do and can manage waste challenges in those areas.

“Take, for example, Ijede, Eredo and Agbowa, which are far from Ijora. We are taking waste management closer to the people. That is the next level we are looking at,” he said.

The LAWMA boss said the proposed restructuring would be pursued in collaboration with the Commissioner for the Environment, local governments and other relevant stakeholders, adding that the authority would focus on regulation and policy direction.

On concerns about the efficiency of Private Sector Participation operators, particularly the rising cost of waste evacuation, he said LAWMA would intensify engagement and public enlightenment to improve residents’ understanding of the operational challenges facing the operators.

He noted that the cost of acquiring specialised waste collection trucks had increased significantly, explaining that vehicles which previously cost between N5m and N10m now cost between N18m and N19m.

He added that the rising cost of diesel, which he put at between N1,800 and N2,000 per litre, had further increased the financial burden on the operators.

According to him, the authority would engage residents, Community Development Associations, Community Development Committees, market associations and local governments to encourage cooperation with PSP operators.

“We need to engage a lot. We need to do advocacy and enlightenment. Every waste generator should understand that he must be responsible for the waste he generates.

“Imagine if you are the one who invested in that business and want to break even, not to talk of recouping your investment. We strongly believe that Lagosians will understand when we engage them more,” he said.

He added that the authority would also work with the state government on the rehabilitation of landfill sites and the development of material recovery centres to strengthen waste processing and recycling.

Akinyemi said the new measures were aimed at improving service delivery and ensuring that waste management operations were more responsive to the needs of residents.

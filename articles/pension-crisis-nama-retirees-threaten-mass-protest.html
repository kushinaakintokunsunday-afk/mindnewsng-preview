Pension crisis: NAMA retirees threaten mass protest

By Punch Punch 17h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Retirees under the Contributory Pensioners Union of Nigeria, Nigerian Airspace Management Agency branch, have threatened to stage a two-day peaceful protest ove

The union said the protest would hold from 25-26 November, 2026, in Lagos and the agency’s headquarters in Abuja.

This was contained in a statement made available to The PUNCH on Monday and signed by the union’s branch Chairman, Olopha Steven, and Secretary, Lawal Wasiu.

The planned action is aimed at demanding the payment of outstanding pension increases and arrears allegedly approved by the National Salaries, Incomes and Wages Commission.

The union said the decision followed the failure of NAMA management to respond to several letters sent on 30 September, 12 October and 17 November, 2026, seeking the resolution of the outstanding payments.

The statement read, “We refer to our letters dated 30th September, 12th October and 17th November, 2026, which regrettably remained unanswered.

“The Contributory Pensioners Union of Nigeria,  NAMA Branch, hereby gives notice to embark on a two-day peaceful protest from 25th to 26th November, 2026, at the NAMA Pension Office, Lagos, and Headquarters, Abuja, to demand for the payment of the accumulated 20 years pension increases and arrears approved by the Salary, Incomes and Wages Commission.”

The latest development comes amid a lingering disagreement between the retirees and the agency over the payment of their outstanding entitlements and the channel of communication between both parties.

In October, the union had rejected a meeting proposed by the agency for 3 November, 2026, insisting that NAMA management should meet with its leadership by October 21 and settle the outstanding arrears before the end of the month.

In a message to pensioners, Wasiu told The PUNCH that the agency responded to the planned industrial action, initially scheduled for 22 October, with a letter dated 19 October.

He, however, alleged that the correspondence was an attempt to mislead the retirees, arguing that it was addressed to NAMA Contributory Pension Scheme retirees rather than the Contributory Pensioners Union of Nigeria, which he described as the recognised body representing their interests.

Wasiu also accused NAMA management of deliberately refusing to communicate with the union since a meeting held on 10 June, 2026, while allegedly continuing to engage with the elders’ forum.

However, responding to the planned protest, NAMA Managing Director, Umar Farouq, said the agency had approved a payment schedule for the outstanding pension arrears and had commenced disbursements to the retirees.

Farouq disclosed that the agency had made provision for about N6bn to settle the payments, adding that the money would be released in batches.

He said, “I have approved their payment schedule and we have started paying them. We came up with about N6bn and we have started paying in batches. I got the paper in November and I immediately approved. I have told the DFA that we should do between N400m-N500m on monthly basis to off set the said money.”

The Managing Director also questioned the union representative’s awareness of the ongoing payments, insisting that the agency was engaging with what he described as the authentic leadership of the retirees.

“Meanwhile, this particular man speaking is not even aware that we have started paying because retirees are not refining with him. We are dealing with the authentic leaders of the retirees and they are cooperating with us,” he said.

NCC seeks telecom VAS pricing benchmark to address industry disparities

By Chinenye Anuforo

The Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) has commissioned a study to establish a cost-based pricing benchmark for value-added services (VAS) in the telecommunications industry, amid concerns over pricing disparities, revenue-sharing arrangements and consumer protection.

The study is expected to provide a common cost reference for operators and other players in the VAS value chain, while guiding future regulatory decisions on pricing and revenue distribution.

The Executive Vice-Chairman of the NCC, Dr Aminu Maida, disclosed this at a stakeholders’ consultative forum on the cost determination of value-added services in the Nigerian telecommunications industry in Lagos.

Maida, who was represented by the Head of the Competition and Tariff Unit at the commission, Omotayo Mohammed, said the exercise would provide an evidence-based assessment of costs across the VAS value chain.

He explained that value-added services had become an important part of the telecommunications ecosystem, supporting information, entertainment and financial applications beyond traditional voice and data services.

According to him, the commission introduced the VAS Aggregator Framework in 2017 and amended it in 2018 to improve connections between content providers and network operators, address market access challenges and strengthen revenue-sharing arrangements.

He said the commission had also introduced consumer protection measures, including the Do-Not-Disturb framework and short codes, to address unsolicited subscriptions and unauthorised deductions.

“Our objective is not to predetermine pricing outcomes, but to establish a credible and transparent cost basis that supports fair competition, efficient cost recovery, continued investment, innovation and consumer welfare,” Maida said.

He added that the study would identify inefficiencies and structural imbalances within the value chain rather than impose predetermined prices on industry players.

Maida urged telecommunications operators, aggregators, content providers and other stakeholders to cooperate with the consultants, Skyquest Concept Projects Ltd, by providing accurate and relevant information.

He warned that delayed, incomplete or inconsistent submissions could undermine the quality and reliability of the findings.

The NCC chief assured participants that commercially sensitive information provided during the exercise would remain confidential, noting that the consultants were contractually obligated to protect such data.

He also reminded operators of their obligations under Section 64 of the Nigerian Communications Act 2003, which empowers the commission to request relevant information and documents from industry players.

Also speaking, the Project Director, Skyquest Concept Projects Ltd, Olusegun Ekundayo, said Nigeria currently lacked a cost-based regulatory benchmark for VAS pricing and revenue-sharing arrangements.

He explained that existing agreements among mobile network operators, aggregators, VAS providers and content developers operated without a common cost reference, contributing to differences in service prices, quality and profit margins.

Ekundayo also identified billing transparency, unsolicited subscriptions and consumers’ difficulty in understanding charges for certain value-added services as challenges requiring attention.

He highlighted the growing economic importance of the sector, citing a global VAS market projection of $1.07 trillion in his presentation.

According to him, the study will examine developments in the Nigerian VAS market between 2017 and 2025, covering traditional services such as Short Message Service (SMS), Unstructured Supplementary Service Data (USSD), Interactive Voice Response (IVR) and web-based services.

The assessment will also cover emerging technologies and services, including artificial intelligence, fifth-generation (5G) networks, Internet of Things (IoT), Over-the-Top (OTT) services, cloud computing and application services.

The outcome is expected to provide the commission with a stronger basis for regulatory decisions aimed at improving pricing transparency, competition and consumer protection in Nigeria’s value-added services market.

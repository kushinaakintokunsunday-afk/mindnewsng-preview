Four killed as gunmen attack Omo-Agege’s campaign convoy

By Punch Punch 17h ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Delta State Government has imposed a curfew on Ughelli North Local Government Area following a violent attack on the campaign entourage of former Deputy Pre

The violence occurred on Monday during a campaign visit by Omo-Agege to Uduere community, where gunmen allegedly opened fire on his supporters, throwing the area into panic.

Omo-Agege reportedly escaped unhurt after bullets allegedly flew past his head while he was addressing a crowd gathered to receive him.

Among those killed was Moses Anaughe, a former member of the Delta State House of Assembly representing Ughelli North II and Personal Assistant to Omo-Agege. Anaughe was also a former Commissioner representing Delta State at the Federal Character Commission.

He reportedly died at the Ughelli Central Hospital, where he was taken for medical attention after sustaining gunshot injuries during the attack.

Another victim was an Urhobo youth activist, Comrade Akima Efemena Umukoro, popularly known as Efe Umukoro.

In a statement issued on Monday by his Media Adviser, Sunny Areh, the Omo-Agege Campaign Organisation confirmed that Anaughe and Umukoro were among four persons killed in the attack.

The statement partly read, “Senator Ovie Omo-Agege was saved by a divine distraction, when bullets whizzed past his head as he was addressing the crowd that gathered to receive him.

“We are pained to report that FOUR PEOPLE, including two of our aides — Moses Anaughe and Akima Efemena Umukoro — were shot dead.

“Four others — Chief Evans Iwuvrie, Chuks Erhire, Tobore Agba (who was shot in the neck) and Ayodo Wanogho — were shot at very close range and wounded critically. They are currently receiving medical attention. We pray for their speedy recovery, and we stand with their families at this dark hour.

“Witnesses allege that the attackers were shouting, “It’s Omo-Agege we want to kill! It’s Omo-Agege we want to kill!”

The campaign organisation alleged that armed men opened fire on Omo-Agege and his entourage at close range while the former deputy Senate president was addressing the crowd.

It also described the incident as an assassination attempt, alleging that the Chairman of Ughelli North Local Government Area, Jaro Egbo, led the attackers and acted on the orders of Governor Sheriff Oborevwori.

However, the allegations against the council chairman and the governor had not been independently established.

Earlier accounts of the incident said no fewer than eight other persons were in critical condition following the confrontation. One account identified Evans Ekurie, a former member of the Delta State House of Assembly, and Mr Tobore Agbah among those who sustained gunshot wounds. The campaign organisation, however, identified Chief Evans Iwuvrie, Chuks Erhire, Tobore Agba and Ayodo Wanogho as the four persons critically injured.

The Delta State Police Command, in a statement, said the state government had imposed a curfew in Ughelli North Local Government Area from 6:00 pm to 6:00 am daily until further notice.

The command said it had commenced investigations to establish the circumstances surrounding the incident and identify those responsible.

It urged residents to comply strictly with the curfew, remain calm, and cooperate with security personnel in the interest of public safety.

The Commissioner of Police, Delta State Command, CP Samuel Erale, directed intensified security operations in the affected area to prevent further violence and maintain law and order.

The police said the commissioner had ordered a full-scale investigation and assured residents that anyone found culpable would face the full weight of the law, irrespective of political affiliation or status.

He also urged members of the public to provide credible information to assist the ongoing investigations.

Erale extended his condolences to the bereaved families and wished the injured a speedy recovery. He assured residents of the command’s commitment to protecting lives and property and ensuring a peaceful environment for lawful political activities.

The commissioner warned political actors and their supporters against thuggery, intimidation and the unlawful use of firearms, stressing that political differences must never be allowed to degenerate into violence or loss of life.

The Omo-Agege Campaign Organisation called on the Inspector-General of Police, the Director-General of the Department of State Services and the Delta State Commissioner of Police to investigate the incident.

It also accused police officers deployed to the area of failing to prevent the attack despite their alleged presence with six patrol vehicles.

The organisation urged its supporters not to retaliate, saying its ward-to-ward campaign across Delta Central would continue.

“Delta State is not a war zone. Campaigning is not a crime. Seeking the votes of the people should not be a death sentence,” the statement said.

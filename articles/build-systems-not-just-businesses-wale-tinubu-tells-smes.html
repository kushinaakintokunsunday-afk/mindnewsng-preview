Build systems, not just businesses, Wale Tinubu tells SMEs

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Oando Plc Group Chief Executive Wale Tinubu has urged Nigerian entrepreneurs to build businesses around strong systems, financial discipline and sound governanc

Tinubu said businesses must adopt structured operations, keep proper financial records and embrace long-term planning to grow from small enterprises into medium-sized, large and globally competitive organisations.

He spoke while delivering the keynote address at GROW Nigerian 2026, the National MSME Conference organised by the Small and Medium Enterprises Development Agency of Nigeria at the Ecobank Pan African Centre, Victoria Island, Lagos, according to a statement sent to Saturday PUNCH on Friday.

Lagos State Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu, SMEDAN Director-General Charles Odii, German Deputy Consul-General Katharina Ziegler and other business leaders, policymakers and development partners attended the conference.

Highlighting the role of small and medium-sized enterprises in the economy, Tinubu said they accounted for 96.9 per cent of businesses, 88 per cent of employment and 46 per cent of Nigeria’s Gross Domestic Product.

He said the priority should be strengthening their capacity to scale beyond small businesses through deliberate decisions, consistency and disciplined execution.

“Nigerian giants are not built overnight. They are built through a series of bold decisions, executed with long-term commitment and persistence,” he said.

Drawing on Oando’s three-decade journey, Tinubu recounted how the company began with modest family contributions in a converted garage before moving into diesel trading and expanding into petroleum marketing, gas distribution and upstream oil and gas operations.

He linked the company’s growth to calculated risk-taking, reinvestment, reliability and quality, stressing that business opportunities require deliberate strategies and sustained execution.

On building businesses that attract investment, the Oando chief executive stressed the need for proper financial records, documented processes and audited accounts, saying they are vital for credibility and growth.

“One of the most important steps for an SME is to begin documenting its operations and processes, and to maintain audited financial statements. These are essential foundations for building a structured business and preparing it for growth,” he said.

Tinubu also named working capital management as vital to business survival, urging entrepreneurs to track their cash positions, receivables, liabilities and inventory.

He advised business owners seeking funding to explore different sources, including commercial banks, development finance institutions, fintechs, suppliers and joint venture partnerships.

“There is no single source of capital that is more important than another. Every source of financing, wherever it comes from, has a role to play in supporting business growth,” he said.

He also pointed to ambition, capability, competitiveness and endurance as key traits for building lasting enterprises, urging entrepreneurs to invest in their staff, pass on institutional knowledge and build systems that can be replicated as their businesses grow.

“The principles required to build a successful SME remain the same as those required to run a large enterprise. As you scale, the critical difference is your ability to replicate your systems, processes, and organisational DNA,” he added.

On operating conditions, Tinubu acknowledged government efforts to improve infrastructure but said businesses must prepare to handle policy shifts, delayed approvals and other operational hurdles.

He called for better electricity supply, logistics, port operations and ease of doing business, saying such reforms are crucial to helping small businesses grow into larger firms.

“These are areas that require commitment rather than significant financial resources. SMEs depend on improvements in logistics, port operations, and the ease of doing business to scale from small to medium-sized and ultimately large enterprises,” he said.

Looking back at Oando’s growth from a small trading firm into an integrated energy group, Tinubu said the company still relies on the entrepreneurial values that shaped its start.

“Even though we are a large conglomerate today, we still think like an SME. The principles that shaped our beginnings continue to inform how we build and grow the business,” he said.

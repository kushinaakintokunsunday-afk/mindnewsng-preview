INEC backs code for independent tabulation of 2027 election results

By Vanguard Vanguard 5d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Vanguard

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has endorsed a voluntary Code of Conduct for independent tabulation and publication of election results ahe

INEC Chairman, Prof. Joash Amupitan, gave the backing at a stakeholders’ roundtable convened by Yiaga Africa in Abuja.

The Executive Director of Yiaga Africa, Samson Itodo, states this in a statement he signed on Thursday in Abuja.

According to Itodo, Amupitan, who was represented by INEC’s Director of Information and Communication Technology, Dr Lawrence Bayode, said the code would strengthen transparency and public confidence.

He warned that independently aggregated figures, projections and partial tallies must never be presented as official election results.

“Independent verification should complement electoral accountability, not create competing centres of statutory authority,” the INEC boss stated.

Amupitan outlined six guiding principles for the framework, including methodological integrity, accuracy, information integrity, data security, error accountability and voluntary compliance.

Itodo said independent tabulation helps citizens to scrutinise election outcomes and assess credibility.

The Yiaga Africa boss noted that differing methodologies and publication standards could cause confusion during sensitive collation periods if left uncoordinated.

He stressed that a common code would establish minimum standards, curb premature declarations and ensure findings are backed by reliable verification.

Itodo said Yiaga Africa was expected to lead further engagements to translate the agreed principles into a practical framework before the 2027 general elections.

Participants at the forum included civil society groups, political parties, election observers, the National Broadcasting Commission and the Broadcasting Organisations of Nigeria.

Also in attendance were representatives of the Nigerian Press Council (NPC) and the Nigeria Union of Journalists (NUJ).

Stakeholders examined different tabulation models, including Yiaga Africa’s Processing Results Voter Tabulation (PRVT) and Election Results Analysis Dashboard (ERAD), alongside INEC’s Result Viewing Portal (IReV) aggregations.

They agreed that organisations must clearly disclose their methodologies, data sources and margins of error to prevent public misinterpretation.

A technical session also highlighted the need for human verification, audit trails, data security and transparent error-correction procedures.

Media stakeholders emphasised the duty of journalists to verify figures and clearly distinguish independent tallies from official results declared by INEC. (NAN)

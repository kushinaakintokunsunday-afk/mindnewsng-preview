FG’s oil revenue tumbles N1.44tn despite price boom

By Punch Punch 16h ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government recorded a N1.44tn decline in gross oil revenue in the first half of 2026 despite a 41.23 per cent increase in international crude oil pr

An analysis of the Second Quarter and Half-Year 2026 Budget Implementation Report showed that gross oil revenue fell to N7.88tn between January and June 2026, compared with approximately N9.32tn recorded during the corresponding period of 2025.

The decline represented a 15.45 per cent year-on-year contraction, highlighting Nigeria’s continued difficulty in translating favourable international oil prices into higher government revenue.

The report, prepared by the Budget Office under the Ministry of Budget and Economic Planning, disclosed that the N7.88tn collected during the first six months of 2026 was also N10.78tn below the government’s half-year revenue target of N18.65tn.

This translated into a budget performance rate of just 42.22 per cent, leaving a substantial gap in the revenue expected to finance government expenditure.

The development occurred despite international crude oil prices averaging $104.52 per barrel in the Q2 of 2026, compared with $74 per barrel in the corresponding period of 2025.

The increase of $30.52 per barrel represented a 41.23 per cent rise, while the average price was also $39.67, or 61.17 per cent, above the 2026 budget benchmark of $64.85 per barrel.

However, the government was unable to fully benefit from the price increase because of persistent production challenges, weak oil sales receipts, lower-than-expected petroleum taxes and deductions from oil revenue.

Explaining the situation, the Budget Office said, “The low Net oil revenue performance in the second quarter of 2026 compared with the quarterly budget projection could be attributed to the decrease in oil production and lifting, which was also due to low sector investment and legacy oil production constraint issues.”

It added, “The performance could also be ascribed to high fiscal deduction as provided in the PIA.”

The Petroleum Industry Act provides the framework for fiscal obligations, deductions and revenue administration in Nigeria’s petroleum industry.

The report identified crude oil theft, pipeline vandalism, security challenges, inadequate investment in modern infrastructure, corruption and regulatory uncertainty among the difficulties confronting the sector.

Although average crude oil production increased to 1.72 million barrels per day in the Q2 of 2026 from 1.68 million barrels per day in the corresponding period of 2025, output remained below the government’s budget target of 1.84 million barrels per day.

This represented a production gap of approximately 120,000 barrels per day, equivalent to 6.52 per cent of the budget benchmark.

Production nevertheless improved from the 1.55 million barrels per day recorded in the first quarter, suggesting a gradual recovery in output that had yet to translate into the expected fiscal gains.

The Budget Office observed that the improvement in oil prices was insufficient to overcome the structural challenges affecting production and government receipts.

It said, “Despite the favourable international crude oil price environment during the quarter, average production remained below the budget benchmark, limiting the revenue gains available to the Federation.”

The agency consequently recommended stronger security around petroleum infrastructure, improved operational efficiency, greater investment and stricter enforcement of royalty and other statutory payment obligations.

A breakdown of the half-year performance showed that petroleum profit and gas taxes recorded the largest shortfall, generating N2.92tn against a projected N11.96tn.

The N9.05tn deficit represented 75.62 per cent of the expected receipts from this revenue category, making it the biggest contributor to the overall oil revenue gap.

Similarly, gross profit oil from crude oil and gas sales generated only N285.64bn, compared with a half-year target of N1.17tn. This represented a shortfall of N884.81bn, or 75.60 per cent.

Oil and gas royalties performed relatively better, generating N4.07tn against a projected N5.42tn, although the government still missed its target by N1.35tn, equivalent to 24.87 per cent.

Royalties consequently accounted for approximately 51.65 per cent of total gross oil revenue collected during the first six months of 2026, compared with 37.04 per cent contributed by petroleum profit and gas taxes.

The figures showed that royalties had become the largest component of reported gross oil receipts, even as direct crude oil and gas sales contributed just 3.63 per cent.

Other revenue categories recorded mixed performances.

Concessional rentals generated N25.02bn against a half-year target of N19.43bn, exceeding projections by N5.59bn, or 28.74 per cent.

Miscellaneous oil receipts, including pipeline fees, reached N444.73bn, compared with a target of N3.08bn, representing an increase of N441.65bn above the projection.

Incidental oil revenue, comprising royalty recoveries and marginal field licences, generated N113.31bn against a target of N82.01bn, exceeding expectations by N31.30bn.

Gas-flaring penalties, for which no budgetary provision was made, contributed N324.56bn during the first half of the year.

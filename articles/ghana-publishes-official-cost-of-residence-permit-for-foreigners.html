Ghana Publishes Official Cost Of Residence Permit For Foreigners

By Mindnewsng Ghana Immigration Service (GIS) fee schedule 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Ghana Immigration Service (GIS) fee schedule

Ghana has published the official cost of residence permits for foreigners, and the figures are now sitting on the website of the Ghana Immigration Service (GIS) for anyone to check. The schedule runs from GH¢259 for a student residence permit to US$18,000 for an indefinite residence permit taken by a non-ECOWAS national.

The story matters in Nigeria because thousands of Nigerians live, trade and work in Ghana under the ECOWAS protocol – and while the protocol gives free entry, it does not give a free residence permit.

What Ghana actually published

The GIS fee schedule is grouped by three categories of applicant: Ghanaians holding foreign passports, ECOWAS citizens, and other nationals. Every permit type has its own line, and the currency differs – some items are quoted in Ghana cedis, others in US dollars. Dollar-denominated fees are paid at the prevailing exchange rate on the day of payment.

What Nigerians pay

Nigeria is an ECOWAS member state, so Nigerians fall in the ECOWAS column, which is consistently cheaper than the rate charged to other nationals:

  • Company-sponsored work and residence permit: US$647, against US$1,293 for a non-ECOWAS national – a saving of US$646.
  • Dependant residence permit: GH¢259, against GH¢388.
  • Student residence permit: GH¢194, against GH¢259.
  • Expired residence permit penalty: GH¢200 per month, against GH¢300.
  • Right of abode: GH¢1,940, against GH¢3,879.

Free movement under ECOWAS covers entry for up to 90 days. Staying longer, working, or setting up a business still requires a residence permit, and that permit still costs money.

Where the real cost hides

The government fee is rarely the whole bill. A work and residence permit in Ghana normally needs an employer that holds an immigrant quota or GIPC automatic quota position, plus a Ghana Card, a medical report and police clearance. Companies usually pay the quota fee on top of the permit fee, and that is the part agents quote separately.

The penalties are also steep. Overstaying a residence permit attracts GH¢200 per month for ECOWAS nationals and GH¢300 for other nationals. An expired visitors permit carries the same monthly penalty, and renewing a visitors permit costs GH¢39 per month for ECOWAS citizens.

How to apply without being scammed

  1. Apply only through the Ghana Immigration Service – gis.gov.gh – or its regional commands. The full fee schedule is published there.
  2. Pay into the official GIS account and insist on an official receipt. A receipt from an individual is not a permit.
  3. Check the exchange rate used on the day. Dollar fees converted at a friendly rate can quietly add hundreds of cedis.
  4. Keep your permit valid. The monthly penalty for a lapsed permit is cheaper to avoid than to settle.

Bottom line

Ghana has not invented a new charge – it has published an existing schedule openly. For Nigerians the headline number is US$647 for a company-sponsored work and residence permit, and GH¢259 for a dependant. Anything quoted far above that deserves a second look before money changes hands.

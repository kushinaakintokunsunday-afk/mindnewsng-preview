NLNG, NCDMB invest $9m in research at six varsities

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria LNG Limited and the Nigerian Content Development and Monitoring Board have committed $9m to establish professorial chairs at six Nigerian universities t

The event, which took place at the NLNG Corporate Headquarters in Port Harcourt on Tuesday, 10 November, 2026, was aimed at developing commercially viable solutions to industry challenges, strengthening local research capacity and advancing technological innovation.

Under the arrangement, each university would receive $1.5m to establish a specialised professorial chair focused on agreed industry research priorities.

In his keynote address titled ‘Reshaping Nigeria’s Future Through Innovation, Research and Capacity Development’, Managing Director/Chief Executive Officer of NLNG, Engr. Adeleye Falade, described the programme as an investment in Nigeria’s capacity to translate academic knowledge into practical solutions.

Falade listed the beneficiary institutions and their focus areas as University of Ibadan: Natural Gas Processing, Pollution Control, and Environmental Management; Rivers State University: Digital Education, Intelligent Energy Systems, Automation, and Asset Integrity; Modibbo Adama University: Adamawa State, Energy Transition and Environment.

Others are Aliko Dangote University of Science and Technology (formerly Kano State University): Sustainable Energy Innovation and Climate-Resilient Food System; University of Nigeria, Nsukka, Enugu State: Green Energy Production and Geo-Environmental Engineering; and University of Abuja (now Yakubu Gowon University): Geoscience and Satellite Intelligence for Resilient Gas Infrastructures.

The institutions were selected across the country. NLNG’s General Manager, External Relations and Sustainable Development, Dr Sophia Horsfall, said: “As advised by NCDMB, we have ensured a pan-Nigerian approach by ensuring each geo-political zone of the federation is represented. NLNG will establish and endow professorial chairs in six universities across the zones, with each university bringing its own academic strength, experience and diversity just to enrich the programme.”

Falade explained that these universities will focus on their identified research areas based on earlier agreed critical research targets and commercialisation pathways to measure progress.

“They will also collectively host an information management hub for exchange programmes and knowledge sharing. Inclusive as part of the funding will be minor civil works and upgraded infrastructure to transform identified spaces into future-ready pieces of proposed research centres,” he stated.

Falade relished NLNG’s partnership with NCDMB, which birthed the initiative, describing the synergy as “very strong, and the foremost at this scale in Nigeria.”

The e-programme, he added, would support research into challenges central to the energy industry’s future, including improving operational efficiency, managing emissions, strengthening safety, maintaining critical infrastructure and making industrial processes more sustainable.

Beyond the research endowments, Falade outlined opportunities for a “triple helix” approach that brings government, which the NCDMB represents, the industry and academia together through specialised programmes, sabbaticals, exchange opportunities, practical testing of research assumptions and the joint development of postgraduate curricula.

According to the NLNG MD/CEO, continually reviewing how the initiative can translate research findings into practical industrial solutions will meet its objectives.

“What we’re looking forward to is to have people who come out from academia that will be able to solve today’s problem. They will be able to solve tomorrow’s problem, and academic institutions can continue to thrive on research, having people who are qualified but funded in partnership between NCDMB and NLNG.”

Falade stressed that research and development drive institutional progress, noting that organisations making significant breakthroughs invest heavily in research.

The Executive Secretary of the NCDMB, Engr. Felix Ogbe, represented by his Senior Technical Adviser, Engr. Austin Uzoka, shared Falade’s views, saying the $9m endowment programme represents a strategic investment in the future of Nigeria’s research and innovation ecosystem.

Ogbe added that these interventions demonstrate the Nigerian oil and gas industry’s commitment to creating an integrated research ecosystem that connects academic expertise, modern research infrastructure, industry requirements, and commercialisation opportunities.

By supporting specialised professorial chairs in critical areas of industry operations, he said NLNG and NCDMB are creating opportunities to bridge the longstanding gap between academic research and industrial application.

The NCDMB boss charged the institutions to “deliberately transition from research for publication to research for application, and from innovation as an academic pursuit to innovation as a driver of industrial transformation.”

“The beneficiary universities must see this endowment as both an opportunity and a responsibility, and they would be expected to deliver clearly defined research objectives, measurable outcomes, prudent resource utilisation, effective stakeholder engagement, and sustained collaboration with industry partners,” he added.

Responding, Vice-Chancellor of the Rivers State University, Prof. Isaac Zeb-Obipi, described the initiative as an investment in the intellectual sovereignty of Nigeria.

“For many years, we in the university have talked about industry-academia collaboration. Today, you have moved us from Memorandum of Understanding to Memorandum of Action.”

Meanwhile, Vice-Chancellor of the University of Abuja, Prof. Hakim Fawehinmi, said the area assigned to his institution helps protect Nigeria’s critical infrastructure such as gas pipelines.

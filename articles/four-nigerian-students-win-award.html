Four Nigerian students win award

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Four Nigerian students have earned global recognition after developing an innovative solution to water pollution by converting invasive water hyacinth into biod

The students, representing Destiny Christian Academy, Abuja, placed second in the Middle Division category of the Future Problem Solving World Solutions Challenge, an international academic competition involving schools from six continents, nine countries and 13 American states.

Their project, titled “Menace to Money: Wealth in Water Hyacinth”, proposed turning water hyacinth, which clogs waterways in Nigeria, into useful packaging materials while creating employment and income-generating opportunities for local communities.

The school’s administrator, Paul Onoja, disclosed the achievement while speaking with journalists in Abuja on Thursday, saying the institution was determined to win first place in future editions of the competition.

Onoja said the school scored over 93 per cent, narrowly missing the top spot despite competing against institutions from countries with access to advanced technology.

“You can imagine a school from Africa, where it is believed we are disadvantaged, proving to the world that we are not disadvantaged.

“The competition, as it is globally recognised, had given us the best platform to scale and compete amongst other leagues of institutions around the world,” he said.

He added that the school had identified areas requiring improvement and was already preparing for its next international outing.

“We are not preparing for second; we are preparing for the first position. I believe the second outing is going to be not just first position, but it’s going to be with a reasonable gap that even the person who will come next to us will not be close,” he said.

Onoja said the achievement reflected the institution’s commitment to academic excellence and the vision of its founders, the Senior Pastors of Dunamis International Gospel Centre, Dr Paul Enenche and his wife, Dr Becky Enenche, who established the school in 2005.

He said: “Like the Senior Pastor would always say, ‘You live ready.’ That is part of our principle. We live ready. You can call up any of our students for anything as he stands right now, and he will engage any competition globally.”

The administrator described the academy as an institution focused on developing children’s intellectual, spiritual, emotional and physical capacities to prepare them for life.

He disclosed that the school had participated consistently in international competitions, including events in South Africa, over the past four years.

Onoja added that the institution was preparing to represent Nigeria at a global robotics and engineering competition in Asia later this year.

The academy was represented in the competition by four students—Amos, Bamidele, Chukwura and Grace—who competed as the “DCA All-Stars”.

New Zealand’s Dilworth School placed first in the Middle Division category, while J. M. Robinson Middle School, North Carolina, United States, placed third.

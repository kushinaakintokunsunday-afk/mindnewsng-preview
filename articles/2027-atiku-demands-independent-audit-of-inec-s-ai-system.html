2027: Atiku demands independent audit of INEC’s AI system

By Punch Punch 5d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has called on the Independent National Electoral Commission to subject its proposed Artificial Intelligence system to an in

Atiku said the use of AI in election result management without adequate transparency and safeguards could undermine public confidence in the electoral process, particularly if algorithms were allowed to delay, reject or interfere with the transmission of polling unit results.

He asked the electoral commission to disclose the technical and operational details of the system, including its source code, training data, discrepancy-detection criteria and the identities of its developers and third-party vendors.

The former vice president made the demands in a petition addressed to the INEC Chairman on behalf of the African Democratic Congress and allied democratic stakeholders.

In a statement issued on Thursday by the Director of Strategic Communication of the ADC Presidential Campaign Council, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku said independent cybersecurity experts, political party representatives and civil society technologists should be granted access to the system for a comprehensive audit before the nationwide mock accreditation exercise scheduled for November 28, 2026.

He expressed concern over INEC’s announced integration of AI and intelligent document processing into the process linking its Data Verification and Authentication System with the INEC Result Viewing portal.

According to him, Nigerians should be informed about how the technology works and its role in election result management.

“INEC cannot ask Nigerians to trust a system whose rules they are forbidden to see. If an algorithm can flag a polling unit result sheet, the public deserves to know what triggers that flag, who set the threshold, who can change it and what happens to the result afterward,” he said.

Atiku said technical limitations, including poor lighting, unfamiliar handwriting and worn-out paper, could cause legitimate result sheets to be flagged or delayed.

He argued that such difficulties should not be grounds for withholding valid election results and called for discrepancies to be treated consistently across polling units.

The former presidential candidate also asked INEC to establish and publicise a procedure for reviewing results flagged by the AI system.

He said the commission should explain who would review such results, how decisions would be documented, how political parties would be notified and how erroneous assessments could be challenged.

Atiku said AI should serve only as an administrative tool under human supervision and should not be allowed to delete, alter or permanently block election results.

He also asked the commission to reaffirm the legal status of Form EC8A, the polling unit result sheet completed and signed by presiding officers and party agents.

“The polling unit result sheet, Form EC8A, records what happened where the people voted. INEC must publicly reaffirm the legal status of the physical form completed and signed at the polling unit, and explain how it will resolve any conflict between that document and an AI assessment.

“A machine’s verdict cannot be allowed to quietly displace the evidence witnessed by voters and party agents,” he added.

The ADC presidential candidate called for real-time monitoring access for political parties through a dedicated dashboard or observer interface to enable them to monitor result flagging, upload delays and blocked submissions during elections.

He urged the National Assembly to summon INEC’s leadership and its Information and Communication Technology Directorate to explain the system’s design, safeguards and independent audit arrangements.

Atiku also called on election observers and international partners to include the digital handling and transmission of results in their monitoring activities.

He advised voters to document results as announced and displayed at their polling units to provide records for comparison with electronically uploaded results.

The former vice president linked the demand for transparency to his campaign’s promise of an affordable life, saying Nigerians should be allowed to determine their political future without technological interference.

“Voters must be free to accept or reject that promise at the ballot box. No hidden algorithm should stand between a struggling family’s vote and the change that family hopes to see,” he said.

He urged INEC to address the concerns before public mistrust over the technology escalated into an electoral crisis.

INEC’s IReV portal provides public access to scanned copies of polling unit result sheets. The portal is distinct from the statutory process of collating and declaring election results.

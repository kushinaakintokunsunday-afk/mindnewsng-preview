Nigeria losing revenue by criminalising cannabis, Sowore insists

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The human rights activist and presidential candidate of the African Action Congress for the 2027 general election, Omoyele Sowore, has renewed his call for Nige

Sowore made the call on Tuesday in a post on X, where he cited revenue generated from cannabis sales and taxation in Canada and the United States as examples of the economic benefits of legalisation.

He said Nigeria was losing potential revenue while authorities continued to arrest people, destroy cannabis farms and prosecute those involved in the trade.

“Since 2018, I have advocated cannabis legalisation, arguing that Nigeria should stop criminalising an economically valuable plant and develop a properly regulated cannabis industry.

“While Nigerian authorities continue to arrest citizens, destroy cannabis farms, and criminalise the plant, countries that have legalised and regulated cannabis are collecting substantial government revenue,” he said.

Citing figures for the 2023-2024 fiscal year, Sowore said Canada generated approximately CA$2.2bn in government revenue from recreational cannabis. He also cited about $1.067bn in cannabis tax revenue generated by California in 2024 and $255.4m in marijuana taxes and fees collected by Colorado in the same year.

Statistics Canada reported that federal and provincial governments earned CA$2.2bn from recreational cannabis in the fiscal year ending March 31 2024. The figure included income from cannabis authorities, excise duties, retail sales taxes, other specific taxes and licensing fees.

California’s tax authority reported approximately $1.067bn in cannabis tax revenue for 2024, while Colorado’s Department of Revenue recorded $255.36m in marijuana tax and fee collections that year.

Sowore said Nigeria could generate between $550m and $2.2bn annually under different revenue scenarios, depending on the size of a legal market and the effectiveness of its taxation and regulatory systems.

Using an illustrative exchange rate of N1,300 to the dollar, he put the possible annual revenue at between N715bn and N2.86tn.

He stressed that the figures were hypothetical and that Nigeria’s actual earnings would depend on factors including market size, taxation, licensing, exports and regulatory effectiveness.

The activist also argued that legalisation could create employment opportunities in agriculture, pharmaceutical manufacturing and industrial hemp production, while supporting scientific research and export earnings.

He called for a legal framework that would regulate production, protect public health, support farmers and generate government revenue.

Cannabis remains illegal in Nigeria under the country’s Indian Hemp Act, which prohibits its cultivation, unlawful importation, sale, exportation, smoking and possession. The law defines Indian hemp to include plants and parts of plants belonging to the cannabis genus.

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control has also previously stated that cannabis cultivation, consumption, handling and possession remain illegal under Nigerian law.

Sowore has advocated cannabis legalisation since 2018, arguing that the country should consider its economic potential through a regulated industry.

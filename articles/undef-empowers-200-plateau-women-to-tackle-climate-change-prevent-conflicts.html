UNDEF empowers 200 Plateau women to tackle climate change, prevent conflicts

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

No fewer than 200 women in four communities in Plateau State are being equipped with skills to tackle climate change and prevent conflicts under a 24-month init

The project, implemented by the Grassroots Researchers Association, covers Angwan Rogo, Farin Gada, Bukuru and Anglo Jos communities in Jos North and Jos South Local Government Areas of the state.

It seeks to establish women-led advocacy platforms to enable participants to influence community decisions, engage stakeholders on environmental challenges and promote peaceful coexistence.

Speaking with journalists on Friday during a workshop in Bukuru, Jos South LGA, the Executive Director of GRA, Friday Bitrus, said women were among the most affected by climate change because of their dependence on natural resources and their responsibilities in sustaining household livelihoods.

“Women are always at the receiving end when climate crises occur. They bear the consequences of flooding, changing weather patterns and other environmental challenges, yet they often lack the resources and capacity to respond effectively,” he said.

Bitrus said the initiative was designed to bridge the gap by equipping women with practical knowledge and skills to adapt to climate change and participate actively in community decision-making.

“We want women to move beyond being victims of climate change to becoming active participants in finding solutions. They must have the knowledge, confidence and platforms to engage those responsible for decisions affecting their communities,” he added.

He said the project, which would run from July 2026 to June 2028, would also bring community leaders together to develop action protocols for responding to environmental challenges and preventing conflicts.

According to him, participants would be encouraged to identify problems affecting their communities and collectively demand appropriate interventions from relevant government authorities.

The GRA Mobilisation Officer, Bashir Ibrahim, said the project was also aimed at addressing the exclusion of women and young people from community governance and peacebuilding processes.

“Women and young people are important stakeholders in community development, but they are often left out when decisions are being made. We want to change that by ensuring their voices are heard and their contributions recognised,” Ibrahim said.

He disclosed that 25 traditional rulers, religious and youth leaders had undergone training and signed a pledge to promote inclusive governance by involving women and youths in peace committees and decision-making processes.

Ibrahim said the women would also serve as knowledge multipliers by taking the lessons acquired during the workshops back to their communities.

“Our expectation is that the knowledge will not end with the participants. They will return to their communities, educate other women and encourage collective action on climate resilience and conflict prevention,” he said.

A community leader, Habu Suleman, said the training had exposed participants to practical ways of addressing environmental challenges and managing disagreements before they escalated into conflicts.

“The training has opened our eyes to the connection between environmental challenges and community conflicts. We have learnt that people can prevent many disagreements through dialogue, cooperation and proper management of available resources,” he said.

A participant, Hannatu Gobim, said she was prepared to share the knowledge acquired with other women to strengthen community awareness.

“I have learnt that women should not remain silent when issues affecting their environment and livelihoods arise. We can contribute to finding solutions, and I will take this knowledge back to other women in my community,” she said.

Another participant, Zainab Danladi, said flooding and changing weather patterns had continued to affect farming, livestock and household livelihoods.

“Flooding and unpredictable weather affect our farms, animals and sources of livelihood. This training has helped us understand some practical ways of responding to these challenges. I will also share what I have learnt with other women,” she said.

The women-led multi-stakeholder workshops, tagged “Empowering Women for Climate Resilience and Conflict Prevention in Plateau State,” also exposed participants to practical climate adaptation strategies, including environmental hazard mapping, soil conservation, rainwater harvesting and seasonal resource-sharing schedules.

Other areas covered included alternative dispute resolution, gender governance, constitutional rights, local government budget tracking, engagement with ward development committees and legislative advocacy.

ADC crisis deepens as BoT orders Atiku, others to halt campaigns

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

• Demands removal of Mark, Aregbesola, delisting of candidates From Charity Nwakaudu, Abuja The leadership crisis rocking the African Democratic Congress (ADC)

• Demands removal of Mark, Aregbesola, delisting of candidates

From Charity Nwakaudu, Abuja

The leadership crisis rocking the African Democratic Congress (ADC) has taken a fresh turn, with the Board of Trustees (BoT) aligned with Nafiu Bala Gombe demanding that former Vice President Atiku Abubakar and other members of the David Mark-led faction immediately suspend all political campaigns, rallies and public engagements in the party’s name.

The board also asked the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to remove former Senate President David Mark and former Osun State Governor Rauf Aregbesola from its records as national leaders of the party and delist all candidates submitted by the disputed leadership for the 2027 general elections.

The demands, which could further deepen the opposition party’s internal crisis ahead of the January 16 presidential election, followed the November 5 judgment of the Federal High Court in Abuja invalidating the emergence of the Mark-led leadership and decisions taken under it.

In a statement issued yesterday, the board’s chairman, Rufus Ikanmi, insisted that the judgment delivered by Justice Peter Lifu in Suit No. FHC/ABJ/CS/1819/2025 remained valid and binding, having neither been stayed nor overturned by a superior court.

“The judgment of the Honourable Court is valid, binding and subsisting. It has not been stayed, varied or set aside by any superior court. INEC, as a statutory body bound by the rule of law, has a constitutional duty to give full effect to it without further delay,” Ikanmi said.

The board demanded that INEC recognise and deal exclusively with the leadership associated with Bala Gombe, maintaining that the continued recognition of Mark and Aregbesola amounted to disregard for the court’s decision.

It also called for the delisting of all candidates uploaded to the commission’s portal by the Mark-led leadership, alleging that their nominations violated the 1999 Constitution, as amended, the Electoral Act and the party’s constitution.

The board specifically called on Atiku and others associated with the disputed leadership to stop all campaigns and public engagements conducted in the ADC’s name until the judgment was complied with.

Ikanmi warned that continued disregard for the court’s decision would amount to contempt and an affront to constitutional democracy and the rule of law.

He appealed to party members and supporters to remain calm and law-abiding, assuring them that the board would pursue lawful measures to secure compliance with the judgment.

The latest development comes amid continuing legal efforts by the Mark-led ADC to overturn the November 5 judgment, which invalidated the leadership arrangement that produced the party’s national executive and the process through which Atiku emerged as its presidential candidate.

The ADC Presidential Campaign Council had earlier announced that it filed an appeal and an application for a stay of execution, urging INEC to refrain from taking steps that could prejudice the pending appellate proceedings.

Its Director of Strategic Communications, Phrank Shaibu, maintained that the party would challenge the judgment through the courts and continue its political activities.

The rival positions have left the party facing uncertainty over its national leadership and the status of its candidates as preparations intensify for the 2027 general elections.

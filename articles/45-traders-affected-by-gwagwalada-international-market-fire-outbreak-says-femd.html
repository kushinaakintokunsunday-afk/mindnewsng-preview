45 Traders Affected By Gwagwalada International Market Fire Outbreak, Says FEMD

The FCT Emergency Management Department (FEMD) has confirmed that about 45 traders operating 28 open stalls and two lock-up shops were affected by a fire outbreak at the Gwagwalada International Market in Gwagwalada Area Council.

The FEMD said the incident, which occurred last Saturday, prompted a post-disaster assessment visit to the market by the Director, Muhammad Dikko, accompanied by officials of the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA).

“During the assessment, the Chairman of the Market Association, Mr. Aminu Lawal Bakinruwa, informed the delegation that security personnel on duty noticed smoke emanating from one of the shops at about 8:00 p.m., approximately 20 minutes after electricity supply was restored by the Abuja Electricity Distribution Company (AEDC),” a statement by the Head of Public Affairs at FEMD, Nkechi Isa, disclosed this in a statement on Monday,” partly read.

It explained that some of the affected traders, who spoke with the FEMD delegation, expressed deep distress over the losses incurred, describing the incident as a major setback to their businesses and livelihoods.

“One of the traders, Mrs. Salome Israel, who was visibly emotional, disclosed that she lost goods worth approximately ₦400,000, including yams, tomatoes and peppers. Other items destroyed by the fire included clothing, herbal medicines and beverages,” it stated.

“Addressing the affected traders, the FEMD Director, Muhammad Dikko, urged market operators to prioritise fire safety by ensuring that all electrical appliances are switched off before leaving their shops.

“He also emphasised the importance of insurance coverage for businesses, noting that insured traders would be in a better position to recover their losses in the event of disasters.”

It further said that Dikko stressed the need for the market to be equipped with adequate firefighting equipment to facilitate prompt intervention during emergencies, and appealed to the Market Board of Trustees and the leadership of the traders’ union to explore ways of providing support to the affected traders and easing their hardship.

“Speaking on behalf of NEMA, Mrs. Mariam Dantanko clarified that the Agency’s disaster response support is primarily provided through relief materials rather than direct cash payments,” the statement added. “She explained that such assistance, which may include food and building materials, is subject to assessment and approval by the agency’s headquarters.”

Meanwhile, the Relief and Rehabilitation Division of FEMD was also present at the scene to conduct a needs assessment to determine the immediate requirements of the affected traders.

The department reiterated the importance of fire safety consciousness, regular inspection of electrical installations, and the provision of functional firefighting equipment in markets and other public facilities to prevent avoidable fire incidents.

Osun CSOs demand law against indecent dressing

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Two Osun State-based civil society organisations, Dialogue 365 and Nigerians for Good Governance, have demanded laws stipulating minimum dress standards, especi

They said the demand was prompted by the growing trend of scantily dressed women walking on streets and major roads in the state.

In a statement jointly signed by the conveners of the two groups, Waheed Saka and Wole Oladapo, respectively, and obtained in Osogbo on Friday, they argued that many young women were often found on streets and in public places without proper clothing.

The groups acknowledged the right of individuals to dress as they wished, provided they did not infringe on the rights of others.

However, the CSOs argued that appearing in public places in revealing dresses could also infringe on the rights of people who did not subscribe to such a way of life.

“We have in recent months observed the growing trend of people, especially ladies, appearing on streets in Osogbo in particular without being properly dressed.

“Many of those doing it move around major roads in Osogbo, the state capital, without wearing underwear. This is alien to our culture as Omoluabi.

“People are displeased and we cannot pretend not to observe this trend. Those involved are almost no longer covering their nakedness. The sense of decency associated with us as Omoluabi appears to be disappearing fast.

“We understand that people have the right to appear the way it is comfortable for them. But when your appearance is offensive to other people, you are also infringing on their rights.

“Curiously, the disturbing development has found its way into secondary schools in the state, where many female pupils who are expected to appear decent in class by wearing proper underwear are often found doing the opposite, with the excuse that they are not comfortable wearing underwear. There must be action to deal with this situation,” the groups said.

The groups subsequently urged the Osun State House of Assembly to enact laws stipulating minimum dress standards for women appearing in public places.

“Hiding behind excuses of excessive heat may not be tenable. Government should protect this society before more damage is done.

“There should be a law that will state the minimum dressing for women and even men appearing in public.

“The idea of walking around streets without underwear should not be allowed to gain more followers in the state,” the statement further read.

Commenting on the demand by the CSOs, the member representing Obokun State Constituency in the Osun State House of Assembly, Adewumi Adeyemi, said the concerns expressed by the groups were genuine.

Adeyemi, however, said enacting a law to regulate how people dress might not be the solution, adding that enforcing such a law would be difficult.

“Coming up with a law to check that trend may be challenging. How do we enforce it? Such a law can only be enforced in tertiary institutions and secondary schools where dress codes can be introduced. If a school says you must appear in a certain way, if you are displeased, you can leave and move elsewhere.

“But how do we enforce such a law on the streets? Are we going to ask those involved to leave Osun or Orita Sabo or wherever they live and go elsewhere?

“The constitution allows you to appear the way you want. But if CSOs are really interested in helping us in curbing this trend, enlightenment is important, not necessarily the law,” Adeyemi said.

The lawmaker also challenged the CSOs to organise visits to schools in the state to engage pupils and educate them about what he described as the negative aspects of indecent dressing.

He also emphasised parental influence, saying those involved might even be copying the act from their parents.

“I am a teen teacher. Sometimes, we observe that some of the things these teenagers are doing, they learn from their parents.

“These CSOs should go out and educate teachers, parents and others about this menace. I believe gradually, things will change. I share the concerns expressed by the CSOs. Things are getting bad,” Adeyemi said.

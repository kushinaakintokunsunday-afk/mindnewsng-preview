2027: Opposition Coalition reschedules second summit to Nov 30

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 4d ago 3 min read

The Coalition for Democracy, an alliance of six major opposition political parties in Nigeria, has postponed its second summit from Monday, November 23, to Monday, November 30, 2026.

The coalition said the decision was taken to accommodate the busy nationwide campaign schedules of its member parties ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The change of date was announced in a statement issued on Friday in Abuja by the Convener of the G100 and signatory on behalf of the coalition parties, Malam Salihu Moh. Lukman.

According to the statement, the coalition’s Central Leadership Committee approved the postponement following requests from some member parties.

The requests were reportedly made because their presidential candidates and campaign teams are currently engaged in intensive mobilisation activities across the country.

The coalition said the adjustment would allow the affected parties to manage their campaign commitments while preparing for the summit.

Explaining the rationale behind the postponement, the Committee emphasised that securing the physical presence of all key political actors outweighed holding the gathering on its previously announced date.

“It is more important that every party, every presidential candidate and every national chairman is in the room than that the Summit holds on its original date,” the Committee stated.

To guarantee full participation at the rescheduled event—earlier slated for the NAF Conference Centre in Abuja—all participating political parties have resolved to temporarily halt their electioneering activities across the federation on the new date.

“In consultation with key stakeholders, the Committee has agreed on 30 November to secure the widest possible participation. To make that commitment real, all member parties have agreed to suspend campaign activities nationwide on Monday, 30 November, so that their leadership can attend the Summit in full,” the statement read.

Describing the agreement to pause politicking as a deliberate demonstration of collective resolve, the coalition noted that safeguarding Nigeria’s democratic space supersedes individual partisan ambitions.

“That decision is itself a statement. Parties competing hard for votes are prepared to stand down their campaigns for a day, together, because the defence of Nigeria’s democratic space is bigger than any one campaign,” the Committee added.

At the November 30 gathering, opposition leaders are expected to finalise and formally sign the coalition’s common agreement—a Covenant for Democratic Renewal—while mapping out a coordinated strategy ahead of the January 16, 2027 elections.

“We will walk into that room united, and walk out of it committed,” the Committee assured.

While apologising to delegates, party leaders, and partners for any inconvenience caused by the rescheduling, the Committee confirmed that the previously communicated venue and accreditation arrangements remain unchanged. It said it would provide further updates to delegates and the media in due course.

Emerging from the inaugural G100 Opposition Summit held in Abuja on August 31, 2026, the Coalition for Democracy is spearheaded by six political parties: the African Democratic Congress (ADC), Allied Peoples Movement (APM), Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), Peoples Democratic Party (PDP), Peoples Redemption Party (PRP), and Social Democratic Party (SDP). The platform aims to foster opposition coordination, defend constitutional democracy, uphold the rule of law, and safeguard the credibility of the 2027 electoral process.

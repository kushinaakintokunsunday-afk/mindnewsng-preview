2027 poll: Showunmi writes Ribadu, urges him to set firm boundaries for political conduct

Says Nigeria navigating delicate security environment

_______________________________________________________________________

From Laide Raheem, Abeokuta

Convener of The Alternative Movement, Segun Showunmi, has called on the National Security Adviser, Mallam Nuhu Ribadu, to set firm boundaries for political conduct and initiate decisive action against politicians and their supporters whose words and actions threaten national security.

Showunmi, who made the call in an open letter addressed to the National Security Adviser, a copy of which was made available to Daily Sun on Monday, said Nigeria was navigating a particularly delicate security environment that demanded vigilance, institutional coordination and responsible leadership.

In the open letter, titled “On the Urgent Need to Establish Firm Boundaries for Political Conduct and Hold Those Who Threaten National Security Accountable”, he warned that political competition, personal ambition and partisan disagreements must not be allowed to become additional threats to national stability.

“There is a difference between robust political engagement and conduct that deliberately or recklessly threatens public peace. There is a difference between criticising the government and deliberately inciting citizens against one another,” he said.

Showunmi expressed concern over what he described as the normalisation of provocative statements, ethnic and religious insinuations, threats, inflammatory public performances and the deliberate manipulation of sensitive national issues for political advantage.

He said some individuals seemed to believe that because they were politicians, party loyalists, public commentators or influential supporters of powerful political interests, they could say virtually anything, mobilise others irresponsibly and walk away without consequences.

“That assumption must be firmly challenged. National security cannot be held hostage to political ambition,” he added.

He stressed that the right to participate in democratic politics did not include the right to endanger the Republic, adding that political disagreements must never become a licence to incite violence, threaten citizens, encourage lawlessness or create conditions that could trigger communal tensions.

Showunmi urged Ribadu’s office, working with relevant security and law-enforcement institutions, to establish and publicly communicate clear boundaries for political conduct, particularly as the country moved deeper into an increasingly competitive political period.

He proposed six urgent measures to the Office of the NSA: the establishment of clear national boundaries for political communication; application of the law without regard to political status; development of a coordinated rapid-response mechanism against political misinformation and incitement; holding political parties and their leadership structures accountable for the conduct of individuals acting under their banners or on their behalf; establishment of a clear protocol for early intervention; and making accountability independent of political hierarchy, to curb the political menace.

Showunmi, however, clarified that his letter was not a call to suppress legitimate opposition, silence critical voices or criminalise political disagreement, noting that dissent, criticism, protest and competing political visions were essential ingredients of democratic governance.

“But democracy must also protect the Republic from those who would exploit its freedoms to threaten its existence and security,” he said.

He added that the government must be able to distinguish between legitimate political expression and conduct that crossed the threshold into criminality, through established legal standards, credible evidence, due process and respect for fundamental rights.

“We cannot continue to behave as though national security is the exclusive responsibility of soldiers, policemen, intelligence officers and other security operatives. Political actors also have a responsibility to preserve the environment in which democracy can function,” Showunmi emphasised.

He appealed to Ribadu to send an unmistakable message to every political actor who believed political relevance conferred immunity, that the rules applied to everyone.

“We must not wait for an avoidable national tragedy before demonstrating the seriousness of our institutions. Enough is enough. Nigeria must come first, and nobody’s political ambition is worth the peace, stability and security of our country,” he concluded.

The letter was copied to President Bola Ahmed Tinubu, the Minister of Defence, General Christopher Musa (Rtd), the Special Adviser to the President on Homeland Security, Major General Adeyinka A. Famadewa (Rtd), the Inspector-General of Police, Olatunji Rilwan Disu, the Director-General of the Department of State Services, Mr Oluwatosin Ajayi, and the Minister of State for Defence, Alhaji Bello Muhammad Matawalle.

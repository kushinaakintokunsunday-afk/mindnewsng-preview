Appeal Court declares war on corruption, manipulation of election petitions

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 17 Nov 2026 5 min read

From Godwin Tsa, Abuja

Ahead of the 2027 general elections, the Court of Appeal has declared zero tolerance for corruption, manipulation of court records, backdating of processes and other fraudulent practices capable of undermining the credibility of election petition tribunals, warning that officials found compromising the administration of electoral justice would face severe sanctions.

President of the Court of Appeal, Justice Monica Dongban-Mensem, who issued the warning in Abuja yesterday, said the judiciary would not hesitate to deal with tribunal secretaries and registry officials who allow themselves to be compromised by politicians, litigants or their lawyers in the course of handling election petitions.

Speaking at the opening of a two-day capacity-building workshop for Election Petition Tribunal registry staff at the Body of Benchers, Dongban-Mensem cautioned that the approaching elections would place the judiciary under intense public scrutiny, making integrity, transparency and strict adherence to legal procedures indispensable.

The workshop, themed: “Modern Registry Practices for Effective Election Petition Tribunal Administration,” was organised to prepare tribunal officials for the challenges of managing election petitions arising from the presidential, National Assembly, governorship and state Houses of Assembly elections.

Dongban-Mensem warned participants against regarding their appointments as opportunities for personal enrichment, stressing that their conduct could either strengthen public confidence in the judiciary or undermine the credibility of the electoral dispute resolution process.

“Your integrity and that of the Tribunal and its members are in the public searchlight,” she declared, urging the officials not to allow themselves to be used “as weapons to truncate the process.”

She reminded them that the responsibility entrusted to tribunal secretaries and registry personnel went beyond routine administrative duties, as their actions could have far-reaching consequences for the determination of election petitions and the protection of democratic institutions.

The Court of Appeal president advised them to place professional integrity above financial inducements, noting that a good name was better than silver and gold.

She explained that Section 285(1) and (2) of the 1999 Constitution, as amended, empowered her to constitute election petition tribunals across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory, while Section 134(3)(a) and (b) of the Electoral Act 2026 required the tribunals to be constituted at least 30 days before an election and their registries opened seven days before polling.

Describing the registry as a vital organ of the tribunal system, Dongban-Mensem said its responsibilities included receiving petitions, serving court processes, issuing hearing notices, preparing proceedings and safeguarding judicial records.

She stressed that modern registry administration demanded efficient electronic filing, reliable case management systems, accurate documentation, proper archiving and prompt service of processes, particularly because election petitions were governed by strict constitutional and statutory timelines.

In a separate warning, Chief Registrar of the Court of Appeal, Umar Bangari, said tribunal officials who engaged in backdating of court documents, collusion with litigants, tampering with case files or unauthorised disclosure of confidential information would face severe consequences.

Bangari specifically warned against giving litigants or their counsel advance copies of court processes, manipulating filing dates to accommodate petitions submitted outside statutory deadlines and leaking draft rulings, panel deliberations or other privileged judicial information.

He identified integrity, strict adherence to timelines, proactive communication and professionalism as essential requirements for the effective administration of election petition tribunals.

“A single administrative lapse, delayed endorsement, or misplaced process can occasion a damaging miscarriage of justice,” he warned.

According to him, the credibility of election petition tribunals rests heavily on the conduct of their registry officials, whose handling of documents, processes and records could determine whether justice is properly administered.

He cautioned that any secretary found conniving with a political party, candidate or legal practitioner to manipulate tribunal proceedings would be sanctioned, adding that officials who demonstrated outstanding diligence and professionalism would be rewarded.

Bangari noted that the presidential and National Assembly elections were scheduled for January 16, 2027, while the governorship and state Houses of Assembly elections would take place on February 6, stressing that the judiciary must be adequately prepared for the petitions that could arise from the contests.

He said the spotlight of the entire nation would turn on the judiciary as candidates and political parties challenged election outcomes, making it imperative for tribunal officials to maintain the highest standards of professional conduct.

The chief registrar disclosed that the Chief Justice of Nigeria had, the previous week, inaugurated 375 judicial officers drawn from trial courts across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory to serve on election petition tribunals.

He explained that the workshop was a continuation of preparations for the tribunals, with seven sessions covering the Electoral Act, the Judicial Electoral Manual 2026, electronic case management, record-keeping and statutory deadlines for filing and serving court processes.

Also speaking, Acting Deputy Chief Registrar in charge of the Election Petition Tribunal Unit, Maitumbi D.A., disclosed that the Court of Appeal was strengthening its case management system to address recurring concerns over conflicting judgments by election petition tribunals.

She said a committee had reviewed the 2022 edition of the Judicial Electoral Manual and produced an updated 2026 edition, which would soon be made available to tribunal officials.

According to her, the Election Petition Tribunal Case Management System was being upgraded to ensure that tribunal decisions were scanned and uploaded to a central database, providing judicial officers with access to previous decisions for reference and guidance.

She explained that the initiative was intended to improve consistency in tribunal decisions, strengthen record management and enhance the efficiency of election petition adjudication.

Maitumbi also outlined a code of conduct requiring tribunal secretaries to submit regular reports to the Election Petition Tribunal Unit and provide weekly updates through the electronic reporting platform.

She warned that tribunal officials were prohibited from granting media interviews without express authorisation, soliciting gratification from lawyers, litigants or members of the public, or releasing official documents and confidential information without approval.

The warnings come as the judiciary intensifies administrative and judicial preparations for the 2027 general elections, with particular attention to the integrity of tribunal proceedings, compliance with statutory timelines and the proper handling of election petitions.

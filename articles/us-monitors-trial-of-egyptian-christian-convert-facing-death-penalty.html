US monitors trial of Egyptian Christian convert facing death penalty

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The United States Department of State said it is closely monitoring the trial of an Egyptian man who converted from Islam to Christianity and could face the dea

The case involves Said Mansour Rezk Abdelrazek, whose prosecution has drawn international attention over concerns that his religious conversion and expression of Christian beliefs are being treated as criminal offences.

A State Department spokesperson told Fox News Digital that the US was following the proceedings and urged the Egyptian government to protect the religious rights of its citizens.

“We are monitoring his trial closely. Religious freedom is a fundamental human value, and we encourage the Egyptian government to protect the religious freedom rights of all its citizens,” the spokesperson said.

According to Fox News, Abdelrazek’s lawyer, Said Fayez, said prosecutors requested the maximum penalty during a hearing in early November. The punishment could be execution or 25 years in prison.

The lawyer’s office said the prosecution had concluded its arguments and asked the court to impose the maximum sentence available under the law.

Abdelrazek’s defence is expected to present its closing arguments on December 8.

The 30-year-old was arrested at his home in Cairo on July 15, 2025, according to Amnesty International. He was held for nine months before being referred to trial before the Terrorism Circuit of the First Criminal Court in Badr.

The charges against him include joining a banned terrorist organisation, contempt for Islam, inciting unrest and spreading false information.

However, Coptic Solidarity, an advocacy organisation representing Egyptian Christians and other religious minorities, said the allegations had not been established in court.

Its spokeswoman, Lindsay Rodriguez, told Fox News that the case raised concerns about religious freedom and the fairness of the trial.

“These remain prosecution allegations, not findings of guilt,” she said.

Rodriguez added that the organisation was concerned about reports of religious remarks attributed to the presiding judge during the November 3 hearing, saying they raised questions about whether Abdelrazek could receive an impartial trial.

“Peaceful religious conversion and expression must not be treated as terrorism or national-security offences,” she said.

She urged the United States, the European Union and Australia to use the December 8 hearing to press Egypt on religious freedom and fair-trial standards.

Abdelrazek’s fiancée, Sophia, an Australian-Egyptian citizen who declined to disclose her surname over security concerns, has also been campaigning for his release.

“I am pleading for them to release him,” she told Fox News.

Amnesty International said Abdelrazek’s prosecution stemmed from his conversion and religious beliefs and called on Egyptian authorities to release him.

The rights organisation also raised concerns about his treatment in detention and his access to legal representation.

Fox News reported that it contacted Egypt’s Embassy in Washington and its Foreign Ministry for comments but did not receive a response.

Egypt has an estimated population of 120 million people, with Coptic Christians accounting for about 10 per cent of the population.

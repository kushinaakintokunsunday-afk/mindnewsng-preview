Residents lament as flooding, bad roads cripple Ogun community

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The residents who spoke with PUNCH Metro correspondent last Wednesday said the poor condition of the roads had made movement difficult, particularly during the rainy season, forcing landlords and community members to contribute money towards repairs.

Our correspondent, who visited the area, observed that a section of the road was flooded after a brief rainfall.

It was also observed that commercial motorcyclists resorted to using water from the flood to wash their motorcycles after the rainfall.

The residents lamented that despite repeated appeals to the government, the community had yet to receive any intervention to address the worsening road conditions.

A resident, Omoniyi Familola, said, “For about 25 years, our community has not enjoyed good road infrastructure.”

He attributed the flooding partly to the flow of water into the community during rainfall, saying it had worsened the condition of the roads.

Familola added that the situation was usually worse between June and August, when residents struggled to move around, and parents faced difficulties taking their children to school.

He also said floodwater sometimes carried snakes and other dangerous reptiles into the community, posing additional risks to residents.

According to him, the Community Development Association and the Community Development Committee had made several efforts to draw the government’s attention to the situation, adding that the community had also featured on Channels Television.

Another resident, Odunsi, who said he had lived in the community for almost 20 years, attributed part of the road deterioration to the movement of heavy-duty vehicles, including trucks and tippers transporting sand and granite.

Odunsi said, “This place leads to my house. Whenever I see that the road is in bad condition, I call two people so that we can contribute money to fill it and make it motorable.”

Similarly, Adebijo Omowunmi described the situation as distressing, saying heavy rainfall often left the roads submerged, making it difficult for residents to identify drainage channels.

She appealed to the government to visit the community and provide a lasting solution to the problem.

Also speaking, a CDA chairman who identified himself simply as Mr Ahmed said residents had repeatedly appealed to the government since he moved into the area in 2005.

“Since I moved into this community in 2005, we have been appealing to the government to rehabilitate, repair or reconstruct the road,” Ahmed said.

Ahmed said schoolchildren and adults had fallen into damaged sections of the roads, while vehicles sometimes struggled to pass through certain areas.

He added that residents spent millions of naira annually on grading and maintaining the roads, but their condition continued to deteriorate.

When contacted for a reaction, the Special Adviser to Governor Dapo Abiodun on Media and Strategy, Kayode Akinmade, attributed the community’s vulnerability to flooding partly to its geographical location, describing Oke-Afa as a naturally low-lying floodplain.

The government said the area’s topography presented challenges for road construction, drainage infrastructure and physical development, particularly during heavy rainfall.

He said, “The Ogun State Government is aware of the concerns of residents of Oke-Afa and the surrounding communities in Magboro. However, it is important to appreciate the peculiar geographical and environmental characteristics of the area.

“Oke-Afa is situated within a naturally low-lying floodplain, making it particularly vulnerable to flooding, especially during periods of heavy rainfall. This geographical reality presents significant challenges for road construction, drainage infrastructure and physical development.

“In areas with such environmental characteristics, sustainable development requires proper land-use planning, adequate drainage infrastructure and compliance with established building regulations. The government has undertaken flood-mitigation interventions in the wider Magboro axis, including the dredging of Oke-Afa Stream, to improve water flow and mitigate flooding.”

On the proposed road rehabilitation, the government said the scheduling of specific projects depended on technical assessments, prioritisation, funding and administrative considerations.

It added that any lasting intervention would require adequate drainage and water-management solutions, noting that road repairs without addressing the drainage challenges could provide only temporary relief.

On allegations that drainage water was being diverted into the community, Akinmade said the claims required technical investigation to determine whether there had been unauthorised alterations to natural waterways or drainage channels.

He continued, “Allegations of drainage water being diverted into the community require proper technical investigation to establish the facts.

“The relevant authorities will need to examine the natural waterways, existing drainage channels and adjoining developments to determine whether there have been unauthorised alterations to the natural flow of water.”

He also urged property owners in the flood-prone area to comply with building regulations, including raising building foundations and floor levels to minimise flooding risks.

He concluded that the OGSG would continue to work through relevant ministries and agencies to identify appropriate interventions to improve drainage infrastructure and address road rehabilitation needs.

Black teen charged for shooting identical twin to death in Washington DC

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

A 16-year-old boy, Jamari Young, has been arrested and charged as an adult in the fatal shooting of his identical twin brother, Jabrell Young, inside their home

A 16-year-old boy, Jamari Young, has been arrested and charged as an adult in the fatal shooting of his identical twin brother, Jabrell Young, inside their home in Washington, DC, United States.

The shooting occurred on October 25 but Jamari was taken into custody on Wednesday, November 18, by the US Marshals Service, according to the Metropolitan Police Department.

He has been charged with second-degree murder while armed in the death of his twin brother.

On Thursday, a judge ordered him held without bond.

Police said officers responded at about 8:36 am on Sunday, October 25, to a report of a shooting at a home in the 200 block of 46th Street NE, in Northeast DC.

They found Jabrell unconscious and not breathing with an apparent gunshot wound.

He was pronounced dead at the scene.

Family members said he died in his mother’s arms in the basement of the home.

According to court documents, the brothers had been arguing after Jabrell allegedly went into Jamari’s room and touched his belongings.

A parent told police they found the twins tussling outside their adjacent basement bedrooms and tried to separate them when a gun went off.

The affidavit states that Jamari told his mother in the aftermath: “I didn’t mean to, it just went off. I didn’t mean to, I’m sorry ma.”

Police investigated the death as a homicide and launched a search for Jamari, who had left the house after the shooting.

Investigators recovered two .45-caliber shell casings inside the home but have yet to recover the weapon used in the killing.

Police also said a loaded 9mm pistol found wrapped in a blanket under a nearby parked car was linked to an unsolved fatal shooting in July, though it is not believed to be the murder weapon.

Family members have insisted the shooting was an accident.

The twins’ mother, Chantel Poe, and their aunt told reporters that the brothers were inseparable and that Jamari was terrified and did not intend to shoot his brother.

At Thursday’s hearing, Jamari’s defence lawyer argued for his release on personal recognisance, saying witnesses indicated the shooting was an accident and that he turned himself in.

Prosecutors countered that he did not surrender until nearly three weeks later.

The judge said he did not believe Jamari intended to kill his brother, but found probable cause for second-degree murder.

As she left the courtroom, Poe shouted to her son: “I love you. I’m here to support you.”

She later told reporters, “This is a lie. They are making it seem like this is not an accident. They paint a narrative that this was done malicious[ly]. And it was not.”

Nigeria establishes direct flights to Ireland

From Isaac Anumihe, Abuja

As part of efforts to strengthen bilateral relations, trade, tourism and people-to-people connectivity between Nigeria and Ireland, the Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, has established direct flight operations between both countries.

The minister made this known when he received the Irish Ambassador to Nigeria, Ms Lesley Ní Bhriain, who paid him a courtesy visit to discuss areas of mutual interest and opportunities for enhanced co-operation in the aviation sector.

During the meeting, Ambassador Ní Bhriain identified improved visa services as a critical requirement for expanding bilateral engagements, particularly in aviation, trade and tourism. She disclosed that since assuming office in September, she had recognised the need to improve Ireland’s visa processing capacity and had engaged her Ministry of Justice on the deployment of additional personnel to visa offices.

The ambassador explained that improving visa services would be essential to facilitating discussions around direct flights, business partnerships and other areas of co-operation between Nigeria and Ireland.

Responding, the minister welcomed the ambassador and commended her decision to make aviation one of the priority areas of her diplomatic engagement with Nigeria.

He described the relationship between both countries as longstanding and mutually beneficial, noting that Dublin occupies a strategic position in the global aviation industry, particularly as a major centre for aircraft leasing and aviation finance.

In a statement, the minister recalled Nigeria’s longstanding participation in the annual Airline Economics Conference in Dublin, where global aircraft lessors and key aviation stakeholders converge to explore business opportunities and negotiate industry partnerships.

He also acknowledged the support and co-operation extended to Nigeria by the ambassador’s predecessor, particularly in facilitating Nigeria’s participation in the Ireland-Africa Summit in April 2025.

Keyamo noted that Nigeria’s signing and implementation of the Cape Town Convention had significantly improved the country’s standing in the global aircraft leasing market, creating fresh opportunities for engagement with international lessors.

He specifically highlighted the growing relationship with AerCap, one of the world’s leading aircraft leasing companies, which has supported Nigeria’s efforts in establishing an aircraft leasing company and facilitating access to dry-lease arrangements for domestic airlines.

On the possibility of direct flights between Nigeria and Ireland, the minister expressed strong interest, observing that the growing Nigerian community in Ireland, coupled with increasing movements for education, tourism, business and family engagements, makes direct air connectivity increasingly necessary.

He proposed that both countries establish a technical team to examine the existing Bilateral Air Services Agreement (BASA), determine whether it requires updating and identify opportunities for Nigerian and Irish airlines to commence direct flight operations, even if initially on a limited weekly schedule.

To ensure that the discussions translate into concrete action, the minister immediately set up a team to follow up engagements with the relevant directors and technical officials of the ministry and to work with the Irish Embassy on the proposed BASA review and connectivity arrangements.

Explaining the importance of the approach, Keyamo said that diplomatic engagements must be backed by structured follow-up mechanisms to ensure that agreements and discussions produce measurable results.

The minister further welcomed the ambassador’s disclosure that Ireland was preparing for another Africa Trade Horizons Conference in the last week of June 2027, assuring her of Nigeria’s interest in sustaining participation in initiatives that promote trade and investment between both countries.

In her remarks, Ambassador Ní Bhriain disclosed that the Irish Government had recently hosted a Doing Business in Sub-Saharan Africa Summit, which attracted about 200 Irish companies interested in exploring business opportunities across the continent.

She emphasised that improved air connectivity would be fundamental to unlocking these opportunities, noting that while digital technology had transformed business engagements, physical interaction and direct access to markets remained indispensable.

Both parties expressed optimism that the engagement would open a new chapter in Nigeria-Ireland aviation relations, with improved visa services, a possible review of the BASA and the establishment of direct air links identified as immediate areas for collaboration.

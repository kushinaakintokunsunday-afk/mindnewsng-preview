Tinubu wouldn’t allow son marry Igbo woman if he hated Ndigbo – Shettima

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Vice-President Kashim Shettima has defended President Bola Tinubu against allegations of hostility towards the Igbo, saying the President would not have allowed

Shettima made the remark on Thursday at the All Progressives Congress’ SoluTinubu 2027 campaign rally in Awka, Anambra State, where he appealed to the South-East to support Tinubu’s re-election bid.

“President Tinubu is a friend of Ndigbo. His son, Seyi, is married to a lady of Igbo extraction. If he hates the Igbo, he couldn’t have allowed his son to marry an Igbo lady,” he said.

The Vice-President added, “So we are essentially one people. The Igbos are an essential component of the Nigerian configuration.”

Seyi is married to Layal Jade Tinubu, a Nigerian-Lebanese entrepreneur and philanthropist.

She is of Lebanese and Nigerian descent, with her Nigerian maternal heritage linked to Anambra State.

The couple got married in August 2016, with their wedding celebrations held in Lagos and at Villa Erba, Lake Como, Italy. Their Italian wedding ceremony took place on August 10, 2016.

Layal studied Politics and African Studies at the School of Oriental and African Studies, University of London, and holds a master’s degree in Entrepreneurship and Business Management from the University of Surrey, United Kingdom.

She is the founder of Tots Toys, a children’s educational toy business, and co-founded the Noella Foundation with her husband. The couple have two children.

Seyi had also cited his marriage to an Igbo woman while campaigning for his father’s presidential ambition in Anambra State ahead of the 2023 general election.

During a visit to the state in January 2023, he appealed to residents and the wider South-East to vote for Tinubu, saying, “I am appealing to Ndi-Anambra and the Igbo region at large to vote for my father because I married an Igbo lady, Chinwe Layal.”

He was subsequently conferred with the chieftaincy title of Nwannedinamba by the traditional ruler of Mbaukwu Kingdom, Igwe Peter Anukwui, in Awka South Local Government Area of Anambra State.

Meanwhile, Shettima assured the South-East that the region could produce Nigeria’s president in the next political dispensation when power rotates to the South.

“And in the next dispensation, when power comes to the South, I can assure you that power will find its residence in the eastern region,” he said.

The rally was part of the APC’s efforts to strengthen its political support in the South-East ahead of the 2027 presidential election.

Create jobs, tackle national challenges, Crawford varsity VC urges graduates

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Vice-Chancellor of Crawford University, Ogun State, Prof. Solomon Makinde, has urged graduating students to embrace entrepreneurship and create jobs rather

Makinde gave the charge on Wednesday at the institution’s 18th convocation ceremony in Igbesa, where 630 students, comprising 528 undergraduates and 102 postgraduate students, graduated with various degrees and postgraduate diplomas.

Addressing the graduands, whom the university described as the Unicorn Class of 2025/2026, the vice-chancellor said they were entering a world being reshaped by artificial intelligence, digital innovation, economic changes and evolving professional demands.

He said academic qualifications alone would not be enough to succeed in the changing environment, urging the graduates to develop critical thinking, problem-solving, communication and adaptability skills.

Presenting five charges to the graduands, Makinde urged them to uphold the university’s philosophy of knowledge with godliness, pursue excellence, develop entrepreneurial mindsets, demonstrate responsible leadership and maintain relationships with their alma mater.

He said, “Let Knowledge with Godliness remain your enduring compass. Never allow the pursuit of material success to diminish your commitment to truth, integrity, humility and godly living.

“Knowledge without character can become destructive, while achievement without integrity is unsustainable. Let your lives demonstrate that intellectual excellence and spiritual depth reinforce one another.

“Do not define your future exclusively by the availability of paid employment. Develop an entrepreneurial mindset, identify societal problems, create solutions and contribute to employment generation and national development. Nigeria and the wider world need graduates who can transform challenges into opportunities.”

He also urged them to embrace continuous learning and use technology responsibly.

Giving a breakdown of the undergraduate results, Makinde said 54 graduands earned First Class degrees, 233 obtained Second Class Upper Division, 191 graduated with Second Class Lower Division, while 50 received Third Class degrees.

The postgraduate category comprised 57 Master of Science graduates, seven Master of Business Administration graduates, 21 Postgraduate Diploma recipients and 17 Doctor of Philosophy graduates.

He said the number of doctoral graduates reflected the university’s efforts to strengthen its postgraduate research culture and develop advanced human capital.

The best graduating students in the undergraduate category were Anthony Eromonsele Ibazebo of Computer Science and Technology and Chiamaka Jasmine Njoku of Biochemistry, who both finished with a cumulative grade point average of 4.92.

In the School of Part-Time Studies, Perpetua Ndidiamaka Uzoma of Physics with Electronics emerged as the best graduating student with a CGPA of 4.96.

The vice-chancellor also announced that the university had secured approval from the National Universities Commission for six new undergraduate programmes under its proposed College of Allied Health Sciences.

The programmes are Nursing Science, Medical Laboratory Science, Physiotherapy, Radiography, Public Health and Community Health.

Meanwhile, the convocation lecturer, Dr Alban Igwe, Director-General of the Dangerous Goods Academy, Lagos, urged the university to build an internationally connected institution that combines global academic standards with practical solutions to local and African challenges.

Delivering a lecture titled, “The Future of Crawford University: Globalizing the Locals and Localizing the Globals,” Igwe said the institution’s growth would depend on its ability to preserve its local identity while connecting its academic programmes, research and graduates to international opportunities.

He argued that globalisation should not mean abandoning local realities, while local relevance should not become an excuse for academic isolation.

“My proposition is uncompromising: Crawford’s future will not be secured by choosing between local relevance and global excellence. It must master both. It must be deeply rooted in its locality and confidently connected to the world—local in identity, global in standard, African in purpose, international in reach,” he said.

Igwe also urged the university to leverage its location in Igbesa and the surrounding industrial and commercial environment, particularly its proximity to major trade and logistics networks.

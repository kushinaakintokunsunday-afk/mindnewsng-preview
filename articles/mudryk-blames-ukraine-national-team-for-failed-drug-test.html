Mudryk blames Ukraine national team for failed drug test

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Tottenham winger Mykhailo Mudryk has blamed the administration of the Ukrainian national team for his failed drugs test and subsequent suspension from football.

The 25-year-old, who joined Tottenham on a season-long loan from Chelsea, was suspended from November 2024 until July 2026 after testing positive for meldonium, a banned substance.

Mudryk believes the substance entered his system while he was on international duty with Ukraine, insisting that he could only have been exposed to the drug in his home country.

Speaking in an interview with Ukrainian YouTube channel Guest of Gordon, on Wednesday, the winger said he had never heard of meldonium before the positive test.

“The environment, the situation in the Ukrainian national team. It should never have existed. I probably haven’t said it publicly yet, but I’m saying it now.

“I had never heard of a drug like meldonium. It is a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe. It could only have got into my body in Ukraine. There is no such drug in England,” Mudryk said.

Asked whether he directly blamed the national team, Mudryk replied: “Yes, exactly.”

He explained that his only visits to Ukraine during the period were for national team commitments, suggesting that the substance could have entered his system while he was at the national team camp.

Mudryk also criticised the Ukrainian Association of Football (UAF), accusing the organisation of failing to support him adequately during the case.

He claimed that UAF president Andriy Shevchenko offered little assistance and alleged that the association was responsible for leaks surrounding the investigation.

The winger further argued that the association’s handling of the matter contributed to delays in his return to professional football.

Mudryk initially faced a possible four-year ban from the Football Association (FA), but successfully appealed to the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS).

He eventually accepted that he had breached anti-doping regulations and agreed to a suspension equivalent to the period he had already served, allowing him to return to football in July 2026.

The former Shakhtar Donetsk player also revealed that he spent millions of pounds on legal expenses, personal coaching, pitch hire and private physiotherapy while serving his suspension.

He said the positive test came as a devastating shock and had a significant impact on him mentally.

“It was a bolt from the blue. I couldn’t believe it was true,” he said.

“It was like falling off a skyscraper. In a dream you would hit the ground. In life, I just kept on falling. It hit me very hard, mentally.”

Mudryk admitted that he cried and turned to alcohol following his initial suspension but maintained that he never lost confidence in his ability to return to top-level football.

After making an appearance for Chelsea during their pre-season tour of Asia, he completed a deadline-day loan move to Tottenham, where he reunited with former Shakhtar Donetsk manager Roberto De Zerbi.

The Ukrainian Association of Football has yet to respond to Mudryk’s latest allegations. BBC Sport reported that it had contacted the association for comment.

Last year, a UAF spokesperson told BBC Sport that the organisation was in close contact with Mudryk’s lawyers but had not received any communication from the FA regarding the case.

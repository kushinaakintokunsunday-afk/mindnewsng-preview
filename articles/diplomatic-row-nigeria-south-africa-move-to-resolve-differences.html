Diplomatic row: Nigeria, South Africa move to resolve differences

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 5d ago 3 min read

• Abuja reviews Fani-Kayode’s engagement with Pretoria

Federal Government has called for renewed diplomatic engagement with South Africa to resolve outstanding differences between the two countries following a disagreement over the treatment of Nigeria’s High Commissioner, Femi Fani-Kayode, during the presentation of his credentials to President Cyril Ramaphosa.

Ministry of Foreign Affairs disclosed yesterday that it was reviewing a comprehensive report on recent developments, including Tuesday’s meeting between Fani-Kayode and the Director-General of South Africa’s Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO), Zane Dangor.

The intervention followed a diplomatic controversy triggered by Fani-Kayode’s allegation that his introduction and speech were deliberately excluded from the live broadcast of the credentials presentation ceremony in Pretoria, although those of other ambassadors were aired.

South African authorities disputed the allegation of deliberate exclusion, describing the incident as a broadcasting issue rather than an intentional slight against Nigeria.

The disagreement subsequently escalated into exchanges between the Nigerian High Commission and South African presidential officials, prompting DIRCO to invite the Nigerian envoy for discussions.

In a statement issued after Tuesday’s meeting, DIRCO said Dangor expressed South Africa’s concern over the events surrounding the credentials ceremony and the envoy’s subsequent remarks on social media.

The South African official also reminded Fani-Kayode of Article 41 of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, which requires diplomatic representatives to conduct official business with their host governments through established diplomatic channels.

However, the Nigerian High Commission rejected suggestions that Fani-Kayode had been reprimanded, describing the meeting as cordial and constructive and insisting that both sides had exchanged views on matters of mutual concern.

The envoy also raised concerns about the safety and treatment of Nigerians living in South Africa, an issue that has repeatedly strained relations between the two countries.

Against this background, spokesperson for Nigeria’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Oluwafemi Adeniyi, said the Federal Government was closely monitoring developments and reviewing reports of the discussions between its envoy and South African authorities.

Adeniyi stressed that both countries must deepen engagement to resolve outstanding issues while preserving their longstanding relationship founded on African solidarity, friendship and mutual respect.

“Particularly, as we both work assiduously to revitalise our longstanding bilateral relations, such is a relationship founded on African solidarity, friendship, and mutual respect.

“In this regard, the ministry reiterates commitment to sustained diplomatic engagement, while reviewing comprehensive report on the events that transpired in recent days,” he said.

The latest disagreement comes against a history of diplomatic tensions between Africa’s two major economies, particularly over attacks on foreign nationals, including Nigerians, in South Africa.

Relations between Abuja and Pretoria have periodically been strained by xenophobic violence, immigration disputes and concerns over the protection of Nigerian citizens and businesses.

In 2019, attacks targeting foreign nationals and their businesses in South Africa triggered diplomatic protests from Nigeria and retaliatory attacks on some South African-linked businesses in Nigeria.

Fresh concerns over attacks on foreigners in South Africa resurfaced in 2026, renewing demands for stronger protection of Nigerians and more effective bilateral cooperation.

Despite these differences, both countries have maintained significant diplomatic, economic and political ties, with cooperation extending to trade, investment and regional affairs.

South Africa’s foreign affairs department reaffirmed its commitment to strengthening the strategic partnership with Nigeria, stressing that the relationship should continue to be guided by mutual respect, cooperation and African solidarity.

The Nigerian Foreign Affairs Ministry did not specify the outcome of its review or announce further diplomatic measures, but maintained that sustained engagement remained the preferred approach to resolving the outstanding issues.

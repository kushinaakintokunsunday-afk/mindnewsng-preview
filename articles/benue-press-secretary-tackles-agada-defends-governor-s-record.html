Benue: Press Secretary tackles Agada, defends governor’s record

APC leadership, insists governor has no rift with Akume

_____________________________________________________________________

From Scholastica Hir, Makurdi

The Chief Press Secretary to the Benue State Governor, Tersoo Kula, has strongly defended Governor Hyacinth Alia’s administration against criticisms by the erstwhile All Progressives Congress (APC) Chairman in the state, Mr Austin Agada, dismissing his allegations as laughable and disconnected from the realities of governance in Benue.

Kula, who spoke during an appearance on Arise News Television on Friday, was responding to various allegations and criticisms raised by Agada during an earlier interview on the same platform.

Responding to Agada’s position that Governor Alia had achieved nothing since assuming office in 2023, Kula said such a position showed that the former APC chairman was not on the ground and was therefore unaware of developments in the state, or lacked an understanding of development and of the challenges inherited by the present administration.

Kula disclosed that Governor Alia inherited local debts amounting to approximately N380 billion, alongside substantial outstanding obligations involving salaries, pensions and gratuities.

According to him, some categories of workers were owed between 10 and 20 months’ salaries, while employees of the Benue State University, now Rev. Fr. Moses Orshio Adasu University, Makurdi (MOAUM), had experienced salary delays of up to 18 months under the previous administration.

Kula further disclosed that the Alia administration had been able to repay approximately N100 billion of the inherited liabilities within three years, describing the feat as evidence of the governor’s commitment to restoring the state’s financial stability.

He said the situation had significantly improved, noting that retired civil servants now undergo approximately one month of documentation before commencing pension payments, describing these improvements as verifiable records of the administration’s efforts to address inherited liabilities and restore confidence in the public service.

Kula also highlighted the administration’s intervention in the welfare of staff of Benue State University, Makurdi, disclosing that Governor Alia had cleared the institution’s earned allowances, which had remained unpaid since 2018.

He said the development was another indication of the administration’s determination to address longstanding financial obligations and improve the welfare of workers in the state.

The governor’s spokesperson also highlighted the administration’s revenue performance, disclosing that Benue’s monthly Internally Generated Revenue (IGR) had increased to between N2.5 billion and N3.5 billion.

He contrasted the figure with the approximately N400 million to N600 million reportedly generated monthly under the immediate past administration. Kula said the improvement demonstrated the Alia administration’s efforts to strengthen the state’s revenue base, enhance fiscal capacity and reduce dependence on federal allocations, maintaining that the revenue figures were supported by official records.

On infrastructure, Kula said the Alia administration had made significant progress in reversing the deterioration of public infrastructure inherited in 2023, adding that approximately 500 kilometres of roads had been constructed across the state, while several roads in Makurdi were either completed or under construction.

He also cited the installation of streetlights and ongoing urban renewal projects as evidence of the administration’s determination to restore the outlook of the state capital, adding that the visible transformation of Makurdi and other parts of the state contradicted Agada’s claim that the government had recorded no achievements.

Addressing Agada’s allegation that Governor Alia imposed businessman Benjamin Omale as APC chairman in Benue without due process, Kula questioned the former chairman’s knowledge of the party’s current national activities, challenging Agada to disclose when he last attended a meeting of the party’s National Executive Committee (NEC), arguing that Omale’s participation in such meetings demonstrated his recognition within the national party structure.

Kula argued that the APC’s national leadership would not continue to recognise Omale’s participation if his emergence had not followed the required procedures.

He said Governor Alia was entitled to call on qualified individuals, irrespective of their professional backgrounds, to contribute to the development of the state.

Kula said Agada’s criticisms of Governor Alia were an attempt to divert attention from questions surrounding his own political record.

He described Governor Alia as an incorruptible leader who would not compromise his principles for political or financial interests.

He also rejected claims that Governor Alia was responsible for divisions within the APC and had deliberately alienated prominent political figures, including a former Minister of Justice, Chief Michael Aondoakaa, SAN, and a former governor, Samuel Ortom.

He challenged that both politicians had worked for Alia’s emergence as governor, recalling that Aondoakaa had challenged Alia’s election in court, while Ortom supported the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) candidate, Titus Uba, against Alia during the 2023 governorship election.

On the alleged strained relationship between Governor Alia and the Secretary to the Government of the Federation (SGF), Senator Dr George Akume, Kula insisted that there was no disagreement between the two leaders.

He described Akume as a respected political father, explaining that the governor had remained committed to carrying him along in the affairs of the party and government.

As evidence, Kula cited the inclusion of Akume in the APC campaign council, alongside several political associates of the SGF, describing the gesture as a demonstration of Alia’s commitment to inclusiveness and party unity.

He, however, expressed concern that some individuals identified with Akume’s political camp were making statements capable of creating unnecessary misunderstanding between the two leaders.

Kula maintained that Governor Alia remained focused on governance, reforms and the development of Benue State, insisting that his administration would not be distracted by what he described as politically motivated allegations.

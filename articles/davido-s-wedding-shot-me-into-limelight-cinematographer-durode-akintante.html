Davido’s wedding shot me into limelight — Cinematographer, Durode Akintante

By Punch Punch 3d ago 10 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Chief Executive Officer of Duro Weddings and lifestyle cinematographer, Durode Akintante, speaks with DAMOLA ADEDIGBA about his journey into content creatio

The Chief Executive Officer of Duro Weddings and lifestyle cinematographer, Durode Akintante, speaks with DAMOLA ADEDIGBA about his journey into content creation, the wedding that brought his brand into the spotlight and the evolving business of wedding storytelling. He has 186,000 followers on Instagram

Can you take us back to the beginning of Duro Weddings? What inspired you to start the brand?

Duro Weddings didn’t start as a wedding content page. It started with a guy who enjoyed making videos of his friends in school with his phone. There was always that one person in a class who liked taking pictures and keeping people’s throwback moments. I was that person. I would take pictures of people, keep them and show them the following semester.

So, documenting people has always been a part of me. Later, when I started attending House on the Rock in Asaba, Delta State, I joined the church media team. That was where my love for content creation really grew because I was constantly creating content for the church.

People began to know me as the person making sure the church was visible online. The creative content we produced also made people take an interest in the church’s social media platforms. Through that, I met some friends who were in the wedding industry.

It started with creating behind-the-scenes content for them whenever they had shows or weddings. At some point, they encouraged me to start doing it professionally, even if it meant charging a small fee.

At the time, I was also a civil servant, so balancing both became difficult because they were demanding. Eventually, when it became clear that content creation was the direction I wanted to take, I left the civil service to focus fully on it.

How would you describe the journey so far?

It has really been from school to church and then to weddings. Church, in particular, taught me resilience and confidence. When you are creating content in church, you have to move around the congregation and interact with different people, so it naturally builds confidence.

It also taught me speed. If you attend the first service, for instance, the content has to be ready before the second service. That experience helped develop the fast turnaround that later became part of the Duro Weddings brand.

Why did you choose wedding spaces for your content?

When I looked at the wedding content available online, I noticed that most of it focused on Lagos weddings. Being based in Asaba made me realise that weddings in the East were not getting enough visibility online.

That made me think, “Why not try something different?” The goal was to consistently document weddings in the East and give them the visibility they deserved.

Benin, for example, already had people documenting weddings, but the quality and consistency of the content were not quite where they are today. I also noticed that couples wanted their content delivered quickly. So, fast delivery became another gap we wanted to address.

With consistent content, good storytelling and a fast turnaround, the brand gradually started getting more bookings, including from Lagos. That was how Duro Weddings developed before eventually moving to Lagos.

Which events brought Duro Weddings into the spotlight?

There have been several events, and it feels more like a ladder because each one opened the door to the next. I remember the first wedding featured by BellaNaija on my page. It was an event in the East, and from there, things started moving upwards.

Then came Davido’s wedding, which was one of the major events that pushed the brand into the spotlight. That event alone brought about 30,000 followers, making it a significant turning point for Duro Weddings.

What makes Duro Weddings different from others?

I feel every brand has something unique that makes people connect with it, and that uniqueness can change over time. At the beginning, what stood out about Duro Weddings was the speed of our delivery. With time, people began to realise that Duro Weddings could connect with a couple’s story.

It is not just about capturing the outfits, makeup or beautiful locations. The goal is to go deeper into the couple’s love story and make people feel what brought them together. That is what we try to achieve. We want the videos and the overall experience to feel like real love because that is the foundation of the wedding in the first place.

How do you use lifestyle cinematography to tell a couple’s story?

The world has changed over time. In the past, wedding videography was more about taking your time to document almost everything.

Now, we are in the content age, where attention spans are shorter, and people want to be entertained while watching. So, it is important to make the content lively and introduce a lifestyle element into it.

Lifestyle cinematography allows the story to feel more natural, relatable and engaging, rather than simply documenting what happened at the wedding.

What is the biggest challenge you face when covering a wedding?

The biggest challenge is the number of content creators involved in weddings today. A bride may book one content creator, but by the time you get to the wedding, there could be several others covering the same event. Sometimes, it becomes overwhelming for the couple.

For instance, when there are fewer creators, the bride can trust you to properly capture her love story. But when there are too many people around her, she can begin to feel anxious, and the whole experience can start looking more like a fashion shoot than a wedding. We meet different people who have been booked for different aspects of the wedding, and sometimes managing all of that as a creator can be challenging.

What are some of the biggest mistakes couples make when choosing a wedding content creator?

There are different types of content creators, and many specialise in particular aspects of wedding content.

The mistake some couples make is booking a creator without first understanding what that person actually does. For example, a creator may be very good at showcasing bridal outfits, but the couple may expect that person to also capture the event highlights, the venue and the entire wedding experience.

So, before booking a content creator, couples need to study the person’s page and previous work. If you want to buy something, you look at what the brand has done before. The same thing applies here. Understanding a creator’s style beforehand helps couples know exactly what they are getting and prevents unnecessary pressure or disappointment on the wedding day.

How do you balance trends on TikTok and Instagram with creating timeless wedding memories that couples can still appreciate years later?

When creating wedding content, there are so many people to satisfy. There are the couple, the vendors, makeup artist, the stylist, planner, hairstylist and, of course, the audience watching online.

The goal, therefore, is to create content that different people can appreciate. For the bride, the focus may be on telling her story because that is something she will continue to value years later. At the same time, there can be fashion content for those interested in the looks and lifestyle content for people who enjoy that style.

So, it is really about finding a balance and making sure everybody gets to see themselves in the content. That way, the content can remain trendy while preserving the memories that matter.

We are in the age of AI. How do you embrace new technologies while maintaining creativity in your work?

AI can create things like birthday shoots, visuals and even entire productions. However, when it comes to real-life experiences, there is still a difference.

A wedding is an experience that happens live and cannot easily be recreated. AI cannot decide that a mother should suddenly cry or that a father should react in a particular way. Those spontaneous human moments are difficult to replicate.

Perhaps that may change in the future, but for now, the wedding space remains largely about real human experiences. Fashion shoots and visual concepts may be influenced by AI, but the actual wedding experience, with all its emotions and unexpected moments, cannot be completely replaced.

How do you manage the pressure of working on someone’s most important day?

When a couple books Duro Weddings, there is already a level of trust. They have seen the brand’s work and chosen it because they believe in what we do. That trust makes the process easier because it gives us creative freedom. We have been fortunate that most of our couples trust our creative direction.

From the consultation stage to the morning of the wedding and throughout the event, they give us the freedom to create. Knowing that they trust the brand makes it easier to relax, be creative and deliver the best possible work.

The Nigerian wedding industry has become expensive. How can couples get quality visual content without exceeding their budgets?

Couples need to do their research. It is similar to buying a phone. If you have the budget for an iPhone 15 Pro Max, you can go for it. But if that is beyond your budget, there are other options that can still meet your needs. The same thing applies to wedding content. Couples can research different creators and find those whose services fit their budgets. A wedding planner can also help with the research and recommend creators based on what the couple can afford.

However, if there is a particular brand whose work you genuinely love and you have the means to afford it, you can decide to go all out for that creator.

Ultimately, it comes down to understanding your budget, researching your options and choosing what works best for you.

As CEO, what has running Duro Weddings taught you about leadership, building a creative team and dealing with clients?

One thing running a team has taught me is that you have to treat it like a company.

When you work with people, you have to understand that their journey with you may not be permanent. It could be a three-month, six-month, one-year or two-year cycle. At some point, a team member may decide that it is time to leave and build something of their own. That can be a difficult reality to accept, but it is something you have to understand when working with people. You cannot always expect someone to remain with the company forever.

That is one of the biggest lessons leadership has taught me, both the hard way and the soft way. When it comes to clients, the experience has also taught me the importance of communication, trust and understanding what people expect from the brand.

Looking ahead, where do you see Duro Weddings in the next five years?

In the next five years, Duro Weddings should be a brand that not only caters to weddings in Nigeria but also covers weddings across Africa, Europe, America and other parts of the world.

The goal is to get to a point where the brand can experience and document different cultures, not just Nigerian culture.

More importantly, Duro Weddings should become a brand people can relate to. A brand where you watch the videos and think, “I want to get married.” A brand that makes people want to take themselves seriously, prepare for marriage and experience something beautiful of their own.

What should your audience and clients expect from the brand?

More amazing stories, more experiences and, ultimately, a brand that continues to expand across borders.

What advice do you have for upcoming content creators?

The best advice is not to take every piece of advice you receive. Sometimes, things are done a certain way simply because that is how everyone has always done them. But when you come into the industry, it does not mean you have to follow the same path.

Be that rebellious person who says, “I don’t want to do it the way everybody else does it. I want to try something different.” People thrive when they are different. So, be different, understand your craft and be daring enough to try new things.

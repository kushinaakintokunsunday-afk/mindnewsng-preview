‘Nothing Suggests To Me Tinubu Is Not Fit,’ Fashola Dismisses Health Concerns

Former Lagos State Governor Babatunde Fashola has dismissed concerns about President Bola Tinubu’s physical and mental fitness for office, saying he has seen nothing to suggest that the president is incapable of performing his duties.

Fashola, a former Minister of Works and Housing, made the remarks on Tuesday during an appearance on Channels Television’s Politics Today, where he was asked whether he considered Tinubu physically fit to lead the country.

“Nothing suggests to me that he’s not fit for it. Yes, he’s older. That doesn’t mean his mental capacity is gone. He’s still giving directives. And when I see him, he’s working. He’s still reading, authorising, minuting on things,” he said.

Questions about Tinubu’s health and physical condition have featured in public discussions since the 2023 presidential election campaign.

During the campaign, concerns were raised by critics over his physical appearance, movements and stamina at public events. The debate continued after he assumed office in May 2023, with opposition figures and other critics occasionally questioning his capacity to discharge the responsibilities of the presidency.

The concerns resurfaced in 2026 amid renewed scrutiny of his public appearances, including videos circulated online showing him moving cautiously at some events.

The Presidency and the All Progressives Congress (APC) have repeatedly defended the president, maintaining that he is capable of carrying out his constitutional responsibilities.

Tinubu has also dismissed speculation about his health. In September 2026, following an extended stay in Europe, he told reporters that he was ready to “work, walk and kick.”

‘More Mature’

The former governor, who worked closely with Tinubu during his eight-year administration in Lagos from 2007 to 2015, said the president had become more experienced over the years.

Asked to compare the Tinubu he knew in the early 2000s with the president he knows today, Fashola said the president had become more mature.

“Well, a much more mature one, certainly, without doubt. I am not the chief of staff he employed in 2002. That’s 39 years. I was 39 then. I’m 63 now. So we must give him that credit for the experience he has gained,” he said.

Fashola acknowledged that Tinubu might no longer maintain the same work routine he had in the past, particularly when it came to keeping late nights, but maintained that the president remained capable of handling demanding responsibilities.

“Maybe not keeping late nights that he used to do. I’m not sure. He can still run the engine,” he added.

When asked about opposition figures who have described Tinubu as feeble and frail, Fashola said such views were their opinions, insisting that the president’s actions and the activities of his administration should also be considered.

He argued that the president’s leadership had produced results in government, adding that Tinubu often moved ahead of those who underestimated him.

“Every time he looks at you, you think there is nothing there. Before you know it, he has left you behind,” he said.

Fashola’s remarks add to public responses by political allies who have defended the president against questions about his health and capacity to govern.

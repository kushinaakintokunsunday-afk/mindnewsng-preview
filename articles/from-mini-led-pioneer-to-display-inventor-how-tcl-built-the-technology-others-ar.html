From Mini LED pioneer to display inventor: how TCL built the technology others are still trying to reach

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 3 min read

TCL’s launch of SQD-Mini LED TV is not the beginning of a story. It is the result of a decade of manufacturing investment, panel science and a strategic decision to own the entire display chain, from the quantum dot lab to the living room wall.

Attributed to TCL MEABG SPOKESPERSON / REGIONAL DIRECTOR: Mr. Daniel Ray, Director of Sales / General Manager of West Africa

In Nigeria, as in every major market across the Middle East and Africa, televisions have become one of the most consequential purchase decisions a household makes. Not because the price is high (though it often is) but because the screen at the centre of the living room is now expected to replace the cinema, host the football match, enable competitive gaming, and do all of this without compromising when the afternoon sun floods the room.

Meeting that expectation requires more than a product launch. It requires a foundation that most television brands simply do not have. TCL does.The company’s introduction of SQD-Mini LED TV to Nigeria in 2026 is the sixth generation of a display technology journey that began in 2019, when TCL launched its first QD-Mini LED television, two years before the term “Mini LED TV” had entered mainstream media coverage. That head start was not accidental. TCL CSOT, the company’s display panel manufacturing division, had been developing quantum dot and Mini LED integration for years before the first commercial product reached a showroom.

Today, TCL CSOT is the world’s number one manufacturer of televisions at 85 inches and above, holding a 28.8 percent global market share in that segment. It operates twelve panel production lines with cumulative investment exceeding 300 billion RMB. When TCL says it has invented a new display technology, the claim comes from a company that owns the factory, the quantum dot chemistry, the backlight engineering and the panel itself.

That distinction matters in a market where premium television claims are abundant but engineering credentials are rarely scrutinised. Most television brands do not manufacture their own panels. They design products around components sourced from a small number of suppliers, including, in many cases, TCL CSOT itself. When TCL develops SQD-Mini LED TV, competitors are buying panels from the same ecosystem TCL helped build.

SQD-Mini LED TV combines three core advances: Super QLED, which improves quantum dot particle purity to eliminate colour imprecision at the conversion stage; Precise Dimming Series, which enables one dimming zone to perform with the efficacy of multiple, eliminating blooming and halo effects; and Ultra Color Filter, which reduces colour impurities at the filter layer for accurate colour reproduction across the entire screen. Together, they address brightness, colour and contrast at the panel level, not through software correction after the fact. The result: 10,000 nits peak brightness, more than 20,000 local dimming zones, zero colour crosstalk, and a 60,000-hour lifespan, twice that of OLED.

In Nigeria, where household entertainment investment is on the rise, the timing of this launch reflects TCL’s understanding that the premium display conversation has permanently changed. In 2026, that conversation belongs to SQD-Mini LED TV.

Two killed in fresh attack on Plateau communities

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 3 min read

From Jude Owuamanam, Jos

Two persons have been killed and several others wounded in an attack by gunmen on Sho village in Barkin Ladi and Sambak community in Riyom local government areas of Plateau State.

According to the National Publicity Secretary of the Berom Youth Moulders Association (BYM), Rwang Tengwong, the first incident occurred at about 1:57 a.m. on Sunday, when the attackers, whom he identified as Fulani terrorists, from the direction of Fass, invaded Sambak community in Hoss District, Riyom LGA, killing Dauda John Davou, 42.

The same gunmen also attacked Jol community at around 12:10 a.m. but were repelled by vigilantes from the community.

Tengwong, in a statement on Monday, said that later the same day, the same group from the same direction attacked Sho village in Barkin Ladi LGA at about 5:20 p.m., killing Da Ishaku Pam Vwos, aged 79.

The latest round of killings came less than 24 hours after the All Progressives Congress (APC) North Central campaign train berthed in Jos, where President Bola Ahmed Tinubu promised to rebuild villages ravaged by age-long crises in the state.

They also came less than two days after the lifting of the state of emergency by the Plateau State government.

Tengwong said: “BYM, under the leadership of its National President, Dalyop Solomon Mwantiri, Esq., is deeply concerned over renewed attacks on Berom communities in Riyom and Barkin Ladi Local Government Areas of Plateau State, which have claimed two lives within hours.

“These incidents followed Wednesday’s shooting of Joshua Doro Dafwel in Jol, who is currently receiving medical attention.

“On the same day, Ina Arin was killed when he and his friend, Abok Gyang, were ambushed on their way home to Dorong village in Foron District. Gyang narrowly escaped.

“BYM is particularly concerned by reports from communities of suspicious movements of Fulani individuals from the direction of Fass, which has reportedly been renamed Tafawa. These reports require urgent investigation to establish the facts, determine the circumstances surrounding the movements and identify anyone connected to the attacks.”

While commending the vigilance and courage of local security volunteers, BYM stressed that communities should not be left to defend themselves against armed attacks without adequate and sustained support from security personnel.

Tengwong therefore called on President Tinubu, Governor Caleb Mutfwang and all relevant security agencies to urgently strengthen security deployments in vulnerable communities, investigate the latest killings and ensure that those responsible were identified and brought to justice.

He added: “It is particularly troubling that these attacks continue despite repeated security assurances and high-profile political engagements in Plateau State. The killing of a 79-year-old man in his community further underscores the vulnerability of innocent residents, including the elderly, who deserve protection and the right to live without fear.”

He said that the people of Plateau deserved more than promises, but concrete action and the assurance that their lives and communities were protected.

“Behind every casualty is a grieving family and a traumatised community. Our people deserve to sleep peacefully, return home safely and go about their daily activities without the constant fear of being attacked or killed,” the statement said.

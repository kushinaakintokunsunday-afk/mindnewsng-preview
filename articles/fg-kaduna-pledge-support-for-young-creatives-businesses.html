FG, Kaduna pledge support for young creatives, businesses

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government and the Kaduna State Government have pledged stronger support for Nigeria’s creative industry, urging young people to turn their artistic

The Minister of Art, Culture and Creative Economy, Hannatu Musa Musawa, and Kaduna State Governor, Senator Uba Sani, made the commitment on Friday at the opening of the sixth edition of the Kaduna Fashion and Art Exhibition (KAFART), a three-day event in Kaduna.

Musawa, represented by the Curator of the National Gallery of Arts, Mrs Nana Hajara Tijjani, described the creative industry as a major economic asset that could contribute significantly to national development if properly harnessed.

She said the Federal Government, under President Bola Tinubu’s Renewed Hope Agenda, recognised arts, culture, innovation and creative enterprise as important instruments for economic diversification and employment generation.

The minister said, “Nigeria’s creative industry is more than a platform for artistic expression; it is a major economic opportunity. Our fashion designers, artists and cultural practitioners have the capacity to create jobs, attract investments and position the country competitively in the global economy.”

She urged governments at all levels, private sector operators and creative practitioners to strengthen partnerships to build an inclusive and sustainable creative industry.

According to her, “We must move beyond celebrating creativity to building an industry that provides opportunities, attracts investment and enables our talented young people to earn sustainable livelihoods from their skills.”

Musawa said emerging and established creatives needed access to finance, mentorship, professional training, markets and international networks to transform their talents into successful enterprises.

She described KAFART as an important platform for showcasing creative abilities, developing professional relationships and connecting practitioners with opportunities in the industry.

The minister urged participants to use the exhibition to establish partnerships and explore opportunities to promote Nigerian arts, fashion and cultural heritage beyond the country’s borders.

In his remarks, Sani pledged his administration’s continued support for the creative sector, particularly through vocational training, entrepreneurship, innovation and access to business opportunities.

Sani, represented by the Commissioner for Business, Innovation and Technology, Patience Fakai, said Kaduna was endowed with talented young people whose creative abilities could contribute significantly to the state’s economic development.

He said, “Our young people possess exceptional talents in fashion design, tailoring, painting, sculpture and other creative disciplines. We must provide them with the skills, mentorship, funding and platforms required to transform these talents into sustainable businesses.”

The governor described the creative economy as an emerging sector capable of generating employment, attracting investment and creating economic opportunities, particularly for young people and women.

He said his administration would continue to support vocational training and small and medium-sized enterprises through relevant government institutions.

Sani cited the Kaduna State Institute of Vocational Training and Skills Development as part of the government’s efforts to equip young people with practical skills in fashion design, carpentry, metalwork, hospitality, catering, interior design, welding and fabrication.

He urged the organisers of KAFART to strengthen collaboration with vocational institutions, noting that such partnerships would provide trainees with practical exposure, industry experience and employment opportunities.

The governor also said the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Technology would explore partnerships to support creative businesses through access to finance, mentorship, business development services, markets and professional networks.

He said KAFART’s international connections offered opportunities for cultural exchange and professional collaboration while promoting Kaduna as a destination for arts, culture and tourism.

On the theme, “To Have and to Hold,” Sani said the exhibition underscored the importance of preserving cultural heritage while embracing innovation and emerging opportunities.

Also speaking, the Technical Adviser to the Vice President on Women, Youth Engagement and Impact, Mrs Hauwa Liman, described the creative economy as an emerging source of wealth creation and employment.

She said Northern Nigeria had talented women and young people who needed platforms, financing and an enabling environment to translate their creativity into sustainable businesses.

The exhibition featured cultural performances, including a presentation by Mud Art Company titled “Anfara” and an indigenous leopard dance.

It also featured awards for outstanding creatives, with a N1m creative grant presented to Jessy Josh for embroidery, while Hassana Yusuf received the Queen Amina Embroidery Award.

Joshua Akubo received the Social Impact Award, while Liman was honoured with the Global Arewa Fashion Award.

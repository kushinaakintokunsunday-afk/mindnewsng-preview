Jimmy Odukoya sues sister, seven others over removal as senior pastor

By Punch Punch 2d ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Senior Pastor of the Fountain of Life Church, Pastor Jimmy Odukoya, has approached the Federal High Court in Lagos to challenge his purported removal from o

Odukoya, alongside the Incorporated Trustees of the Fountain of Life Church, instituted the action against eight defendants, including Pastor Tolu Odukoya, Mr Biodun Balogun, Mr Biodun Awosika, Pastor Rotimi Okpaise, Pastor Tobi Enuha, Pastor Gbenga Onabanjo, Pastor Fred Ohiani and Pastor Akin Osinbajo.

The court processes, dated November 20, 2026, include an originating summons, a motion on notice and affidavits sworn to by Odukoya in support of the applications.

PUNCH Online sighted the originating summons and other court processes on Saturday.

In the originating summons, the plaintiffs are asking the court to determine whether meetings held by some members of the church’s Board of Trustees on January 22, February 28 and November 16, 2026, and the decisions taken at those meetings complied with the church’s constitution.

Odukoya said he was appointed Senior Pastor and Chairman of the Board of Trustees at an emergency meeting of the board on September 14, 2023, and was subsequently installed in September of the same year.

He said the church and its organs were governed by its constitution, amended by a special resolution dated January 16, 2003, and that the defendants were members of its Board of Trustees.

According to his affidavit, the disagreement began after a December 19, 2025, meeting of the board, attended by Bishop Mike Okonkwo, at which the possibility of handing over the church’s administrative arm to the Associate Senior Pastor was discussed.

He said no resolution or final decision was reached at the meeting.

Odukoya said he subsequently convened a board meeting for January 5, 2026, but it could not hold because there was no quorum. He added that some trustees later presented proposals to him, many of which he declined because, according to him, they were inconsistent with the church’s constitution.

He said the trustees subsequently sent him a letter dated January 7, 2026, requesting a response within three days, but he did not see it until after the deadline had elapsed.

Odukoya said he later directed the board secretary to schedule a meeting for January 30, 2026, after a request for a special meeting was made by a board member.

However, he alleged that a separate notice for a meeting on January 22 was circulated by the fourth defendant and did not come from the board secretariat.

“I received the notice late in the evening of January 20, 2026, effectively giving me one day notice of the purported meeting,” he stated.

He said he declined to attend the meeting, but the defendants proceeded with it under the name of “concerned members of the Board”.

According to him, they “purportedly adopted a number of resolutions” which included separating the offices of Senior Pastor and Chairman of the Board of Trustees, suspending the board secretary and a church pastor, dissolving the church’s Executive Council and amending its constitution.

Odukoya said a series of conciliatory meetings followed but produced no meaningful result. He added that another meeting was held on February 9, 2026, where he reiterated his position on the disagreement.

He said he was subsequently invited to a meeting with Bishop Okonkwo and, following an understanding reached there, wrote to the concerned members of the board on February 13. The trustees replied on February 15, while he responded on February 20.

At the February 28 meeting, Odukoya alleged that some individuals convened a gathering which was neither a board meeting nor a meeting of the church council.

“Nonetheless, a vote was taken among those present, and I was purportedly removed as the Chairman of the Board,” he stated.

He said he responded to the purported removal in a letter dated March 2, 2026, addressed to the concerned trustees.

The pastor further alleged that the defendants caused an individual describing herself as an interim secretary to issue notices for board meetings, despite what he said was the failure to validly remove the substantive secretary in accordance with the church’s constitution.

Odukoya said the defendants later issued a notice for another board meeting scheduled for November 16, 2026, which he said was served on November 14.

He said he objected to what he described as the continued disregard for the church’s constitution in a letter dated November 15, but the meeting went ahead.

“The Defendants upon their meeting on November 16, 2026, wrote to me the letter dated November 17, 2026, which purports to remove me as the Senior Pastor and Chairman, Board of Trustees, upon some bogus facts,” he stated.

He maintained that the three disputed meetings and the decisions taken at them violated the church’s constitution.

“I know, by my knowledge of the provisions of the 1st Plaintiff’s Constitution, that the meetings on 22nd January, 2026, 28th February, 2026 and 16th November, 2026 held by the Defendants either amongst themselves or with other persons in attendance and all the decisions and resolutions emanating therefrom are in contravention of the constitution of the 1st Defendant,” he said.

In his application for urgent intervention, Odukoya also alleged that the defendants had plans to disrupt church meetings to prevent him from carrying out his duties.

“Despite their disregard for the constitution of the 1st Plaintiff, the Defendants have perfected the plan to violently disrupt any meeting of the 1st Plaintiff with a view to violently prevent me from performing my duties as the Senior Pastor of the church,” he stated.

He further alleged that the defendants were influential and had the capacity to use law enforcement agents to enforce what he described as illegal resolutions.

Odukoya said he feared that the dispute could disrupt church activities and lead to a breach of peace if the defendants were not restrained.

“Unless the Defendants are urgently restrained by an order of this Honourable Court, the Defendants will continue with their nefarious activities, wreaking havoc on the activities of the church which may lead to counter reactions by those opposed to them and which may result in a breach of peace,” he stated.

Explaining why he considered the matter urgent, he described himself as a public figure and an inspiration to young people.

“I am a clergy, a public figure and an inspiration to many young persons all over the world; a single public embarrassment and scandalous encounter would have irreparable effect on my personality, and this would definitely negatively affect those persons, especially the young ones, who, by the grace of God, look to me for guidance,” he said.

The pastor is asking the court to restrain the defendants from interfering with his functions and the management of the church’s affairs pending the determination of the dispute.

The court had yet to make a determination on the issues raised in the processes as of the time of filing this report.

The dispute became public on November 17, when some members of the church’s Board of Trustees announced Jimmy’s removal, citing a December 2021 video in which the late founder, Pastor Taiwo Odukoya, reportedly named Pastor Kunle Areogun as his preferred successor and assigned Jimmy an administrative support role and leadership of the youth ministry.

The trustees also raised objections to Jimmy’s remarriage.

However, Jimmy and the church rejected the removal, insisting that his appointment in September 2023 was valid and that the decision violated the church’s constitution.

The trustees later named Pastor Rotimi Okpaise interim spiritual and administrative leader.

On November 18, the Lagos State High Court issued an interim order restraining Jimmy from presenting himself as the church’s senior pastor, a decision he is now challenging at the Federal High Court.

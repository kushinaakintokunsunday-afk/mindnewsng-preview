Strait of Hormuz Effectively Closed As Tankers Come Under Attack, Oil Price Hits $92 - Check Below For Full Details

By Mindnewsng Mindnewsng 12 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Mindnewsng

Only about 3 ships a day are passing through the Strait of Hormuz against a normal 85, after Iran declared the waterway closed and tankers came under attack. Br

The Strait of Hormuz - the narrow waterway that carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil - remains effectively closed, with ship-tracking data showing just about 3 commercial crossings a day against a pre-crisis average of 85.

What is happening

  • Iran declared the strait closed to "hostile" shipping and now demands transit permits and tolls through a body it created, the Persian Gulf Strait Authority
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guard says several tankers caught fire after striking naval mines while using what it calls "unauthorised routes" - claims carried widely but not independently verified
  • Ships have been switching off tracking systems, making the true picture harder to verify
  • Iran says the strait will not reopen until the US meets seven conditions; Washington says it is ready to use force if necessary

Oil prices

Brent crude, the global benchmark, has been trading around $92 a barrel, swinging with every headline about attacks or peace hopes.

Why Nigerians should pay attention

Nigeria imports refined fuel and feeds its domestic market partly through supplies that price off this waterway. A sustained closure pushes up global crude and product costs, which flows into landing costs, pump prices and inflation - on top of the pressure already felt in 2026. It also matters for the Dangote refinery's economics and for naira stability as oil earnings shift.

Maritime insurers have raised war-risk premiums to severe levels, and any lasting resolution still depends on diplomacy that both sides describe as far from reach.

Editor's note: Claims from the warring parties remain unverified by independent maritime authorities. Mindnewsng will continue tracking the strait and what it means for fuel prices in Nigeria.

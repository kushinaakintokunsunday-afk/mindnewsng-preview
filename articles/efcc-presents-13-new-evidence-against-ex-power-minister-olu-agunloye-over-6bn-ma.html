EFCC presents 13 new evidence against ex-power minister Olu Agunloye over $6bn Mambilla project

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has submitted 13 additional pieces of evidence in the ongoing trial of former Minister of Power and Steel, Olu Agunloye, before the Federal Capital Territory High Court in Abuja.

Agunloye is standing trial on an amended seven-count charge bordering on disobedience of presidential directives, receiving gratification and forgery.

The case is connected to the award of the $6 billion Mambilla Hydroelectric Power Project contract to Sunrise Power Transmission Company Limited by Agunloye during his time as minister between 2002 and 2003.

The EFCC is prosecuting the former minister over allegations linked to the controversial contract award.

At the resumed proceedings on Thursday, Abba Mohammed, prosecution counsel, told the court that the business of the day was the re-examination of Liyel Imoke, the sixth prosecution witness, who is a former minister of power and steel.

Mohammed said the prosecution had filed 13 additional proofs of evidence dated November 18, 2026, to address some salient issues in the trial.

“The documents attached are 13 proofs of evidence that relate to issues raised by the defence during cross examination,” Mohammed was quoted as saying in a statement issued by the EFCC.

“With your kind permission, My Lord, I am ready to proceed with the re-examination.”

Adeola Adedipe, the counsel to Agunloye, told the court that the documents were served on him on Wednesday.

Adedipe said he would not be prepared to proceed with the re-examination if the prosecution intended to rely on the documents.

Jude Onwuegbuzie, the presiding judge, asked the prosecution to proceed with the re-examination on other matters.

The judge said the defence should be given until the next adjourned date to enable Adedipe to study the documents.

During the re-examination, the prosecution showed Imoke “exhibits EFCC 3D and 3K”, which contain the minutes of the federal executive council (FEC) on May 21, 2003.

Mohammed asked Imoke to confirm whether the deliberations, conclusions and resolutions of the FEC meeting on that day regarding the memorandum submitted by the defendant were the same in form or shape.

Imoke responded that the exhibits were the same.

The judge adjourned the matter until November 26, 2026, for continuation of trial.

On November 11, Imoke told the court that FEC did not approve the $6 billion Mambilla power project contract awarded to Sunrise Power by Agunloye.

Imoke said the contract award was invalid because it had not been approved by the council.

He added that the May 21 meeting was the last FEC meeting before the cabinet was dissolved.

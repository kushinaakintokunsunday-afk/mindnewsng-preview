Eagles face AFCON qualifiers hurdle as Chelle admits trouble

By Punch Punch 6d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Super Eagles coach Eric Chelle has admitted that Nigeria have a mountain to climb in order to qualify for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations after Tuesday’s goalles

Super Eagles coach Eric Chelle has admitted that Nigeria have a mountain to climb in order to qualify for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations after Tuesday’s goalless draw against co-hosts Tanzania left them three points behind Group L leaders Guinea-Bissau, PUNCH Sports Extra reports.

Nigeria were held to a 0-0 draw by Tanzania in Zanzibar, leaving the Super Eagles with seven points from four matches, while Guinea-Bissau moved to 10 points after beating Madagascar 2-0 in Bissau.

Éric Chelle’s side had beaten Tanzania 3-1 in Uyo on Friday, with substitute Tolu Arokodare scoring twice after the visitors cancelled out Alhassan Yusuf’s early opener.

However, there was nothing to separate the teams in Zanzibar as the hosts threatened to hurt Nigeria, who also squandered several scoring opportunities.

Tanzania were forced into two first-half substitutions, with Novatus Chilambo and Charles M’Mombwa coming off injured, while Nigeria introduced Akor Adams and Alex Iwobi shortly after the hour before bringing on Alhassan Yusuf.

The Super Eagles also lost Bruno Onyemaechi to injury late in the game, with Ola Aina replacing the defender.

With Tanzania already assured of a place at the finals as co-hosts alongside Kenya and Uganda, only one additional qualifying spot is available in Group L, leaving Nigeria and Guinea-Bissau in a battle for the ticket.

“A draw away from home is not a bad result, but in our situation, because we lost to Guinea-Bissau, that is why we are in trouble,” Chelle said after Tuesday’s match.

“We are in trouble because if they win, we have to score many goals. Now we have to win in Madagascar and the last game.

“We had to win both games to be in the same situation, but in football, if you cannot score goals, you cannot win. We had a lot of chances but didn’t score. Every time something was missing, maybe the last kick, the last pass or the pass before the last pass.

“This is not about some players missing and others not missing, but in my opinion, we have to be more aggressive,” he added.

Nigeria’s 3-0 defeat to Guinea-Bissau in September has left their qualification hopes outside their complete control, with the Super Eagles needing maximum points from their remaining fixtures and hoping the group leaders drop points.

Chelle’s men will travel to face Madagascar on March 24 before hosting Guinea-Bissau four days later in what could be a decisive encounter for the group’s remaining ticket.

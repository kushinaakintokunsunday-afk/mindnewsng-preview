How to know if FG selected you for N100,000 grant

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read

Applicants who registered for the Federal Government’s N100,000 cash support programme for vulnerable households have been advised to monitor their phones for text messages from designated payment providers to know if they have been selected.

The programme’s management board said successful applicants would receive SMS notifications informing them of their selection and providing further instructions on how to access the financial support.

According to a statement signed by the management board and obtained by PUNCH Online on Friday, the notifications would be sent to the phone numbers obtained from the records used during the beneficiary selection process.

This means applicants should pay attention to the phone numbers associated with their registration details, as the designated payment providers are expected to communicate their selection through the numbers.

The board explained that the selection process would not happen at once, as beneficiaries would be identified and selected in phases across the country’s six geopolitical zones.

It said the exercise would involve identifying eligible households through the Social Safety Net Systems, validating their national identity records and assessing their poverty and vulnerability levels before determining those who would receive the financial support.

“The selection process involves identifying eligible households through the Social Safety Net Systems, using verified national identity records, assessing households based on poverty and vulnerability criteria, and selecting beneficiaries in phases across the six geopolitical zones,” the board stated.

The management board explained that beneficiaries would not be selected or paid simultaneously because the intervention would cover a limited number of households in each phase.

Consequently, applicants who registered for the programme on the website – nfsp.ng/signup may receive notifications at different times, depending on the phase in which their households are considered.

The board said the phased arrangement was part of the beneficiary selection process, which would be implemented across the six geopolitical zones.

It also cautioned applicants against concluding that they had been rejected simply because they had yet to receive a notification.

“If you have not received a notification, it does not necessarily mean you have been permanently excluded from the programme,” the board said.

The clarification means that the absence of an SMS should not automatically be interpreted as a final rejection, as the selection exercise is expected to continue in batches.

What happens after receiving an SMS?

The management board said applicants who receive selection notifications would be required to follow the instructions contained in the messages before their payments could be processed.

It explained that selected beneficiaries would receive the N100,000 through the bank accounts they submitted during registration.

“Selected beneficiaries will receive an SMS notification from their designated payment provider,” the board stated.

It further explained that the phone number used for the notification would be obtained from the records used during the beneficiary selection process.

Recipients are expected to follow the instructions in the message and provide the required consent before the money is credited to their submitted bank accounts.

This means receiving an SMS notification does not necessarily translate to an immediate credit alert, as selected beneficiaries must first comply with the instructions communicated by their designated payment providers and provide the required consent before the transfer is completed.

The management board’s published guidelines explain that selected beneficiaries will be contacted through SMS notifications from designated payment providers.

However, the board did not provide a separate USSD code or online beneficiary-status checker through which applicants can independently confirm their selection.

It also did not state that applicants who have yet to receive notifications should register again or submit another application.

Applicants are therefore advised to rely on official communications from the programme rather than individuals or websites claiming to offer instant confirmation of beneficiary status.

For enquiries and clarification, the programme provided the email address help@nfsp.ng and advised Nigerians to contact the Federal Ministry of Youth Development through its recognised communication channels.

Healthy, productive workforce, critical to sustainable national development – NSITF

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

NSITF also urged employers to prioritise workers’ welfare by enrolling their employees in the Employees’ Compensation Scheme to promote workplace safety and sustainable economic growth.

The call was made by the Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of NSITF, Oluwaseun Faleye, during the NSITF special day at the 40th Lagos International Trade Fair, according to a press statement issued on Tuesday.

Speaking on the theme, “The employee at the centre of growth, secured by the employees’ compensation scheme”, Faleye said employees are not simply participants in the production process, but are the human force behind enterprise and economic progress.

He said, “This year’s theme, “40 years of trade, growth and global connection”, celebrates the enduring importance of commerce in building prosperous economies and connecting nations. I congratulate the Lagos Chamber of Commerce and Industry on sustaining this important platform for 40 years.

“But as we celebrate the achievements of enterprise, we must also recognise a fundamental truth: behind every successful business is a workforce whose contributions make that success possible.

“Every product manufactured, every service delivered, every transaction concluded and every innovation brought to life depends on the knowledge, commitment and effort of people.

“Employees are not simply participants in the production process; they are the human force behind enterprise and economic progress.

“This is why, at the NSITF, our message is clear: the employee must remain at the centre of economic growth, and the employee must be protected.

“Our theme, ‘The employee at the centre of growth, secured by the employees’ compensation scheme’, captures a conviction that guides our work at the NSITF: that economic growth is only truly meaningful and sustainable when the people who create that growth are protected, valued and supported.

“A business may have capital, technology, infrastructure and an ambitious vision, but without a healthy, secure and productive workforce, its capacity to grow is limited. Protecting workers is therefore not separate from building successful businesses. It is an investment in productivity, business continuity, industrial harmony and sustainable national development.”

According to him, this conviction underpins the Employees’ Compensation Scheme, administered by the NSITF under the Employees’ Compensation Act, 2010, adding that the Scheme provides compensation and support to employees who suffer work-related injuries, occupational diseases, disabilities or death, with benefits extended to eligible dependants where the law so provides.

He said the scheme provides a safety net for workers and their families when an unfortunate workplace incident threatens their health, livelihood and financial security.

“The Employees’ Compensation Scheme is a social protection arrangement designed to ensure that when a worker suffers an injury, disease, disability or death arising from work, the worker or eligible dependants are not left to bear the consequences alone.

“The process is straightforward. Employers can register through the NSITF Employer Self-Service Portal at ecsonline.nsitf.gov.ng, by creating an account and following the registration process. They can also walk into any NSITF office for guidance and assistance with registration.

“To our employers, particularly those operating small businesses and enterprises, I encourage you to take advantage of these channels. Do not allow a lack of information or uncertainty about the process to keep your workers outside the protection available under the law. Visit our stand, speak with our officers and take the first step towards securing your workforce.

“At the NSITF, we recognise that expanding social protection in Nigeria requires us to go beyond the traditional formal workplace. This is why, over the past few years, we have progressively intensified efforts to reach micro, small and medium-sized enterprises as a pathway towards broader engagement with the informal economy,” he added.

He said in July 2011, the Fund registered 214,519 employers and covered 8,005,027 employees across the public and private sectors, integrating 73 Ministries, Departments and Agencies into the Scheme.

“In 2025, we registered 21,937 employers and covered 160,971 employees. Between January and October 2026, a further 17,435 employers were registered, and 250,144 employees were brought under coverage. Significantly, the number of employees covered within the first 10 months of 2026 has already exceeded the total recorded for the whole of 2025.”

In his goodwill message, the President and Chairman of the Council, Leye Kupoluyi, said workers’ protection was essential to economic progress.

He noted that behind every successful business was a workforce responsible for driving production, innovation and service delivery.

Some beneficiaries of the scheme also expressed appreciation to the Fund for the financial support received following work-related incidents, urging employers to enrol their workers to guarantee access to compensation when necessary.

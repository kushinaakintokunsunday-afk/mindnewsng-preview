APM fires back at Tinubu over Ibadan remarks

By Punch Punch 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Allied Peoples’ Movement in Oyo State on Sunday slammed President Bola Tinubu for attacking the APM during the All Progressives Congress rally held at Mapo

 The party also described the President’s remark as a desperate distraction from the mounting realities confronting Nigerians and the glaring failures of governance.

 This was contained in a statement issued by the APM State Director of Communication/Spokesperson, Campaign Council, Yinka Oladoyinbo, in Ibadan, the state capital.

 The PUNCH reports that President Tinubu had, on Saturday, in Ibadan, taken a swipe at the APM, whose presidential candidate for 2027 is Governor Seyi Makinde, deploying the Yoruba expression “Egbé Palemo” in what appeared to be an attempt to mock or discredit the political platform.

 APM, in a statement on Sunday, said, “We consider the expression a curious choice of words from a President whose administration has become synonymous with economic hardship, public frustration, political manoeuvring and an increasingly desperate search for electoral validation.”

 The statement read, “In Yoruba political parlance, ‘palemo’ evokes the hurried gathering, packing or clearing away of belongings, sometimes suggestive of preparing to leave or carrying away what one can before departure.

 “Mr President may wish to explain why such vocabulary comes so readily to him. As the saying goes, it takes a master of a craft to recognise the language of fellow practitioners. And when an accuser points one finger at another, the remaining four are invariably directed at himself.

 “The APM will not be distracted by presidential name-calling. Nigerians are far more concerned about the cost of living, the purchasing power of their incomes, insecurity, unemployment, collapsing economic confidence and the daily struggle to survive than the linguistic theatrics of a campaign podium.

 “A President seeking another mandate should be explaining what he has done with the first, not trading taunts with political opponents.

 “Instead of unnecessary distractions like the one at the Mapo Hall jamboree, President Tinubu should have apologised to the people of the state for the unprecedented hardship and suffering that he and his party (APC) have unleashed on them since his assumption of office in 2023.

 “We are equally alarmed by the apparent attempt to draw revered traditional institutions into the APC’s partisan electoral machinery in the state. The presence and engagement of the Olubadan of Ibadanland, Oba Rashidi Ladoja, and the Soun of Ogbomoso, Oba Ghandi Afolabi Olaoye, must not be misconstrued as political endorsements or electoral commitments by their communities.

 “Our traditional rulers are custodians of culture, history, peace and communal identity. They are fathers to all, irrespective of political affiliation. Their palaces must remain places where the ruling party, the opposition, the undecided and the politically disenchanted can find counsel, mediation and a sense of belonging. They are not campaign surrogates, electoral assets or instruments for manufacturing political legitimacy.

 “The APC should therefore abandon the illusion that palace visits, royal courtesies, ceremonial appearances or photographs with traditional rulers can substitute for public approval. A royal handshake does not constitute a ballot. A palace audience does not confer electoral authority. Political choreography cannot manufacture the consent of the people.

 “The electorate in Oyo State is politically conscious, discerning and increasingly intolerant of empty promises. Voters will judge political parties and candidates by performance, competence, credibility, economic relief, security and the quality of leadership they have experienced, not by the spectacle of campaign rallies or the proximity of politicians to royal stools.

 “The APM is, therefore, unimpressed by the President’s grandstanding at Mapo Hall. Political arrogance, verbal assaults and carefully staged displays of influence will not erase the hardship Nigerians endure. Neither will the attempted appropriation of revered institutions conceal the fundamental question confronting this administration: what tangible improvement has it delivered to the lives of ordinary citizens that justifies another four years?

 “The answer will not be supplied by campaign slogans, presidential taunts or palace endorsements. It will be supplied by Nigerians themselves at the ballot box.

 “As the Yoruba proverb reminds us, Bí ikún lọ́ ní oko, bí pàkúté, ìpàdé d’ọjọ́ ìbò. However boldly the squirrel roams the farm, the trap awaits on voting day. Political swagger may dominate the rally ground, but the ballot box is where pretensions meet reality.”

 Oladoyinbo noted that the people would have their say on January 16, 2027, adding that no amount of presidential bravado, royal proximity or political intimidation could substitute for the sovereign will of the electorate.

 “The APM remains confident that Oyo voters will distinguish between political noise and credible leadership, between borrowed prestige and earned trust, and between the spectacle of power and the substance of governance,” the statement said.

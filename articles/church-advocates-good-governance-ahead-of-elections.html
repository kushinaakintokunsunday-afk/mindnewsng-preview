Church advocates good governance ahead of elections

By Punch Punch 16h ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Eternal Sacred Order of the Cherubim and Seraphim has urged Nigerians to seek divine guidance before voting in the February 2027 general elections. The Vice

The Vice Baba Aladura of the church, Elder John Oladapo, stated this recently during the international press conference and flag-off of the centenary celebrations of the United Fogo Olorun Han Band at the church’s international headquarters, Ojokoro, Lagos State.

Oladapo said the church was committed to promoting peace, praying for the country, and encouraging those in government to embrace good governance and do what was right for Nigerians.

He said, “We keep on praying for this nation and the world at large because whatever affects one nation affects the other. We are preaching peace because we cannot live in a country where things are not going the way they should. We talk to people in government. We encourage them to be nice, to do the right thing and to make peace available in the country.

“To our members, we tell them that we are not partisans. We permit everybody to go and get their PVC and vote for the candidate of their choice. In doing that, ask God for direction. Don’t use your human decision alone; God will always direct His people to do the right thing.”

The cleric expressed optimism that the February 2027 elections would be successful, adding that the church would continue to pray for divine intervention in Nigeria and other parts of the world.

The press conference was held as part of activities marking the 100th anniversary of the United Fogo Olorun Han Band, established in 1926 by the founder of the Cherubim and Seraphim Church, Saint Moses Orimolade Tunolase.

In his keynote address, titled “The United Fogo Olorun Han Band at 100: Preserving the Legacy of Our Founding Father Through Service and Righteousness in Worship,” Oladapo said the centenary was an opportunity to reflect on the band’s history, spiritual responsibilities and future.

He explained that the band was among four groups established by Orimolade in 1926 to support critical areas of the church’s ministry as its membership increased.

According to him, the establishment of the bands was guided by the Holy Spirit and reflected the founding father’s application of the principle of delegation in managing the growing church.

Describing the centenary as a celebration of faith, dedication and service, Oladapo said successive generations had sustained the vision despite changing social circumstances.

He said, “Our centenary is therefore a celebration of God’s faithfulness across generations. We stand on the shoulders of men whose commitment made this milestone possible, and we owe it to them and to future generations to preserve and strengthen this inheritance.”

Speaking on the theme of the celebration, ‘Righteousness in Worship,’ drawn from Isaiah 60:21, the cleric urged members to demonstrate godly character and integrity in their worship and service.

Oladapo also urged the band’s leadership to prepare younger members to sustain its legacy, stressing that its 100-year history should inspire renewed commitment rather than complacency.

He said the next century should be marked by stronger spirituality, effective organisation, unity, accountability and deliberate mentoring of younger generations.

“Our relevance will not be determined by how many years we have existed but by the quality of our contribution to the Kingdom of God,” he said.

He added that the band’s first 100 years testified to God’s faithfulness, while its next century should demonstrate the members’ faithfulness to God.

The Chairman of the Centenary Committee, Elder Niyi Afolabi, said the band had proudly carried the torch of the Cherubim and Seraphim Church relentlessly over the last 100 years.

“Composed mostly of individuals who are elders and leaders across our churches, the band has served at the forefront of preserving the heritage and legacy of Saint Moses Orimolade Tunolase – contributing to church development and developing a new generation of leaders for the future,” he added.

NANS rejects N10,000 drug test fee for students

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The National Association of Nigerian Students has opposed the proposed N10,000 drug test fee for students, calling on the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency t

The students’ body made the demand in a statement on Monday, signed by its President, Akinteye Afeez.

NANS said it was concerned that the NDLEA was demanding N10,000 from Nigerian students for drug tests, describing the move as an additional financial burden on students already grappling with economic challenges.

The association said it was not opposed to drug testing, the promotion of drug-free institutions or efforts to address substance abuse among students, but rejected the decision to make students pay for tests initiated by the agency.

“While we are not opposed to drug testing, the promotion of drug-free institutions or any genuine effort aimed at addressing drug abuse among students, we stand fiercely opposed to any attempt to transfer the financial burden of such exercises to Nigerian students, particularly when the initiative is being driven by the Agency itself and students have not independently requested these tests,” the statement read.

It said students were already facing rising tuition fees and the increasing cost of living, adding that imposing further charges under the guise of drug testing was unacceptable.

“It is disheartening to see government agencies turn students into business ventures, exploit them and impose additional financial burdens on Nigerian students despite the government’s various interventions and policies aimed at making education accessible and improving students’ welfare,” NANS said.

The association called on the NDLEA Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, Brig. Gen. Mohamed Buba Marwa (retd.), to immediately stop the enforcement and collection of the fee.

“If the Agency must carry out its official responsibilities, it must do so without imposing any financial burden on Nigerian students. Charging students for an exercise initiated by the Agency itself is utterly unacceptable, and we therefore demand that the Chairman and Chief Executive Officer immediately halt this practice,” it said.

NANS maintained that the welfare of students should not be turned into a source of revenue for government agencies, warning that it would not remain silent over what it described as financial exploitation.

“The welfare of Nigerian students must not become a revenue-generating venture for government agencies.

“We remain committed to supporting every initiative that advances the welfare, safety and development of Nigerian students. However, we shall not fold our arms and watch students being subjected to avoidable financial exploitation,” the statement added.

The association reaffirmed its commitment to affordable education and the protection of students’ welfare.

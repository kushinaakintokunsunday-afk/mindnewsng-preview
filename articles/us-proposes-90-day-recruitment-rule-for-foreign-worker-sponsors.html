US proposes 90-day recruitment rule for foreign worker sponsors

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The United States is proposing tougher requirements for employers seeking to sponsor foreign workers for permanent residency, including a mandatory 90-day recru

The proposal, introduced by the US Department of Labour, seeks to overhaul the Permanent Labor Certification programme, commonly known as PERM, which is a key requirement for many employer-sponsored green card applications.

According to the proposed rule published in the Federal Register for public inspection and seen by PUNCH Online on Wednesday, employers would have to recruit qualified US workers for at least 90 calendar days before submitting permanent labour certification applications.

The proposed changes represent the first comprehensive update to the PERM programme in nearly two decades.

“Under the proposed requirements, employers seeking to sponsor foreign nationals for permanent employment would have to demonstrate that they made sufficient efforts to find qualified American workers before proceeding with foreign worker sponsorship.

“The Department of Labor plans to modernise recruitment requirements by allowing employers to use platforms that reflect current hiring practices. These include company websites, online job boards, social media platforms, job fairs and campus recruitment programmes.

“Employers would also be required to document their recruitment activities and demonstrate that they properly considered qualified American applicants,” the Federal Register stated.

The proposal further prohibits employers from tailoring job requirements to match the qualifications of a particular foreign candidate.

Additionally, businesses that routinely advertised the same or similar positions for more than 90 days during the preceding 12 months would be required to apply the longer recruitment period.

This means the proposed 90-day requirement would serve as a minimum rather than a fixed recruitment period for all employers.

The PERM programme is designed to establish whether qualified and available US workers can fill positions employers intend to offer to foreign nationals.

Labour certification is a key stage in many employment-based green card applications, particularly those under the EB-2 and EB-3 categories.

The proposed overhaul would also introduce tougher restrictions on employers that have recently laid off American workers.

Under the changes, certain employers that laid off US workers within the preceding two years would be barred from seeking permanent labour certification for the same or similar positions.

Employers would also be required to continue considering qualified American workers affected by layoffs throughout the labour certification and immigrant petition processes.

This would extend the obligation beyond the initial recruitment exercise and require employers to consider eligible workers who previously lost their jobs.

The Department of Labor also plans to remove recruitment exemptions currently available to certain occupations.

These include professional nurses, physical therapists and individuals with exceptional ability in the sciences or arts.

If adopted, employers sponsoring foreign workers in these occupations would have to comply with the standard recruitment requirements and demonstrate that qualified American workers are unavailable.

The proposed changes are intended to strengthen protections for domestic workers and ensure that employers give qualified US applicants appropriate consideration before recruiting foreign nationals for permanent positions.

The Department of Labor has proposed a 60-day public comment period beginning from the publication of the proposal in the Federal Register.

During this period, employers, immigration stakeholders and other interested parties would be able to submit comments on the proposed requirements.

The department would consider the submissions before deciding whether to proceed with the rulemaking process.

The proposed requirements are not yet final and would not take effect until the applicable regulatory process has been completed.

The latest proposal follows other US efforts to review employment-based immigration requirements and the obligations of employers hiring foreign nationals.

In March 2026, the Department of Labor was reviewing a separate proposal that could increase prevailing wage requirements for employers hiring foreign workers.

The proposed wage changes could affect H-1B workers and other foreign employees, including positions covered by the PERM programme.

Prevailing wage requirements determine the minimum compensation employers must offer for certain foreign worker positions.

Meanwhile, in December 2025, the United States announced plans to replace the traditional random H-1B visa lottery with a weighted selection system that favours higher-paid positions.

While the H-1B changes concern temporary employment visas, the latest PERM proposal focuses on recruitment requirements for employers seeking to sponsor foreign nationals for permanent employment and green cards.

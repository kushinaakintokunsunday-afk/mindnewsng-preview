‘Even A Tomato Seller Knows Strait Of Hormuz’, Fashola Blames Global Conflicts For Economic Pressures

Former Lagos State Governor Babatunde Fashola has defended the economic policies of President Bola Tinubu’s administration, arguing that global developments, including the COVID-19 pandemic, the Russia-Ukraine war and conflicts in the Middle East, have contributed to the cost-of-living challenges Nigerians are grappling with.

Fashola, a Senior Advocate of Nigeria, said Nigeria was not insulated from global economic pressures, particularly disruptions to supply chains, energy supplies and food markets.

Speaking during an appearance on Politics Today, the former Minister of Works said the global economy was still recovering from the effects of the pandemic, which disrupted economic activity and production worldwide.

He explained that shortages of goods following the pandemic had pushed up prices, while the Russia-Ukraine war further affected the supply of major energy and food commodities, including diesel and wheat.

Fashola also identified the ongoing conflict in the Middle East as another factor affecting global markets, particularly energy supplies and shipping costs.

“It’s also important to contextualise the factors and the circumstances that have exacerbated the cost-of-living challenges,” he said.

“Even the tomato seller knows the Strait of Hormuz and prices his or her goods according to replacement costs now.”

‘Helping Families In Need’

Fashola, a former Minister of Works, also backed President Tinubu for a second term, defending the administration’s response to challenges.

Defending the Tinubu administration’s response to economic pressures, Fashola listed the minimum wage review, the Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND), tax reforms, and the introduction of compressed natural gas (CNG) as part of measures intended to ease the burden on Nigerians.

He said the minimum wage review was one of the government’s early responses to the rising cost of living, aimed at putting more money in workers’ hands.

However, he argued that increasing wages alone could not address the problem, warning that continuously injecting cash into the economy could create what he described as “hyper-demand inflation”.

Fashola said the government had also sought to address some of the major expenses that reduce household disposable income, including rent, transportation, healthcare and children’s education.

He identified NELFUND as one of the interventions aimed at easing financial pressure on families by supporting students’ education.
“Your disposable income goes to rent, goes to transport, and goes to health care, and goes to your children’s education. And NELFUND was, okay, we will help families in need,” he said.

On the tax reforms, Fashola highlighted provisions he said were designed to reduce the tax burden on lower-income earners and ease household expenses.

He said people earning ₦800,000 or less annually were exempt from personal income tax, while about 10.7 million people had experienced reductions in their tax liabilities.

He also cited provisions he said would exempt certain rental agreements valued at ₦10 million or less from stamp duty, as well as the removal of value-added tax on petroleum products.

Fashola argued that the measures were intended to protect disposable income and reduce some of the financial pressures facing households.

‘I’m Not Saying It Is Enough’

The former governor acknowledged that the government’s interventions were insufficient to resolve the economic difficulties immediately, noting a gap between policy announcements and the resources needed to implement them.

“I’m not saying it is enough. There are initiatives like the introduction of CNG, but there’s a distance between policy, between programme, between projects and resources,” he said.

“But we are seeing the rollout slowly. And in the fullness of time, we will see the impact.”

With the 2027 elections less than three months away, Fashola dismissed the opposition, suggesting they can’t do any better.

He questioned whether their proposals offered a compelling reason for Nigerians to abandon the current reform programme.

“What are the other parties offering that is different, that is better, that is compelling as a reason to interrupt the process of reform, growth, and restructuring that is already underway?” he asked.

He also queried what he described as the opposition’s alternative message, asking, “What is the alternative message to what we are currently dealing with? What is the compelling message?

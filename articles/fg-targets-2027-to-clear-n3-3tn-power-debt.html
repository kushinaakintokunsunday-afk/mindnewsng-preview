FG targets 2027 to clear N3.3tn power debt

By Punch Punch 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government is targeting the settlement of its outstanding N3.3tn debt to power generation companies by 2027, while promising significant improvement

The Minister of Power, Joseph Tegbe, disclosed this during an exclusive interview on ARISE News on Monday, where he outlined the government’s plans to address longstanding infrastructure deficiencies, outstanding obligations to power generation companies and other challenges affecting the electricity sector.

Tegbe said the government had negotiated the debt down from N6tn to N3.3tn and had already paid N1.23tn, adding that it was exploring different arrangements to settle the outstanding balance by 2027.

“Today, we have paid 1.23 trillion. We’re speaking 1.23 trillion in this economy in a speed of about one and a half years.

“And we’re on track to finish paying the rest next year. Because we are looking at different ways to settle it,” he said.

The minister explained that the government was considering offsetting some of its outstanding obligations against royalties and corporate income tax as part of efforts to meet its financial commitments to generation companies.

“We want a net of royalties against the debts we have. You can’t take money with one hand and give it with the other hand. So let’s net it off.

“Corporate income tax, we can net it off. We’re negotiating with them. They are in agreement that we can take that out as long as we go down,” he said.

He also disclosed that the government was engaging the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited to explore ways of reducing the cost of gas supplied to power generation companies.

“We’re also sitting down with NNPC to say, gas for power. And we look at how we can cut down the cost to Gencos, to the gas suppliers. Because we own this gas.

“It’s a comparative advantage. And if we want to give power to Nigerians, let’s use what we have to get what we want,” Tegbe said.

On electricity supply, the minister said Nigerians should expect massive improvements within the next three to five years, acknowledging that infrastructure deficiencies in the sector were more extensive than initially estimated.

He attributed the challenges partly to decades of neglect and poor investment priorities, arguing that successive administrations had spent money on projects without adequately considering how they connected to the wider electricity network.

“The bulk of the challenges is not in generation.

“I’ll tell you that, look, infrastructure decay is enormous. These things have happened over 50 years. Don’t expect us to fix it in two years,” he said.

Tegbe also identified poor planning and politically motivated investment decisions as factors affecting electricity delivery, saying his ministry was reviewing ongoing projects to identify priority investments.

He said the government was examining the relationship between projects and their potential contributions to improved electricity supply to ensure that available resources were spent on the right infrastructure.

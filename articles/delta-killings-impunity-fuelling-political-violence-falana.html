Delta killings: Impunity fuelling political violence – Falana

By Punch Punch 1d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Falana spoke on Channels Television’s Politics Today on Monday while reacting to the deadly violence at a political rally in Ughelli North Local Government Area of Delta State.

He urged the Nigerian Bar Association, civil society organisations and security agencies to ensure that those responsible for electoral violence were brought to justice.

His comment came following a deadly incident at a campaign rally in Delta State.

The attack occurred during a campaign rally in Uduere community, Ughelli North Local Government Area of Delta State, on Monday.

Former Deputy Senate President, Ovie Omo-Agege, who is seeking election to the Delta Central Senatorial District in the 2027 general election on the platform of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, said gunmen opened fire shortly after he began addressing supporters.

He said four members of his campaign team were killed, while 15 others sustained critical injuries.

The former Deputy Senate President also credited his Department of State Services security detail with helping him escape unharmed.

The incident prompted the Delta State Government to impose a 12-hour curfew on Ughelli North and order an investigation into the violence.

The police also announced a probe into the incident.

Reacting, Falana described the incident as avoidable but unsurprising, citing previous attacks involving prominent political figures.

He recalled previous incidents, including an attack on the convoy of former presidential candidate Peter Obi, in Calabar, Cross River State, in which a person reportedly died, as well as the stoning of President Bola Tinubu’s convoy in Akwa Ibom State.

“Well, it is very sad. What happened today is extremely sad, avoidable, but not surprising.

“We’ve had attacks on politicians. I think Omoyele Sowore was lucky in Ekiti. I think somebody died in Calabar when Mr Peter Obi’s convoy was attacked. The president’s convoy was stoned in Akwa Ibom about two weeks ago. So the stakes are for the members of the political class. Impunity is already institutionalised,” he said.

Falana argued that the absence of effective prosecution had emboldened perpetrators of political violence, warning that the country could face a serious crisis if politicians were left to determine the course of public affairs without accountability.

He added, “From this unfortunate incident, nothing may come out of it. Yesterday, we lost scores of people in Bayelsa State in a boat accident. Nothing will happen.

“Now, electoral offences are taking place. Nobody is being prosecuted.”

The senior lawyer challenged the Nigerian Bar Association, human rights organisations and other stakeholders to take a more active role in ensuring that electoral offenders were brought to justice.

“I am challenging the Nigerian Bar Association, the human rights community and other stakeholders to intervene very decisively because if we leave the affairs of our country in the hands of politicians, we may run into a very serious crisis. And the earlier, the better,” he said.

Falana proposed that the NBA should mobilise lawyers across the country to assist the Independent National Electoral Commission in prosecuting electoral offenders.

He said the arrangement had been adopted during previous elections and could help address the commission’s limited capacity to handle prosecutions alongside its other responsibilities.

“I have suggested, as we have done in the past. Go and sit down with INEC and mobilise lawyers to assist INEC to prosecute electoral offenders. And that has to be done,” he said.

“We did it in 2011, 2015, 2020, 2023. So that all those who have been arrested for committing electoral offences can be nabbed and prosecuted because impunity is the order of the day,” he said.

He maintained that perpetrators would think twice before engaging in violence if they knew they would face the consequences of their actions.

“Nobody would have thought we were going to kill people in a rally if they knew that the law would catch up with them,” he said.

Falana also criticised the National Assembly for failing to establish an Electoral Offences Commission, despite recommendations by successive electoral reform panels dating back to 2009 and 2010.

He said the absence of a dedicated body had left INEC with the responsibility of prosecuting electoral offenders, despite the commission’s existing workload.

According to him, the electoral body was already handling hundreds of pre-election cases and would have more post-election disputes to contend with after the polls.

He urged security agencies, particularly the police and the Department of State Services, to ensure that political violence was investigated and that those responsible were prosecuted, irrespective of their political affiliations.

He stated, “The security forces in our country, particularly the police and the State Security Service, must actualise the wishes of Nigerians, i.e. to have a peaceful election.

“Let the people decide. Give the people the opportunity to choose among their oppressors. It’s their right. But the way we are going, we are going to lose more lives unnecessarily.”

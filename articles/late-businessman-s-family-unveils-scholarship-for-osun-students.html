Late businessman’s family unveils scholarship for Osun students

By Punch Punch 2d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The family of late engineer and businessman, David Abiola, has launched a N10m education trust to sponsor university education for indigent but academically gifted students, particularly those from his state, Osun.

The initiative, unveiled on Friday at the 10th memorial lecture in his honour at the Metropolitan Club, Lagos, is aimed at extending the educational opportunities that helped shape Abiola’s life to disadvantaged young Nigerians.

Speaking at the event, his daughter, Yetunde Falore, said the trust would initially sponsor two students annually, covering their university education and providing mentorship and other support to help them build successful careers.

She explained that the family decided to establish the initiative because their father benefited from scholarships throughout his academic journey and strongly believed in the power of education.

“Our father was a product of the scholarships that he got, and we felt that it was time for us to pay it forward,” Falore said.

According to her, the family would provide an initial N10m to establish the fund, with the first beneficiaries expected to begin their academic programmes in 2027.

She said the scholarships would cover tuition and other academic expenses throughout the beneficiaries’ university education, subject to satisfactory academic performance and good conduct.

Falore added that beneficiaries would also receive mentorship from professionals and members of the family to improve their career prospects.

The deceased’s first son, Tolu Oni, described his father as a disciplined mentor whose commitment to education, intellectual development and personal integrity shaped his children’s lives.

“My father was a mechanical engineer by profession. He saw the world through the lens of precision, structure, and integrity. But at his true core, he was a master builder of human potential,” he said.

Oni recalled how his father personally supervised his education, ensuring that he developed strong analytical and communication skills while maintaining discipline.

According to him, Abiola’s passion for learning influenced his decision to study abroad, first at the University of Nottingham and later at the University of Oxford.

Oni explained that his father’s commitment to education was rooted in his humble background, noting that scholarships enabled him to attend prestigious institutions, including Niger Grammar School and King’s College, Lagos, before proceeding to the United Kingdom.

He said the deceased later earned an MPhil in Critical Management at Lancaster University, demonstrating that intellectual development should continue regardless of age.

“Age was never a barrier to his intellectual curiosity. At over 60 years old, at an age when most men were comfortably settling into quiet retirement, my father enrolled at Lancaster University in the United Kingdom,” he said.

Oni, a chartered accountant and entrepreneur, said the family hoped to extend the same opportunities to children who might otherwise be unable to pursue higher education because of financial constraints.

He also appealed to friends, corporate organisations and well-meaning Nigerians to support the trust, explaining that additional contributions would enable it to sponsor more students.

In his lecture, titled “The Lamp Kept Lit: Curiosity, Learning and the Making of a Nation,” immediate past Pro-Chancellor and Chairman of the Governing Council of Olabisi Onabanjo University, Professor Oluwatoyin Ashiru, called for a fundamental shift in Nigeria’s education system from certificate acquisition to practical knowledge and problem-solving.

He said the country had continued to produce talented individuals without adequately translating their intellectual abilities into national development.

“Our problem is not a shortage of intelligence; it is our difficulty in turning intelligence into broad national progress,” Ashiru said.

The don argued that educational institutions must prioritise critical thinking, technical competence, innovation and character development rather than concentrate on academic qualifications.

He also raised concerns about the country’s learning crisis, citing UNICEF estimates of millions of out-of-school children and poor mathematics proficiency among primary school pupils.

Ashiru said universities should be assessed not only by the number of graduates they produce but also by the practical problems their research and innovations solve.

“Learning must become capability and capacity. You can hold many certificates and still struggle to solve a practical problem,” he stated.

Ashiru also proposed a legacy-of-learning initiative to promote annual student problem-solving competitions, career counselling and mentorship for young Nigerians in memory of the deceased.

He described Abiola as a lifelong learner whose curiosity, humility, generosity and integrity remained important lessons for the younger generation.

Earlier, Chairman of the occasion and former Managing Director of Daily Times of Nigeria, Dr Yemi Ogunbiyi, described the deceased as a man whose life positively influenced many people.

He said celebrating Abiola’s legacy required examining the different areas of his life, particularly his commitment to education, professional excellence, friendship and service.

Ogunbiyi also acknowledged the support of the deceased’s friends, describing their continued commitment to his family over the past decade as a reflection of the relationships he built during his lifetime.

Also speaking, founder of Let It Shine Academy, Dami Fayide, shared her experience working with disadvantaged children and young people in correctional facilities, highlighting the importance of educational access, vocational training and rehabilitation.

The memorial lecture brought together family members, friends, associates and other dignitaries to celebrate Abiola’s life and renew their commitment to the values he represented.

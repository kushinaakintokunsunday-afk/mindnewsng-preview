Democrats secure 56-seat Senate majority as Peltola wins Alaska

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Democrat Mary Peltola has defeated Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan to win Alaska’s United States Senate race, ending the last unresolved Senate contest in the

Peltola’s victory, projected on Tuesday, November 17, gives Democrats a 56–44 majority in the Senate after the party flipped nine Republican-held seats in the midterm elections.

According to the Alaska Division of Elections’ preliminary results, Peltola secured 50.1 per cent of first-choice votes, crossing the threshold required to win outright under the state’s ranked-choice voting system without a further round of counting.

Sullivan received 45.8 per cent, while Daniel J. Sullivan, another candidate whose name became central to the controversy surrounding the election, received 1.7 per cent. Republican Gerald Heikes polled 2.1 per cent.

The Associated Press, in a report published by ABC News on Wednesday, November 18, said Peltola overcame allegations that members of her campaign helped recruit a candidate who shares the incumbent senator’s name.

The election was marked by allegations of political manipulation, competing investigations and legal battles over the possibility of voters confusing the two candidates named Sullivan.

Controversy over two Dan Sullivans

The controversy intensified following an October 28 investigation by The New York Times, which reported that operatives aligned with Peltola’s campaign, her lawyers and senior aides at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee were involved in efforts to recruit Republican challengers to enter the race.

One of the candidates was Daniel J. Sullivan, a resident of Petersburg, Alaska, whose name closely resembles that of incumbent Senator Dan S. Sullivan.

The newspaper reported that the recruitment effort, internally referred to as a ballot-composition project, was intended to dilute support for the incumbent and improve Peltola’s chances of winning.

ABC News also reported that the Times based its investigation on interviews, text messages and documents.

However, Peltola’s campaign denied involvement in recruiting either Sullivan candidate.

The controversy prompted President Donald Trump to accuse Peltola of election fraud and call for her prosecution.

The Justice Department subsequently filed a lawsuit seeking to prevent certification of the election if the incumbent Sullivan lost by a margin smaller than the votes received by the similarly named candidate.

A federal judge dismissed the lawsuit, but the Justice Department appealed the decision.

Victory despite legal battles

Despite the controversy, Peltola secured a decisive enough lead to win without relying on additional ranked-choice calculations.

Alaska Public Media reported that her 50.1 per cent share of first-choice votes exceeded the combined votes received by the two Sullivans and Heikes.

The Justice Department’s investigation into the alleged recruitment effort remains ongoing, while the civil case continues through the appeals process.

Peltola’s victory makes her the first Democrat elected to the US Senate from Alaska since 2008 and the first Alaska Native person to serve in the chamber.

She previously represented Alaska in the House of Representatives, becoming the first Alaska Native woman elected to Congress in 2022.

Her campaign focused on affordability, healthcare costs, living expenses and the interests of Alaska residents.

The result also completes the Senate race count in the 2026 midterm elections, giving Democrats a substantial majority when the new Congress convenes in January.

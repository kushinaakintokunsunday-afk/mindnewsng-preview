Navy seizes 11,700 litres of petrol, recovers boat engine in Lagos

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 5d ago 2 min read

By Philip Nwosu

The Nigerian Navy said its operatives had seized 11,700 litres of suspected Premium Motor Spirit (PMS), valued at ₦15.795 million, during an intelligence-driven operation against petroleum smuggling off Ayito Beach, near the Seme Border in Badagry, Lagos State.

The operation, conducted by personnel of the Forward Operating Base (FOB) Badagry Special Forces at about 7:30 p.m. on Friday, November 13, 2026, also led to the recovery of a 60-horsepower outboard engine.

The Navy said the operation followed credible intelligence about suspected illegal bunkering activities in the area, with the petroleum products allegedly being transported to the Republic of Benin by criminal networks operating across the border.

According to the Navy, its personnel intercepted a Cotonou boat conveying the suspected petroleum products. However, the occupants reportedly abandoned the boat and fled on sighting the approaching naval team.

A search of the abandoned boat led to the recovery of 390 jerrycans, each containing 30 litres of suspected PMS, bringing the total volume of the seized products to 11,700 litres.

The recovered petroleum products were valued at ₦15,795,000.

In a related development, FOB Badagry handed over 316 bags of 50-kilogramme foreign parboiled rice to the Nigeria Customs Service (NCS), Western Marine Command, Badagry, on Saturday, November 14.

The handover, which took place at the base jetty at about 10 a.m., comprised 231 bags of good rice and 85 damaged bags suspected to have been smuggled into the country.

The Navy said the rice was intercepted during intelligence-led operations within the base’s area of responsibility and was subsequently transferred to the Customs Service in accordance with existing regulations.

The handover was completed at about 11:30 a.m. without incident.

The two operations, according to the Navy, form part of its sustained efforts to curb petroleum products diversion, cross-border smuggling and other maritime-related criminal activities.

The Director of Naval Information, Navy Capt. Abiodun Folorunsho, said the Navy would continue to sustain intelligence-led operations, strengthen maritime surveillance and collaborate with relevant security and regulatory agencies to protect Nigeria’s maritime environment and economic interests.

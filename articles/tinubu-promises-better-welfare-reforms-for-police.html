Tinubu promises better welfare, reforms for police

By Punch Punch 2d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

President Bola Tinubu has said that he would continue to pursue reforms that would strengthen the capacity of the Nigeria Police Force, improve personnel welfar

The president gave the assurance on Saturday during the passing out of the 8th Regular Cadets Course at the Nigeria Police Academy, Wudil, Kano State.

Represented by the Secretary to the Government of the Federation, George Akume, the President noted that the success of these efforts would only depend on the personal commitment of the newly commissioned officers.

He assured that the present administration remained committed to building a police force that is adequately equipped, professionally trained, technologically enabled and sufficiently motivated to fulfil its constitutional responsibilities.

“The Federal Government will continue to pursue reforms and provide the necessary support to strengthen police capacity, improve personnel welfare and create an enabling environment for effective service policing.

“However, the success of these efforts will only depend on your personal commitment to excellence. The nation expects much from you, and you must never lose sight of the honour and responsibility that comes with wearing the uniform of the Nigeria Police Force,” Tinubu said.

He urged the officers to go out with courage, humility and an unshakable sense of duty.

“Let your service be defined by integrity, your leadership by compassion and your commitment to Nigeria by the highest standards of patriotism,” he urged.

Tinubu congratulated the graduating cadets on their successful completion of five years of intense academic, professional and physical training, noting that the achievement testifies to their discipline, resilience, determination and commitment to a noble cause.

“I also extend my sincere appreciation to your parents, guardians and families whose sacrifices and encouragement have contributed immensely to this milestone. I commend the leadership, management, academic staff, instructors and personnel of the Nigeria Police Academy for their dedication to moulding and preparing these young men and women for the enormous responsibilities ahead.

“You must understand that the uniform you are about to wear represents more than authority. It represents public trust, responsibility and a solemn obligation to protect every Nigerian, irrespective of ethnicity, religion, political affiliation or social standing. The true measure of your service will not be the rank you attain, but the lives you protect, the communities you secure and the confidence you inspire in the people you serve,” Tinubu said.

The President further charged the newly commissioned officers to uphold the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, respect the fundamental rights of citizens and discharge their duties with professionalism, fairness and accountability.

“Never allow the authority of your office to become an instrument of oppression, intimidation or personal enrichment. Be firm in enforcing the law, but always guided by justice, respect and respect for human dignity,” he said.

According to him, Nigeria is confronted by evolving security challenges that require a modern, intelligent, responsive and professionally equipped police force.

“You are graduating at a time when we are overhauling our policing structure by establishing state police. Wherever you find yourself, either at the state or federal level, your generation must be prepared to embrace innovation, leverage technology, strengthen intelligence-led policing and build meaningful partnerships with communities and other security agencies,” he stated.

In his address, the Commandant of the Academy, AIG Auwal Muhammad, described the day as one of celebrating discipline and readiness to sacrifice for the nation.

He commended the FG for the support it has been giving to the academy, stressing that the support had encouraged them to pursue higher standards in academic programmes and prepare the cadets to address the security challenges confronting the country.

A total of 845 Assistant Superintendents of Police were commissioned during the event, while awards were presented to some of them for their outstanding performances.

The PUNCH reports that Tinubu has received the Constitution (Sixth Alteration) Bill establishing state police after all 36 state Houses of Assembly approved the proposed amendment, paving the way for its presidential assent.

The Chief of Staff to the President, Femi Gbajabiamila, announced the development in a statement on Wednesday, saying Tinubu would assent to the bill at a formal signing ceremony.

He said all 36 state Houses of Assembly had approved the bill, exceeding the constitutional requirement of at least 24 states, representing two-thirds of the 36 states, under Section 9(2) of the 1999 Constitution.

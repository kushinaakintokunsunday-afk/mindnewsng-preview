2027: Tinubu may win only two states – Baba-Ahmed

By Punch Punch 4d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The National Chairman of the People’s Redemption Party, Hakeem Baba-Ahmed, has predicted that President Bola Tinubu will lose the 2027 presidential election if

Baba-Ahmed argued that widespread dissatisfaction with the administration could cost the ruling All Progressives Congress its hold on power.

He also said the Tinubu government is concentrating more on retaining political power than addressing the country’s economic and security challenges.

He added that Nigerians were increasingly frustrated by the cost of living, insecurity and other difficulties confronting households.

Speaking in an interview with ARISE NEWS on Friday, the PRP chairman said his assessment was informed by his party’s interactions with ordinary Nigerians and their experiences under the current administration.

“If the elections are free and fair, President Tinubu, I repeat, will not win this election. Nigerians as a whole, not just Northwest, will reject him because he has not performed anywhere near what they expected.

“The Tinubu administration was more interested in just being in power than actually fixing problems in the country,” he said.

He maintained that the party’s position was based on direct engagement with citizens rather than assumptions about public opinion.

He stated, “We don’t just assume things. We mix with them. We don’t give them money. We don’t have the money that APC has to give them. So, we engage with them. We follow their lives. We follow their opinions. We are in touch with their sentiments.”

Baba-Ahmed also clarified that his earlier prediction about Tinubu’s electoral prospects was a nationwide assessment, not one restricted to the North-West or North-East.

“I said if the elections are credible, free and fair in 2027, President Tinubu is unlikely to win more than two states. I didn’t even say anything about the Northwest. I said in the country,” he said

The PRP chairman said his party had been involved in opposition coalition discussions from the beginning, describing the effort as an attempt to offer Nigerians an alternative to the APC ahead of the next general election.

“Our party has been part of the coalition from day one and we’re very proud of the role we have played. We have participated in all of it,” he stated.

He identified the Social Democratic Party, PRP, Peoples Democratic Party, Allied Peoples Movement, African Democratic Congress and Nigeria Democratic Congress as parties involved in the discussions. He added that more parties were expected to join before the end of November.

Baba-Ahmed said the coalition had set out two major objectives, the first being to work towards removing the APC from power.

He stated, “We have identified two fundamentals that we have to achieve. One is to work with Nigerians to make sure that we remove the APC administration from the neck of Nigerians. It is absolutely necessary that this administration does not come back.”

He expressed confidence that the opposition could defeat the ruling party in 2027 if it presented Nigerians with a credible alternative.

He said, “We are providing Nigerians a credible option to the APC, the most credible option to the APC. And we have a very comfortable expectation that we will win this election because Nigerians will reject from top to bottom. They will reject APC.”

On the political climate ahead of the election, Baba-Ahmed raised concerns about increasing violence and what he described as less visible restrictions on opposition parties’ ability to campaign.

“The escalating incidences of violence. Violence has now become a major factor in this politics. Not just overt violence, people beating opponents, people disrupting rallies and killing people. But the violence that is subtle.

“The antics of the APC closing the campaigning space for the opposition. You cannot mount billboards, you can’t have access to places to campaign,” he added.

He also rejected suggestions that northerners would be hypocritical if they withdrew their support for Tinubu after backing him in the 2023 presidential election.

According to him, voters who supported Tinubu in the last election had the same democratic right to change their minds based on their assessment of his performance.

He noted, “If the north elected Tinubu in 2023, it was the rights of northerners who felt that Tinubu was a better candidate than Obi and Atiku. And they have the same right now to reject Tinubu. There’s nothing hypocritical about this. That is what politics is all about.”

Baba-Ahmed linked the growing discontent in the North to worsening insecurity and the pressure of rising living costs, saying many families were struggling to meet basic needs.

He said, “There are towns, there are states where huge numbers, huge parts of the states in the north are under bandits or are vulnerable to bandit attack at any minute. Cost of living is impossible. You can’t pay school fees. You can’t take your children to the hospital. You can’t feed your family.”

He blamed the Tinubu administration for what he described as a deterioration in Nigerians’ living conditions.

He said, “Tinubu’s administration has destroyed lives and has made lives wretched. And this is why Nigerians are angry with him.”

Baba-Ahmed also defended his decision to criticise the president despite having served in his administration as special adviser on political matters. He said his departure followed concerns that the government was not giving sufficient attention to governance.

“I left the administration because I saw no evidence that President Tinubo’s administration was serious about governance,” he noted.

He said he had initially been part of the administration but left about a year to a year and a half after it began, having become convinced that its direction was wrong.

Baba-Ahmed said, “I was there when he started. I left a year into his administration, a year and a half into his administration, precisely because I saw the trajectory.”

He argued that the government’s public presentation of economic figures did not reflect the difficulties Nigerians were experiencing in their daily lives.

He added, “And rolling out statistics and figures and telling Nigerians we’re doing well, when in fact, life was becoming more and more impossible to deal with.”

The PRP chairman further criticised the state of the country inherited by Tinubu from his predecessor, former President Muhammadu Buhari, saying the current administration had made an already difficult situation worse.

He stated, “President Buhari handed over to Tinubu a country in a mess. And President Tinubu took it and made it considerably worse.”

Nigeria’s next general election is scheduled for 2027, with opposition parties facing the challenge of building a united front against the APC.

The coalition discussions involving the PRP, ADC, PDP, SDP, APM and NDC reflect efforts by opposition figures to coordinate their political activities and improve their chances of defeating the ruling party.

The debate over opposition unity comes against the background of the 2023 presidential election, in which Tinubu won on the APC platform while major opposition parties fielded separate candidates. Atiku Abubakar contested for the PDP, Peter Obi for the Labour Party and Rabiu Kwankwaso for the New Nigeria Peoples Party.

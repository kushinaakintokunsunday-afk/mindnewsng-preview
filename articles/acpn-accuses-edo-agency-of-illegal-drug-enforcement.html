ACPN accuses Edo agency of illegal drug enforcement

By Doris Obinna

The Association of Community Pharmacists of Nigeria (ACPN) has accused the Edo State Consumer Protection Committee of regulatory overreach, misinformation and unlawful enforcement in the pharmaceutical sector.

It warned that the committee’s actions could undermine patient safety and violate federal laws.
The association, in a petition to the Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, Lateef Fagbemi, SAN, called for an end to pharmaceutical enforcement activities by agencies operating outside their statutory mandates.

The petition, signed by ACPN National Chairman, Ambrose Ezeh, and National Secretary, Mrs. Obiageri Ikwu, specifically faulted the committee for allegedly declaring Camosunate a banned medicinal product in Nigeria without statutory authority or verifiable regulatory evidence.

ACPN maintained that the regulation, registration, suspension and prohibition of medicines fall within the statutory mandate of the National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC), stressing that state agencies must not assume powers not expressly conferred on them by law.

Citing NAFDAC’s official Green Book, the association said Camosunate Children Powder, with registration number 04-7908, manufactured by Front Pharmaceuticals PLC and marketed by Geneith Pharmaceuticals Limited, remains an active registered medicine in Nigeria.

It said the product’s registration was approved on November 26, 2025, and remains valid until November 25, 2030.
According to ACPN, there is no official NAFDAC declaration establishing that the specific formulation described as banned by the Edo committee has been prohibited, suspended, withdrawn or deregistered.

The association described the committee’s reported declaration as misleading and a violation of established regulatory procedures, insisting that public claims about the safety or registration status of medicines must be backed by credible evidence.

It also questioned the reported enforcement action against ALSO CARE Pharmacy in Evbuodia community, along Airport Road, Benin City, demanding transparent disclosure of the products seized and their registration details.
The association also called for the release of independent regulatory evidence supporting allegations that the pharmacy stocked counterfeit or prohibited medicines.

ACPN warned against publicly condemning pharmaceutical establishments on the basis of unverified allegations, insisting that enforcement actions must be evidence-based and conducted in accordance with the law.

On the jurisdiction of state agencies, the association cited the Pharmacy Council of Nigeria (PCN) Establishment Act 2022, particularly Sections 4, 22, 31 and 32, which provide the framework for the regulation, inspection, registration and licensing of pharmaceutical premises.

It argued that the Act vests the PCN with responsibility for regulating pharmaceutical premises in both the public and private sectors, through designated Pharmaceutical Inspection Officers.

The association also cited Sections 7 and 8 of the Counterfeit and Fake Drugs and Unwholesome Processed Foods (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, Cap C34, Laws of the Federation 2004, which provide for the constitution and powers of State Task Forces.

It, however, maintained that such statutory provisions do not automatically empower state consumer protection committees to exercise specialised pharmaceutical regulatory functions.

ACPN further argued that a general consumer protection mandate does not confer the authority to regulate medicines or inspect and control pharmaceutical premises outside the powers established by relevant federal laws.

Consequently, the association called for an end to pharmaceutical enforcement activities carried out without appropriate statutory authority, particularly in Edo State.

It urged the Federal Ministry of Justice to work with the Attorneys-General and Commissioners for Justice across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory to sensitise government agencies on their constitutional and statutory limits.

The association said respect for the respective mandates of NAFDAC and PCN was essential to protecting public health and preventing regulatory confusion.

ACPN reaffirmed its commitment to combating fake, counterfeit and falsified medicines, warning that consumer protection must not become a cover for misinformation, administrative arbitrariness or regulatory intimidation.
It insisted that public officials must be held accountable for claims made in the course of enforcement.

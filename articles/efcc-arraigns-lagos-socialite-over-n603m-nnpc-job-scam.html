EFCC arraigns Lagos socialite over N603m NNPC job scam

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission on Wednesday arraigned socialite Halimat Tejuosho before the Federal Capital Territory High Court in Gwagwalada, Ab

Tejuosho was arraigned before Justice A.S. Adepoju on a five-count charge bordering on advance fee fraud, obtaining money by false pretence and criminal conduct.

EFCC announced the arraignment in a statement posted on its X handle on Wednesday.

The charge, marked FCT/CR/473/2026, accused her and one Gidado Ibrahim of obtaining N603.4m from Oluseye Yomi-Sholoye between August and October 2024 under the pretence that they would facilitate her appointment as NNPC GMD.

Count one of the charge read, “That you Halimat Adenike Tejuosho and Gidado Ibrahim between August 2024 to October 2024 within the jurisdiction of this Honourable Court obtained the sum of N603,400,000.00 (Six hundred and three million four hundred thousand naira) from Oluseye Yomi-Sholoye under the false pretence that you will facilitate her appointment as the Group Managing Director, GMD of Nigerian National Petroleum Corporation (NNPC), which pretence you knew to be false.”

The commission also accused Tejuosho of receiving N259.9m and N50m, among other sums, into bank accounts allegedly linked to her and Haleems Integrated Services Limited, knowing that the money was proceeds of crime.

She pleaded not guilty to all five counts when they were read to her.

Following her plea, defence counsel, Chuks Ugwuru, urged the court to grant her bail on liberal terms, arguing that all five counts were bailable offences.

He said the application was based mainly on her health condition, citing a medical report which, he argued, showed that she was critically ill.

“The ill health of an accused person is a consideration worthy enough to be reckoned as a ground of special circumstances,” he said, urging Justice Adepoju to exercise her discretion in the defendant’s favour.

However, lead prosecution counsel, Yahaya Tarfa, told the court that the EFCC opposed the bail application and had filed a 12-paragraph counter-affidavit, with one exhibit, alongside a written address.

Tarfa acknowledged that the decision to grant bail rested with the court but urged the judge to impose conditions that would ensure Tejuosho’s availability for trial.

In her ruling, Justice Adepoju held that the offences were bailable and granted Tejuosho bail in the sum of N100m, with one surety in like sum.

The judge ordered that the surety must reside within the court’s jurisdiction and own land or property in the Federal Capital Territory.

She directed that the property’s title documents be verified at the FCT Land Registry, with a copy deposited with the court’s registrar.

The defendant was also ordered to deposit her international passport with the registrar pending the hearing and determination of the case.

The EFCC had earlier declared Tejuosho wanted in April 2026 over an alleged case of obtaining money by false pretence.

The commission said she was 41 years old and gave her last known address as Aso Grove Estate, Maitama Extension, Abuja.

Kwankwaso denies sending Yusuf to negotiate APC return

By Punch Punch 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Vice-Presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, Senator Rabiu Kwankwaso, has denied sending Kano State Governor, Abba Yusuf, to President Bo

The denial was contained in a statement issued on Monday by the spokesperson for the Kwankwasiyya Movement, Dr Habibu Mohammed, a copy of which was made available to The PUNCH.

“Our attention has been drawn to the interview granted by Kano State Governor, Alhaji Abba Kabir Yusuf, to BBC Hausa, in which he alleged that Senator Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso personally sent him to President Bola Ahmed Tinubu to negotiate an arrangement for their return to the All Progressives Congress (APC), allegedly asking him to swear on the Holy Qur’an as evidence of their commitment.

“We consider this allegation another unfortunate attempt by the Governor to shift responsibility for his political decisions to Senator Kwankwaso, rather than demonstrate the courage and maturity expected of a leader entrusted with the mandate of millions of Kano citizens,” it said.

According to the statement, “The Governor is entitled to explain his political decisions and defend his defection. However, he must not confuse the right to offer an explanation with the right to make sweeping allegations against another person without providing credible evidence.”

It explained that Kwankwaso was a nationally recognised political leader whose political decisions and public positions should be judged by his record, publicly expressed convictions and verifiable facts, not by allegations introduced to justify another politician’s changing political affiliations.

“Let it be clearly understood that political consultations, meetings and discussions do not automatically constitute a binding commitment by an entire political movement or its leader to abandon its political convictions. No individual should unilaterally claim to have secured the political consent of Senator Kwankwaso or the Kwankwasiyya Movement without credible evidence,” it further said.

“Let us make one thing clear: Senator Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso is not responsible for Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf’s decision to defect to the APC. If the governor believes that joining the APC is in the best interest of Kano State, he should defend that decision on its own merits and explain the benefits the people of Kano are expected to derive from it, rather than attempting to transfer responsibility to another political leader.

“The statement reminded Governor Yusuf that the political history of their movement demonstrated a consistent tradition of consultation, collective deliberation and engagement with relevant stakeholders. From our political engagements during the days of the PDP, through the formation and development of the APC, the developments around NNPP, and subsequent political engagements involving the ADC and NDC, major decisions have always required consultation with Kwankwasiyya stakeholders, elected representatives, party leaders and relevant organs of the movement.

“These are not decisions that can be made by one individual through private conversations or informal arrangements. No individual can legitimately claim to have secured the consent of an entire political movement simply because of an alleged personal understanding.

“Kwankwaso challenged Governor Yusuf to provide specific and verifiable details if such an arrangement existed.

“If Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf insists that such an agreement existed, we challenge him to provide specific and verifiable details. When exactly was this agreement reached? Who were the participants? Which delegations were involved? What were the terms? Are there documentary records or independent witnesses who can corroborate his account?

“The Governor also needs to explain the implications of his claim that President Tinubu was approached and asked not to interfere in the legal proceedings challenging the 2023 Kano governorship election. The Supreme Court eventually determined that dispute and affirmed his election. Nigerians deserve clarity on what precisely was discussed, without creating an impression that the judicial process was subject to a political bargain.”

“We must also ask why an alleged understanding dating back to 2023 is being presented as the principal justification for a defection that took place in January 2026,” the statement added.

Mega churches face succession heat

By Punch Punch 3d ago 15 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The leadership crisis rocking the Fountain of Life Church, Lagos, has brought succession arrangements in some of the country’s biggest Pentecostal churches unde

The controversy, which has pitted the church’s Senior Pastor, Jimmy Odukoya, against its Board of Trustees, has raised questions about how religious organisations choose successors.

While the Fountain of Life crisis has resulted in a court battle, conversations surrounding succession have extended to other major denominations, including the Deeper Christian Life Ministry, the Redeemed Christian Church of God and Living Faith Church Worldwide.

The dispute over the leadership of the Fountain of Life, founded by the late Pastor Taiwo Odukoya, intensified following the emergence of a video in which the cleric identified another pastor as his preferred successor.

The development prompted some Nigerians to revisit previous comments by leaders of other prominent ministries and question whether adequate arrangements exist to ensure smooth leadership transitions.

Kumuyi’s succession comments resurface

The General Superintendent of the Deeper Christian Life Ministry, Pastor William Kumuyi, came under renewed public attention following the circulation of a video in which he addressed questions about who would succeed him.

A strategy and transformational consultant, Ayo-Bankole Akintujoye, and an enterprise resource planning consultant, Oluwatobi Bamidele, were among those who drew attention to the cleric’s comments.

The two shared a video in which Kumuyi discussed succession.

Akintujoye wrote, “Pastor Kumuyi is an incredibly blessed man in his generation, but absolutely no leader at over 80 years old should be this afraid and rattled about a discussion on succession. Something is wrong somewhere.”

Bamidele, linking the remarks to the crisis at the Fountain of Life, wrote, “This Fountain of Life Church successor saga made me remember when Pastor Kumuyi said this about his successor.”

In the video, reportedly recorded during a Bible study programme in August 2025, Kumuyi maintained that the choice of his successor rested with God.

He was responding to comments attributed to a younger preacher in the church who stressed that there could be no leadership without a successor.

Kumuyi said he considered the repeated emphasis on succession inappropriate while he was still leading the ministry.

“The brother who taught the other Tuesday kept saying there is no leadership without a successor. He said it twice. I felt that was too much, more so since your father is still here. God will give a successor at His own time,” he said.

The cleric also warned church members against attempting to influence or dictate his decisions concerning leadership.

“You are not going to fight your shepherd, your pilot. I reject any member of the church, whether you are a worker, choir member, or security person, who tries to control me.

“Don’t try to kill my conviction. It will only make me resist you so that when you get to the other side, you won’t say I did not tell you. When the time comes, God will choose a man after His own heart. Don’t try to drive me away, even though I know you cannot,” he added.

Kumuyi later revealed the rotational leadership structure put in place at various levels of the church.

When Saturday PUNCH contacted a senior pastor in the church on the matter, he said, “We don’t discuss succession plans in Deeper Life when our father, Pastor Kumuyi, is still alive.”

Adeboye awaits divine direction

Similarly, previous remarks by the General Overseer of the Redeemed Christian Church of God, Pastor Enoch Adeboye, attracted attention in the wider conversation about succession.

Adeboye had stated in August 2018 that God had not revealed the identity of the person who would succeed him as the church’s General Overseer.

According to Tribune, the cleric made the disclosure during the church’s ministers’ conference at the Redemption Camp in Ogun State.

He addressed speculation over possible successors, including suggestions that he could hand over to Bishop David Oyedepo, Pastor Joe Olaiya or one of his spiritual sons.

“Some even said I would hand over to Bishop David Oyedepo, some said to Pastor Joe Olaiya because the two are very close to me and they do minister during our major events such as the conventions, Holy Ghost congresses and yet another quarter said one of my sons is the one succeeding me,” Adeboye said.

He explained that he was waiting for God to reveal the identity of his successor before making any announcement.

“So, let me say it loud and clear again that God has not told me who my successor is. What God had told me several years ago was that He would tell me who my successor would be at least five years before I would be called home, and as at this time, He had not told me anything as regards that, which means He is not ready to call me home in the next five years,” he added.

Oyedepo outlines succession procedure

The Presiding Bishop of Living Faith Church Worldwide, also known as Winners’ Chapel, Bishop David Oyedepo, publicly described a structured succession procedure for the ministry.

In a sermon reported by Daily Times Nigeria in June 2020, Oyedepo explained that the process was contained in The Mandate, a document he described as the church’s governing guide.

According to him, the presiding bishop is required to submit the names of three potential successors to the Council of Priests upon attaining the age of 70.

The nominees are subsequently examined by the church’s secretariat to determine whether they possess the required qualifications for the position.

Oyedepo explained that where none of the nominees meets the stipulated requirements, the list would be returned to the presiding bishop for another submission.

If the second set of nominees also fails to meet the requirements, the Council of Priests would take responsibility for the process, while the board would ratify the final decision.

He said the arrangement was designed to prevent uncertainty or confusion whenever a leader leaves office.

“It is a process, not that somebody dies and then we don’t know what to do,” Oyedepo said.

The bishop also cautioned against treating churches as personal or family property, warning that such an approach could undermine the purpose of the ministry.

Nigerians raise concerns

Beyond the comments by the religious leaders, the Fountain of Life controversy prompted some Nigerians to express concerns about the future leadership of churches established by prominent founders.

An X user, Adegbola John, described the crisis as a wake-up call for church founders and warned that similar challenges could emerge where succession arrangements were not clearly established.

John said he had repeatedly raised questions about what could happen at Deeper Life after Kumuyi’s departure.

He argued that many African political and religious leaders failed to plan adequately for leadership transitions because they were reluctant to relinquish power or publicly groom and endorse successors.

Drawing a comparison with the reluctance of some Africans to write wills before death, he said church founders often avoided succession conversations until circumstances made them unavoidable.

Another X user, Victor Abu, linked the Fountain of Life dispute to concerns about churches being treated as family businesses.

According to him, similar conflicts could emerge in other prominent Nigerian churches after their founders’ departure if leadership arrangements were not properly addressed.

Godswill Ikouwem, who directed his comments at Jimmy Odukoya, urged the pastor to allow the church’s leadership to be determined through spiritual guidance rather than family ties.

“Just because your dad, God bless his soul, was the founder doesn’t mean you must lead the church. Allow the Holy Spirit to take charge, and if the Holy Spirit is leading the church along a new leadership, just submit yourself to that leading. Don’t fight what God is doing in the now,” he wrote.

Another commentator, Oluwatosin Bukun-Joseph, expressed concern about the consequences of the dispute, warning that no church gathering could withstand the kind of turmoil he believed was developing within the congregation.

Fountain of Life crisis deepens

Meanwhile, the succession controversy at the Fountain of Life Church has continued to generate tension, with members expressing concerns over the future of the ministry amid the legal confrontation between Jimmy Odukoya and the Board of Trustees.

Saturday PUNCH gathered that the leadership disagreement, which dates back to 2023, had resulted in the departure of prominent figures. Pastor Kunle Areogun resigned his membership of the Board of Trustees, while Pastor Akin Osinbajo, a longstanding member, reportedly left the congregation.

The disagreement over the succession arrangement made by the late founder, Pastor Taiwo Odukoya, escalated after Jimmy’s remarriage and the introduction of his new wife, Motunrayo.

A longstanding member of the church, who spoke with Saturday PUNCH on condition of anonymity, said the disagreement had divided the congregation and placed the reputation of the church at risk.

“Those of us who have been members for years are not happy. We love Jimmy, but doesn’t he need to step away from that position? I think he does. Some of these issues were probably part of the reasons Pastor Akin Osinbajo left the church recently; he was also a longstanding member,” she said.

Another member, who previously served in an official capacity in the church, said the prolonged controversy had left many worshippers disappointed.

The member expressed concern that more people could eventually leave the congregation if the disagreement remained unresolved.

“In fact, right now, I’m considering changing church because the drama is becoming too much in TFOLC,” the source said.

However, another insider familiar with the church’s activities disclosed that some stakeholders had begun efforts to secure Areogun’s return to help stabilise the ministry.

“Things are really getting messy and they (stakeholders) are trying to see how they will get Areogun back to the church for stability,” the insider said.

The source also claimed that the Overseer of the Citadel Global Community Church, formerly known as The Latter Rain Assembly, Pastor Tunde Bakare, had intervened in the matter.

According to the insider, Bakare was likely to make a public statement on the dispute in the coming days.

Jimmy, trustees battle in court

The confrontation between Jimmy and the trustees took a legal turn after a Lagos State High Court issued an order restraining him from parading himself as the senior pastor and spiritual head of the church.

Justice O. O. Adewunmi-Oshin granted the order on Wednesday in a suit instituted by the Incorporated Trustees of the church.

The trustees approached the court on the grounds that Jimmy had ceased to occupy the position following the appointment of a successor by his late father in accordance with the church’s constitution.

However, an insider disclosed that Jimmy was making moves to challenge the order, insisting that the Lagos State High Court lacked jurisdiction over the matter because the church was registered under Part F of the Companies and Allied Matters Act, 2020.

“The order will not stand; Jimmy will file an appeal. The Lagos State High Court does not have jurisdiction over the matter. I’m sure the order will be vacated,” the source said.

Following the court order, reports circulated that the church premises had been sealed.

But Jimmy, in a Facebook post on Friday, dismissed the reports and urged members to disregard them, maintaining that the premises remained open.

Although the church’s thanksgiving service scheduled for Sunday was postponed because of the crisis, the embattled pastor encouraged members to attend the regular Sunday service.

Saturday PUNCH also learnt that the disagreement extended to control of the church’s social media accounts.

Sources disclosed that the Board of Trustees attempted to take over the accounts but encountered resistance from Jimmy’s aides, who allegedly refused to release the login details.

Succession video, Jimmy’s response

The dispute in the church escalated on Tuesday when the Board of Trustees announced that it had withdrawn its recognition of Jimmy as the church’s senior pastor.

The trustees cited a recorded video instruction by the late Taiwo Odukoya, in which he reportedly identified Pastor Kunle Areogun as his preferred successor.

The video, said to have been recorded in December 2021, reportedly showed the late founder expressing his preference for Areogun to lead the ministry in the event of his death or incapacity.

Jimmy was assigned administrative and youth church responsibilities under the arrangement described in the recording.

According to the trustees, their examination of the video and relevant provisions of the church’s constitution established that the late founder had exercised his authority to determine succession and communicated his intentions before his death.

Consequently, the board declared that Jimmy was no longer recognised as the church’s senior pastor with immediate effect.

The trustees subsequently appointed Pastor Rotimi Okpaise as the interim spiritual and administrative leader pending further consultations and the establishment of a substantive leadership structure.

However, Jimmy presented a different account of the circumstances surrounding the video during an emergency workers’ meeting held on Wednesday.

Saturday PUNCH gathered that the pastor acknowledged the existence of the recording but explained that it was made when his father was preparing to undergo surgery in the United States.

According to Church Times, Jimmy explained that the late founder had been uncertain about surviving the operation when the recording was made.

He also disclosed that Taiwo’s elder brother, John Odukoya, was aware of the video.

Jimmy further claimed that after the operation was successful, his father instructed him to delete the recording because it was no longer valid.

He admitted that retaining the video had been a grave mistake on his part.

Commenting on the disagreement with the trustees, Jimmy said the board, which he identified as comprising Pastors Areogun and Rotimi, had presented seven demands to him.

He disclosed that he accepted four of the demands but rejected the remaining three.

Jimmy also claimed that Areogun resigned his membership of the Board of Trustees after being asked to do so.

According to him, Areogun had previously declined an offer from the late Taiwo Odukoya to succeed him.

The embattled pastor maintained that he would not step down as chairman of the Board of Trustees.

Instead, he said he had asked the trustees, including his elder sister, to resign from the board.

A source who attended the emergency workers’ meeting told Saturday PUNCH that some church workers had demanded to know the identities of the trustees.

According to the source, several workers expressed support for Jimmy during the meeting, while resentment towards the trustees appeared to be growing among some members.

“At the meeting, the church members were cheering Jimmy up; they were supporting him. You could see a growing resentment against the board members. They asked him to name the BoTs, but he was reluctant. He eventually mentioned Gbenga Onabanjo and Areogun, though he said the latter had resigned,” the insider said.

Jimmy absent from pulpit

During a visit to the church’s premises in the Ilupeju area of Lagos for its Thursday Showers service, Saturday PUNCH observed a sizeable turnout of worshippers despite the ongoing leadership dispute.

However, Jimmy was not seen presiding over the service, although he shared a livestream link to the programme on his social media platforms.

Another pastor, identified as Dipo Adeda-Osinloye, conducted the service.

Addressing the congregation, Adeda-Osinloye urged members to remain steadfast in their faith and expressed confidence that the church would survive the challenges confronting it.

He also warned that attempts to destabilise the church would fail.

“Nobody can bring this church down, no weapon of the enemy formed against us shall prosper, and when they gather because they do not gather for the sake of the Lord, they will scatter,” the pastor added.

When Saturday PUNCH approached a member of the church’s staff to request an interview with Jimmy, the official confirmed that he had been present but left shortly after the service.

Asked why Jimmy did not preside over the programme, the official explained that Thursday Showers was primarily a praise and worship session, unlike the church’s regular Sunday service.

The official added that the pastor would ordinarily remain available for counselling and meetings with members after the programme.

Members split over crisis

Despite concerns expressed by some members, others maintained that the controversy would not destabilise the church.

One member, who spoke with Saturday PUNCH on condition of anonymity, dismissed suggestions that Jimmy had been removed as the senior pastor.

According to the worshipper, Jimmy continued to perform his responsibilities while the church remained operational.

“Our senior pastor has not been removed. He is performing his functions as he should, and the church is moving forward,” the member said.

The source also questioned the legitimacy of the statement announcing Jimmy’s removal.

“It was not the church that issued the purported statement removing Pastor Jimmy. What is happening is just a minor distraction. The church will come out stronger,” the member added.

Meanwhile, Jimmy’s wife, Motunrayo, had earlier posted a series of cryptic messages on her Instagram story, which Saturday PUNCH sighted.

“Stay grounded. Stay kind. But don’t shrink just to make someone else more comfortable,” one of the messages read.

Another stated, “When someone is insecure, your confidence can feel like a threat. Not because you’re showing off, but because your growth confronts what they are avoiding.”

She also wrote, “Your happiness can look like arrogance to someone who is unhappy. Your success can look like bragging to someone who feels behind.”

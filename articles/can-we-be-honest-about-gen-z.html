Can we be honest about Gen Z?

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A family friend (who is a university professor) contacted me recently to help out with the accommodation of one of his students who was said to be in town for ‘

He told me that she is about 21 years old and someone he is trying to help because “her family is poor.” It was not an easy favour for me to dispense (because she’s a total stranger whose way of life I don’t know), but my heart was becoming swayed because she was coming from someone that I have immense respect for.

Also, the fact that I am a mother made me keep imagining my own child in her shoes. I am a firm believer that the easiest way to ask for God’s grace over our own children is by being mindful of how we treat other people’s children.

So, I asked him to give the young lady my phone number and insisted that a phone conversation and a face-to-face meeting would have to happen first, as I believe that speaking with her and observing her for some minutes would enable me to make up my mind.

He did, and the young lady texted me on WhatsApp. I called her. From our conversation, I understood that she was actually done with the ‘clearance’ and just looking for somewhere to stay until the National Youth Service call-up.

Apparently, she does not find the house of the friend she was putting up with comfortable. I learned that it’s a family house and her friend’s parents are present.

Another thing I learned is that there’s a job she’s considering doing in the meantime. This aspect of the story was unknown to our mutual contact.

I was actually impressed by her job efforts until she got back to me later in the day to explain that meeting me the next day (Saturday), as scheduled, was no longer feasible. She postponed the meeting to Monday, even though I expected her to suggest Sunday as someone in dire need.

Monday came, and I didn’t hear from her all day until around 7 pm, when she contacted me via text to explain how busy she had been. She then asked if I was at home so she could be on her way.

I told her to revert to our mutual contact, as I had communicated my decision to him. The decision was that it was not convenient for me to take in someone whose duration of stay in my house was indefinite.

If she just needed accommodation for a week or two while in town, I could handle that. I also pointed out her mannerisms, which made it look like the meeting was dependent on her convenience, as if she was doing me a favour.

Well, if I expected “I am sorry. Thank you for the correction” or “That was not intentional,” it did not come. While communicating my observation to our mutual contact, I said to him, “Her body language is not some poor-girl behaviour but her-royal-highness.” He chuckled and then added, “Honestly, it’s a common Gen Z behaviour. We have lost standard courtesy and humility in them.”

This is actually the aspect of this Gen Z behavioural nightmare that I find more disturbing!

Are we now going to relax with a repulsive mannerism (from children who don’t know any better) as the new normal? How many of them even understand that ‘a Gen Z behaviour’ is rarely referred to in a positive light?

There is something painfully entitled, self-absorbed and not-humble about the Gen Z mindset. Even when your intervention is technically a lifeline to them, they still behave like they are doing you a favour!

An acquaintance who lives abroad shared his dating experience with me. According to him, he had just met the young lady, and their talks ‘flowed’ on the first day until the third day, when he asked for her pictures.

She explained that her ‘main phone,’ where her current pictures were saved, was with a phone repairer. He said that she harped on her ‘phone that has become difficult to repair’ for the next two days and then stopped picking up his calls. That was how that chapter was closed.

Sometimes, men who have honourable intentions for a relationship usually don’t have a principle of throwing money at the setting right away, especially those of them who have become ‘once bitten, twice shy.’

But knowing who he is, I can confidently say that the young lady just missed a man who could have effortlessly changed her life for good. He had actually begun to consider the fastest means of sending a phone down to her in Nigeria. And it was meant to be a surprise for her.

Rude impatience is another malady of Gen Z, and I shudder at their losses.

CJN swears in new A’Court Justices, urges judicial independence

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Chief Justice of Nigeria, Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, has warned 12 newly appointed Justices of the Court of Appeal against political, financial, social an

She also cautioned the justices against allowing public opinion, professional relationships or the desire for unanimous decisions to influence their judgments.

Kekere-Ekun gave the warning on Tuesday during the swearing-in ceremony of the 12 justices at the Supreme Court complex in Abuja.

Addressing the newly appointed appellate court justices, the CJN stressed that their elevation came with greater responsibilities, particularly the need to balance collective judicial deliberations with individual independence.

She said, “Your judicial independence must be guarded against political, financial, social and personal pressures, including those that may arise through professional familiarity or established relationships.”

The CJN also warned that public approval must not determine judicial decisions, just as criticism should not discourage judges from reaching decisions required by the law.

“My Lords, judicial office is increasingly exercised in an environment in which decisions are subjected to immediate public commentary. You must remain conscious of this reality without allowing it to influence your adjudicatory responsibilities.

“Public approval must not be the basis for you to depart from the law, just as criticism must not deter you from reaching a decision that your conscience and the law require,” she said.

She further urged the justices to demonstrate courage in expressing dissenting opinions where necessary, stressing that judicial independence must not be sacrificed for collegiality.

According to her, “You must decide according to the law and your conscience, without being unduly influenced by seniority, reputation, personal affinity or the desire for unanimity.

“Where, after careful consideration, your view differs from that of the majority, you must have the
courage to maintain and express it with clarity, restraint and respect.

“A well-reasoned dissent is a legitimate feature of appellate adjudication and may, in time, contribute to the development or reconsideration of the law.”

Kekere-Ekun explained that unlike their previous positions, where most exercised judicial authority as sole presiding judges, their new responsibilities would require them to sit in panels, deliberate collectively and arrive at decisions through consultations with other justices.

She urged them to approach such deliberations with intellectual openness, mutual respect and a willingness to reconsider their positions when presented with convincing arguments.

The CJN, however, warned that differences in judicial opinions must not degenerate into personal disagreements, adding that the strength of an appellate court depended on the quality of its reasoning rather than an artificial appearance of unanimity.

She reminded the justices that the Court of Appeal played a critical role in Nigeria’s judicial system, exercising appellate jurisdiction over civil, criminal, constitutional, electoral, commercial and labour disputes, among other matters.

Kekere-Ekun also cautioned them against treating appeals as opportunities to rehear cases merely because they might have reached different conclusions from the lower courts.

“An appeal is not an invitation to rehear a case simply because another judicial officer might have reached a different conclusion,” she said.

She urged them to carefully examine records of proceedings, identify properly raised complaints and determine whether the law justified interfering with decisions of lower courts.

The CJN further charged the new justices to embrace continuing judicial education, particularly in emerging areas such as digital commerce, data protection, intellectual property, financial regulation and artificial intelligence.

She noted that although technology could assist judicial research and the organisation of court processes, legal authorities must be verified and judicial decisions must remain products of independent judicial reasoning.

The 12 new justices are Yakubu Abdulhammeed Mohammed, Monisola Oluwatoyin Abodunde, Raphael Ajuwa, Elias Ojie Abua, Mbalamen Jennifer Ijohor, Sabiu Bala Shuaibu, James Kolawole Omotosho, Emeka Nwite, Dauda Njane Buba, Sanusi Kado, Enikuomehin Ademola and Julcit Veronica Dadom.

The justices were drawn from various courts, including the High Courts of Gombe, Bayelsa, Cross River, Benue, Kebbi, Taraba and Ondo states, the Federal High Court, the National Industrial Court of Nigeria and the Customary Courts of Appeal of Ekiti and Plateau states.

The PUNCH recalls that the National Judicial Council had recommended their appointment following its 111th meeting held on May 13, 2026, under the chairmanship of the CJN.

The council said the appointments were intended to fill vacancies arising from the elevation and retirement of judicial officers and strengthen the capacity of the courts for effective justice delivery.

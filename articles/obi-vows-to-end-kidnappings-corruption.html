Obi vows to end kidnappings, corruption

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, Peter Obi, has pledged to tackle kidnapping, corruption and unemployment, promising to generate 1

Obi, who made the pledge during a town hall meeting in Onitsha, Anambra State, on Friday, said his proposed administration would prioritise security, education, healthcare and electricity, while moving the country from a consumption-driven economy to one based on production.

He said Nigerians had suffered enough under the current administration, promising that his partnership with his running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso, would unite the country and restore public confidence in governance.

“Kwankwaso and I have formed a team and that is the team that will unite Nigeria. We are not going to have a Nigeria where people will come and kidnap our schoolchildren.

“We will fight corruption, we want to use public money for public good, and from day one, you will feel it and you will know that a new team is in charge.

“We will move Nigeria from consumption to production, and our people will get jobs. We want a country where if you are sick, you go to hospital and not church,” he said.

The former Anambra governor also promised to establish functional primary healthcare centres in every ward across the country and build 50,000 primary school blocks as part of his proposed investment in education and healthcare.

On electricity, Obi said Nigeria’s current power generation, transmission and distribution capacity of about 4,000 megawatts was inadequate, promising that his administration would generate 10,000 megawatts.

“Today, Nigeria only generates, transmits and distributes 4,000 megawatts, but Kwankwaso and I will generate 10,000 megawatts of power.

“Everything we say here, please take note of it because we will do it. We will not give you excuses because we know that the country is already bad, and we will not tell you we didn’t know it was this bad,” he said.

Obi urged Nigerians to turn out for the January 16, 2027 election and defend their votes, asking supporters to remain at polling units until votes were counted and transmitted.

He said, “Nigerians have suffered enough, so all of you are contestants in this election through us. So go out there on that day, take it as a day of sacrifice for Nigeria.

“Carry your food to the polling unit on that day, vote, stay there for counting and transmitting and make sure there is no rigging. That day is now declared a public holiday for vote defence.”

The NDC candidate also criticised President Bola Tinubu’s borrowing record, accusing him of failing to fulfil his promise not to borrow after removing the petrol subsidy.

He said his campaign was seeking to change what he described as the failures of the present administration, adding that Nigeria needed an energetic president committed to addressing the country’s challenges.

Earlier, Obi led a road show through major parts of Onitsha, attracting large crowds as he moved from his residence in Onitsha GRA to the First Niger Bridge Head, Ochanja Market, Main Market, Ose Okwodu Market and other locations.

The procession ended at the Main Bowl of the Archbishop C.J. Patterson Building, All Saints Cathedral, venue of the town hall meeting.

Before the engagement, Obi visited his alma mater, Christ the King College, Onitsha, where he interacted with students and inspected a teachers’ residential building he said he and others had supported.

He said the project was intended to provide teachers with comfortable accommodation and improve the conditions under which they worked.

Obi reiterated his commitment to education, saying it would receive substantial investment if he became president.

“Education to me remains the tool for development. It is one investment that if I have the opportunity to serve Nigeria, everybody will visibly and measurably see our investment in education,” he said.

Also speaking, the senator representing Anambra Central, Victor Umeh, described Obi as the best candidate for the presidency, expressing confidence that the NDC would secure overwhelming support in the South-East and attract votes from the North through Kwankwaso.

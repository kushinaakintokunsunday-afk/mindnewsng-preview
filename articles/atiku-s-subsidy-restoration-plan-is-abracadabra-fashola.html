Atiku’s subsidy restoration plan is ‘abracadabra’ — Fashola

By Vanguard Vanguard 6d ago 3 min read

…Demands figures, details on funding, crude oil supply

Former Lagos State Governor, Babatunde Fashola, has described former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar’s proposal to restore petrol subsidy as “abracadabra without proof”, challenging him to provide details of how he intends to implement the policy if elected president in 2027.

Atiku, the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential candidate, had in August said he would restore petrol subsidy if elected in the 2027 general elections.

Fashola, who served as Minister of Power under former President Muhammadu Buhari, stated this on Tuesday during an interview on Channels Television’s Politics Today.

He recalled that major presidential candidates in the 2023 general elections had supported the removal of petrol subsidy, questioning what had changed to warrant a reversal of that position.

“For credibility’s sake, all major candidates, at least as at the last election, promised to remove this fuel subsidy. So what has changed?” he asked.

Fashola said politicians advocating a different approach should explain the reasons for changing their positions and provide concrete details of how they intended to sustain the proposed arrangement.

He questioned the source of funding for the subsidy, warning that its restoration could reduce allocations to state governments and undermine their ability to meet financial obligations.

“I was in a government that was paying subsidy. And the states were not getting money. So we are going to go back to partial, near-zero returns, states borrowing money to pay salaries. Is that where you want us to go?” he asked.

He urged voters to consider the financial implications of the proposal and use their votes to prevent a return to the revenue challenges experienced by state governments during the subsidy regime.

Asked whether Atiku’s proposal was unrealistic, the former minister said he wanted detailed explanations and supporting figures before reaching a conclusion.

“With the sketchy pronouncements that come with it, I want to see rigour, I want to see detail; I want to see numbers,” he said.

Fashola also questioned Atiku’s argument that increased local refining would promote competition and reduce petrol prices, asking where the crude oil required to supply domestic refineries would come from.

He explained that Nigeria’s crude oil production involved joint ventures and production-sharing contracts with international oil companies, meaning the country did not own all the crude produced.

According to him, the assumption that Nigeria could simply supply local refineries with cheap crude overlooked the fact that crude oil was traded internationally at benchmark prices.

Fashola further questioned Nigeria’s capacity to supply sufficient crude to domestic refineries, noting that the country had yet to fully optimise its production quota.

He attributed the production challenges to legacy issues, insecurity and vandalism.

“If we have refineries working, what is our current oil production quota? Are we optimising it yet? No, because there are legacy issues; there are challenges with security, vandalism, and so on and so forth, so where is this magic crude oil going to come from?” he asked.

He said Atiku needed to provide evidence and demonstrate how his proposal would address the country’s crude oil supply constraints to avoid disappointing Nigerians.

“Let people choose. But without proof, it’s like abracadabra,” Fashola said.

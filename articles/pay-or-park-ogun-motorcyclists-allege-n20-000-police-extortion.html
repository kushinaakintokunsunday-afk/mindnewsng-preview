Pay or park: Ogun motorcyclists allege N20,000 police extortion

By Punch Punch 3d ago 8 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

After spending several hours battling traffic on their way from Lagos, some commuters arrive at the Ibafo junction only to discover that commercial motorcyclists, popularly known as okada riders, are no longer readily available to take them to their destinations.

While some are left stranded at the junction, others resort to trekking for several minutes before finding a means of transportation to their homes.

The situation, Saturday PUNCH gathered, is linked to alleged repeated arrests and extortion of commercial motorcyclists by police officers attached to the Ibafo Police Station over their late-night operations.

Some of the affected riders alleged that officers, particularly those operating in plain clothes, seize their motorcycles and demand between N20,000 and N30,000 before releasing them.

They claimed that at least 10 motorcyclists fall victim to the alleged practice every night, forcing many riders to abandon their operations after certain hours for fear of being arrested.

The development, according to the riders, has not only affected their daily earnings but also made transportation difficult for residents who return from work late at night.

‘We pay to retrieve seized bikes’

One of the affected commercial motorcyclists, Haruna Sanni, told Saturday PUNCH that he paid N25,000 to retrieve his motorcycle after it was allegedly seized by police officers for operating at night.

“I paid N25,000 last week after my bike was seized,” he said.

Another rider, Mahmud Kebbi, said he paid N20,000 after he was arrested around 11 pm in the previous month.

According to him, retrieving his motorcycle was a frustrating experience, as he had to make several visits to the police station before he could secure its release.

“I paid N20,000 in cash. I was caught around 11 pm last month. I had to go with our chairman before I could retrieve the bike. If I had gone alone, I would not have been able to get it.

“I went there early in the morning, but I was told that the person who arrested me was not on site. Again, I went there around 12 pm, and I was told he was yet to arrive. It was not until 5 pm that I was able to get the bike.

“It was not easy at all. In fact, I only had N10,000 with me. I had to borrow another N10,000 from someone to complete the money,” he said.

Kebbi appealed to the state government and police authorities to intervene, arguing that commercial motorcyclists provided an important transportation service to residents who returned from Lagos late in the evening.

He said, “The government has to intervene because we are always out at night so that people returning from Lagos after work can find motorcycles to take them home.”

Similarly, another rider, Salisu Ahmed, alleged that the situation was peculiar to Ibafo, claiming that commercial motorcyclists operated at night without similar harassment in neighbouring communities such as Mowe, Arepo and Magboro.

He said his motorcycle was once taken to the Ibafo Police Station, where he paid N20,000 to secure its release.

“They carried my bike to Ibafo Police Station. I had to go there early in the morning, around 5 am, to retrieve it, and I paid N20,000. It was my people who contributed the money. It was not easy at all.

“Since then, I have been running away from them. Even now that you see me, I will not get to the junction. Even when I carried you, I had watched you for some time before approaching you because those policemen do not wear uniforms. They appear like civilians. You will not know they are police officers,” Ahmed alleged.

Ahmed said he had been arrested several times, with the amounts allegedly demanded from him varying on different occasions.

“I have been caught multiple times. The first time they caught me, I paid N5,000. Subsequently, I paid N10,000, N15,000 and now N20,000. They said it was because I was working at night,” he added.

He further alleged that his brother, Kabiru Mohammed, was arrested last Friday and also paid N20,000 before his motorcycle was released.

Two days in police custody

The alleged enforcement has also affected residents who use motorcycles for personal transportation rather than commercial activities.

A bricklayer, Sunday Ojo, who lives in Halleluyah Quarters, Ibafo, told Saturday PUNCH that he was arrested by police officers wearing jackets while travelling to pick up his wife from her shop last Friday.

Ojo said he explained to the officers that he was not a commercial motorcyclist but was allegedly ignored.

He added that his motorcycle was seized, forcing him to make several trips to the police station before it was eventually released on Tuesday night.

“I was going to pick my wife up from her shop when they stopped me and seized my bike, saying I was working at night. I told them I was not a commercial motorcyclist, but all my pleas fell on deaf ears.

“I visited the police station multiple times before I could get the bike on Tuesday night. I offered the officer who arrested me N15,000, but he rejected it, saying it had to be N20,000,” he alleged.

Ojo said the incident disrupted his work for several days, leaving him without his motorcycle and affecting his ability to move around.

Lagos workers stranded

Beyond the financial implications for the riders, residents say the alleged police operations have created transportation challenges for people who depend on commercial motorcycles to complete their journeys home after work.

Many residents of Ibafo work in Lagos and return to the community late at night after enduring heavy traffic on the Lagos-Ibadan Expressway.

A civil servant in Lagos State, Taofeek Adedeji, said he had repeatedly struggled to find motorcycles at the Ibafo junction after returning from work.

According to him, the absence of riders on the roads at night has forced some commuters to trek considerable distances to their homes.

“There are many times when I will get stuck in traffic in Lagos and, on getting to Ibafo, I will find it difficult to get a bike because of their fear of being caught by the police.

“If you get to the junction past 10 pm, sometimes you will see people stranded, waiting for bikes, but the riders will not come because they are running away from the police. We will have to trek for about 10 minutes before finding one,” he said.

A petrol attendant at the Ibafo junction, who requested anonymity, also corroborated the account, saying he had made arrangements with a private motorcyclist to pick him up after work because of the difficulty in getting transportation late at night.

He said he had witnessed several motorcycles being seized by police officers on different occasions.

“Because of this issue, I have made efforts to get a private motorcyclist to pick me up after work because we close late.

“The police have arrested many motorcyclists in my presence on different occasions. I hope the government can intervene. The money they are demanding from these people is too much,” he said.

‘This has persisted for two years’

These allegations, according to the unit chairman of the Amalgamated Commercial Motorcycle, Tricycle Repairers, Owners and Riders Association of Nigeria, Sunday Idowu, are not new.

Idowu said the union had been dealing with complaints of motorcycle seizures and alleged extortion by police officers in the area for more than two years.

He disclosed that union officials frequently visited the police station to secure the release of motorcycles belonging to their members, adding that several complaints had been lodged with the authorities without any lasting solution.

Speaking with Saturday PUNCH, Idowu said the situation had become increasingly difficult for the union and its members.

“It is almost every day that we are visiting the police station to bail the bikes of our members. It is getting out of hand.

“Sometimes, they have to pay N30,000 before retrieving their bikes. It is only if you know someone in the station that your bike can be released with N20,000 or N15,000,” he alleged.

The union chairman’s account suggests that the alleged practice has become a recurring challenge for commercial motorcyclists operating in the community, with some riders depending on union officials and personal connections to retrieve their impounded motorcycles.

Police keep mum

Saturday PUNCH sought clarification from the state Police Public Relations Officer, Oluseyi Babaseyi, on whether the police were enforcing any directive prohibiting commercial motorcyclists from operating at night in the state.

A text message was sent to seek his reaction to the allegations of extortion and the reported demand for between N20,000 and N30,000 to release seized motorcycles, and whether there were legal provisions backing such enforcement.

However, Babaseyi had yet to respond to the enquiries as of the time of filing this report.

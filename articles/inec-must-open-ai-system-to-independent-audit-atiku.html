INEC must open AI system to independent audit –Atiku

• Asks NASS to summon commission’s leadership

From Ndubuisi Orji, Abuja

Presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), Atiku Abubakar, has demanded an independent audit of the artificial intelligence (AI) system the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) plans to deploy in processing polling-unit results for the 2027 general elections, warning that undisclosed algorithms could delay, reject or interfere with valid election results.

Atiku, who called for access to the system’s source code, training data and decision logs, also urged the National Assembly to summon INEC’s leadership and Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Directorate to publicly explain its design, safeguards and arrangements for independent scrutiny.

In a statement by the Director of Strategic Communication of the ADC Presidential Campaign Council, Phrank Shaibu, the former Vice President disclosed that he had written to INEC Chairman, Joash Amupitan, demanding algorithmic transparency, independent auditing and clear procedures for human oversight.

The demand followed INEC’s announcement that it would integrate AI and intelligent document processing into the process linking its Data Verification and Authentication System (DIVAS) with the INEC Result Viewing Portal (IReV).

Atiku insisted that the commission must disclose the criteria for flagging discrepancies, the identities of developers and third-party vendors, and the procedures for reviewing results rejected or delayed by the system.

“INEC cannot ask Nigerians to trust a system whose rules they are forbidden to see. If an algorithm can flag a polling unit result sheet, the public deserves to know what triggers that flag, who set the threshold, who can change it and what happens to the result afterward,” he said.

He warned that legitimate polling-unit results could be wrongly flagged because of poor image quality, unfamiliar handwriting or damaged result sheets, raising concerns about the possibility of arbitrary delays.

“The danger is plain. A valid result sheet photographed in poor light, bearing unfamiliar handwriting or produced on worn paper must not be held back because a machine fails to recognise it.

“Nor can INEC leave room for a system that treats errors in one area as harmless while treating similar errors elsewhere as grounds to delay results. Such decisions would affect real votes, cast by real people, whose rights cannot be made dependent on secret software settings,” he stated.

The ADC candidate demanded that independent cybersecurity experts, political party technology representatives and civil society technologists be granted access to the system for a comprehensive audit before the nationwide mock accreditation scheduled for November 28, 2026.

He also called for a dedicated monitoring dashboard through which political parties could track flagged results, transmission delays and blocked uploads in real time during elections.

According to him, INEC must publish procedures identifying who reviews flagged results, how decisions are documented, how political parties are notified and how errors can be challenged promptly.

Atiku further insisted that the commission clarify the legal status of physical polling-unit result sheets, particularly where an AI assessment conflicts with Form EC8A completed and signed at the polling unit.

“The polling unit result sheet, Form EC8A, records what happened where the people voted. INEC must publicly reaffirm the legal status of the physical form completed and signed at the polling unit, and explain how it will resolve any conflict between that document and an AI assessment.

“A machine’s verdict cannot be allowed to quietly displace the evidence witnessed by voters and party agents,” he said.

The former Vice President maintained that the credibility of the elections would depend partly on whether political parties and independent experts could scrutinise the technology before its deployment.

“INEC has an opportunity to answer these questions before mistrust hardens into a crisis. Open the system. Let independent experts test it. Publish the rules for correcting its mistakes. Guarantee that no valid vote will disappear behind a screen marked ‘flagged’,” he declared.

Atiku warned that Nigerians would not accept an electoral process in which results counted openly at polling units could subsequently be subjected to digital decisions whose rules remained hidden from voters and political parties.

“Nigerians will not accept an election in which they can watch the counting at their polling unit but cannot see what happens when their result enters INEC’s digital system,” he said.

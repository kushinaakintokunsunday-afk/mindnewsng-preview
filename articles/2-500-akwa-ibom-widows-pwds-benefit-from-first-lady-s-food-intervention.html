2,500 Akwa Ibom widows, PWDs benefit from First Lady’s food intervention

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 4 min read

No fewer than 2,500 widows, persons with disabilities (PWDs) and other vulnerable persons across Akwa Ibom State have received food assistance under the Renewed Hope Initiative (RHI), led by Nigeria’s First Lady, Oluremi Tinubu.

The intervention, which targets households struggling to meet basic nutritional needs, has now extended the initiative’s Food Outreach Scheme to 33 states and the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), since its launch in March 2024.

According to a statement by her media aide, Busola Kukoyi, the latest distribution took place at the Banquet Hall of the Government House, Uyo, on Monday, with the wife of the Vice President, Hajiya Nana Shettima, representing the First Lady at the event.

She handed over the food items to the Coordinator of the Office of the First Lady of Akwa Ibom State, Lady Helen Eno-Obareki, for distribution to the identified beneficiaries.

Speaking at the event, Mrs. Shettima disclosed that the programme had been sustained through the contributions of two partners who provide two truckloads of food items monthly, enabling the initiative to support two states every month.

She identified the Abdul Samad Rabiu Africa Initiative (ASR Africa) and an anonymous donor as the partners, explaining that the food consignment for Akwa Ibom was provided by ASR Africa.

Nigeria’s First Lady expressed appreciation to the donors for their sustained contributions, saying their generosity had enabled the initiative to extend humanitarian assistance to vulnerable communities across the country.

She said the RHI remained committed to complementing President Bola Tinubu’s Renewed Hope Agenda by translating its vision of inclusive development into practical interventions that improve lives.

Tinubu also acknowledged the support of Governor Umo Eno and commended Obareki for her commitment to advancing the objectives of the initiative in the state.

Governor Eno, represented by his deputy, Akon Eyakenyi, described the intervention as a demonstration of compassion and commitment to caring for the less privileged.

He noted that the initiative had consistently supported vulnerable groups, particularly women, older persons and people in need, through various humanitarian and empowerment programmes.

The governor recalled that the First Lady had, barely two weeks earlier, sent a delegation to the state to provide financial assistance to women.

“I recall barely two weeks ago, on November 5 precisely, the First Lady sent a delegation to empower our women with cash. This further demonstrates the RHI’s commitment to improving livelihoods,” he said.

Obareki, in her remarks, commended the First Lady for what she described as a sustained commitment to improving the welfare of vulnerable Nigerians.

She said the First Lady’s return to the state for the second time within a month underscored her dedication to the welfare of the people.

Obareki highlighted the initiative’s interventions in Akwa Ibom, particularly programmes focused on women’s empowerment and inclusion across the state’s 31 local government areas.

She added that the interventions were designed to ensure that assistance reached those who needed it most, making a tangible difference in vulnerable households.

Also speaking, the Managing Director of ASR Africa, Dr Ubon Udoh, assured the initiative of the organisation’s continued support and collaboration.

He said the organisation was ready to partner with the RHI on further interventions aimed at improving citizens’ welfare, strengthening social protection and providing meaningful assistance to disadvantaged groups.

In a separate interview, the Governor’s Special Adviser on Persons with Disabilities, Mrs Nnene Bassey, said the First Lady had consistently supported the disability community through financial assistance, food distribution and skills acquisition programmes.

She disclosed that 100 persons with disabilities had previously received N100,000 each, while another 100 beneficiaries received N200,000 each under the initiative.

According to her, the programme had also provided skills acquisition opportunities to promote financial independence, improve livelihoods and discourage street begging among persons with disabilities.

Bassey added that new beneficiaries were introduced annually to ensure that more members of the disability community benefited from the interventions.

The 2,500 beneficiaries were drawn from the state’s 31 local government areas, with Uyo accounting for the highest allocation of 500 beneficiaries.

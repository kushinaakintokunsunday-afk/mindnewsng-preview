Akwa Ibom urges students to embrace science, technology

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Akwa Ibom State Government has urged students to embrace science and technology and apply their knowledge to solve practical problems in their communities.

The call was made at the 2026 grand finale of the Igniting Minds through Science programme held on Tuesday in Uyo, where secondary school students showcased innovative solutions to everyday challenges.

The programme, organised by Vcare for Development Foundation, with support from Sterling Oil Exploration and Energy Production Company Limited, was implemented in partnership with the Akwa Ibom State Ministry of Education.

Speaking at the event, the Permanent Secretary, Akwa Ibom State Ministry of Education, Okon Effiong, expressed delight at the programme’s growth and expanding reach across the state.

He commended Sterling Oil and Vcare for Development Foundation, for encouraging students to develop an interest in science and, more importantly, apply their knowledge practically.

He said, “I want to sincerely thank Sterling Oil and Vcare for Development Foundation because you have encouraged our students to be interested in science subjects, not just to be interested but to take a step further by being practical.”

He reaffirmed the importance of the partnership and encouraged VCDF to further expand the Igniting Minds through Science programme across Akwa Ibom State.

‎‎Effiong said the exercise showcased innovative student projects addressing water distribution, renewable energy, flood monitoring and alternative heating systems.

‎‎”Since its introduction in 2024, IMS has provided secondary school students with opportunities to move beyond classroom theories and apply science, technology, engineering and mathematics knowledge to challenges within their communities.

‎‎”The 2026 edition reached 4,186 students across 141 secondary and technical schools in Akwa Ibom State”, he said.

‎‎Following STEM workshops and teacher orientation sessions, students worked in teams under the guidance of their coordinating science teachers to identify challenges in their communities, examine their possible causes and develop practical, technology-driven solutions with 14 selected schools, out of 75 funded projects, presented their innovations.

‎‎Government Technical College, Ewet, Uyo, emerged overall winner with Sharepump, an automatic water pump and power-switching prototype designed to regulate water supply and improve water distribution in homes and multi-storey buildings.

‎‎Secondary Grammar School, Ibeno, secured second place with its Footstep Energy Harvester, a prototype that converts the mechanical energy generated by footsteps into electrical energy, demonstrating the possibilities of alternative and renewable energy sources.

‎‎Afaha Inang Secondary School, Onna, came third with Community FloodGuard, a flood-monitoring and protection prototype designed to help communities detect potential flooding, strengthen early-warning systems and respond more effectively to flood risks.

‎‎On his part, Ubong Ukpo, Coordinating teacher of the winning team from Government Technical College, Ewet in Uyo LGA, said the journey involved challenges that required teamwork, collaboration and determination to overcome.

‎‎Similarly, Imaobong David of Adiahaobong Girls Secondary School, Ikot Imo, Nsit Ubium LGA, expressed appreciation for the opportunity to participate and showcase her school’s creativity.

‎The participants and relevant stakeholders hailed Sterling Oil, stressing that through its corporate social responsibility investments, Sterling Oil Group continues to strengthen education, build capacity and expand opportunities for communities across Nigeria, reinforcing the role of private-sector partnerships in advancing educational development and youth empowerment.

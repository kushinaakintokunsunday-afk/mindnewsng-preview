Obasanjo should take a back seat, allow Tinubu to govern — Fayose

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former Ekiti State Governor, Ayodele Fayose, has urged former President Olusegun Obasanjo to take a “back seat” and allow President Bola Tinubu to run his admin

Fayose also urged Nigerians to focus on critical issues affecting the nation’s economy rather than trivial matters on social media.

The former governor stated this during an interview on TV Continental on Monday.

Asked whether criticism from figures such as Obasanjo could influence Nigerians to vote against Tinubu, Fayose said that while everyone was entitled to support any candidate of their choice, successive administrations could adopt different approaches to governance.

Fayose said, “The moment you go away from contesting, you are no more on the ballot year after year. Another set of superstars will come up. Yes, they will remember you, but don’t tell me you have the followership of a man who is contesting.

“Obasanjo should count himself lucky. Because Obasanjo still has the privilege of living; that’s why you still hear about him. One day, people will ask, ‘Who is Obasanjo? I used to hear (about him).’ That is life.

“So let him speak as much as he can. He will be in the annals of history on the net. Obasanjo should not teach Tinubu. He has spent his time. Let Obasanjo allow Tinubu to do his own time.

“You can support anybody you want to support. It’s not a crime. You don’t have to support anybody the way you imagine they will be supported. Even if you hand over power to your own son, he will govern differently. He has paid his dues; he’s tried his best. Let him take a back seat.”

Fayose further clarified that he did not share the former president’s political views, adding that people should criticise governments in power responsibly.

“We don’t agree, and that’s not out of place. We don’t have to agree.

That’s why we have different political parties. That is why I am Ayo Fayose, and he is Olusegun Obasanjo.”

The former Ekiti governor also referred to the military invasion of Odi, a predominantly Ijaw community in Kolokuma/Opokuma Local Government Area of Bayelsa State.

According to an interview with an indigene of Odi, Mr Tari Bolou, a two-time commissioner in the state and council chairman during the 1999 massacre, the attack occurred amid conflicts in the oil-rich Niger Delta region over widespread agitation for control of oil resources and environmental protection.

There were reports that armed bandits had murdered 12 police officers near Odi. Unconfirmed reports also indicated that military personnel deployed to the community were ambushed near the town, further heightening tensions in the area. The soldiers broke through the ambush and exchanged gunfire with the armed bandits in the town, further escalating the tension.

In retaliation, the military, allegedly acting on Obasanjo’s orders, invaded the town and razed the community. During the invasion, many people were killed, while most of the houses in the community were set ablaze.

Fayose, in the interview, condemned the military’s actions, allegedly carried out on Obasanjo’s orders.

“Obasanjo, under him, went to level Odi. Odi is a community. The military levelled Odi at Obasanjo’s instruction.

“Do you want people to be behaving like that? A life is sacred. You can’t, because of a crime by a group of people, level the whole community.

“Tinubu is careful; the military is careful. Because the moment you get so mad and use power arbitrarily, the innocent will suffer. That is why when our Air Force team misses the target, the gravity is heavy,” Fayose lamented.

Fayose also said the Tinubu-led administration was better than that of his predecessor, the late Muhammadu Buhari.

He said his conviction was based on his experience serving as governor under the Buhari-led administration, describing those who disagreed with his assessment as people driven by hatred for Tinubu’s government.

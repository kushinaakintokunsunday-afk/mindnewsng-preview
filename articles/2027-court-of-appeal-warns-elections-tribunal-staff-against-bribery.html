2027: Court of Appeal warns elections tribunal staff against bribery

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The President of the Court of Appeal, Justice Monica Dongban-Mensem, has warned secretaries and registry staff of election petition tribunals against accepting

She warned that any staff found engaging in corrupt practices would be sanctioned, stressing that the assignment was a call to national service and not an opportunity for personal enrichment.

Dongban-Mensem gave the warning on Monday at the opening of a two-day capacity-building workshop on the theme, “Modern Registry Practices for Effective Election Petition Tribunal Administration”, in Abuja.

The Court of Appeal President said the exercise was designed to prepare tribunal staff for the administrative demands of handling electoral disputes.

She described the registry as the engine room of every tribunal, noting that its efficiency, integrity and transparency were critical to the timely administration of electoral justice.

The PUNCH reports that the presidential and National Assembly elections would hold on January 16, 2027, while the governorship and state Houses of Assembly elections were scheduled for February 6, 2027.

The Chief Justice of Nigeria, Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, inaugurated 375 judicial officers from different jurisdictions across the country last week to serve on election petition tribunals ahead of the 2027 general elections.

According to the PCA, the responsibilities of registry staff include receiving and filing petitions, serving processes on parties, issuing hearing notices, preparing records of proceedings and orders, preserving tribunal documents and compiling records, adding that the registry’s performance directly affects the efficiency of tribunals and public confidence in the judicial process.

Citing Sections 285(1) and (2) of the 1999 Constitution, Dongban-Mensem said the President of the Court of Appeal was empowered to constitute election petition tribunals in every state of the federation and the Federal Capital Territory, among others.

Dongban-Mensem charged the tribunal secretaries to familiarise themselves with the Electoral Act 2026 and the Election Petition Tribunal Proceedings Practice Direction 2026, warning that ignorance of the relevant provisions could expose them to manipulation and costly administrative errors.

“As tribunal secretaries, as prescribed by the Electoral Act 2026 and the Election Petition Tribunal Proceedings Practice Direction 2026, you must know the Practice Directions and have them on your fingertips. If you do not know them, you will be intimidated, you will be misled, and you will permit serious errors, and that will fetch serious consequences.

“There is no room for that now. You are given a good chance to equip yourself now, and we are giving you materials, so there will be no excuses. And we shall be constantly monitoring every aspect of your conduct,” she added.

Addressing the integrity of tribunal staff, the PCA warned against allowing financial inducements to influence their conduct, saying their activities would be subjected to intense public scrutiny.

“Let me be clear: you must discharge your duties with utmost good faith, and not be swayed by pecuniary inducements. Your conduct, and indeed, that of the tribunal, will be under intense public scrutiny.

“You must not allow yourselves to be used as instruments to truncate the judicial process. You must live above board and protect the reputation of the judiciary,” she said.

Describing the assignment as an opportunity to serve the country, she said the court would not hesitate to sanction erring officials.

“This assignment is not an opportunity for self-enrichment, but a call to national service, which is also an opportunity to build a good name. It is said that a good name is better than silver and gold. We will not hesitate to sanction any staff found wanting,” she added.

She further urged the secretaries to ensure prompt service of processes and maintain regular communication with the Election Petition Tribunal Unit at the Court of Appeal headquarters.

She said the presiding justice of each tribunal was the immediate supervisor of its administrative staff and should be approached whenever they encountered difficulties.

The PCA described the participants’ selection as a demonstration of the confidence reposed in them by the court, urging them not to betray the trust.

Also speaking, the Chief Registrar of the Court of Appeal, Umar Bangari, said the integrity of Nigeria’s democratic process depended on the efficient, transparent and timely resolution of electoral disputes.

He described the workshop as an administrative continuation of the training, designed to equip tribunal staff with the technical knowledge required for the assignment, among others.

Earlier, the Acting Deputy Chief Registrar of the Election Petition Tribunal, Maitumbi Altine, disclosed that 655 staff and chief registrars were attending the workshop.

She said 223 of the participants had previously served in election petition tribunals, while 432 were first-timers.

Altine also disclosed that the Court of Appeal was working to update its Case Management System to “forestall the incidences of conflicting judgments which the tribunals had experienced in time past.”

She added that the court would upload judgments delivered by election petition tribunals since 1999 into the system to serve as judicial precedents on legal issues that might arise during the proceedings.

The Acting Deputy Chief Registrar also warned participants against granting interviews without express authorisation, cautioning them against any activity capable of undermining the neutrality of the tribunals and the Court of Appeal.

Australian music icon Molly Meldrum passes away at 83

Australian music icon Molly Meldrum has died at the age of 83 following a prolonged illness.

The veteran music promoter and broadcaster, whose real name was Ian Alexander Meldrum, died peacefully at a hospital in Melbourne in the early hours of Monday, according to a statement released by his family.

Meldrum was widely recognised for his major contributions to the growth of Australia’s music industry over several decades.

He played an important role in promoting local music talents, including INXS and Kylie Minogue, while also helping to bring international music stars such as ABBA to Australian audiences.

Affectionately known as ‘the Guru’, he became a household name through his energetic personality, distinctive Stetson hat and remarkable contribution to music broadcasting.

In a statement announcing his death, his family described him as the heartbeat, champion and spirit of Australian music.

“Affectionately dubbed ‘the Guru’, Molly was the heartbeat, the champion and the spirit of Australian music,” the statement read.

Throughout his illustrious career, Meldrum interviewed several international celebrities, including King Charles and British music icon Elton John.

John once described him as the “single most important influence in Australian pop music”, recognising his enormous contribution to the country’s entertainment industry.

Meldrum rose to national prominence as the host of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation’s music television programme Countdown, where he helped introduce audiences to emerging Australian artists and international music stars.

He later featured on the popular variety programme Hey Hey, It’s Saturday, further establishing himself as one of Australia’s most recognisable television personalities.

One of his most memorable moments came during a 1977 interview with King Charles, then Prince of Wales.

Clearly nervous during the encounter, Meldrum stumbled over his words before famously admitting: “Your Royal Highness… Oh, oh, I’m sweating like a pig”.

Reflecting on the interview during Countdown’s 40th anniversary special, King Charles joked: “There is an old showbusiness saying which warns never to work with animals or children. But nobody prepared me for Molly Meldrum”.

Born in Victoria in 1943, Meldrum developed a passion for music from an early age and went on to become one of the most influential figures in Australian popular culture.

His life was marked by health challenges after he suffered an acquired brain injury following a fall from a ladder in 2011.

In recognition of his contribution to the music industry, Meldrum made history in 2014 as the first person who was not a recording artist to be inducted into the Australian Recording Industry Association (ARIA) Hall of Fame.

Australian Arts Minister Tony Burke also paid tribute to the late broadcaster, describing his death as a significant loss to the country.

“One of his greatest legacies will be the generations of Australians who grew up with his unmistakable enthusiasm and love of music. A true champion of Australian talent, you will be missed, but never forgotten,” Burke said.

Meldrum leaves behind a lasting legacy as a broadcaster, music promoter and passionate supporter of Australian talent, whose influence helped shape the country’s music scene for generations.

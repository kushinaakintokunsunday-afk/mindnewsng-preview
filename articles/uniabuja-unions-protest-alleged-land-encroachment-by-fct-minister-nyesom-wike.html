UniAbuja unions protest alleged land encroachment by FCT Minister Nyesom Wike

​Members of the Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU) at the University of Abuja (UniAbuja) are currently protesting the alleged “encroachment” on the institution’s land by Nyesom Wike, Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT).

​The union was joined by members of the Senior Staff Association of Nigerian Universities (SSANU), the Non-Academic Staff Union of Educational and Associated Institutions (NASU), the Students’ Union Government (SUG), and the university’s alumni association.
​Speaking during the march in Abuja on Monday, Sylvanus Ugoh, chairman of the union, accused Wike of disregarding the rule of law regarding the alleged takeover of land allocated to the university.

​Ugoh stated that the land was allocated to the institution upon its establishment in 1988, and he accused the minister of transferring plots to private individuals.

​Describing the minister’s actions as a breach of the rule of law, Ugoh implored President Bola Tinubu to intervene and direct Wike to reverse course.

​“The land has been allocated to the University of Abuja since 1988, when it was founded. There have been several FCT ministers since then, and none of them has done what he is doing,” he said.

​“A university is a public good and serves the public interest. Nowhere in the world do you sacrifice the public interest for individual gain.

​“We say no to the honourable minister taking land that belongs to the university.”
​The ASUU chairman warned that the dispute could undermine the federal government’s commitment to preventing industrial action in universities under the current administration.

​“We reject this, and we call on His Excellency, President Bola Tinubu, to call the honourable minister to order so that he reverses his decision,” he added.

​“This administration has consistently reiterated that ASUU and other university unions will not go on strike, yet the FCT minister’s actions are capable of undermining that peace.

​“The unions in this university will not fold their hands and watch land meant for unborn generations be taken away.

​“We cannot allow property meant for future generations to be handed over to private individuals. University development benefits everyone.”

​Nurudeen Yusuf, chairman of the UniAbuja branch of SSANU, stated that the unions would resist any attempt by the government to seize the university’s land.

​Yusuf emphasized that adequate land is a critical requirement for the growth and development of any higher institution.
​“At the University of Abuja, we have a Department of Nuclear Engineering, which requires strict buffer zones to safely conduct research that is why those lands were provided. We also have a Department of Aeronautical Engineering, which requires vast space for its operations,” he added.

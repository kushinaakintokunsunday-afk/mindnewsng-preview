‘It’s too late to make amends’: Baba-Ahmed speaks on Tinubu’s 2027 chances

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

Former presidential adviser Hakeem Baba-Ahmed has expressed doubts about President Bola Tinubu’s chances of recovering lost political support ahead of the 2027

Former presidential adviser Hakeem Baba-Ahmed has expressed doubts about President Bola Tinubu’s chances of recovering lost political support ahead of the 2027 general election, saying the administration may have waited too long to address Nigerians’ concerns.

Baba-Ahmed argued that the level of hardship, public dissatisfaction and anger across the country could make it difficult for Tinubu to secure another term if the 2027 election is free, fair and credible.

He made the remarks during an interview on Truth TV News, where he questioned the promises by some governors to deliver millions of votes for the president.

Reacting to reports of governors promising Tinubu between 1.5 million and 2.5 million votes, Baba-Ahmed said such assurances should not be accepted without scrutiny.

“On what basis would you deliver, would you promise 2 million votes? On what basis?” he asked.

Describing the promises as deceptive, he said governors should not be trusted simply because they occupy political offices, adding that some of them were also facing public resentment.

“I think President Tinubu, even at his current state, must know that you don’t trust governors. He was a governor, too, for some time,” Baba-Ahmed said.

He warned that relying on such promises could create complacency within the administration and prevent the president from recognising the level of dissatisfaction among Nigerians.

“If indeed they tell him that kind of thing and he believes them, then I think he deserves to lose even more,” he said.

Baba-Ahmed also criticised governors for what he described as their late response to the worsening security situation, particularly in northern Nigeria.

He questioned why governors and ministers from the region had not approached Tinubu earlier to demand urgent action against insecurity.

“Why couldn’t northern governors go to the president two years ago? All these ministers, their governors, they are defecting. And say, look, first discuss it,” he said.

He argued that political leaders should have collectively presented the security situation to the president as an emergency requiring immediate intervention.

“The North is being virtually invaded by crime. Killers, bandits, kidnappers, religious extremists. A whole army of all sorts of people. They’re taking on the lives of our people,” Baba-Ahmed said.

He added that criminal groups were taking over territories and keeping some villages and communities under their control for extended periods.

According to him, the government needed to act decisively before the situation further undermined its chances in the next election.

“We need to do something dramatic. We need to do something. Otherwise, if we leave it late, we’ll have no ground to cover to win the election,” he said.

The former presidential adviser also recalled an earlier meeting between religious leaders, including Catholic cardinals, and Tinubu, during which they reportedly raised concerns about the difficult living conditions in the country.

He said the president had previously dismissed suggestions that Nigerians were experiencing severe hardship, but members of the administration had since acknowledged the situation.

Baba-Ahmed referred to comments by the Chief of Staff to the President during the launch of the administration’s manifesto, saying the official had acknowledged that anyone denying the country’s difficulties was not being truthful.

He maintained that the government had a limited opportunity to address public grievances and restore confidence.

“There is a time to make amends. And there’s a time when it’s too late,” he said.

Asked whether it was already too late for Tinubu, Baba-Ahmed pointed to the level of public dissatisfaction, economic hardship and insecurity as major challenges facing the president ahead of 2027.

He said: “If this election in 2027, if it is free and fair and credible, there is no way with the level of disenchantment and suffering and anger that Nigerians bear towards this administration will not come back.”

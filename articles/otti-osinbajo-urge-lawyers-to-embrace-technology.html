Otti, Osinbajo urge lawyers to embrace technology

By Punch Punch 18 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Abia State Governor, Alex Otti, has urged legal practitioners in the country to embrace modern technology and continuously update their knowledge to remain rele

Otti gave the charge at the International Conference Centre, Umuahia, while speaking at the Annual Conference of the Nigerian Bar Association Section on Public Interest and Development Law (NBA-SPIDEL).

The governor, who described technology as an emerging reality that legal practitioners must prepare for, said the state government was already investing in modern judicial infrastructure to support efficient justice delivery.

“Technology is real. Sometimes we pretend, but the point you (Prof. Yemi Osinbajo) raised is very, very critical. It does not call for us to be afraid but for us to start thinking ahead of time,” Gov. Otti stated.

Otti disclosed that his administration would, in a few days, commission 10 newly constructed courts across the state, explaining that the facilities were designed to meet the demands of modern-day judicial administration.

According to the governor, the court facilities have been equipped with renewable energy systems, digital infrastructure, broadband connectivity and other technological facilities to improve the efficiency of judicial operations.

“Renewable energy, digitalised systems and all the things that you require will be available in the court. We have also been able to digitalise court records. So, there will be no need to carry files.

“All the court halls have Wi-Fi. So, the challenge is for us to now catch up with the technology that we are installing. And they all have multiple broadband technologies, not one, just in case it fails. So, we are thinking of the future,”  Otti added.

The governor further announced plans to flag off the construction of new Courts with 1,000-capacity auditoriums in Aba and Umuahia, assuring that the facilities would have independent renewable energy sources.

Otti also urged lawyers to look beyond financial gains and embrace their responsibility to provide legal assistance to indigent citizens, vulnerable persons and members of society who cannot afford professional legal fees.

He said the impact of the NBA-SPIDEL conference should ultimately be measured by how its deliberations translate into improved access to justice and protection of the rights of ordinary citizens.

“Due to the fact that you are lawyers, we need to go the extra mile of asking ourselves, beyond all the things that have been said, how can we be of service to people who cannot pay? How can we be of service to the vulnerable? How can we help people who, on their own, cannot afford to help themselves?” he added.

The governor noted that while infrastructure such as roads, schools, hospitals, electric buses and environmental sanitation represented the visible aspects of his governance, the rule of law, justice and protection of citizens’ rights are equally important though less visible components.

Otti illustrated the importance of public-interest legal practice with instances of alleged unlawful and prolonged detention, revealing that shortly after assuming office, his administration discovered that some people were being held in correctional facilities despite having no sufficient reason to remain there, noting that he directed the state Attorney-General to work with the Chief Judge to ensure that nobody was kept in a correctional facility without lawful justification.

The governor emphasised that the NBA-SPIDEL conference should not merely be an avenue for professional discussions but should inspire lawyers to make meaningful contributions to society.

He commended the organisers of the NBA-SPIDEL Annual Conference for choosing Abia State as the host, expressing appreciation for the opportunity to welcome legal practitioners and other stakeholders to Umuahia.

Earlier, the keynote speaker and former Vice President of Nigeria, Prof. Yemi Osinbajo (SAN) called on legal practitioners to move beyond the traditional interpretation and application of the law to embrace their responsibility as social engineers and agents of positive change, committed to advancing justice, strengthening democracy and promoting national development.

Prof. Osinbajo emphasised the importance of people-centred public interest advocacy that prioritises citizens’ welfare, access to justice, job creation, healthcare, education and the protection of vulnerable groups.

He noted that the true measure of democracy lies not merely in the conduct of elections or formulation of government policies, but in its capacity to improve the living conditions of ordinary citizens, warning that poverty, corruption, unemployment and declining public confidence in democratic institutions remain significant threats to democratic growth.

He further noted that democracy must be judged on how people feel and access justice, insisting that “a just society protects everyone.”

Prof. Osinbajo challenged lawyers to embrace continuous learning and develop competencies in technology, negotiation, conflict resolution, institutional management and critical judgment, while ensuring the proper verification of AI-generated information.

In her opening address, the President of the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA), Oyinkansola Badejo-Okusanya, SAN, called on legal practitioners to uphold the principles of justice, accountability and public interest by ensuring that their professional responsibilities translate into meaningful improvements in the lives of citizens.

She identified persons held in detention without legal representation, traders unable to afford litigation and children treated as adults in detention facilities as examples of people whose circumstances should inform discussions on justice and development.

She explained that justice should guarantee effective remedies for those who suffer wrongs, pointing out that democracy should provide citizens with meaningful voices in decisions affecting their lives, while development should improve their opportunities and living conditions.

She condemned political violence across the country, calling for credible investigations and prosecution of those responsible for attacks on citizens participating in lawful political activities. The NBA President thanked  Otti for sponsoring the 2026 NBA-SPIDEL Conference.

The event featured a panel of discussants led by Justice Obisike Orji, with dignitaries like Prof. Pat Utomi, Dr. Sam Amadi, among others participating, while the Chair of the NBA-SPIDEL Conference, Prof. Uju Agomuo, Chief Okey Amaechi, among others, participated in the presentation of plaques to appreciate the contributions of some individuals to the success of the event.

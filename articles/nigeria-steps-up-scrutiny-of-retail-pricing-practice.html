Nigeria steps up scrutiny of retail pricing practice

By Punch Punch 18 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Retail businesses in Nigeria are facing increased regulatory scrutiny over pricing practices, product information and competition as the Federal Competition and

Speaking at the Africa Retail Congress held at Lagos Business School on Saturday, the FCCPC’s Zonal Coordinator, Olubunmi Otti, who represented the Executive Vice-Chairman of the commission, said consumers were entitled to fair prices, quality products, accurate information and effective channels for resolving complaints.

The commission warned that businesses engaging in price fixing, price gouging and other anti-competitive practices risk sanctions under the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018, which empowers it to prosecute offenders, impose fines, seal businesses and withdraw products from the market.

The warning comes as Nigeria’s retail industry expands across fast-moving consumer goods, pharmaceuticals, food and other merchandise, raising concerns about the need to balance business growth with consumer protection and fair competition.

She said the commission’s responsibility extended across formal retail outlets, informal markets and digital platforms, stressing that the growth of the sector must not undermine consumer rights.

“For us, scale must never come at the expense of fairness,” Otti said.

She identified transparent pricing, the absence of hidden charges, quality goods and services, and ethical business practices as essential elements of consumer trust.

Otti disclosed that the commission had sealed about three businesses in Lagos this year as part of its enforcement activities, although she said the regulator preferred voluntary compliance through consumer and business education.

“We want voluntary compliance by the business. So, consumer and business education is critical to our mandate,” she said.

She explained that the commission was working to increase awareness of consumer rights and responsibilities while educating businesses about the implications of violating the law.

The FCCPC also highlighted its role in reviewing mergers and acquisitions to prevent transactions that could create market dominance and restrict competition.

According to Otti, the commission assesses proposed mergers based on whether they promote innovation, expand the variety of goods and services available to consumers or are designed to stifle competition and prevent smaller businesses from thriving.

She said the regulator would not approve transactions intended to undermine competition, adding that it had mechanisms to distinguish business combinations that benefit consumers from those that concentrate market power.

The commission also said it was collaborating with sector-specific regulators to address consumer complaints and improve accountability across industries.

Otti cited recent engagements with electricity distribution companies following complaints from consumers, saying the commission had brought relevant operators and regulators together to resolve disputes.

She urged consumers to report grievances through its complaint resolution channels, adding that consumer protection required cooperation among regulators, businesses, the media and civil society organisations.

Meanwhile, industry discussions at the congress highlighted the importance of business strategy and technology in strengthening the retail sector.

The Alternative Academic Director of the Africa Retail Academy at Lagos Business School, Chidi Okoro, said retail businesses needed viable business models and the capacity to execute their strategies before pursuing expansion.

He cited the collapse of several retail companies in Kenya as an example of how businesses could struggle when their models were not grounded in market realities or supported by adequate execution capabilities.

Okoro, who presented a book titled Strategy to Execution, said Nigeria’s retail industry was expanding across several categories, making strategic planning and operational capacity increasingly important to sustainable growth.

He said having a strategy was not sufficient without the ability to implement it, urging businesses to consider their capabilities before seeking financing or expanding their operations.

The congress also examined the role of digital infrastructure in retail operations, with MTN Nigeria presenting broadband solutions designed to support business connectivity.

The company’s Manager of Broadband Regional Sales, Fixed Broadband, Anastasia Ehidiamhen, said reliable internet access could help businesses monitor transactions, maintain records and support multiple users.

She said MTN’s fixed broadband plans ranged from 50 megabits per second to one gigabit per second, with the entry-level 50Mbps package priced at N30,000 for 30 days and the 100Mbps plan costing N45,000.

The Chief Executive Officer of SLOT Systems Limited, Nnamdi Ezeigbo, also highlighted the growing importance of after-sales services in the smartphone retail market.

He said the company had introduced its Slot Elite Card, an annual N300,000 membership programme for eligible customers with smartphones valued at N1.5m and above.

The programme offers services including screen replacement, repairs, priority attention, trade-in opportunities and free home delivery, reflecting efforts by retailers to improve customer experience beyond the initial sale of devices.

The Africa Retail Congress was themed ‘Leading Africa’s Retail Strategy: People, Process, Planning and Resources’.

World Children’s Day: UNICEF urges greater investment in children

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

As part of activities to commemorate World Children’s Day, the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) has called for the inclusion of children’s voices in poli

The Chief of UNICEF’s Bauchi Field Office, Dr Nuzhat Rafique, made the call on Saturday during the 2026 World Children’s Day celebration held at the New Banquet Hall of the Government House, Bauchi.

Represented by the office’s Education Specialist, Abdulrahman Ado, Rafique said children should be recognised as important members of society whose opinions and aspirations must influence decisions affecting their lives.

She said, “This year’s theme, ‘Choose a Future for Children,’ is both a call to action and a reminder that the future of our children depends largely on the choices we make today.

“World Children’s Day is more than just a celebration. It is an opportunity to reflect on our responsibilities towards children and to remind ourselves that children are not merely the leaders of tomorrow; they are important members of society today.

“Their voices matter, their opinions deserve to be heard, and their dreams and aspirations should influence the decisions that affect their lives.”

The UNICEF chief stressed the need to prioritise children’s development from pregnancy through the first 1,000 days of life, noting that adequate healthcare, nutrition and protection were essential for their growth.

She also identified poverty, insecurity, gender inequality, disability and geographical location as some of the barriers preventing children from accessing quality education.

“Every child deserves access to quality education, regardless of their background or circumstances,” she added.

Rafique further urged the government and other stakeholders to ensure that children were given opportunities to express their concerns, share their experiences and communicate their expectations.

“After all, decisions about children should not be made without children,” she said.

She explained that choosing a better future for children required deliberate investments in their health, nutrition, education, safety and protection.

“It means developing policies that put children first, allocating resources to essential services, strengthening our communities and ensuring that no child is left behind,” she said.

Rafique disclosed that the 2026 celebration also marked the commencement of activities towards UNICEF’s 80th anniversary, which would be commemorated on December 11.

She recalled that the organisation was established in 1946 with a commitment to protecting children irrespective of their nationality, background or circumstances.

According to her, children continue to face challenges associated with poverty, conflict, climate change, inequality and limited access to essential services, requiring renewed commitment and collective action.

She called on parents, teachers, communities, the private sector, government and development partners to work together to protect children’s rights and improve their living conditions.

Addressing the children, Rafique urged them to speak up, ask questions and express their expectations, assuring them that their voices and contributions mattered.

Also speaking, the Bauchi State Commissioner for Education, Abdulkadir Ibrahim, emphasised the importance of involving children in policy formulation, noting that they needed to understand decisions and policies affecting them.

He said the ministry was making efforts to provide a conducive learning environment in public schools in line with international best practices.

The commissioner commended UNICEF and other development partners for their continued interventions in the education sector, saying their contributions had helped improve basic education in the state.

PUNCH reports that World Children’s Day is celebrated annually on November 20.

It is observed worldwide to promote children’s rights, welfare, education, protection and participation in decisions affecting their lives.

The day was established by the United Nations and marks the adoption of the Declaration of the Rights of the Child in 1959 and the Convention on the Rights of the Child in 1989.

This year’s theme is “Choose a Future for Children”.

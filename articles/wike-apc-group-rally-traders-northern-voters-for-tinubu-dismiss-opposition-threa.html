Wike, APC group rally traders, northern voters for Tinubu, dismiss opposition threat

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 9 min read

From Juliana Taiwo-Obalonye, Abuja and Noah Ebije, Kaduna

Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Nyesom Wike, has directed market associations across Abuja to nominate representatives for President Bola Tinubu’s 2027 re-election campaign, as a Kaduna-based All Progressives Congress (APC) support group declared that the opposition lacked the political strength to unseat the President.

Wike, who gave the directive at a town hall meeting with traders and market leaders in Abuja yesterday, warned that verbal endorsements of Tinubu would amount to little unless translated into votes, while the Tinubu Uba Sani Consolidation Front (TUSCOF) dismissed the opposition’s chances of defeating the President and Kaduna State Governor Uba Sani despite the country’s economic and security challenges.

The minister urged each market association to nominate two representatives to participate in the FCT presidential campaign structure, promising support for grassroots mobilisation and regular engagement with traders.

His directive followed endorsements of Tinubu, Vice-President Kashim Shettima and other candidates backed by the administration, including former FCT senator Philip Tanimu Aduda, by market leaders who pledged to mobilise voters through trade unions, cooperatives, religious groups and community associations.

“So for me, if you agree with me, you know what I want. What you want, you have to demonstrate it. You know me, I’ve been in this game for years. When you come and tell me, ‘We are for you, we are for you,’ no problem. You must show it. And I will know,” Wike told the traders.

In Kaduna, TUSCOF Chairman Mohammed Sada Jalal, speaking ahead of the inauguration of the group’s state campaign committee, argued that internal disputes and political uncertainty had weakened opposition parties, leaving them poorly positioned to challenge the APC in 2027.

Although he acknowledged that insecurity and economic hardship could cost the ruling party some votes, Jalal maintained that the challenges would not prevent Tinubu and Uba Sani from winning their respective elections.

Wike, meanwhile, used the Abuja engagement to defend Tinubu’s removal of petrol subsidy, arguing that the policy had prevented an economic collapse and improved the Federal Government’s capacity to fund infrastructure, pay outstanding obligations and increase allocations to other tiers of government.

He said successive administrations had acknowledged that the subsidy regime was unsustainable but lacked the political courage to end it, leaving the country with mounting financial obligations.

“I want to appeal to all of us to keep this at the back of our minds: we have grown too used to living a fake life. Yes, it is true. We like to live a fake life. This country was nearly gone. Nearly gone,” he said.

According to him, Tinubu took the difficult decision because the country could no longer sustain paying trillions of naira in fuel subsidies while struggling to meet salary and pension obligations and fund critical services.

“For the President to change what had been happening, he had to lay a solid foundation first, and a foundation needs time to grow.”

Wike urged Nigerians to reject promises by opposition politicians to reverse the policy, insisting that the benefits of the reforms would become more evident if they were allowed to mature.

“If we allow these policies that the government has put in place to mature, what is going to happen in the next four years will be good. Forget about people coming to tell you stories,” he said.

The minister cited increased revenue allocations to states and local governments, the settlement of pension arrears and what he described as the absence of strikes by the Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU) for more than three and a half years as evidence that the reforms were producing results.

He also cited the payment of more than N80 billion in outstanding gratuities owed to former Nigeria Airways workers as one of the benefits of improved government revenue.

Wike credited Tinubu with providing funds for major infrastructure projects in the FCT, including the Apo-Karshi road, the reconstructed Saburi road corridor and the Bwari-Kubwa road, which he said was expected to be completed before the end of the year.

“All these roads you are talking about, all these things you are talking about in the FCT. Without the President, how will it be? Who is releasing the money?” he asked.

He said road construction and rehabilitation across the city centre and satellite towns were intended to improve commercial activities, ease transportation difficulties and reduce costs for traders.

The minister also highlighted institutional changes introduced in the FCT under Tinubu, including the appointment of an indigene of the territory to the Federal Executive Council, the establishment of the FCT Civil Service Commission and the creation of Mandate Secretariats for Women and Youth.

Wike challenged traders to judge political leaders by their performance rather than ethnic or regional affiliations.

“Governance has nothing to do with where I come from. It is not about Hausa, Yoruba, or Igbo; it is about ‘If I do my business here, am I happy?’”

He also criticised prominent opposition figures, particularly former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar and former Rivers State Governor Rotimi Amaechi, accusing them of presenting misleading accounts of their records in public office.

Wike questioned Atiku’s role in past privatisation exercises and alleged that Amaechi’s administration wasted N14 billion on a mosquito fumigation project, paid $39 million for a specialist hospital that was never built and spent N68 billion on an uncompleted monorail project in Rivers State.

He urged Nigerians to assess presidential aspirants by their records rather than campaign appearances or public displays of humility.

Earlier, a representative of Arewa market traders in the FCT, Adams Hassan, pledged support for Tinubu, Shettima and APC candidates, arguing that traders constituted an important voting bloc because of their numerical strength and contributions to employment, revenue generation and local security.

He, however, urged the FCT Administration to give market associations greater involvement in policies and programmes affecting commercial activities.

“We are ready to make every necessary contribution to ensure the success of His Excellency, President Bola Ahmed Tinubu, GCFR, Vice President Senator Kashim Shettima, and all APC candidates in the FCT in the 2027 general election,” he said.

A representative of Wuse Market traders, Okerie Okechukwu, announced plans for cluster-by-cluster mobilisation under what he described as a “rainbow coalition.”

He said the campaign would extend to trade unions, cooperatives, community groups and church unions, with traders supporting Tinubu, Aduda and candidates seeking election into the House of Representatives.

“Today, Your Excellency, we want to confirm to you that we are going into cluster mobilisation and campaign under the rainbow coalition,” he said.

Okechukwu attributed the traders’ support to infrastructure and market development initiatives by the FCT Administration and appealed for the construction of additional shops and malls in emerging districts, including Katampe and Kaura.

Chairperson of the National Assembly Quarters Market, Abiola Adekanbi, also pledged the support of market women for Tinubu’s re-election, citing road construction and rehabilitation projects as evidence of the administration’s performance.

She said traders would not only vote for the President but would remain at polling units to protect their votes.

“It is very simple: we are going to follow who knows the road. We are going to follow the ‘talk-and-do,’” she said.

Group Managing Director of Abuja Investment Company Limited (AICL), Maureen Tamuno, commended the minister for approving a dedicated fire truck and fire station for Wuse Market, reconstructing the Saburi road corridor and allocating additional district markets.

Acting Managing Director of Abuja Markets Management Limited (AMML), Onya Ojiji, also highlighted road and drainage projects around Dei-Dei and Kubwa markets, as well as the commissioning of Karu Modern District Market, Utako Farmers Market and Gwagwalada International Market.

He listed the distribution of free shops and the relocation of mechanics and traders to Wassa among the administration’s interventions.

Responding to requests from the traders, Wike approved a proposal for AICL to develop additional markets across the FCT and promised to institutionalise consultations with market associations at least twice yearly.

Meanwhile, in Kaduna, Jalal said TUSCOF’s confidence in Tinubu and Uba Sani was based on what he described as the administration’s achievements and the opposition’s internal weaknesses.

He cited interventions in education, healthcare, infrastructure and other public services as achievements that would strengthen the Kaduna governor’s re-election prospects.

According to him, Uba Sani’s efforts to promote unity across the state had helped address longstanding political divisions between Kaduna North and Southern Kaduna.

Jalal dismissed complaints about the distribution of projects and political appointments, citing road construction in Zone One and appointments involving individuals from Zaria as evidence that the administration had spread development opportunities.

He also blamed the country’s electricity crisis for some of the difficulties confronting businesses, criticising the outcome of power-sector privatisation undertaken during the administration in which Atiku served as Vice-President.

Defending Tinubu’s second-term ambition, Jalal argued that the South should be allowed to complete an eight-year presidential tenure under the country’s informal power-rotation arrangement.

He recalled Tinubu’s political support for northern leaders, including former President Muhammadu Buhari, and urged the region to reciprocate the gesture in 2027.

The TUSCOF chairman also cited federal infrastructure projects in Kaduna, including the Abuja-Kaduna road and railway projects, as reasons residents should support the President’s re-election.

On the political influence of former Kaduna State Governor Nasir El-Rufai and his son, Bello El-Rufai, Jalal dismissed suggestions that they posed a serious electoral threat to the APC.

He expressed optimism that political differences involving Uba Sani and his predecessor could eventually be resolved, maintaining that there was no crisis between the two politicians.

Speaking on preparations for the elections, Jalal said TUSCOF had established grassroots mobilisation structures, including a constitution, bank account and formal registration.

He disclosed that the organisation had appointed more than 50 patrons and about 60 advisers, with an organisational structure modelled after a campaign council.

Jalal, who said he participated in previous campaign organisations, including the 2023 presidential campaign, expressed confidence that the group’s experience and political network would strengthen mobilisation for Tinubu and Uba Sani ahead of the elections.

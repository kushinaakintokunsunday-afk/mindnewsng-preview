NADDC wins The Sun Parastatal of the Year award

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG

  • Osanipin dedicates honour to staff, stakeholders

By Moses Akaigwe

The National Automotive Design and Development Council (NADDC), Abuja, was on Friday, November 13, presented with The Sun Parastatal of the Year 2026 award. It was one of the major highlights of this year’s The Sun Awards ceremony held at Eko Hotels and Suites, Victoria Island, Lagos.

The award marked a historic milestone in the history of The Sun Awards, as the Parastatal of the Year category was introduced for the first time at the ceremony, which was described at the venue as the Best (awards ceremony)  in Africa.

The Director-General of NADDC, Otunba Oluwemimo Joseph Osanipin, received the prestigious award on behalf of the Council. He was accompanied to the stage by his wife, the Chairman of the Council’s Governing Board, Chief Emma Eneukwu, and a Board member and National Coordinator of the Association of Local Content Manufacturers of Nigeria (ALCMAN), Chief Anselm Ilekuba.

Speaking with journalists after receiving the award, Osanipin described the recognition as pleasantly surprising, saying that neither he nor anyone in the Council had lobbied for or anticipated the honour.

He said it was particularly gratifying to discover that the editors of a reputable national newspaper like The Sun had been quietly monitoring the efforts of the NADDC’s management and staff over the past few years to reposition and uplift Nigeria’s automotive industry.

Describing the award as credible and highly rated, the Director-General said the recognition would further motivate the Council to intensify efforts towards fulfilling its mandate of developing and transforming the Nigerian automotive industry.

Osanipin also described the honour as an acknowledgement of the collective efforts being made to advance local automotive manufacturing, promote investment, strengthen indigenous capacity and drive the implementation of the Nigerian Automotive Industry Development Plan (NAIDP) 2023–2033.

He dedicated the award to the Council’s management and staff, while acknowledging the support of the Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment, automotive industry stakeholders, development partners and private-sector collaborators.

He reaffirmed NADDC’s commitment to strengthening Nigeria’s automotive value chain, attracting investments and positioning the country as a competitive destination for automotive manufacturing.

In the Nigerian automotive sector, the recognition is expected to provide further impetus to the Council’s ongoing initiatives in industrial development, technology transfer, skills acquisition, local content promotion and the transition towards cleaner automotive technologies.

Since Osanipin assumed office as Director-General in October 2023, NADDC has embarked on a series of initiatives and interventions aimed at strengthening the country’s automotive ecosystem and creating opportunities for local manufacturers, technicians and other industry participants.

Among the notable achievements is the expansion of Automotive Training and CNG Conversion Centres across the country. The Council has established and commissioned centres in Kano, Lagos, Potiskum in Yobe State, Zamfara and Kebbi states, providing facilities for technical training, CNG vehicle conversion, certification and skills acquisition.

The Council has also played a significant role in advancing Nigeria’s transition towards alternative automotive energy sources through the nationwide accreditation of CNG conversion workshops. More than 300 workshops have been accredited, helping to promote safety, quality assurance and standardisation in the growing CNG conversion industry.

In furtherance of its clean mobility agenda, NADDC introduced National Occupational Standards for the conversion, calibration and maintenance of autogas-powered vehicles, as well as electric vehicle servicing and maintenance.

It also partnered with the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) to equip young Nigerians with practical skills in CNG conversion and alternative fuel technologies.

Another significant intervention was the Council’s partnership with the Nigerian Consumer Credit Corporation (CrediCorp), through which a N20 billion consumer credit fund was launched in December 2024 to facilitate the purchase of locally assembled vehicles. The initiative is designed to improve vehicle affordability, stimulate demand and support domestic automotive production.

On the regulatory front, NADDC, in collaboration with the Standards Organisation of Nigeria (SON), introduced the Vehicle Conformity Assessment Programme (VehCAP) to improve vehicle safety, quality assurance and consumer protection. The Council also launched the End-of-Life Vehicle Recycling Regulation to promote environmentally sustainable vehicle disposal and resource recovery.

In April 2026, it further introduced the National Vehicle Dealership Registration Framework to modernise dealership operations, improve transparency and strengthen consumer protection in the Nigerian automotive market.

The Council has equally intensified efforts to develop indigenous automotive technology and encourage innovation among young Nigerians. These include the CNG Hybrid Vehicle Design Challenge and an eight-seater electric shuttle bus design competition involving universities across the country’s six geopolitical zones.

In the area of human capital development, NADDC trained more than 1,200 artisans and automotive technicians in 2025 in areas including mechatronics, CNG conversion, heavy-duty vehicle maintenance, modern automotive diagnostics and vehicle servicing.

A further boost to local manufacturing came in February 2026 with the launch of the Import Deletion Programme for Local Automotive Components and Parts. The initiative is aimed at reducing dependence on imported components, encouraging indigenous manufacturing, increasing local content utilisation and strengthening Nigeria’s automotive supply chain.

The Council has also strengthened its institutional collaboration and policy advocacy through strategic engagement with the Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP), aimed at promoting the patronage of locally assembled vehicles and advancing the Federal Government’s Nigeria First Policy.

To deepen public awareness and industry engagement, NADDC launched Automotive Hour, described as Nigeria’s first dedicated automotive television talk show. The programme provides a platform for discussions on automotive policies, local manufacturing, investment opportunities, clean mobility and skills development.

These initiatives form part of the Council’s broader efforts to address longstanding challenges confronting Nigeria’s automotive industry, particularly low local production capacity utilisation, dependence on imported components, inadequate technical skills and limited market patronage for locally assembled vehicles.

The Sun Parastatal of the Year 2026 award, therefore, comes as a recognition of the Council’s contributions to automotive industry development and the implementation of programmes intended to strengthen the country’s domestic manufacturing capacity.

Apart from the NADDC Governing Board Chairman, Chief Eneukwu; and Member, Chief Anselm Ilekuba who is also the Chairman of Cedric Masters Group, others that accompanied  the DG to the stage included the Director of Human Resources Management (HRM), Mrs. Ronke Amodu; Director of Press and Public Relations, Mrs Susan Bisong-Taiwo;  and the Deputy Director of Press and Public Relations, Ike Okoha.

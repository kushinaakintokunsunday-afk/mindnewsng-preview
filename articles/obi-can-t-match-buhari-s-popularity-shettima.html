Obi can’t match Buhari’s popularity – Shettima

By Punch Punch 5d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Vice-President Kashim Shettima has insisted that the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, Peter Obi, cannot be more popular than former Pr

Shettima also emphasised that the ultimate goal of ensuring strong regional alignment was to ensure that the South-East was firmly connected to the “national grid in the federation.”

The Vice-President stated this at Dr Alex Ekwueme Square in Awka on Thursday, where he led a presidential campaign rally for President Bola Tinubu alongside Anambra State Governor, Prof Chukwuma Soludo.

The rally, tagged “SoluTinubu 2027”, attracted a large crowd of party supporters drawn from the All Progressives Congress and the All Progressives Grand Alliance, who turned out in colourful party attire at the square.

Addressing the supporters, Shettima said, “Don’t let anyone tell you that Igbos are not an essential component of the Nigerian configuration. And I want to tell you, as someone who is close to this election, there are 17 million Igbos living in Northern Nigeria. There are 10 million Igbos living in Lagos alone.

“And I know that they have a lot of opportunities to come here. You have to reach out.

“Peter Obi cannot be more popular than Muhammadu Buhari. Muhammadu Buhari tried to become President of Nigeria in 2003—he was already a former head of state—he tried again in 2007, he lost.

“Again, he had a strong base of 15 million votes in Northern Nigeria. He tried again in 2011, he lost. Because having a strong base across the nation is what you need to win a presidential election in a country like Nigeria.

“I am telling you, let’s be honest with each other. Let’s talk to the people. Let’s make informed judgment, and Nigeria will be better for it.

“I have a confession to make: some of my best friends in this political dispensation are Igbo. And I want to tell you that I have grown up in the South-East, as a governor, and I call my brothers and sisters to remember that.

“That shows how much I love you guys. So pass it to your people that I love you. We are together; we have come a long way together. You are our brothers and sisters. When I became the Vice-President, I had to put in my life for two months, my resources, to fight for Igbo people.

“Two of my closest aides are Igbo people. To my brothers and sisters in action, let me tell you that I have human dignity, and I can come to you with a clear conscience. We are in this together. In 2015, we went out, one by one, and we won in Anambra.

“Next, I want to talk about our brothers and sisters in the South-East who are educated and capable. I have to be honest with you: we might have some challenges, don’t let anyone fool you. Anambra State is going to be a key state for us.

“Once again, I want to tell you on behalf of the President that we are committed to you. We won’t let you down. This is a massive and real partnership that we have.

“It’s a partnership that works, and it’s a partnership that will keep working. We are proud of you. We are proud of your achievements. You are the best, and we are proud of you.”

Shettima expressed delight at the mobilisation and support for Tinubu, assuring the people that the administration would deliver on its mandate if re-elected.

While acknowledging the constitutional right to freedom of association, he noted that voters could only be persuaded and not forced to support a candidate.

He also endorsed the push for regional alignment, emphasising that the ultimate goal was to ensure that the South-East was firmly connected to the “national grid in the federation” by mobilising votes for Tinubu at polling units, irrespective of party affiliations.

Earlier, the Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives, Benjamin Kalu; Imo State Governor, Senator Hope Uzodimma; Minister of Works, Dave Umahi; former Anambra State Governor and APC chieftain, Senator Chris Ngige; and Senator Adams Oshiomhole, among others, echoed Soludo’s call for mainstream political integration and commended the governor for convening the cross-party mobilisation.

Coalition threatens sanctions over CSOs’ role in fraud trial

By Punch Punch 5d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Coalition of Civil Society Organisations on Human Rights, Democracy and Good Governance in Nigeria has threatened to investigate and sanction civil society

The coalition said it would query any civil society organisation, community-based organisation, faith-based organisation or non-governmental organisation found promoting illegalities, obstructing justice or conducting media trials, warning that such activities could erode public confidence in the judiciary and compromise the credibility of the civil society sector.

Its Secretary, Peter Nwokolo, stated this at a press briefing in Abuja on Thursday, where the coalition addressed the involvement of civil society groups in the ongoing alleged $1.5m fraud case involving the Federal Republic of Nigeria, Ufoma Joseph Immanuel and Intermediate Investment Holdings Limited before the Special Offences Court, Ikeja, Lagos.

The coalition’s intervention comes amid public commentary surrounding the case, with Nwokolo alleging that some protests and public statements issued in the name of civil society were capable of exerting pressure on the court and undermining the right to a fair hearing.

He, however, did not identify the organisations allegedly involved in paid advocacy or provide evidence at the briefing to substantiate the allegation.

Nwokolo said, “The basic role of civil society organisations in the areas of justice, human rights, good governance and anti-corruption is to serve as an independent voice of the people by monitoring state power, promoting the rule of law and equal access to justice, and defending the fundamental rights and dignity of citizens.”

He added that organisations were expected to demand transparency and accountability in public governance, rather than become instruments for advancing the interests of individuals facing prosecution.

The coalition also warned against pronouncing individuals guilty before the courts had determined their cases, insisting that public advocacy must not displace established judicial procedures.

“An accusation is not a conviction. An investigation is not persecution. Every citizen invited by the courts or anti-corruption agencies must submit to due process, with full respect for the presumption of innocence and the right to a fair hearing,” Nwokolo said.

Giving its account of proceedings in the case, the coalition said it monitored the hearing on November 16, when the prosecution informed the court that it had written to the Lagos State University Teaching Hospital to authenticate a medical report submitted in support of the defendant’s bail application.

According to Nwokolo, the prosecution told the court that LASUTH’s response, dated July 22, 2026, indicated that the medical report did not originate from the hospital.

He said the court, on November 18, dismissed the fresh bail application, ordered an accelerated hearing, granted the defendant 30 days to engage new counsel and adjourned the case until January 25, 2027.

The coalition acknowledged that Immanuel had pleaded not guilty, stressing that it was not pronouncing on his guilt or innocence.

“The determination of guilt or innocence rests solely with the court,” Nwokolo said.

The coalition said it observed the proceedings alongside the Support for Civil Society Organisations Initiative, Centre for Leadership and Democratic Ethics, The People’s Care and Development Initiative, and Elorah Initiative Hub.

It also called on the National Leadership of Accredited Civil Society Organisations in Nigeria to examine the conduct of organisations allegedly using the civic space to obstruct judicial processes, engage in media trials or present paid legal representation as independent advocacy.

Nwokolo said the national leadership would query, investigate and, where necessary, sanction organisations found to have crossed those boundaries.

The coalition also drew attention to public statements issued in the name of the late Dr Tunji Braithwaite’s foundation concerning the case, asking the foundation to clarify whether the publications represented its official position.

It argued that where questions arose over the role of an organisation in a pending case, transparency was necessary, adding that legal arguments should be presented in court by properly briefed counsel and placed on the record.

The intervention raises questions about the boundaries between legitimate civil society advocacy, public criticism of law-enforcement agencies and attempts to influence judicial proceedings. While organisations have a responsibility not to obstruct justice or pronounce defendants guilty, scrutiny of prosecutions and defence of the rights of accused persons also form part of the civic space the coalition says it seeks to protect.

The coalition urged the judiciary to remain impartial, while calling on the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission and the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission to discharge their mandates professionally and avoid media trials.

It also asked the media not to promote narratives capable of undermining court proceedings and urged professional bodies to examine alleged conflicts arising from dual roles in line with the Rules of Professional Conduct.

“We will support no criminal, and we will oppose no innocent person. We stand with the law,” Nwokolo said.

African ministers move to establish annual conference to tackle fake news, misinformation

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

• Nigeria to host the inaugural meeting as Idris calls for a united continental voice on global communication challenges African ministers of information have a

African ministers of information have agreed to establish an annual continental conference to develop a common position on misinformation, fake news and other communication challenges confronting the continent.

Nigeria’s Minister of Information and National Orientation, Mohammed Idris, disclosed this on Wednesday in Abuja, saying the initiative aimed to ensure African countries speak with one voice on issues affecting their collective interests.

Idris, who spoke on the sidelines of the 2026 World Public Relations Forum (WPRF) in Abuja, said the ministers had resolved that African countries could no longer afford to confront common communication challenges independently.

He said Nigeria would host the inaugural edition of the annual African Ministers Conference, which is expected to provide a platform for ministers across the continent to harmonise their positions and develop common strategies for engaging the international community.

According to him, although the conference existed in the past, it was abandoned but would now be revived for the benefit of African countries.

“The outcome is that ministers of information from the African continent have found it necessary to meet on the sidelines of the World Public Relations Forum happening in Abuja.

“One of the key resolutions is that, as Africans, we have found it necessary to confront our challenges collectively.

“The challenges of misinformation and fake news are what every country, not just on the African continent, is facing. So, we thought that as Africans, we should forge a common front, and as Africans, we have agreed to speak with one voice,” he said.

The minister said the absence of a coordinated African communication strategy had often weakened the continent’s ability to effectively project its interests on the global stage.

Using the COVID-19 pandemic as an example, Idris said African countries largely communicated their positions individually rather than adopting a coordinated continental response, a situation he described as undesirable.

He also cited the emergence of African rating agencies as an area requiring stronger continental collaboration, particularly in addressing concerns around access to capital and credit for businesses operating across the continent.

“If you look at the issue of the African rating agencies that the African continent came about, if we feel there is the necessity for us to communicate to our people so that access to capital, access to credit can be easier for our business people on the continent, this can only be achieved if we come together and form a common front for our people,” he said.

On concerns over the alleged use of doctored artificial intelligence-generated images by the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) at its campaign rallies, Idris rejected the suggestion that the ruling party was responsible for misinformation.

He instead alleged that opposition elements were involved in the misuse of artificial intelligence, though he provided no specific evidence to substantiate the claim.

“It is wrong for you to say that the ruling party has been accused of misinformation. We have seen the issue of misinformation, fake news and misuse of AI, but we feel this is not our forte. We actually feel the opposition is using them,” he said.

The minister, however, acknowledged the potential dangers of the irresponsible use of artificial intelligence and digital platforms, stressing that technology should promote understanding, unity and constructive communication.

He said technological innovation is irreversible and could offer significant benefits if properly harnessed.

“AI is very useful to us, but responsible use of AI is what is desirable to foster greater understanding and greater unity amongst us.

“The misuse of it can create chaos, division and disunity. And that is why all of us must use only responsible communication, responsible use of the digital platforms, including AI, to foster better understanding, communication and unity for our people,” Idris said.

He said the proposed annual conference would provide an opportunity for African countries to collectively address these challenges and strengthen the continent’s voice in international affairs.

Violence threatens election voter turnout, credibility of polls

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

From Fred Itua, Romanus Ugwu, Okwe Obi, Abuja; Scholastica Hir, Makurdi; John Adams, Minna; Emmanuel Adeyemi, Lokoja; Jude Owuamanam, Jos Attacks on campaign ga

From Fred Itua, Romanus Ugwu, Okwe Obi, Abuja; Scholastica Hir, Makurdi; John Adams, Minna; Emmanuel Adeyemi, Lokoja; Jude Owuamanam, Jos

Attacks on campaign gatherings, destruction of political offices and billboards and alleged restrictions on opposition activities are heightening tension across the North Central, raising concerns about voter safety, participation and the credibility of the 2027 general elections.

Reports from Benue, Niger, Plateau, Kogi and the Federal Capital Territory (FCT) show that supporters of both the ruling and opposition parties have suffered attacks, while disputes over access to venues and political advertising are deepening accusations of an uneven campaign environment.

In Niger State, armed youths stormed the Trade Fair Complex in Minna after Governor Umaru Bago’s governorship campaign flag-off, hurling stones and sending supporters fleeing. Several people were injured, while police said six suspects were arrested with cutlasses, knives and scissors.

The incident followed an earlier attack in which the windscreens of three campaign vehicles belonging to the African Democratic Congress (ADC) were smashed.

Supporters of Bago and the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) governorship candidate, Mohammed Nami, also allegedly clashed in Lapai in October, leaving several people hospitalised.

In Benue, the PDP alleged that its billboards, including those of senatorial candidate Eunice Ortom, were destroyed, while its Benue Northeast senatorial candidate, Thomas Unongo, was harassed by suspected All Progressives Congress (APC) supporters on his way to a burial in Vandeikya.

The party’s secretary, Fan Nyikwagh, said political intolerance had extended to places of worship.

The ADC also complained that it was denied access to the Buruku Youth Centre and a secondary school for a stakeholders’ meeting.

Governor Hyacinth Alia and the police have appealed for restraint following the signing of a peace accord.

In Kogi, President Bola Tinubu’s campaign office in Lokoja was vandalised and posters pulled down, but almost a month after the incident, no arrest had been made.

The ADC separately alleged that thugs loyal to the APC attacked and set fire to its office in Dekina, threatening members of the party.

APC campaign spokesperson, Kingsley Fanwo, denied the party’s involvement in attacks on the opposition.

Kogi PDP Chairman, Mohammed Gambo, also accused the authorities of using political advertising charges to weaken opposition parties, citing a signage fee of N150 million for presidential candidates.

In Plateau, billboards and posters of Tinubu and Governor Caleb Mutfwang were pulled down before and after the North Central APC rally in Jos.

The APC attributed the destruction to disgruntled elements, insisting that the attacks did not amount to a rejection of the President by residents.

Police, however, said they had received no official complaint.

In the FCT, the Social Democratic Party (SDP) and Action Democratic Party (ADP) accused the ruling party of restricting the opposition’s access to the political space.

SDP spokesperson, Rufus Aiyenigba, alleged that parties were asked to pay as much as N250 million per billboard, describing the charges as an obstacle to political participation.

“This is not the democracy we laboured for,” he said.

The APC, however, accused sections of the opposition, particularly elements within the Obidient Movement, of promoting hostility towards Tinubu and his supporters.

Its National Youth Leader, Dayo Israel, cited the reported attack on the President’s convoy at a mosque in Abuja and warned that further attacks “will not be condoned”.

Security chiefs and political leaders have called for issue-based campaigns and warned against thuggery, while opposition parties are demanding action against attackers and access to public venues.

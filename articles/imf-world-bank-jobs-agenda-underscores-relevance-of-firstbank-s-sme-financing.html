IMF-World Bank jobs agenda underscores relevance of FirstBank’s SME financing

By Babajide Komolafe, Economy Editor

There is a growing global challenge of job creation, and the need to accelerate private-sector investment, particularly through micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs), featured prominently at the just-concluded 2026 Annual Meetings of the World Bank Group and the International Monetary Fund (IMF) in Bangkok, Thailand.

Discussions at the august gathering of finance ministers and central bank governors, among other participants, underscored the importance of small businesses in expanding economic activity, creating employment and improving livelihoods, placing access to finance and business support at the centre of efforts to achieve sustainable growth.

The World Bank Group President, Ajay Banga, underscored the importance of small businesses to the global economy, describing them as “the engine of the economy”.

According to Banga, MSMEs represent 90 per cent of firms and employ 75 per cent of workers in developing countries.

“Job creation is not automatic. It comes from businesses that can start, that can grow, that can hire, that can trade, and that can compete,” he said at the Bank’s October 13 session on MSME financing and job creation.

The scale of the challenge was further highlighted by projections that about 1.2 billion young people in developing countries would reach working age over the next 10 to 15 years, while only about 420 million jobs were expected to be created.

The employment gap, according to the World Bank Group, makes it imperative for governments and development institutions to mobilise private investment and support businesses capable of expanding production and generating jobs.

The IMF Managing Director, Kristalina Georgieva, also called for improvements in the business environment, stronger competition and investment in digital and energy infrastructure as part of measures to strengthen economic performance.

“For governments it means improving the business environment, strengthening competition. Investing in digital and energy infrastructure and embracing active labor market policies,” Georgieva said at the IMF’s October 14 Global Policy Agenda briefing.

For Nigeria, where small businesses operate across trade, agriculture, manufacturing, professional services and other sectors, the discussions underscore the importance of financing viable enterprises and strengthening their capacity to grow.

The issue also brings into focus the role of commercial banks in providing the credit, payment infrastructure, digital services and business support required by small enterprises to expand their operations.

Financing the SME sector

FirstBank of Nigeria’s disbursement of more than N200 billion to small businesses in 2024 provides an example of the financing role commercial banks can play in supporting the sector.

The figure highlights the scale of the Bank’s SME financing activities at a time when access to capital remains a major concern for many small businesses.

The financing included about N100 billion disbursed to businesses across diverse markets and approximately N70 billion to petty traders, according to the report. The Bank also said it assessed businesses’ repayment capacity and provided some financing without collateral.

The approach is relevant to the wider debate over how financial institutions can extend credit to viable enterprises that may lack conventional collateral but have demonstrable business activity and repayment potential.

For many small businesses, access to finance determines whether they can purchase inventory in sufficient quantities, acquire equipment, meet operating expenses, fulfil customer orders or take advantage of new market opportunities.

However, expanding SME credit requires banks to balance access with prudent risk management. Reliable transaction records, sound cash-flow projections, proper business documentation and realistic repayment plans remain important in determining whether lending can be sustained.

FirstBank Group Managing Director/Chief Executive Officer, Olusegun Alebiosu, has argued that expanding business financing requires more than the willingness or capacity of banks to lend. The wider legal and institutional environment also determines whether lenders can extend credit confidently.

Speaking on the challenges confronting business financing in September 2026, Alebiosu said: “When people say, ‘you are not financing enough,’ the issue goes beyond the banks. The law, the legal system, the judiciary and the government must invest in creating a trusted environment and making it safe for everyone to transact.”

He added: “If that trust is broken, you cannot have a viable financial system. It is not going to work.”

The comments highlight an important dimension of the SME financing challenge: banks must be able to assess borrowers’ capacity to repay and have confidence that contractual obligations and legitimate business interests will be protected.

Beyond lending

FirstBank’s SME strategy extends beyond the provision of credit to include business advisory services, training and information intended to help entrepreneurs improve their operations.

Through its SMEConnect initiative, the Bank provides business guidance, seminars and practical resources to help small and medium-sized enterprises navigate operational challenges and pursue sustainable growth.

The programme covers businesses in manufacturing, trading, professional services, agriculture and other eligible sectors. FirstBank says its SME services target businesses with annual debit turnover of between ₦5 million and ₦500 million.

Helen Willie, Head of SME Banking at FirstBank, said the Bank’s support for small businesses went beyond financial solutions.

“At FirstBank, our commitment to SMEs extends far beyond providing financial solutions,” Willie said during the Bank’s third-quarter SMEConnect webinar in September 2026.

The webinar, themed ‘The SME Growth Blueprint: Practical Sales Strategies to Win Customers and Boost Revenue,’ focused on helping entrepreneurs improve customer acquisition, sales conversion and retention.

Willie said sustained growth required deliberate strategy, a deep understanding of customers, effective sales processes, strong brand positioning and the ability to adapt to changing business conditions. She also urged entrepreneurs to apply the lessons from the session to their businesses.

The focus on business capability is significant in an environment where small businesses may struggle not only with access to finance but also with financial management, inventory control, customer retention, marketing and business continuity.

Without adequate management systems, additional borrowing may increase an enterprise’s obligations without necessarily improving its profitability or capacity to repay.

By combining lending with business education and advisory support, banks can help entrepreneurs improve their ability to deploy funds productively, strengthen their market positions and build more sustainable enterprises.

Digital platforms and SME growth

Digital technology is another area in which FirstBank is seeking to support business operations.

In April 2026, the Bank highlighted its digital platforms for business transactions, payments and lending, describing them as tools for improving operational efficiency and supporting SME growth.

Olufemi Odumuboni, Head of Online Banking and Digital Channels at FirstBank, said the platforms were designed to provide seamless digital operations and support business financing.

The development aligns with the World Bank Group’s emphasis on using digital records to improve access to finance for smaller enterprises.

At the Annual Meetings, Banga said the institution’s new MSME strategy was designed to make small businesses “more visible, connected, enabled, and funded”, using data and digital solutions to address constraints limiting their growth.

Under the strategy, digital payment records, electronic invoices and other transaction data can help lenders assess businesses’ repayment capacity, while digital platforms can connect smaller enterprises to buyers, suppliers and markets. The initiative also seeks to simplify regulations and expand financing options for MSMEs.

For Nigerian SMEs, digital payments and transaction records can provide a clearer picture of business activity and cash flow. They can also help entrepreneurs monitor receipts and expenditure, improve payment efficiency and manage relationships with customers and suppliers.

For banks, reliable business data can complement conventional credit-assessment methods, although it does not remove the need to evaluate repayment capacity and other lending risks.

The opportunity is particularly relevant to smaller enterprises seeking to establish formal financial histories and expand their relationships with banks, suppliers and larger corporate customers.

Women entrepreneurs and inclusive growth

FirstBank’s support for SMEs also includes initiatives aimed at women entrepreneurs.

The Bank’s FirstGem offering and Spark A Gem Challenge combine financial services with training, mentoring and opportunities for business development.

The maiden Spark A Gem Challenge attracted 356 applications, with eight finalists participating in training and capacity-building activities. The three winners shared grants totalling N10 million.

Chuma Ezirim, Group Executive, e-Business and Retail Products at FirstBank, said access to finance alone was not enough to ensure that women-led businesses succeeded.

He emphasised the importance of combining capital with business capabilities, networks and other forms of support.

Such initiatives broaden the discussion about SME financing beyond the volume of credit disbursed to consider whether entrepreneurs also have access to the knowledge, relationships and market opportunities needed to grow.

Supporting women-owned enterprises can also widen participation in entrepreneurship and help businesses that might otherwise struggle to access finance and professional support.

Translating the jobs agenda into growth

The discussions at the IMF/World Bank Annual Meetings underscored the importance of private-sector development in addressing the global employment challenge.

For Nigeria, achieving that objective will require a combination of economic reforms, reliable infrastructure, access to markets, affordable and sustainable financing, and improved business capabilities.

Commercial banks have a role to play in that process, not only by lending to viable enterprises but also by providing payment services, digital infrastructure and support that helps businesses become more competitive.

FirstBank’s reported SME lending, SMEConnect advisory programme, digital banking services and targeted initiatives for women entrepreneurs illustrate the different ways a bank can support the development of small businesses.

Ultimately, the contribution of SME financing will be measured not simply by the amount of money disbursed, but by the extent to which businesses survive, expand production, improve productivity and create sustainable jobs.

The challenge for banks and policymakers alike is to ensure that more viable Nigerian enterprises can access the capital and support required to turn business opportunities into growth and employment.

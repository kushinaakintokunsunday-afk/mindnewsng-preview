India To Give $1.6b Aid Package To Flood-Hit Nepal

By Channels TV Channels TV 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Channels TV

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi offered a $1.6 billion “development cooperation” package to neighbouring Nepal on Monday in the wake of devastating Himalayan floods.

“We express our deep condolences for the loss of lives caused by the devastating floods in Nepal in August,” Modi said, standing alongside his Nepali counterpart Balendra Shah in New Delhi.

“To support Nepal’s reconstruction, and give fresh momentum to its long-term development, we announce a development cooperation package worth 240 billion Nepali rupees ($1.6 billion),” he said.

India did not immediately give specific details of what the development package included.

Shah was given a military honour guard parade at the presidential palace in New Delhi, on his first trip to a neighbouring country since taking office in March.

The rapper-turned-politician came to power following a wave of youth-led anti-corruption protests in September 2025.

Shah, 36, wearing his trademark sunglasses and dressed in black, was given a bear hug by Modi, 76, dressed all in white.

Unlike his predecessors, Shah had initially refrained from foreign visits since becoming prime minister.

His first overseas trip was to New York in September, where he addressed the United Nations General Assembly as Nepal reeled from a deadly tsunami-like flood, triggered by a high-altitude glacial mountain collapse, that killed more than 1,400 people and left 5,000 missing.

‘Deal with future disasters’

Nepal, a largely rural country that contributes little to global greenhouse gas emissions, estimates reconstruction will cost $4.78 billion.

Shah said it showed the “imperative to address the challenges of climate change through collective action”.

In October, the United Nations’ climate loss and damage fund agreed the modest sum of $20 million in aid to Nepal — the maximum amount countries can request in a single application — and the first time it has set aside money for any country.

Nepal, a landlocked Himalayan nation of 30 million people, has long had to balance its relations with its two giant neighbours, India and China.

“Our commitment will be to help affected families rebuild their lives, create new opportunities and further strengthen Nepal’s capacity to deal with future disasters,” Modi said.

India is Nepal’s largest trading partner, accounting for more than 60 percent of its imports, worth $8.6 billion, while China accounts for 13 percent, or $1.8 billion, according to World Bank figures.

Modi said the two countries’ relationship was rooted in “shared history, culture and deep people-to-people ties”, as he unveiled a joint vision document for cooperation.

The two leaders also launched projects including a cross-border railway, a 669-megawatt hydropower plant, an expanded petroleum pipeline and a new oil storage terminal.

“May the friendship between Nepal and India be one where trust stands as tall as the Himalayas, cooperation flows as continuously as a river, and the prosperity of both nations flourishes beneath our shared sky,” Shah added.

Lagos retrains sanitation workers on safety, efficiency

By Olasunkanmi Akoni

The Lagos State Government has reaffirmed its commitment to improving the capacity, safety and efficiency of sanitation workers through continuous training and professional development to promote a cleaner and healthier environment.

The commitment was reiterated on Monday at a capacity-building workshop for selected sanitation workers held at the Lagos Chamber of Commerce and Industry (LCCI) building, Alausa, Ikeja.

The Permanent Secretary, Office of Environmental Services, Omobolaji Gaji, represented by the Director of Sanitation Services, Dr Hassan Sanuth, said the workshop was designed to equip participants with the knowledge, practical skills and safety measures required to improve sanitation operations across the state.

He described sanitation workers as indispensable partners in the government’s efforts to maintain a clean, healthy and liveable environment, noting that their daily activities, including street sweeping, drainage clearing and waste collection, were critical to protecting public health and preventing flooding.

Gaji commended the workers for their dedication, observing that many of them begin their duties early in the morning, long before most residents commence their daily activities.

He said their contributions remained essential to maintaining environmental orderliness and ensuring that Lagos remained a city residents could be proud of.

According to him, the selected participants were identified in recognition of their commitment and dedication to duty, adding that the training represented a deliberate investment by the government in improving their welfare, competence and productivity.

He explained that the programme would expose participants to safer working practices, appropriate use of personal protective equipment (PPE), modern waste-handling techniques and improved approaches to sanitation service delivery.

The Permanent Secretary added that the workshop would also provide an opportunity for participants to exchange experiences, discuss operational challenges and share practical ideas for improving environmental sanitation.

He urged the workers to actively participate in the sessions, engage the facilitators and apply the knowledge acquired to their daily responsibilities.

“This capacity-building programme underscores the Lagos State Government’s commitment to developing a more skilled, safety-conscious and efficient sanitation workforce capable of responding effectively to the environmental challenges of a rapidly growing metropolis,” Gaji said.

He also appreciated the facilitators, partners and other stakeholders whose contributions supported the organisation of the programme, calling for sustained collaboration to improve environmental sanitation and public health.

He said a clean and sustainable Lagos required collective responsibility, stressing that sanitation workers remained central to the state’s environmental management efforts.

Gaji assured the workers that the government valued their contributions, respected their dignity and remained committed to their professional development.

In a lecture titled, “Safety Culture: A Need to Prevent Occupational Accidents,” the guest speaker, Mr Adeyinka Adebiyi, urged sanitation workers to prioritise workplace safety while carrying out their daily assignments.

He noted that their duties exposed them to various occupational hazards capable of causing injuries, illnesses and, in severe cases, loss of lives.

Adebiyi emphasised the need for strict compliance with safety procedures, proper use of personal protective equipment, effective risk assessment and responsible handling of waste materials.

He also advised the workers to remain vigilant, avoid unsafe practices and promptly report potential hazards to prevent avoidable accidents and health complications.

The capacity-building initiative is expected to improve sanitation service delivery, promote safer working conditions and contribute to a cleaner and healthier environment for residents across Lagos State.

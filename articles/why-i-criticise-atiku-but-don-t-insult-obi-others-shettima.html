Why I criticise Atiku but don’t insult Obi, others – Shettima

By Punch Punch 1h ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Vice President Kashim Shettima on Tuesday justified his persistent criticism of former Vice President Atiku Abubakar while insisting he refrains from insulting

Shettima said he only responds when President Bola Tinubu is attacked.

He maintained that his relationship with Atiku, whom he described as an elder statesman, was different because they were both Fulani, adding that he had the right to criticise him.

The Vice-President spoke on Tuesday in Maiduguri, Borno State, at the presidential launch and inauguration of the North-East Development Commission’s multi-sectoral development interventions.

Speaking about Atiku, Shettima said, “He is an elder statesman whom we hold in very high esteem. But as a Fulani man, I have the right to throw stones at him, and he has to endure whatever stones I throw at him.

“So, some idiots without any sense of history will come out and say, ‘Oh, he was a Vice President.’ Keep your Fulani banter.”

He also cautioned against attempts to portray political disagreements as personal hostility, saying he was willing to maintain cordial relationships with opposition figures as long as they did not attack his principal.

“So long as they do not attack my boss, we will practise  politics of maturity, politics without bitterness, and politics of tolerance because what binds us together surpasses whatever that divides us,” he said.

He, however, said he had never insulted Obi, Makinde or Kwankwaso, describing them as friends.

“Peter Obi is my friend. We were governors together. I have never insulted the governor of Oyo State. He is my personal friend. The same goes for the former governor of Kano State, Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso.

“Have you ever heard me, Hajiya Laba, insult Kwankwaso? I will not insult him. But whoever attacks our boss, we will attack him too,” he said.

Shettima also recalled that his disagreement with former President Goodluck Jonathan was partly connected to his support for former Rivers State Governor Rotimi Amaechi.

“Part of the problem I had with former President Goodluck Ebele Jonathan had to do with my support for Rotimi Amaechi during the race for the chairmanship of the Nigeria Governors’ Forum. Rotimi Amaechi is my friend,” he said.

He said he would continue to promote mature politics, tolerance and peaceful political engagement, provided those involved did not attack Tinubu.

“As long as they do not attack our boss, we will practise politics of maturity, politics without bitterness and politics of tolerance because what binds us together is greater than whatever divides us,” he said.

The Vice-President urged members of the All Progressives Congress to support the party’s candidates, while insisting that political leaders should work together rather than engage in rivalry.

He also called on Nigerians to support Tinubu, saying the President deserved another term in office.

“There has to be a president of Nigeria at any given time, and, by the grace of God and with the support of the Nigerian people, January 16, Nigerians will massively vote for President Bola Ahmed Tinubu,” he said.

Shettima added that social media activity should not be mistaken for electoral support, saying votes were cast at polling units, not online.

“Since we don’t have polling booths on the internet, there are no polling booths on Facebook or Instagram. These young ladies can go to Instagram and post beautiful pictures, but there are no polling units on Instagram,” he said.

On the NEDC interventions, Shettima described the promotion of electric mobility as both an environmental and economic necessity, noting that the North-East had significant solar energy potential.

“E-mobility is not only an environmental combination, but also an economic imperative. It’s absolutely essential that we support the usage of alternative sources of power and energy in the North-East. The North-East is one part of the country highly endowed with solar potentials,” he said.

He commended the commission for its projects, including 10,000 electric tricycles, 300 electric buses and taxis, and 50-megawatt mini-grids across the region.

“On behalf of President Bola, I want to commend the management and staff of NEDC for this far-reaching initiative. Beyond the 10,000 electric tricycles and 300 electric buses and taxis, they are also embarking on 50 megawatts mini-grids across the nooks and crannies of the North-East. They have built over 1,000 solar-powered boreholes, roads, bridges,” he said.

Shettima also identified poverty as a major factor behind insecurity and other challenges in the North, including ethno-religious crises, Boko Haram insurgency and armed banditry.

“In Nigeria, poverty wears a northern face. We are in the news for the wrong reasons. Ethno-religious crisis, Boko Haram, armed banditry, and behind all this insanity lies the real cause, which is extreme poverty,” he said.

He commended Borno Governor Babagana Zulum and other governors in the region for investing in education, describing it as the greatest driver of change.

The Vice-President also praised Gombe State Governor Muhammadu Inuwa Yahaya for what he described as his development-oriented leadership.

He called for unity among political leaders, saying the Federal Government and state governments should work together to address the challenges facing the country.

Shettima further defended Tinubu’s economic policies, saying the President inherited a difficult economic situation but had avoided insulting his predecessor, late former President Muhammadu Buhari.

He said the President believed he had to inherit both the assets and liabilities of the previous administration, adding that insulting Buhari was one of the quickest ways to incur Tinubu’s displeasure.

Universities tasked to train students on antimicrobial stewardship

By Punch Punch 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Universities have been urged to equip pharmacy students with the knowledge and practical skills to promote responsible antimicrobial use, identify drug misuse a

The Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of Victory Drugs, Folasade Lawal, made the call while delivering the ninth Ladipo Mobolaji Abisogun-Afodu Annual Lecture in Pharmacy, organised in collaboration with the Faculty of Pharmacy, University of Lagos, on Thursday.

Speaking on the topic, “Addressing Microbial Stewardship in Nigeria: Current Perspectives and Future Imperatives,” at the College of Medicine, UNILAG, Lawal said pharmacy students needed to be adequately trained to understand the appropriate use of antimicrobial medicines and help prevent their misuse.

She said antimicrobial stewardship should be incorporated into pharmacy education to prepare future pharmacists to educate patients, promote responsible prescribing and dispensing, and strengthen efforts to tackle drug resistance at the community level.

Lawal identified self-medication, indiscriminate use of antibiotics, failure to complete prescribed doses, weak regulatory enforcement, poor-quality medicines and the misuse of antimicrobials in farming and fishing as factors driving resistance.

She said pharmacists, particularly those working in communities, had a critical role in checking the trend by educating patients, asking appropriate questions before dispensing medicines and refusing to sell antibiotics without prescriptions.

The drug executive noted that poverty contributed to self-medication and the use of different drug combinations, stressing the need for pharmacists to help patients understand the consequences of inappropriate antimicrobial use.

She called for stronger community-level antimicrobial stewardship, urging universities to prepare students to participate in public awareness campaigns, patient education, surveillance and referral systems.

Lawal also advocated student-led campaigns and research to strengthen public understanding of antimicrobial resistance, adding that responsible antimicrobial use should become part of professional and community culture.

She warned that antimicrobial resistance was making infections more difficult to treat, prolonging hospital stays, increasing healthcare costs and contributing to preventable deaths.

She cited figures showing that bacterial antimicrobial resistance was directly responsible for 50,500 deaths in Nigeria in 2021, while 1.14 million deaths were attributable to it globally in the same year.

Lawal said Nigeria needed to move beyond awareness of the problem to effective implementation of measures to contain it.

Among the measures she proposed were stronger enforcement of regulations governing antimicrobial sales, sustainable funding for stewardship activities at federal, state and healthcare facility levels, improved diagnostic services and mandatory antimicrobial stewardship programmes in healthcare facilities.

She also called for a One Health approach that recognises the links between human, animal and environmental health, warning that indiscriminate antimicrobial use in agriculture and fishing was contributing to resistance.

Lawal urged healthcare professionals, researchers, policymakers, students, the media and members of the public to take responsibility for preserving the effectiveness of antimicrobial medicines for future generations.

In her remarks, the Vice-Chancellor of the University of Lagos, Prof Folasade Ogunsola, represented by the Dean of Basic Medical Sciences, Prof Niyi Osuntoki, said resistance was growing as microbes continued to develop ways to survive.

She warned against irrational use of antibiotics, including indiscriminate consumption and failure to complete prescribed doses, and called for the promotion of their appropriate use.

The Special Guest of Honour, Olayioye Abidoye, urged individuals to pursue legacies that would contribute to improving humanity.

The event was attended by the host, Prof Oluwole Familoni; the Dean, Faculty of Pharmacy, UNILAG, Prof Sunday Adesegun; and the Chairman of the occasion, Olumide Akintayo, among other academics and stakeholders.

2027: I have moved on from APC, Obi is my candidate – Omo-Agege

Former Deputy President of the Senate, Ovie Omo-Agege, has reaffirmed his support for Peter Obi ahead of the 2027 general elections, dismissing a viral video showing him endorsing President Bola Tinubu as outdated.

Omo-Agege, who is the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) senatorial candidate for Delta Central, said the footage was recorded in April 2026, when he was still a member of the All Progressives Congress (APC).

In a statement issued on Saturday by his Special Adviser on Strategy and Communications, Godwin Anaughe, the former lawmaker said the video was recorded during an event at the Federal Polytechnic, Orogun, and was now being circulated without regard to subsequent political developments.

He accused the Delta State APC and some officials of the state government of deliberately using the old footage to misrepresent his current political position.

“The clip is genuine. But it’s seven months old, and they are sharing it as if it were filmed this morning.

“A party confident in its record does not need to dress up an old video as breaking news. Only a desperate one does,” the statement read.

“The Delta State APC and some confused elements in the Delta State government are out of arguments, and their desperation is now on full display.”

Omo-Agege said he had consistently declared his support for Obi since joining the NDC in May and had not reversed his position.

He insisted that his political allegiance was now with the opposition party and that he remained committed to Obi’s presidential ambition.

“Nigerians don’t need a history lesson from the APC. They need answers. Food is expensive. Jobs are scarce. Hope is scarce. No recycled clip is going to change that, and none of it will put food on a single table,” he said.

“If the APC wants to talk, it should talk about its record, not its video library.”

The former deputy Senate president said he would not be “distracted or blackmailed with the past”, adding that his attention remained on the 2027 elections.

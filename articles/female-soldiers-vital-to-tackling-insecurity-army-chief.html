Female soldiers vital to tackling insecurity – Army chief

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Chief of Army Staff, Lt.-Gen. Waidi Shaibu, has said that female soldiers are critical to strengthening the Nigerian Army’s operational capacity and tacklin

Shaibu said the professional skills of female personnel, particularly in intelligence gathering, community engagement and specialised military operations, remained vital to the success of the Army’s engagements.

He stated this on Tuesday in Kaduna at the Nigerian Army Women Command’s Fourth Quarter 2026 Workshop, with the theme, “Harnessing the Professional Capabilities of Nigerian Army Female Personnel in Support of Nigerian Army Operational Engagements in a Joint and Multi-Agency Environment.”

The COAS, who was represented by the Commander, Command Engineering Depot, Maj.-Gen. Oladele Arogundade, said the increasingly complex security environment required a professional, adaptable and resilient military capable of responding effectively to emerging threats.

He described female officers and soldiers as important components of the Army’s human resources, stressing that their professional development and effective deployment would enhance the military’s ability to accomplish assigned missions.

“Their effective development and employment are therefore essential to strengthening our institutional capacity to accomplish all assigned missions,” he said.

Shaibu noted that counter-insurgency operations, efforts to protect vulnerable communities, internal security assignments and humanitarian interventions required more than the use of military force.

According to him, such operations also depended on intelligence, cultural awareness, effective communication, discipline and constructive engagement with civilians.

He explained that female personnel could provide access to communities where cultural and religious sensitivities might make it difficult for male soldiers to interact freely with women.

The Army chief cited the contributions of female personnel during stabilisation operations in Jos North, Plateau State, in April, following a breakdown of law and order in the Angwan Ruba area.

He said the female soldiers helped facilitate the restoration of peace and security, adding that their resilience, professional conduct and competence were highly commended.

Shaibu stressed that female personnel were eligible for all operational duties, like their male counterparts, adding that their deployment and career advancement must be guided by competence, merit and established military standards.

“The Nigerian Army remains committed to advancing professionalism, meritocracy and operational competencies across all its formations and units,” he said.

He said the workshop would examine ways of identifying and developing the professional capabilities of female personnel, expanding their opportunities in combat, specialist and leadership appointments, and strengthening mentorship and career progression.

Other areas of focus, he said, included critical thinking, decision-making, peace support operations, humanitarian assistance and civil-military relations.

He urged participants to develop practical recommendations that would improve the professional development and effective deployment of female personnel.

Earlier, the Acting Commander, Nigerian Army Women Command, Col. Sule Ahmed, said the workshop was designed to strengthen the contribution of female personnel to contemporary military operations.

Ahmed said the programme aligned with the United Nations Women, Peace and Security Agenda, particularly United Nations Security Council Resolution 1325 of 2000, which promotes women’s participation in conflict prevention, resolution, peacebuilding and security processes.

He added that the workshop would address drug and substance abuse, as well as mental health management, noting their implications for personnel welfare and operational efficiency.

A retired major-general, Adeniyi Oyebade, who delivered a lecture at the workshop, said the success of military operations depended on effective collaboration between male and female personnel.

Oyebade argued that the growing sophistication of modern warfare made it necessary for the Army to expose female soldiers to the same training and operational opportunities as their male counterparts.

“The women cannot do it alone, that is the truth, but when you have the men there, it enhances the operational capacity and they can deliver effectively on the outcomes,” he said.

Citing the ongoing war in Ukraine, the retired general said the increasing use of drone technology had demonstrated that modern warfare was no longer determined by physical strength alone.

“Look at what is happening in Ukraine, the use of drone technologies. Many of the drone operators are women,” he said.

He added that female personnel could contribute significantly to specialised military operations requiring technical expertise, precision and attention to detail.

“Once you start moving away from physical effectiveness and strength and start seeing what women can bring, you will achieve more,” he said.

Oyebade commended the Army leadership for organising the workshop and urged the Women Command to involve male officers in subsequent programmes to promote collaboration and the exchange of professional experiences between both sexes.

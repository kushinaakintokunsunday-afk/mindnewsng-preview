Blue economy: Coastal communities cry for attention, development

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

From Tony John, Port Harcourt

Creek communities across Nigeria have risen with one voice, demanding special development planning, massive investment in the blue economy and a new spirit of unity summed up in the slogan: “Creek to Creek, We First.”

The demand came in a 30-point communique issued at the end of the 2026 Creek to Creek Movement Summit, held at the Obi Wali Cultural Centre, Port Harcourt, Rivers State.

Signed by the Founder/Director-General of the Movement, Chief Mike Ebi Ayapaye, and the Deputy Director-General, Engr. Okiemute Onovughakpo, the communique declared that coastal communities are not just remote settlements scattered along the Atlantic coastline, creeks, estuaries and waterways.

They are, it said, the foundation of Nigeria’s economic survival.

Under the Exclusive Economic Zone Act, the communique noted, the creeks connect directly to the Atlantic economy and remain central to the country’s blue-economy ambitions.

The summit, themed “Unity, Prosperity & Renewed Hope Across the Creeks”, was convened to unite creek communities, empower youths and women, attract sustainable development and drive total transformation in the creeks.

In its resolutions, the summit commended governors for building safer water transportation infrastructure and hailed the Federal Government for the 750-kilometre Lagos-Calabar Coastal Highway, insisting that it must be delivered as originally planned, alongside the 415-kilometre East-West Road.

On the blue economy, stakeholders said the Niger Delta must no longer watch from the sidelines while others harvest the wealth of its waters. They listed commercial fisheries, aquaculture, fish processing, marine transportation, port services, shipbuilding and repairs, coastal tourism, renewable energy and marine research as areas from which the region must benefit.

They called for a decisive shift from subsistence fishing to modern deep-sea commercial fisheries, backed by technology, cold storage, processing facilities, financing and market access.

They also urged investment in maritime logistics, boat and ship repair, coastal hospitality and eco-tourism.

On the environment, the summit warned that restoration must be treated as an economic priority, not just an environmental duty. It demanded stronger pollution prevention, effective remediation, mangrove restoration, improved waste management, coastal protection and climate adaptation.

It challenged the private sector to recognise that its long-term interest lies in peaceful and prosperous host communities, and called for deliberate policies to attract wealthy sons and daughters of the creeks to invest back home.

The Movement said the future of the creeks must go beyond oil and gas to include modular refineries and gas-based industries such as power generation, mini-LNG facilities, LPG, CNG, petrochemicals and fertiliser production.

The communique recalled that the Willink Commission of 1957 had recommended special treatment for the Niger Delta, lamenting that, nearly seven decades later, the issues raised then remained unresolved.

“Nigeria cannot fully realise its economic potential while neglecting the creeks which help to sustain it,” it stated.

The Movement resolved that “Creek to Creek” must be more than a geographical description. It must stand for inclusion, connectivity and shared prosperity, where waterways are seen not as barriers separating communities, but as natural corridors connecting people, markets, businesses and opportunities.

On education, it demanded stronger links between schools and economic opportunities, with more technical, scientific and vocational training in oil and gas, maritime studies, engineering, fisheries, environmental management, IT, manufacturing and entrepreneurship.

The summit also resolved to establish a Niger Delta Investment and Development Fund to save and invest a substantial portion of collective resources for the future.

The Fund, according to the communique, must pull resources from government, the private sector, communities, corporate organisations and development partners, and operate within a structured capital market framework under the regulatory oversight of the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), in collaboration with the Nigerian Stock Exchange and other relevant financial institutions.

In his welcome address, Ayapaye said the Movement was built on the belief that every creek community deserves to be seen, heard and included in the nation’s progress.

“Creek to Creek means that when one creek rises, all creeks must rise too. When one child succeeds, it becomes a victory for all of us,” he declared.

Delivering the keynote address on “Regional Development, Coastal Development and Sustainable Unity and Peace in the Niger Delta”, Ambassador Godknows Igali said the gathering was a chance to look beyond the daily talk about oil, gas, security and resource control.

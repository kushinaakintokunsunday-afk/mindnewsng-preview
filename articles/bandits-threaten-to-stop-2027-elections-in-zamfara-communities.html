Bandits threaten to stop 2027 elections in Zamfara communities

By Punch Punch 2d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Bandit leaders in Zamfara State told community leaders in the Anka Local Government Area that elections would not be allowed in their communities, according to

The report says the warning came as armed groups tightened a blockade on the state’s roads, burned vehicles, and imposed a N50m levy on communities in Dansadau District, displacing more than 300 residents.

The document, SARI Global’s Nigeria Monthly Security Overview for October 2026, obtained by Sunday PUNCH on Friday, said Zamfara replaced Borno as the country’s most incident-dense state, recording 69 incidents and 137 fatalities during the month.

“In Anka LGA, a bandit leader gathered community leaders in October and told them elections would not be allowed in their communities. If the blockades are not broken decisively, the risk is a permanent zone of prohibition and the depopulation of the affected districts,” the report stated.

It said the threat showed a trend in which banditry in Zamfara “moved from opportunistic ambush to the systematic control of roads.”

According to the report, a bandit group on October 13 released a video threatening to burn any vehicle travelling the Gusau–Dansadau road.

It said, a truck was burned on the Dansadau–Magami stretch the next day, and on October 16, bandits blocked the road, destroyed nine vehicles, and looted travellers.

More vehicles were reportedly burned on October 17.

The report added that the bandits also threatened attacks on communities that failed to pay the N50m levy, while two improvised explosive devices were found on the Magami–Hannutara–Dansadau road on October 8.

SARI Global said the blockade extended beyond one road as bandits also shut the Gurusu–Anka, Abare–Anka, Gusau–Magami, Kaura Namoda–Shinkafi, Mayanchi–Anka, Anka–Kuturu and Maradun–Danbaza roads, among others, abducting travellers and killing drivers and farmers.

According to the report, military escorts offered little protection as an ambush on October 11 near Gurusu killed at least 20 people in an escorted convoy of traders, including soldiers and police officers.

Troops reopened the Gusau–Dansadau road on October 21 and introduced regular escorts, however, bandits blocked it again on October 31.

“The strategy is economic strangulation. Cutting the roads that link Gusau to the Kuyambana Forest axis severs supply chains, cripples local markets and establishes the armed groups as the de facto authority over who moves and who pays,” the report said.

In its trajectory assessment, SARI Global said the armed groups were likely to consolidate control over the Kuyambana axis, with greater use of IEDs.

“Unless the blockades are broken decisively, the armed groups are likely to consolidate control over the Kuyambana axis, with greater use of IEDs and the depopulation of affected districts. The approach is likely to be copied on other corridors,” it added.

The report also noted that bandits attacked Kaura Namoda town directly on October 23, killing seven vigilantes and a boy and abducting at least 15 people.

Nationwide, abduction cases doubled to 55 in October from 27 in September, according to the firm.

SARI Global said the political environment hardened in parallel across the north, with about 46 incidents linked to parties, campaigns, or political thugs, and ruling-party campaigners stoned in at least five states.

The Independent National Electoral Commission revised its timetable in February, bringing forward the 2027 presidential and National Assembly elections to January 16, 2027, with governorship and state assembly elections scheduled for February 6, 2027. The dates were originally February 20 and March 6.

According to INEC, Zamfara has 14 local government areas and 3,529 polling units. The state had 1,952,060 registered voters at the 2023 general elections, with another 313,115 added through continuous voter registration as of August 2026.

Ahead of the 2023 governorship election, bandits reportedly imposed N2m levies on three communities in Zurmi LGA, at N200,000 per polling unit, before elections could be held there.

In October 2022, the then Matawalle administration announced a total lockdown in Anka, Bukkuyum and Gummi LGAs following a resurgence of bandit attacks, and also imposed an embargo on political activities.

In April 2025, bandits imposed a N60m levy on 12 communities in Maru LGA, which residents said was to punish them for allegedly giving information to the military.

Months earlier, bandits reportedly imposed a N172m protection levy on 25 villages in Tsafe West LGA in January 2025, forcing residents to flee.

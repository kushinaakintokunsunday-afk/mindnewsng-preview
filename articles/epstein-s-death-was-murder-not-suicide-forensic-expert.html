Epstein’s death was murder, not suicide — Forensic expert

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A forensic expert has questioned the official conclusion that convicted American sex offender Jeffrey Epstein died by suicide in prison in 2019, saying his exam

Professor Michael Freeman, who reviewed the New York City medical examiner’s files, told the BBC’s Newsnight on Monday that he believed Epstein was killed, most likely by strangulation from behind.

Epstein was found dead in his cell at the Metropolitan Correctional Centre in New York on August 10, 2019, while awaiting trial on federal sex trafficking charges.

The medical examiner who conducted the autopsy ruled that his injuries were consistent with suicide by hanging and said there was no evidence of the defensive wounds typically expected in a homicide.

However, Freeman said his review identified injuries that he believed pointed to an assault, including a deep haemorrhage to Epstein’s left shoulder and marks that appeared consistent with blows to the face.

He said the evidence suggested that a ligature was used to strangle Epstein before the same material was used to suspend his body from the bunk bed.

Freeman also pointed to what he described as fingernail marks above the ligature mark on Epstein’s neck.

He said the marks “would be consistent with strangulation, particularly from behind. And the attempt by the victim to try to remove the ligature from the neck”.

He added, “The injuries and the way the body was found don’t match up. There’s only one remaining explanation then, and that is that the death was as a result of a homicide.”

He said he would have expected investigators examining a death by hanging in custody to have taken a photograph of the body before it was removed.

Freeman, who has served as a cause-of-death expert for US attorneys-general in cases involving deaths in police custody, including that of George Floyd, published his research and the medical examiner’s files after presenting his findings in London on Monday.

BBC reports that he did not examine Epstein’s body. His assessment was based on photographs and documents from the autopsy report supplied by Epstein’s brother, Mark Epstein, who has consistently maintained that his brother was murdered.

Freeman said his research was independent and that he had not been paid by Mark Epstein. However, his findings have not been peer-reviewed.

Asked who might have killed Epstein if his interpretation was correct, Freeman said he had “no idea”.

“That is not something I have an opinion about. That isn’t any part of any investigation that I’ve done,” he said.

Responding to suggestions that his conclusions could fuel conspiracy theories, Freeman said, “I have the luxury of being able to say, don’t take my word for it, let everybody look at it.

“If you think I’m still a conspiracy theorist because I’m looking at something that looks like a strangulation and say, I think it is a strangulation and I don’t think it could be a hanging, show me where I got it wrong.

“I’m wide open to that. I’m an academic. I’m quite happy to hear people’s theories on things that are informed.”

The medical examiner who performed Epstein’s autopsy, Dr Barbara Sampson, previously defended the suicide ruling, telling the BBC, “I stand firmly behind our determination of the cause and manner of death for Mr Epstein.”

Sampson had told the US Department of Justice’s Office of the Inspector General that Epstein’s injuries were more consistent with suicide by hanging than homicide by strangulation, adding that there was no evidence of defensive wounds associated with strangulation victims.

The BBC said it had approached Sampson for further comment.

A US Department of Justice investigation into Epstein’s time in prison said a guard who discovered his body called another colleague for assistance, after which they lowered him to the floor and attempted to resuscitate him.

The report also said Epstein had been briefly placed on suicide watch a month before his death.

Epstein was convicted in 2008 of soliciting prostitution from a minor and was registered as a sex offender. He avoided a lengthy prison sentence through a plea deal.

In 2019, he was charged with operating a sex trafficking network involving underage girls and was awaiting trial when he died.

The case has continued to attract international attention because of Epstein’s connections to influential figures in politics, business and royalty.

His longtime associate, Ghislaine Maxwell, was convicted in 2021 of helping him sexually abuse underage girls and sentenced to 20 years in prison.

Documents released in connection with the investigations have also featured prominent names, including former US President Bill Clinton, President Donald Trump and Britain’s former Prince Andrew, although being named in the records does not establish criminal wrongdoing.

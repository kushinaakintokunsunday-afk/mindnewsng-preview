Abia PDP candidate pledges 552 boreholes in 100 days

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The governorship candidate of the Peoples Democratic Party in Abia State, Dr Kelechi Anosike, has promised to construct 552 solar-powered boreholes across the state’s 184 political wards within his first 100 days in office if elected in 2027.
Anosike said the proposed intervention was intended to end residents’ reliance on untreated stream water for drinking and reduce the incidence of water-borne diseases.
The PDP candidate made the pledge during a meeting with the Council of Ohafia Monarchs at Elu Ohafia, where he also sought the traditional rulers’ support for his governorship ambition; a statement detailing the interaction was made available to The PUNCH on Tuesday.
He said each political ward would receive three industrial solar-powered boreholes, insisting that access to safe drinking water should no longer be a challenge for communities in the state.

“If there is still a community that is drinking water from the stream, that will stop when we come in because that is the genesis of many water-borne diseases. At this age and time, nobody should take a bucket and go to the stream to fetch untreated water to drink,” he said.
Anosike added, “Within 100 days in office, every political ward in Abia will have three solar-powered industrial boreholes. You can go to the stream to fetch water for washing clothes and other uses, but not for drinking. We will make sure that the people have good quality and standard drinking water.”
The candidate said his proposed administration would prioritise residents’ welfare through investments in agriculture, industrial development, youth empowerment and healthcare.
He argued that infrastructure development must be accompanied by policies addressing the immediate economic needs of residents, particularly access to food.

Under his proposed ‘Let-your-money-work-for-you’ policy, Anosike said local governments would involve community stakeholders in deciding how development funds were spent.
He claimed that Ohafia had received about N19bn over three years, translating to between N550m and N600m monthly, but questioned the condition of community roads despite the reported allocations.
He promised that funds accruing to local governments would be deployed towards projects benefiting their respective communities, with residents participating in decisions on development priorities.
Anosike also blamed Abia’s high cost of living on the state’s dependence on other states for food supplies, claiming that about 90 per cent of the food consumed in Abia came from outside the state.
He alleged that the state lacked an effective agricultural programme and argued that greater investment in farming would help reduce food prices and create employment.

The PDP candidate also promised to review the allowances of traditional rulers, saying the existing payments did not adequately reflect their responsibilities as custodians of the state’s traditional institutions.
“I have what I call the Traditional Rulers Council regulation order. We will review it. Whatever they are paying you now, we will pay twice that because you are the custodians of our traditional heritage,” he said.
He described his proposed approach to governance as a welfare-driven model, summarising his development philosophy with the expression, “aruo uzo, aruo afo” — build roads, build stomachs.
At a subsequent rally in Ohafia, where he presented his manifesto, Anosike outlined additional proposals on education, youth employment, healthcare and economic development.

He proposed making skills acquisition compulsory in universities, with the state government providing students with two vocational skills alongside their academic qualifications.
He also promised start-up capital for aspiring entrepreneurs through a proposed Traders Microfinance Bank and pledged to export 30 talented young footballers annually.
The candidate said his administration would train young people for remote and virtual employment opportunities with companies in Europe, America and Asia.

On healthcare, he proposed a state health insurance arrangement under which the government would subsidise 60 per cent of patients’ medical bills, leaving them to pay the remaining 40 per cent.
He also promised to equip primary healthcare centres with functional diagnostic and laboratory facilities.

Responding to the candidate’s presentation, the Udumeze of Ohafia, Ezie Elder Awa Oji Udonsi, described his manifesto as people-centred but said the traditional rulers would hold him accountable for the promises.
The monarchs subsequently endorsed Anosike for the 2027 governorship election and prayed for the success of his ambition.

Other PDP candidates at the rally, including the party’s Abia North Senatorial candidate, Jude Udeachara, Emmanuel Agwu Ukpong and Gracelyn Oko Ejim, pledged to work with Anosike to improve governance in the state.
The Secretary-General of the Abia PDP Campaign Council, Ukpai Ukairo, and the party’s Ohafia Local Government Chairman, Ukaku Ojiegbe, also urged residents to support the PDP and its candidates in the forthcoming elections.

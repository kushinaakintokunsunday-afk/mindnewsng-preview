Tinubu’s aide spends five hours in Lagos-Ibadan gridlock, faults contractor

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Special Adviser to President Bola Tinubu on Media and Communications, Sunday Dare, has criticised the timing of construction work on the Lagos-Ibadan Expres

Dare said the prolonged gridlock had left thousands of commuters stranded, with many forced to abandon their vehicles and trek to their destinations.

He made this known in a post on his Facebook account on Wednesday night, while expressing frustration over the decision to carry out construction work around the busy exit point during peak traffic hours.

Dare, who was travelling to Ibadan ahead of the All Progressives Congress presidential rally scheduled for Saturday, said he was on his way to the city for several radio and television appearances.

He questioned why the Chinese contractor handling the construction chose to work during the busiest hours of vehicular movement rather than at night when traffic was lighter.

He wrote, “Tonight – Lagos -Ibadan Expressway. I have been in a traffic gridlock for over five hours trying to get out of Lagos to Ibadan for multiple radio and television appearances ahead of the APC Presidential Rally coming up on Saturday in Ibadan.

“It beats me why a Chinese Contractor would choose the busiest hours of vehicular traffic to construct the bridge and road around this exit point. Stranded cars, thousands of people trekking to get home.

“What happened to doing construction work during the obituary hours when traffic is lean? This is unnecessary pain. It needs to be corrected.”

The gridlock followed the resumption of construction work on a pedestrian bridge around the Aseese area of the expressway, which disrupted vehicular movement in both directions on Wednesday.

PUNCH Metro reported that the traffic congestion stretched from the Redemption Camp area to Aseese bus stop for motorists heading towards Lagos, while those travelling towards Ogun State were held up from the Long Bridge area to Aseese.

A PUNCH correspondent who boarded a commercial vehicle from Ibadan shortly after 11am arrived in Magboro, Ogun State, at about 5.30pm.

Another motorist reportedly spent eight hours travelling from Mowe to Magboro.

The congestion also forced several commuters to abandon their vehicles and trek several kilometres, while commercial motorcyclists took advantage of the situation to increase their fares.

In September, motorists and commuters on the expressway were also trapped in gridlock for more than 14 hours following a multiple-vehicle crash around Kara Bridge, which compounded ongoing repair works.

Meanwhile, the Ogun State Traffic Compliance and Enforcement Agency had said the latest traffic diversion was expected to last four days, from Wednesday, November 18, to Sunday, November 22.

The agency’s spokesperson, Babatunde Akinbiyi, said the Federal Ministry of Works, in collaboration with CBC Construction Company, was carrying out the temporary diversion to facilitate the installation of the pedestrian bridge.

The agency said the Traffic Compliance and Enforcement Agency and the Federal Road Safety Corps would deploy personnel to the affected section to manage traffic and minimise delays.

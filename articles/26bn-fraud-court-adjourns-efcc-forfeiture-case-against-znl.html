26bn fraud: Court adjourns EFCC forfeiture case against ZNL

By Punch Punch 1d ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal High Court in Abuja has adjourned proceedings in the final forfeiture case involving over ₦26bn linked to Ziklagsis Networks Limited, a company accu

Justice E. U. Akpan adjourned the matter until December 15, 2026, for hearing of pending applications after counsel for the company sought more time to complete the filing of processes.

The EFCC is seeking the final forfeiture of the funds to the Federal Government, alleging that the company failed to execute a mass metering contract awarded in 2003 through the defunct National Electric Power Authority.

However, Ziklagsis Networks Limited is opposing the forfeiture, insisting that the funds were released pursuant to court judgments and agreements with the Federal Government and constituted a loan secured by bank guarantees.

In a counter-affidavit filed by an EFCC operative, Naankus Panwal, on November 12, 2026, the commission alleged that the company, in connivance with some staff members of Polaris Bank Plc who are currently on the run, transferred N39bn from an account with the bank to Providus Bank Plc.

The EFCC alleged that the transfer was made despite the company’s failure to execute the contract for the installation of prepaid electricity meters in Yola Electricity Distribution Company.

“That rather than carry out the installation of prepaid meters in Yola Disco, ZNL surreptitiously and in connivance with staffers of Polaris Bank Plc who are on the run, moved the entire sum of N39 Billion in the account with Polaris Bank Plc to Providus Bank Plc, which lacked the capacity to hold such an amount in its custody,” the document read.

According to the commission, Providus Bank subsequently sought approval from the Central Bank of Nigeria to accommodate the funds, which exceeded its threshold.

The CBN reportedly directed that the funds, being public money, should be invested in Federal Government Treasury bills and bonds rather than left idle.

The EFCC, however, alleged that Ziklagsis Networks Limited continued to misappropriate the funds and the interest generated from the investments.

It further alleged that the company opened a separate account to receive interest accruing on the contract sum, contrary to banking practice.

The commission said between July 2021 and March 2024, the company received N22,642,666,970 through the account and allegedly diverted the money for personal use.

“That since the action in the paragraph above, the ZNL has continued to misappropriate the funds and the accrued interest the funds have generated.

“That to perpetuate this fraud and defraud the Government of Nigeria, ZNL opened a separate account for the interest that will be accrued on the said contract sum contrary to the usual banking practice.

“That between July 2021 and March 2024, ZNL, through the said account, received the sum of N22,642,666,970.00 and misappropriated the same for personal use,” the deponent stated.

The EFCC also accused the company’s management of refusing to refund the funds despite the termination of the contract by the Federal Ministry of Power.

It alleged that the continued retention of the money was intended to generate additional interest for the company and its alleged associates at the expense of the Federal Government.

The commission subsequently approached the court for an interim forfeiture order over the funds.

Justice Akpan granted the application, following which a notice was published in a national newspaper inviting interested parties to show cause why the funds should not be finally forfeited to the Federal Government.

In its affidavit to show cause dated 28th August 2026, deposed to by one Jones Listowel, Ziklagsis Networks Limited denied the allegations, describing the transaction as a commercial contractual arrangement and insisting that the funds were not proceeds of crime.

The company’s Managing Director, Jones O. Listowel, who deposed to the affidavit, said the company had a legal and equitable interest in the bank account and the funds subject to the interim forfeiture order.

He alleged that the EFCC deliberately misrepresented facts to obtain the interim forfeiture order, adding that the commission’s allegations were “substantially incorrect and misleading.”

The company said it entered into a tripartite agreement with the Federal Government and the defunct NEPA on December 5, 2003, for the manufacture, supply, installation and maintenance of three million prepaid electricity meters across 23 states.

It claimed that the contract was subsequently revalidated following negotiations with the Federal Government and that the project was approved under the Presidential Initiative to Supply Meters to Nigerians.

According to the affidavit, the company was to supply 3,315,820 prepaid meters under the revalidated agreement, with funding of $196,835,396.02 approved by the Federal Government.

Ziklagsis alleged that the government failed to release the approved funds for the execution of the project, despite the company’s efforts to meet its contractual obligations.

The company said it instituted arbitration proceedings in 2010 over outstanding payments and obtained an arbitral award in its favour, which was subsequently made enforceable by the Federal High Court.

It further claimed that the Federal Government later reached a settlement with it over outstanding payments and related contractual issues.

The company said the sum of N32bn was eventually released into its account with the then Skye Bank Plc, now Polaris Bank Plc, pursuant to a court judgment delivered on September 30, 2015.

In the judgment, the court reportedly directed the release of the funds for the immediate implementation of the revalidated tripartite agreement.

Ziklagsis quoted the court as ordering that the defendants should not “divert, convert or utilize” the funds for any purpose other than the prepaid meter contract.

The company alleged, however, that although the funds were transferred into its account, it was prevented from accessing them.

It said a subsequent compromise judgment agreement reached with the Federal Government in 2017 provided for the release of N39,171,985,233.95, comprising the N32bn awarded by the court and accrued interest.

According to the company, the funds were released as a loan for the execution of the tripartite agreement, with repayment terms agreed upon by the parties.

It maintained that the transaction was secured by bank guarantees and other instruments, arguing that the funds were not stolen or diverted.

Ziklagsis also cited a judgment delivered on July 17, 2025, which it said restrained the Federal Government from unilaterally revoking the compromise agreement.

The company quoted the court as holding that any attempt by the government to revoke the agreement without complying with the judgment would amount to “a wilful disobedience to a valid Judgment of the Court.”

It further alleged that the Ministry of Power terminated the agreement in September 2025, less than 12 days after the judgment, despite the court’s order.

The EFCC, however, maintained that the company failed to execute the metering contract and continued to retain the funds despite the termination of the agreement.

The commission said the source of the funds was the Federal Government and that their continued retention without executing the project amounted to criminal misappropriation.

Ziklagsis argued that the forfeiture proceedings would undermine pending litigation over the same funds, insisting that the transaction was contractual and should not be treated as a criminal matter.

The EFCC, on the other hand, said its investigation uncovered alleged illegal transactions involving the funds and urged the court to grant the final forfeiture application.

The company, through its counsel, Oladipo Okpeseyi, SAN, filed an affidavit claiming ownership of the funds and opposing the final forfeiture.

The EFCC, represented by J. N. Dogonyaro, filed a counter-affidavit urging the court to grant its application.

When the matter came up on Friday, November 20, 2026, for hearing and determination of the applications, counsel for the company informed the court that some processes were still being filed and sought an adjournment to enable him complete the filings.

Justice Akpan consequently adjourned the matter until December 15, 2026, for hearing of all pending applications.

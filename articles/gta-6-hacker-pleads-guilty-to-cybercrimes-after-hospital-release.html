GTA 6 hacker pleads guilty to cybercrimes after hospital release

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A hacker who stole dozens of unfinished clips of Grand Theft Auto 6 and attempted to extort the game’s developers has pleaded guilty to a series of cybercrimes

Arion Kurtaj, 21, from Oxford, United Kingdom, pleaded guilty on Monday at Woolwich Crown Court to hacking Rockstar Games in 2022 and several other offences committed when he was 16 and 17 years old as a member of the hacking group Lapsus$`.

According to the BBC on Monday, Kurtaj was initially sentenced to an indefinite stay in a secure hospital because of the severity of his autism. However, following a routine assessment late last year, he was deemed fit to stand trial and transferred to a normal prison in January.

Kurtaj hacked Rockstar Games while on bail for multiple other hacking offences and under police protection.

He was staying at a Travelodge hotel in Bicester, where he had been accommodated to protect him from rival hackers who had threatened to attack him over his stolen Bitcoin holdings.

With his computer confiscated, Kurtaj carried out the attack using an Amazon Fire Stick connected to his hotel television, alongside a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse.

He stole internal company data, including 90 clips showing nearly 60 minutes of gameplay from the unfinished Grand Theft Auto 6.

He subsequently accessed the company’s internal Slack messaging system and issued an ultimatum, declaring: “If Rockstar does not contact me on Telegram within 24 hours I will start releasing the source code.”

When the company refused to cooperate, he published the clips and source code on an online forum using the username TeaPotUberHacker.

Rockstar Games said the breach cost the company `$5m (£3.79m) to recover from, in addition to thousands of hours of staff time.

The company also said the hack exposed for the first time that one of the game’s playable characters was a woman, a detail it had been closely guarding for marketing purposes.

Kurtaj was initially considered unfit to stand a traditional trial because of the severity of his autism and was instead subjected to what is known as a trial of the facts.

Under the procedure, the jury was required to determine whether he committed the alleged acts rather than whether he did so with criminal intent.

In December 2023, the court ruled that he would remain in a secure hospital indefinitely unless doctors determined that he was no longer a danger.

However, a routine assessment late last year found him fit to face a criminal trial, which was scheduled to take place at Woolwich Crown Court.

Kurtaj subsequently pleaded guilty to all the offences.

The BBC reported that he appeared in court with short hair and an unkempt beard, wearing a suit and buttoned-up white shirt without a tie.

He reportedly blinked frequently during the hearing and stood to confirm his identity before entering his guilty pleas without visible emotion.

Other companies targeted

The offences to which Kurtaj pleaded guilty also included cyberattacks on BT/EE, Revolut and Uber, where he used social engineering techniques to gain access to internal systems.

The court heard that during his attack on Uber, he posted a picture of a penis as part of his attempts to force the company to pay a ransom.

His attacks on the companies were estimated to have caused about £10m in damage.

Using data stolen from the firms, Kurtaj and other members of Lapsus$` also targeted cryptocurrency investors.

The prosecution argued that the group stole at least `$300,000 from individual victims.

Kurtaj also admitted physically assaulting workers while in custody, including multiple biting incidents.

While in prison, he was caught with a mobile phone after sending a taunting text message to police and posting updates on Snapchat.

He will be sentenced at a later date.

The prosecution described Kurtaj as a “leading light” in the teenage hacking group Lapsus$`.

Lapsus`$ member jailed for five years

Another member of the group, who was 17 at the time, was found guilty at Southwark Crown Court during Kurtaj’s original trial in 2023.

The teenager, whose identity could not be disclosed at the time because of his age, received an 18-month Youth Rehabilitation Order, including intensive supervision and a ban on using virtual private networks online.

Earlier in 2026, the same hacker, identified as Thalha Jubair, was sentenced to five years and six months in prison after continuing his cybercrime activities with another group known as Scattered Spider.

Among the victims of the group’s subsequent activities was Transport for London, which suffered a disruptive cyberattack that caused an estimated £39m in damage and significant disruption within the organisation.

AFCON dispute: Morocco accuses Senegal FA chief of contacting CAS arbitrator

By Punch Punch 19h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation has reportedly accused Senegalese Football Federation of speaking by phone with one of the three arbitrators handling Sen

The RMFF claimed FSF President Abdoulaye Fall called the Court of Arbitration for Sport’s official while the case is ongoing, according to multiple reports, including Reuters and Moroccan media outlet Hespress.

Reuters, citing a source with direct knowledge of the matter, reported on Tuesday that Morocco’s federation had notified CAS of the alleged incident during the proceedings, ahead of its ruling on Senegal’s appeal.

The source, who declined to be named because they were not authorised to discuss the case, alleged that Fall spoke by phone with one of the three arbitrators on Sunday.

Hespress, citing French sports broadcaster RMC Sport, reported that the Moroccan federation’s legal adviser had sent an official letter to CAS seeking clarification over the alleged conversation between Fall and a German legal expert serving on the arbitration panel.

The Moroccan federation reportedly asked the court to clarify the circumstances surrounding the contact, citing concerns about the independence and impartiality of the proceedings.

PUNCH Online could not independently verify whether the alleged call took place or establish what was discussed. CAS had yet to comment on the report as of the time of publication.

The Senegalese Football Federation has denied the allegation.

According to Senegalese media outlet SeneNews, the federation’s Secretary-General, Sow, dismissed the claim, describing it as “an imaginary appeal”.

“President Abdoulaye Fall doesn’t even know the judges involved in this case, because he wasn’t present on the day of the hearing,” he said.

The development comes as CAS is expected to deliver its verdict on Tuesday afternoon in the appeal filed by the Senegalese federation against CAF and the Moroccan federation.

In a procedural update on Monday, CAS said both countries had been notified that its decision would be delivered on Tuesday, November 24, 2026.

The dispute followed the controversial AFCON final played at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat on January 18, 2026, where Senegal defeated hosts Morocco 1-0 after extra time to win their second continental title.

The final was disrupted in stoppage time after the referee awarded Morocco a penalty following a VAR review, shortly after Senegal had a goal ruled out.

Angered by the decision, Senegal coach Pape Thiaw instructed his players to leave the pitch in protest. They returned after about 14 minutes, and Morocco’s Brahim Diaz subsequently missed the penalty before Pape Gueye scored Senegal’s winning goal in extra time.

Morocco later lodged a complaint with CAF, arguing that Senegal’s withdrawal from the pitch disrupted the normal course of the match.

CAF’s Appeals Board, on March 17, overturned the earlier disciplinary ruling and declared Senegal to have forfeited the final for breaching Articles 82 and 84 of the AFCON regulations. It consequently awarded Morocco a 3-0 victory.

Senegal rejected the decision and filed an appeal at CAS on March 25, seeking to have CAF’s ruling overturned and the original 1-0 result restored.

The case was heard at CAS headquarters in Lausanne, Switzerland, on October 8, with representatives of Senegal, Morocco and CAF presenting their arguments behind closed doors.

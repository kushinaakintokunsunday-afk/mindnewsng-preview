Prioritise scholarships over student loans, ex-NANS president urges FG

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former President of the National Association of Nigerian Students, Chinonso Obasi, on Sunday questioned the Federal Government’s growing reliance on student loa

Obasi, who expressed concern over the implications of the Nigerian Education Loan Fund for the economic future of graduates, urged President Bola Tinubu’s administration to strengthen public investment in education, expand scholarship opportunities and create an economy capable of providing employment for graduates.

In a statement released on the state of education, student loans and youth economic prospects in Nigeria, Obasi recalled that “during the 1976/1977 academic session, the Federal Government awarded 9,458 scholarships to Nigerians for undergraduate, technical and postgraduate studies.”

He said, “the intervention reflected an understanding that education was not merely a private investment but a national development responsibility.”

According to him, many beneficiaries of such opportunities subsequently became teachers, engineers, doctors, administrators, academics and other professionals who contributed significantly to national development.

He, however, expressed concern that the country’s approach to educational financing appeared to be changing, with students increasingly expected to assume financial responsibility for acquiring higher education.

Obasi asked whether “access to university education should become a financial obligation that follows young Nigerians from the classroom into adulthood.”

He particularly raised concerns about the economic realities confronting graduates “who might obtain loans to finance their education but encounter difficulties securing meaningful employment after graduation and the mandatory National Youth Service Corps programme.”

He noted that such graduates would be expected to compete for limited employment opportunities, navigate a difficult business environment and eventually meet the repayment obligations attached to their educational loans.

On NELFUND’s repayment arrangement, the former NANS president observed that the published terms provide for a 10 per cent deduction from the monthly salaries of employed beneficiaries, with repayment commencing two years after the completion of the NYSC programme for those who participate in it.

Obasi argued that education without corresponding employment opportunities, entrepreneurship support, industrial expansion and a functional economy could undermine the country’s investment in human capital.

He stressed that the existence of NELFUND should not be interpreted as a substitute for the government’s broader responsibility to adequately finance the education sector.

“The issue is not whether student loans should exist. They may serve as an additional financing option for students who need them. The issue is whether loans should become an increasingly important response to a problem that fundamentally requires stronger public investment, institutional accountability and economic planning,” he said.

He suggested that young Nigerians should graduate with the confidence that their education had opened doors to opportunities rather than the anxiety of unemployment and financial obligations.

“The country must avoid creating a generation that sees higher education primarily as a financial liability instead of an investment in personal development and national progress,” he said.

He maintained that the future of Nigeria could not be secured by transferring the cost of national development to young people who were yet to establish their economic independence.

“Our young people deserve an educational system that gives them not just certificates, but opportunity, dignity, economic independence and a genuine chance to contribute to national development,” he said.

Obasi urged the “Tinubu administration to rethink its educational financing priorities, invest more in universities and other institutions, expand scholarship opportunities and build an economy that rewards knowledge, skills and innovation.”

NELFUND was established by the Tinubu-led Federal Government to increase access to higher education.

Easing inflation raises prospects of interest rate cut

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria’s continued decline in inflation has strengthened expectations of a possible interest rate cut by the Central Bank of Nigeria, as easing price pressures

The National Bureau of Statistics reported on Monday that headline inflation moderated to 14.06 per cent in October 2026 from 14.63 per cent in September, extending the disinflationary trend.

The latest decline represents a 0.57 percentage-point reduction in the inflation rate, suggesting that price pressures are gradually easing.

The moderation could influence the Monetary Policy Committee’s decision at its next meeting, particularly as policymakers weigh the need to sustain disinflation against the cost of maintaining high borrowing rates.

The CBN had reduced the Monetary Policy Rate by 350 basis points to 23 per cent in September 2026 from 26.5 per cent, signalling a shift towards monetary easing after an extended period of aggressive tightening.

However, the committee faces the challenge of ensuring that lower inflation is sustained before making further reductions in borrowing costs.

A reduction in the MPR could lower the cost of credit for businesses and households, although the extent of its impact would depend on how quickly commercial banks adjust their lending rates.

Manufacturers and other businesses have continued to face expensive financing, with high interest rates adding to production costs and limiting access to working capital.

Nigerian manufacturers paid an average interest rate of 32.2 per cent to access credit in 2025, underscoring the heavy financing burden confronting businesses in the sector, according to exclusive documents seen by The Punch.

Data from the Manufacturers Association of Nigeria showed that the average interest rate paid by manufacturers stood at 32.5 per cent in the first half of 2025 before easing marginally to 31.8 per cent in the second half.

The two half-year averages put the cost of borrowing for manufacturers at 32.2 per cent for the full year.

Analysts say a lower policy rate could also affect yields on government securities, as investors reassess the returns available on Treasury bills, Federal Government bonds and other fixed-income instruments.

For the government, declining yields could eventually reduce the cost of raising funds from the domestic debt market, although this would depend on investor demand and broader liquidity conditions.

Nevertheless, inflation remains an important consideration for the CBN, particularly as exchange rate movements, energy costs and food prices could disrupt the downward trend.

An Abuja-based economist and consultant, Chukwunonso Iheoma, said the falling inflation is a positive development, noting, however, that the CBN should not rush into cutting interest rates simply because headline inflation has declined.

“What matters is whether the decline is broad-based and sustainable. Food prices are still high year-on-year, so this decision should not be rushed.

“However, monetary policy cannot remain excessively tight indefinitely without consequences for economic growth. High interest rates increase the cost of borrowing, discourage private investment and make it difficult for manufacturers and small businesses to expand. If inflation continues to moderate, the CBN should consider a gradual easing of rates, while closely monitoring exchange rate movements and liquidity conditions,” he added.

Food inflation stood at 19.13 per cent year-on-year in October, compared with 16.30 per cent in October 2025, representing an increase of 2.83 percentage points.

However, on a month-on-month basis, food inflation stood at -0.75 per cent, compared with -1.55 per cent in September, indicating that average food prices declined for the second consecutive month, although at a slower pace.

Energy prices have gradually declined as US President Donald Trump has adopted a less confrontational stance towards Iran following Republicans’ losses in the US midterm elections. Oil prices were less than $100 per barrel on Tuesday.

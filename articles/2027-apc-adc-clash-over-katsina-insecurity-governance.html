2027: APC, ADC clash over Katsina insecurity, governance

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The All Progressives Congress and the African Democratic Congress have traded accusations over political support, leadership credentials, and the performance of

While Governor Dikko Umaru Radda urged voters to reject what he described as irresponsible and disrespectful politicians and support APC candidates across all levels in the 2027 elections, the ADC governorship candidate, Senator Ahmed Babba Kaita, accused the ruling party of resorting to personal attacks because it lacked convincing achievements to present to the electorate.

Speaking during the APC campaign visit to Kankia on Thursday, Radda described the visit as a homecoming, expressing confidence that the people would support the party’s candidates in the forthcoming elections.

He said Kankia residents had benefited from several development projects facilitated by the former Minister of Housing and Urban Development, Architect Ahmed Musa Dangiwa.

“My visit to Kankia is a homecoming. It shows that Kankia people need good leaders. They are not in support of irresponsible and disrespectful persons.

“I believe that Kankia people have really benefited from a lot of projects brought by the former Minister of Housing and Urban Development, Architect Ahmed Musa Dangiwa. The people have assured and promised me that they will vote for the APC presidential candidate and other APC candidates from top to bottom,” the governor said.

Also speaking, the APC Deputy Chairman in Katsina State, Bala Abu Musawa, urged the people of Kankia to support a candidate from outside their locality, arguing that leadership should be determined by performance rather than place of origin.

He also questioned the ADC’s readiness for the 2027 elections, alleging that irregularities surrounding its governorship primary could affect the party’s chances.

Responding to the criticisms, Kaita dismissed the allegations against his character, insisting that those questioning his upbringing had no moral grounds to do so.

He accused APC campaigners of promoting individuals allegedly involved in drug-related activities while presenting themselves as champions of good conduct.

“Among all those talking about good character, I have not seen anyone who is more decent than me. If you are talking about good character, why would you promote someone involved in selling drugs?” he queried.

Kaita argued that the APC should have focused on assessing his record as a former senator representing the area rather than resorting to personal attacks.

He challenged the ruling party to ask Kankia residents whether his representation in the National Assembly was a success or failure.

The ADC candidate also criticised the state government’s handling of insecurity and the economic situation, questioning why political leaders were celebrating during campaigns when residents continued to face hardship.

He specifically alleged that Kankia General Hospital lacked essential medicines, despite the political activities of local government officials.

“At the Kankia hospital, where they went to tell people to vote for their candidate, there are no medicines. But the chairman keeps collecting money and producing posters. Who, then, is the irresponsible person between us?” he asked.

Kaita further alleged that government workers in the state had gone for about six months without receiving their salaries, while insecurity and economic hardship continued to affect residents.

On the suggestion that voting for the ADC in Katsina would amount to supporting former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar, Kaita dismissed the claim as an attempt to create confusion among voters.

He maintained that the ADC was determined to contest and win elections in Katsina State.

The former senator also alleged that the APC was relying on security personnel and financial inducements to facilitate its campaign activities across local government areas, claiming that some party officials were appealing to him to allow their campaign visits to proceed peacefully.

He said the ADC would concentrate on engaging voters rather than responding to what he described as baseless allegations.

The exchange highlights the growing political rivalry between the APC and ADC in Katsina State as political actors intensify consultations and mobilisation ahead of the 2027 general elections.

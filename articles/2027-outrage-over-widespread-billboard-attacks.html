2027: Outrage over widespread billboard attacks

By Punch Punch 2d ago 12 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The parties, in separate interviews with Sunday PUNCH, also lamented the imposition of exorbitant campaign fees, denial of access to public facilities for political rallies and restrictions on the erection of billboards.

They alleged that the destruction of their campaign materials, coupled with administrative restrictions and stringent conditions for accessing public spaces, had affected their campaign preparations and visibility.

However, the concerned state governments dismissed the allegations of political intimidation and discrimination, insisting that opposition parties were free to conduct their activities.

The African Democratic Congress and some northern state governments have, in recent weeks, traded accusations over the destruction of campaign billboards and the denial of access to venues for political activities.

In Benue State, the Chairman of the ADC, Adikwu Elias, accused the state government of denying opposition parties access to public facilities and using its agents to destroy their campaign billboards.

Speaking with Sunday PUNCH on Friday, Elias described the destruction of campaign materials across the state as a deliberate attack on the opposition, adding that the development had become a source of concern to the coalition of political parties’ chairmen in the state.

He called for a level playing field for all political parties, irrespective of their affiliations.

Elias said, “The state government has been depriving the opposition parties of venues for their programmes. If you have a political occasion and ask for a space, the government will tell you it is not available.

“If you paste posters, they will remove them. Their agents are all over the state, destroying opposition billboards. The coalition of political parties’ chairmen have met on this because we are concerned about it. We must be given a level playing field.

“Why is it called democracy if the government is not allowing the opposition to live? I must say that the All Progressives Congress, which is in government, is even fighting itself. A faction of the APC is not allowing the other faction to hold programmes in the state. It is as bad as that.”

Reacting, the Benue State Commissioner for Information and Orientation, Solomon Iorpev, dismissed the allegations that the government was restricting the campaign activities of opposition parties.

The commissioner disclosed that campaign billboards belonging to the ruling party were also being destroyed across the state, stressing that members of the opposition were not the only victims of vandalism.

Iorpev said the government was taking steps to constitute a task force in collaboration with security agencies to apprehend those responsible for destroying billboards and tearing campaign posters.

“We are trying to set up a task force because our own billboards are being torn and pulled down at every point. We see this as a nuisance, and it is something to take seriously.

“The officer in charge has been told to meet with the security agencies to set up a task force and see how to apprehend those responsible for pulling down billboards and tearing posters in the state,” he said.

The commissioner maintained that the state government had no reason to gag opposition parties, noting that the electorate had the exclusive right to determine who would emerge victorious at the polls.

“The Benue State Government is not afraid of anyone, so we have no reason to gag anyone seeking the votes of our people. Electioneering has nothing to do with government; we are all seeking the votes of our people, and voters have the exclusive right to decide on who they will vote for.

“Whether you gag people or not, the electorate has the decision on who to vote for. We cannot be seen fighting those who are seeking votes from the same people we are also seeking votes from.

“It will amount to making the people have sympathy for the opposition because they would think that they (opposition parties) have something better to offer. Whereas no one has a better offer than our governor.”

Iorpev, however, urged opposition parties seeking to use public facilities for political rallies and campaigns to follow due process.

Kaduna ADC alleges sabotage

In Kaduna State, the ADC accused the state government of frustrating its preparations for the 2027 general elections through the denial of access to public facilities and the alleged destruction of its campaign materials.

Abdulwaheed Adubi, the spokesperson for the ADC governorship candidate, Isa Ashiru, told Sunday PUNCH on Saturday that suspected political thugs linked to the ruling APC had been vandalising and removing the party’s campaign signposts and publicity materials.

He lamented that even small signposts bearing the party’s logo or the image of its governorship candidate were not spared.

“Even the small signposts we put up, whether carrying the ADC logo or the picture of our candidate, are either vandalised or removed. That is the situation in Kaduna State,” he alleged.

Adubi further accused the state government of denying the party permission to use the Ranchers Bees Stadium for a political event, despite what he described as compliance with the necessary application procedures.

According to him, the ADC had formally submitted its request to use the facility through the appropriate government office but was denied access.

“Interestingly, the commissioner later claimed that we did not apply, adding that if we had applied, the facility would have been made available to us. But the same facility was used for another event two days after we were denied access,” he said.

Adubi described the denial of access to the stadium as a deliberate attempt to frustrate the growing activities of the ADC in the state.

The spokesperson said the alleged restrictions and destruction of campaign materials had affected the party’s preparations for the forthcoming elections, particularly its outdoor publicity and public engagements.

When contacted, the Kaduna State Commissioner for Information and Culture, Ahmed Maiyaki, who is also the spokesperson for the APC Campaign Council, promised to call back.

However, he had yet to do so as of the time of filing this report.

Kano ADC petitions police, DSS

The Kano State chapter of the ADC also accused the state government of frustrating its campaign activities by preventing it from erecting billboards.

The state chairman of the party, Alhaji Musa Ungogo, alleged that the few campaign billboards erected by the ADC had been removed on the orders of the state government.

Ungogo said the party had formally complained to security agencies about the destruction of its campaign materials in September, regretting that the situation had persisted despite the petition.

The ADC had submitted the petition, dated September 24, to the Department of State Services and the police, condemning what it described as the illegal removal and destruction of its campaign billboards in the state.

A copy of the petition obtained by Sunday PUNCH read in part, “We write to formally petition your respective agencies regarding the illegal removal, vandalism, and destruction of our legally erected campaign billboards in Kano State. This act is not only a criminal offence but a direct assault on the democratic process and the fundamental rights of our candidates and supporters.”

The opposition party further alleged that the Kano State Agency for Social Intervention was acting on a directive to ensure that “no billboard belonging to the opposition should be allowed” in the state.

Efforts to obtain the reaction of the Kano State Government to the allegations were unsuccessful.

The Commissioner for Information, Sunusi Bature, neither answered phone calls nor responded to messages sent to him as of the time of filing this report.

Zamfara ADC protests hike in charges

In Zamfara State, the ADC alleged that the destruction of its campaign billboards and the imposition of charges on political activities had forced the party to resort to grassroots mobilisation.

The state chairman of the ADC, Kabiru Garba, told Sunday PUNCH that the government had introduced charges for campaign activities, including political rallies, the erection of billboards and the placement of banners.

Garba alleged that the government was unwilling to allow opposition parties to campaign freely in the state.

He disclosed that the ADC had written to the government protesting against the charges and demanding a reduction, but its request had not been granted.

“The state government sent a notification letter to us, asking us to pay some money before we could hold rallies, campaigns or erect billboards. We have written a letter in protest, asking the government to reduce the charges, but it did not.

“As I am talking to you, our billboards are being destroyed across the state. Even the billboards, banners and flags that we put up at our campaign office were removed.

“The government increased the signage charges by 70 per cent. We have to pay before we can hold a rally and before our vehicles can move from one place to another, while our billboards are being destroyed. They are targeting the opposition; the government doesn’t want us to campaign,” Garba said.

But the Special Assistant to the Zamfara State Governor on Media and Communication, Mustafa Kaura, dismissed the allegations of political persecution, challenging the opposition party to provide evidence to substantiate its claims.

Kaura described Governor Dauda Lawal as a liberal leader, maintaining that Zamfara State practised advanced democracy and that opposition parties were allowed to use government facilities for their activities.

“The state government has never witch-hunted any opposition party. They are allowed to use government facilities. Freedom Square is given to them to do whatever they want there,” Kaura stated.

Sokoto ADC decries vandalism

Similarly, the Chairman of the ADC in Sokoto State, Bello Yabo, lamented the destruction of the party’s campaign billboards across the state.

Yabo, who spoke with Sunday PUNCH on Friday, alleged that the party had been denied space to erect its billboards, while the few it managed to install had been vandalised.

He attributed the absence of the party’s campaign billboards in parts of the state to the alleged widespread destruction of its publicity materials.

“Our campaign billboards have been vandalised across the state. That’s why you have not been seeing them around,” he said.

Efforts to obtain a reaction from the Sokoto State Governor’s spokesperson, Abubakar Bawa, were unsuccessful, as he neither answered calls nor replied to messages sent to his phone.

Parties lament exorbitant campaign fees

In Gombe State, the Chairman of the New Nigeria Congress, Badingada Alkali, lamented the difficulties experienced by the party in erecting campaign billboards owing to what he described as high charges.

Alkali also complained about the difficulties associated with securing venues for political activities, alleging that even the use of privately owned facilities attracted scrutiny from government officials.

He said, “To even get a space for political activities is hell unless it is your own property. Even at that, the government would send people to verify if you are paying taxes for that property.”

However, the Publicity Secretary of the APC in Gombe State, Murtala Usman, dismissed the allegations, describing the state as one of the most receptive environments for opposition political parties.

Usman maintained that opposition parties were not being intimidated in the state and were free to conduct their campaigns.

Also, the Kogi State chapter of the NDC decried what it described as exorbitant charges imposed by the state government on billboard placements and other campaign materials.

The state chairman of the party, Monday Onate, told Sunday PUNCH that the charges were not restricted to billboards but extended to other materials used by political parties and their candidates to publicise their activities.

According to Onate, the government had imposed fees on campaign materials, including branded vehicles, T-shirts and face caps.

“The government has imposed fees on all campaign materials, including vehicle branding, T-shirts and others,” he said.

Anambra NDC turns to churches, community facilities

In Anambra State, the NDC lamented the destruction of campaign billboards erected by its candidates despite paying for the spaces.

The state chairman of the party, Ikemefuna Uzoezie, told Sunday PUNCH that the NDC was experiencing difficulties securing billboard spaces for its campaign activities.

He said even when candidates of the party succeeded in securing and paying for billboard spaces, their campaign billboards were subsequently removed or damaged.

Uzoezie also complained about restrictions on the use of government-owned facilities for political activities.

He disclosed that the party had resorted to using churches and community facilities for its programmes because of the restrictions imposed by the state government on access to public facilities.

Enugu defends N150m billboard charge

Meanwhile, the Enugu State chapter of the ADC lamented the reported imposition of a N150m fee on opposition parties for billboard placement in the state.

The state chairman of the ADC, Stella Chukwuma, disclosed this in a telephone interview with one of our correspondents on Saturday.

Chukwuma attributed the absence of opposition campaign billboards in the state to the charge.

She, however, explained that the party had yet to formally apply to the state government for permission to use public facilities for its political activities.

She said, “That is why you have not seen any billboards belonging to opposition parties in Enugu State.

“As for public spaces, we have yet to write an application to the state government to find out if we will be granted permission to use them.”

Reacting to the complaint, the Senior Special Assistant to the Enugu State Governor on Strategic Communications, Dr Collins Ogbu, described the N150m fee as a billboard permit charge.

Ogbu maintained that such charges were not peculiar to Enugu State, citing what he said was the amount charged in Lagos State.

He said, “We have it in Lagos, where it is N700m, and in other states as well.”

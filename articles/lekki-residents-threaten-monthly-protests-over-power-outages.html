Lekki residents threaten monthly protests over power outages

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Residents of Orchid Road, Lekki, Lagos State, have threatened to stage monthly protests over persistent electricity outages, accusing Eko Electricity Distributi

 The residents, under the aegis of the Lekki Conservation Estate Community Development Area, took to the streets at Monife Roundabout over the weekend, displaying placards to express their frustration over what they described as months of darkness.

 Some of the placards read, “We Pay Premium Rates But Get Zero Reliability!”, “Orchid Road Band B = Band Nothing”, “Eko Power is a Charade”, “Orchid Road Deserves a Power Station” and “TCN Needs to Be Unbundled.”

 The protesters, comprising landlords, tenants, business owners and community leaders, said the prolonged outages had disrupted businesses, increased household expenses and forced estates to rely heavily on diesel-powered generators.

 Speaking during the protest, the President of LECECODA, Bode Akinwande, said the community had endured poor electricity supply for more than four months, despite repeated engagements with Eko DisCo.

 “For the last four months, our community has been without meaningful power supply, and our repeated efforts to resolve the matter through dialogue have yielded no result,” he said.

 Akinwande said the community received virtually no electricity throughout October, despite residents continuing to receive electricity bills running into millions of naira.

 “Let us take the month of October as an example. In the month of October, we did not receive two hours of supply cumulatively, and unfortunately, members of this community in various estates have received bills in the millions. This is not acceptable to us,” he stated.

 He explained that Orchid Road was a 7.5-kilometre corridor in Lekki, housing about 200 residential estates, as well as schools, restaurants, shopping malls, supermarkets, churches and mosques.

 According to him, the community receives electricity through the Maroko 33kV feeder at Ajah but has no dedicated injection substation to serve its growing population.

 He alleged that the feeder trips daily, sometimes at the slightest threat of rainfall, leaving residents without electricity for several days.

 “In recent weeks, supply has been restored for as little as two minutes after intervals of four or five days without power,” he said.

 The community leader also accused Eko DisCo and TCN of shifting responsibility for the poor supply between themselves, leaving consumers to bear the consequences.

 Akinwande said the association had formally written to the acting Managing Director of Eko DisCo, Sharifat Adegbenro, on October 20, 2026, requesting a meeting to address the situation.

 He, however, expressed disappointment that the company only responded after learning of the planned protest.

 Akinwande warned that the residents would return to the streets every month until there was an appreciable improvement in electricity supply.

 He said, “If we do not see stable power supply, next month we are going to gather again to let members of the community and Nigeria as a whole know what they have done. So, it will be monthly until we receive an appreciable improvement in power supply to us.”

 Speaking on the economic consequences of the outages, a community stakeholder, Faith Oliver, said businesses along the corridor had suffered substantial losses, with some forced to shut down because of unreliable electricity.

 She said businesses dealing in frozen foods, restaurants, supermarkets and shopping malls had been particularly affected because of their dependence on refrigeration.

 “In terms of businesses as a whole, we have lost billions. So, many businesses here have been shut down, as they cannot even operate functionally because of low power supply,” Oliver said.

 Oliver also lamented the impact of the outages on residents’ daily lives, saying some households had been unable to pump water because of the lack of electricity.

 The immediate past president of the association, Irene Osas, said the electricity crisis had persisted for more than 10 years despite repeated engagements with the distribution company.

 Osas said the community had received several assurances and entered into agreements with the company over the years without any significant improvement in service delivery.

 Giving an indication of the financial burden on residents, Osas disclosed that his estate spent about N6m monthly on diesel to supplement electricity supply, adding that the estate now owed diesel suppliers.

 Another resident, Engineer Olosho Saheed, accused Eko DisCo of issuing estimated bills despite the availability of functioning meters in some estates.

 He said the unreliable supply had forced his estate to increase electricity charges collected from residents while also struggling to meet its diesel expenses.

 Saheed disclosed that the estate operated a 500kVA generator, which consumed between 600 and 800 litres of diesel daily.

 “We cannot continue with this. Eko Power has to do better by us, and they have to improve,” Saheed stated.

 Also speaking, the Chairman of the association’s Board of Trustees, Taiwo Enilolobo, questioned the continued billing of residents despite the poor electricity supply.

 Enilolobo said between 95 and 98 per cent of residents along the corridor paid their electricity bills as and when due.

 The association called on Eko DisCo to restore electricity supply in line with the community’s Band B classification or review its billing to reflect the actual service delivered.

 It also demanded a technical audit of the Maroko 33kV feeder, a time-bound plan to address persistent tripping and a commitment to constructing a dedicated injection substation for the Orchid Road corridor.

 The residents further urged TCN’s Ajah Transmission Station to explain the prolonged outages on the feeder and work with Eko DisCo to restore stable supply.

 They also called on the regulators to investigate the electricity service delivered to the community against its Band B classification over the past four months.

 Eko DisCo had yet to respond to messages and calls from our correspondent seeking its reaction to the allegations as of the time of filing this report.

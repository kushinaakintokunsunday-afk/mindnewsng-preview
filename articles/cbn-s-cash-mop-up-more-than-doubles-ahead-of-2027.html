CBN’s cash mop-up more than doubles ahead of 2027

By Punch Punch 4d ago 9 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Central Bank of Nigeria has intensified its liquidity mop-up operations, withdrawing more than N90tn from the financial system through Open Market Operation

The amount represents a sharp increase from the N40.9tn withdrawn in 2025, reflecting the apex bank’s aggressive monetary tightening efforts to rein in excess liquidity and prevent renewed inflationary pressures amid concerns about election-related spending.

The increased liquidity withdrawal comes as policymakers warn that political spending ahead of the 2027 elections could inject substantial funds into the economy, potentially complicating the CBN’s efforts to sustain the disinflationary trend.

The CBN withdrew N50.23tn through liquidity mop-up operations in 2024, while its sales of OMO securities declined to N40.90tn in 2025, according to the apex bank’s 2025 Annual Report.

However, liquidity absorption has accelerated significantly this year. Data from CBN auctions, FMDQ and the Financial Market Dealers Association show that the apex bank withdrew more than N90tn through OMO operations between January and November. The figure, however, excludes OMO maturities for some months and may not reflect the net amount of liquidity absorbed during the period.

Monthly FMDA data show that the CBN withdrew N13.41tn in January and N6.3tn in February. The amount fell to N2.36tn in March before increasing to N4.48tn in April.

In May, the apex bank absorbed N5.42tn through two OMO auctions held on May 21 and 29, while its liquidity mop-up rose sharply to N13.96tn in June. The amount subsequently moderated to N7.18tn in July and N4.72tn in August.

September recorded the highest OMO allotment, with the CBN raising N17.5tn as it intensified efforts to contain excess liquidity in the banking system.

In October, the apex bank allotted N6.182tn in new OMO bills, while N9.051tn worth of existing securities matured, resulting in a net repayment of approximately N2.87tn before other adjustments, according to FMDA data.

The CBN subsequently withdrew N7.53tn through OMO auctions conducted on 3, 4 and 10 November.

Some sources cite CBN OMO sales at over N103tn so far this year, but The PUNCH relied on data from CBN auctions, FMDQ and the Financial Market Dealers Association.

The aggressive issuance comes as the CBN faces the challenge of maintaining monetary stability while preparing for potential liquidity pressures associated with the 2027 election cycle.

MPC members warn

Members of the Central Bank of Nigeria’s Monetary Policy Committee warned in late October that increased government spending ahead of the 2027 general elections could fuel inflation, weaken fiscal discipline and complicate efforts to maintain monetary stability.

The warnings came as Nigeria’s broad money supply rose to N146.60tn in September 2026, up N28.82tn from a year earlier, while credit to the government climbed by 63.09 per cent to N39.40tn.

The concerns were contained in personal statements issued by MPC members following the committee’s 307th meeting held on 21 and 22 September, 2026.

The members cautioned that election-related expenditure, alongside pressure from fuel, food and production costs, could slow the country’s progress in bringing inflation under control.

Deputy Governor of the CBN’s Corporate Services Directorate, Dr Muhammad Abdullahi, warned that election-related fiscal expansion could transmit inflationary pressure through transportation, energy and production costs.

“I recognise that election-related fiscal expansion and other incipient price pressures may propagate through transport, energy and production cost channels, amplifying economy-wide cost-push inflation,” he said.

Abdullahi called for closer coordination between fiscal and monetary authorities to prevent government measures from injecting additional liquidity into the economy and undermining price stability.

Another MPC member and Senior Fellow at the Brookings Institution, Dr Aloysius Ordu, urged the government to maintain fiscal discipline as political activities intensify.

“And as the election cycle approaches, stronger safeguards against fiscal slippage and pro-cyclical spending will be important,” Ordu said.

He noted that improved oil receipts were strengthening government finances but warned that converting foreign exchange earnings into domestic liquidity could complicate efforts to contain inflation.

Similarly, MPC member, Bandele Amoo, identified political activities and budget implementation as potential sources of renewed spending pressure.

“As global uncertainty remains elevated, domestic political activities may introduce new spending pressures, and supply-side vulnerabilities continue to pose risks to the inflation outlook,” Amoo said.

He expressed optimism that better coordination between the CBN and the Federal Ministry of Finance would help manage spending pressures, stressing that monetary policy decisions should remain guided by economic evidence and medium-term price stability.

Professor Murtala Sagagi acknowledged that election-related spending had historically put pressure on demand and prices but said existing monetary policy tools could help contain the risks.

He cited the CBN’s liquidity management instruments, its Cash Reserve Requirement and a memorandum of understanding on fiscal-monetary coordination between the CBN and the Federal Ministry of Finance.

CBN Governor, Olayemi Cardoso, said the bank had analysed previous election cycles and developed scenarios to prepare for possible liquidity pressures.

“We are ready,” Cardoso said at a press briefing after the committee’s September meeting.

He said the bank would monitor currency in circulation, banking system liquidity, monetary aggregates and foreign exchange demand.

“We will proactively deploy any tools and instruments to mop up any excess liquidity,” the governor said, adding that the CBN would not allow itself to be caught unprepared.

World Bank warns

The World Bank warned in October that Nigeria’s consolidated fiscal deficit was expected to widen from 3.1 per cent of GDP in 2025 to 3.5 percent in 2026, despite higher oil revenues and increased government allocations.

In its Nigeria Development Update titled ‘Beyond the Federal Purse: How Higher Revenues Reshaped State Priorities’, the bank said stronger capital spending and pre-election spending would outweigh the gains from increased revenues.

“Despite the narrower federal fiscal deficit in H1 2026, stronger capital spending by states alongside rising federal personnel, interest, and pre-election spending is expected to widen the consolidated fiscal deficit from 3.1 percent of GDP in 2025 to 3.5 percent in 2026, outweighing stronger revenues,” the World Bank said.

The report said higher oil prices, following the conflict in the Middle East, had supported government revenues and improved the country’s external position, but existing oil sales and financing commitments had limited the fiscal benefits.

The bank warned that spending pressures are expected to increase later in the year, potentially reversing some of the fiscal gains.

Analysts wade in

Economists have attributed the CBN’s aggressive liquidity mop-up in 2026 to increased election-related spending and higher revenue allocations to state and local governments. They said the exercise reflects the apex bank’s efforts to prevent excess money in circulation from triggering fresh inflationary pressures.

In separate phone interviews with The PUNCH, the economists said rising government revenues, increased public sector deposits in commercial banks and political spending had contributed to the liquidity build-up, leaving the CBN with the task of absorbing surplus funds.

A Professor of Economics and Public Policy at the University of Uyo, Akpan Ekpo, said the CBN was seeking to control money supply to prevent excess liquidity from undermining price stability.

“With the election coming, there will be a lot of expenditures. So, the CBN has to mop it up because even inflation is not always a monetary phenomenon; it can also be a fiscal phenomenon,” Ekpo said.

He explained that the apex bank was concerned that excess cash in the financial system could translate into increased consumer spending and push up prices.

“I suspect the CBN wants to make sure that the liquidity in the system does not jack up the consumer price index,” he said.

However, Ekpo expressed doubts about the effectiveness of the intervention, arguing that the scale of spending expected ahead of the elections could undermine the CBN’s efforts.

“With the elections coming, I don’t think the CBN effort will bear much fruit,” he said.

Similarly, the Founder of the Centre for the Promotion of Private Enterprise, Dr Muda Yusuf, linked the liquidity expansion to increased government revenues and allocations to state and local governments, much of which he said remained within the banking system.

“The only source that I think could have been responsible for that is the high government revenue resulting from higher allocations to the state and local governments, most of which are also in the banks,” Yusuf said.

He noted that the CBN had introduced differentiated cash reserve requirements to limit the amount of public sector funds banks could deploy for lending. The apex bank imposes a 75 per cent Cash Reserve Ratio on public sector deposits outside the Treasury Single Account, compared with 45 per cent on regular deposits.

Despite these restrictions, Yusuf said public sector funds continued to contribute to overall liquidity in the financial system.

“The public sector liquidity is feeding into the financial system liquidity,” he said.

He also identified election-related expenditure as another source of pressure, noting that spending by political parties, candidates and government institutions involved in the electoral process could inject substantial amounts of money into the economy.

“Political spending from INEC, security agencies, political parties and, above all, the candidates themselves, all of those things are injecting a lot of liquidity into the system,” Yusuf said.

Also, the Managing Director of Highcap Securities Limited, Mr David Adonri, identified monetary operations, bank recapitalisation and government spending as factors that could have contributed to the liquidity build-up.

He explained that transactions involving Open Market Operations securities could influence the volume of funds available in the banking system, particularly when the CBN redeems previously issued instruments.

Adonri also noted that the reduction in banks’ holdings of certain securities could have left them with more funds to deploy, while the recapitalisation exercise increased the capital available to financial institutions.

He also linked the liquidity situation to political activities, arguing that increased expenditure by governments and politicians could add further pressure to the financial system.

According to him, when investors redeem government securities, the proceeds return to the banking system, potentially increasing available liquidity, particularly where the instruments carry high yields.

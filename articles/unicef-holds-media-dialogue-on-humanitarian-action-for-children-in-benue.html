UNICEF holds media dialogue on humanitarian action for children in Benue

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 4 min read

From Scholastica Hir, Makurdi

The United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF), with support from the Government of Sweden through the Swedish International Development Cooperation Agency (Sida) and in collaboration with the Benue State Government, has commenced a two-day media dialogue on humanitarian action for children in Makurdi, the Benue State capital.

The media dialogue is on “Amplifying Humanitarian Action: Strengthening Media Emergency and Advocacy for Integrated Humanitarian Funding in Benue State”, with the theme “Amplifying Humanitarian Action: Media, Evidence and Advocacy for Children.”

Speaking at the event on Tuesday, the Chief of the UNICEF Field Office, Enugu, Juliet Chiluwe, charged media practitioners to move beyond reporting challenges to amplifying solutions, strengthening accountability and inspiring collective action towards the humanitarian situation in Benue State.

Chiluwe, who was represented by UNICEF Social Policy Officer Dr Victor Chima, said the media dialogue was convened at a critical time when the humanitarian situation in Benue State called for sustained attention, stronger partnerships and collective action to protect and improve the lives of affected children and vulnerable communities.

She noted that the engagement is an important strategic communication and visibility component of that support, providing an opportunity to strengthen media understanding of Sida-supported interventions, amplify evidence of results and tell compelling stories of how the integrated, multisectoral response is making a difference.

She maintained that the media remains an indispensable partner in humanitarian action, pointing out that, beyond reporting events, their platforms have the power to shape public understanding, elevate the voices of affected communities, influence policy conversations and mobilise the commitment and resources required to address humanitarian needs.

While appreciating the Government of Sweden, through Sida, for its continued support for the humanitarian response, the UNICEF Chief of Office said the organisation is always willing to strengthen collaboration with government, development partners and other stakeholders to ensure that the needs, rights and voices of children remain central to humanitarian priorities and response efforts.

She encouraged participants to engage actively, deepen their understanding of UNICEF’s interventions and identify practical opportunities for more informed, ethical and sustained reporting on the humanitarian situation in Benue State.

Declaring the event open, the State Commissioner for Information and Orientation, Solomon Iorpev, represented by the Director of Information and Head of the Department of Enlargement and Orientation, Mrs Comfort Ajunwa, commended UNICEF for the initiative and for its continued commitment to promoting the rights, welfare and protection of children.

He acknowledged the media as a powerful partner in development, saying that, beyond informing the public, the media plays a vital role in shaping opinions, creating awareness, promoting positive values and holding stakeholders accountable.

Iorpev said effective collaboration between government, UNICEF and media practitioners is essential in amplifying issues affecting children, protecting their rights and ensuring that their voices are heard.

Speaking on the objective of the dialogue, UNICEF Communication Officer, Enugu Field Office, Dr Ijeoma Onuoha-Ogwe, explained that it seeks to strengthen media understanding and visibility by enhancing journalists’ understanding of the humanitarian situation in Benue State and the needs of crisis-affected children and vulnerable communities, while amplifying the results and impact of Sida-supported interventions through UNICEF’s integrated multisectoral humanitarian response.

She also said that it is meant to promote ethical, evidence-based and people-centred reporting by strengthening media capacity to produce compelling, child-sensitive and evidence-based stories across health, nutrition, WASH, education, child protection and social policy, while amplifying the voices, experiences and priorities of affected communities. It is also meant to strengthen strategic media partnerships and advocacy by consolidating UNICEF’s collaboration with media organisations to sustain public awareness, shape humanitarian discourse and strengthen advocacy for the rights and well-being of affected children and communities.

Ijeoma further said the dialogue helps to mobilise stakeholder commitment and accountability by fostering constructive engagement among government, humanitarian actors, development partners and the media to promote coordinated action, sustained investment, transparency and accountability in addressing humanitarian needs.

She emphasised that the expected outcome is a better informed, engaged and empowered media community that consistently amplifies Sida-supported interventions and UNICEF’s integrated humanitarian response in Benue State, while contributing to stronger public awareness, government accountability, stakeholder commitment and advocacy for the rights and well-being of crisis-affected children and vulnerable communities.

During the event, the UNICEF Humanitarian Coordinator/Emergency Specialist, Ihuoma Owuama, presented a profile of the Benue humanitarian situation.

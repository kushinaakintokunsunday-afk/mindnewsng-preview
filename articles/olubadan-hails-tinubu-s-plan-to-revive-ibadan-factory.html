Olubadan hails Tinubu’s plan to revive Ibadan factory

By Punch Punch 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Olubadan of Ibadanland, Oba Rashidi Ladoja, has commended President Bola Tinubu over his promise to reopen the defunct Leyland factory and other moribund fa

Ladoja, in a statement issued by his media aide, Chief Adeola Oloko, in Ibadan, Oyo State, on Sunday, appreciated the President for infrastructure development projects across the country, particularly in the South-West.

Ladoja said, “The projects in question are works in progress. It is when they are completed that you can begin to make fresh requests for new ones.

“Let me express gratitude to the President over his promise to reopen the defunct Leyland Motor Assembly, Ibadan; Ibadan-Ijebu-Ode Road and Ibadan-Oyo Road, among others. Many of the roads had fallen into a terrible state of disrepair and exposed commuters to untold suffering.”

The PUNCH reports that Tinubu, during his visit to Ibadan for a political rally on Saturday, promised to reopen the Leyland factory following the signing of a memorandum of understanding with a Chinese firm.

He said, “For Oyo State, we have just signed a Memorandum of Understanding with a Chinese firm to reopen Leyland factory in Ibadan.

“We are re-opening it to produce public transport and automobile assembly plant to them. We will continue to bring industry. You will see it and I guarantee you an improvement in electricity supply. Joseph Tegbe is really working hard with his team.”

It will also be recalled that the Leyland assembly plant was established in the 1970s in Ibadan as a joint venture between the Nigerian government and British Leyland to assemble commercial vehicles and build local technical capacity.

Built on a 125-acre site, with approximately 55,000 square metres of buildings, the £37m plant was officially opened in March 1979 by British Leyland President, Lord Stokes.

As Nigeria’s economy faced severe headwinds and assembly operations struggled, the original Leyland Nigeria firm eventually ceased production and went into receivership.

Speaking further, the Olubadan said in the statement, “The President, Asiwaju Tinubu, arrived to a warm reception from members of the state Council of Obas and Chiefs before proceeding to Mapo for the campaign rally.

“The scores of monarchs, drawn from Ibadan, Ibarapa, Oyo, Ogbomoso, and Oke-Ogun zones one and two, received the President at the Ladoke Akintola Airport, Ibadan, at about 1:30 pm to reaffirm the position of the South-West’s leading monarchs at their recent meeting in Ibadan on his re-election.”

He contended that just as a man’s gifts make room for him, so do the works of his hands.

According to the statement, “Apart from the Olubadan, many monarchs in attendance were the Soun of Ogbomoso, Eleruwa of Eruwa, Aseyin of Iseyin, Okere of Saki, Onpetu of Ijeru, Olugbon of Orile-Igbon, members of the Olubadan-in-Council, Onigboho of Igboho, Alagere of Ago-Are, Olokaka of Okaka, Ona Onibode of Igboho, Akibio of Ilora, Olu of Fiditi, Alajawa of Ajaawa and a host of other prominent traditional rulers in the state.”

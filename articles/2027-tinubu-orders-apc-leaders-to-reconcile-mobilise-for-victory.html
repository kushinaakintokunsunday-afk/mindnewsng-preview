2027: Tinubu orders APC leaders to reconcile, mobilise for victory

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 1d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

President Bola Tinubu has directed leaders of the All Progressives Congress (APC) to resolve internal disputes, reconcile aggrieved members and mobilise voters

President Bola Tinubu has directed leaders of the All Progressives Congress (APC) to resolve internal disputes, reconcile aggrieved members and mobilise voters ahead of the 2027 general election.

He also urged party members and supporters to obtain their Permanent Voter Cards (PVCs) and work towards securing overwhelming votes for the ruling party, warning against complacency.

Tinubu gave the directives on Sunday at a prayer session organised by his extended family at the Yoruba Tennis Club, Onikan, Lagos.

Acknowledging divisions within the party, the president urged its leaders to put aside personal differences, embrace forgiveness and strengthen internal cohesion to consolidate the gains of his administration ahead of the election.

“We must generate enough votes to overwhelm the opponents,” he said, reaffirming his commitment to providing purposeful leadership, strengthening national unity and justice, and building a prosperous country for Nigerians.

He called on party members to return to door-to-door mobilisation, urging them to visit their neighbours and encourage eligible voters to collect their PVCs.

“The challenge of today is much easier than that of yesterday, but we cannot take it for granted. You must knock on your neighbour’s door, tell them to collect their PVC.

“If you have PVC, you have the power of January 16th and February 6th, and that’s what I want to ask you to exercise,” he said.

Tinubu specifically directed his Special Adviser on Political Matters, Alhaji Ibrahim Kabiru Masari, and former Minister of Defence, Alhaji Abubakar Badaru, who were among those present, to help resolve disagreements within the party.

Apparently reacting to reports of a disagreement between Katsina State Governor Dikko Umar Radda and Masari, the president ordered his aide to return home and reconcile with the governor.

“Alhaji Kabiru Masari, go back home and reconcile with everybody. That is the president’s order. When you finish that, you move to Jigawa.

“You move to other places and reconcile them, beg them because we have to reconcile and every anger must dissipate and evaporate,” Tinubu said.

He gave a similar directive to Badaru, a former governor of Jigawa State, urging him to work towards resolving differences among party leaders.

The president cited his own experience of overcoming political disagreements in 2021 and 2022, recalling his relationship with the late former President Muhammadu Buhari as an example of the importance of forgiveness and political accommodation.

“I had my own anger in 2021-22. I can invoke the memory of Muhammadu Buhari. When he called me, I said, okay, he said you are the coordinator, co-chairman of the campaign. I said, oh yeah. I had my own anger full of hurt feeling, but I let go,” he said.

Tinubu also drew lessons from his recent visit to the Holy Ka’aba, emphasising faith, gratitude and forgiveness. He urged supporters to extend to others the forgiveness they seek from Almighty Allah.

He warned that political disagreements should not undermine the party’s collective objectives, calling on members to replace resentment with reconciliation and renewed commitment to its electoral ambition.

On voter participation, the president expressed concern about low turnout in Lagos State, questioning why an electorate of about seven million registered voters would produce only between 100,000 and 200,000 votes.

“Even if you win, and you say a win is a win. No. You have to generate the maximum. And to all of you, it’s not anger. Anger will not do it,” he said.

He urged party supporters to encourage eligible Nigerians to register and collect their PVCs, stressing that electoral victory should be backed by substantial voter participation.

Recalling his use of the Emilokan slogan during the 2022 presidential campaign, Tinubu told supporters that the responsibility of securing his re-election now rested with them.

“It is now your turn (Eyinlokan) to make the re-election possible,” he said, appealing to Lagos residents to turn out in large numbers on election day.

The president also recalled the economic difficulties and cash shortages that preceded the 2023 general election, attributing his eventual victory to divine intervention and the determination of his supporters.

“We invoked the spirit of Almighty Allah and said, ‘Kun faya kun (Be, and it becomes). It got us there, and we have the presidency. Allah gives power to whom he wants, and he takes it from whoever he wants,” he said.

Tinubu thanked members of his family for their longstanding prayers and support throughout his political career. He also urged young Nigerians to embrace leadership responsibilities and contribute to national development.

Reaffirming his administration’s commitment to infrastructure and development, he expressed confidence in the country’s ability to overcome its challenges through collective effort.

“We can do it in this country. There’s nothing we cannot do. Nothing. Go to Abuja, we’ve moved mountains. In Lagos, we’ve tamed the Atlantic. In Abuja, we’ve moved mountains to give way to development. Nothing we cannot do if we put our heart at it,” he added.

The president thanked the organisers and clerics who prayed for him, his administration and the country.

The session was attended by Muslim and Christian clerics, as well as the governors of Lagos, Kaduna, Kwara, Ogun and Imo states.

Other dignitaries included the National Security Adviser, Mallam Nuhu Ribadu; Chief of Staff to the President, Femi Gbajabiamila; Lagos Deputy Governor, Kadiri Obafemi Hamzat; Minister of Education, Tunji Alausa; and Speaker of the Lagos State House of Assembly, Mudashiru Obasa.

Also present were the Grand Chief Imam of Lagos, Sheikh Sulaimon Oluwatoyin Abou-Nolla; Chairman of the Board of Trustees of the Coalition of Northern Groups, Dr Nasturah Ashir Sharriff; members of the Tinubu family and party faithful.

Earlier, Lagos State Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu described the president as an epitome of justice, fairness, equity and inclusive governance, saying the virtues were essential to building a united, peaceful and prosperous Nigeria.

Badaru said Northerners appreciated Tinubu’s support for Buhari’s victories in the 2015 and 2019 presidential elections. He urged voters in the region to reciprocate the gesture and support Tinubu’s re-election bid.

The chairman of the occasion, 94-year-old elder statesman Alhaji Lateef Femi Okunnu, pledged to canvass votes for the president and vote for him on January 16, 2027, saying Tinubu had done enough to merit another term.

Okunnu also stressed the need for justice and praised the administration’s focus on the economy and infrastructure, particularly the Lagos-Calabar Coastal Highway and the Sokoto-Badagry Superhighway, as well as efforts to strengthen the naira and foreign reserves.

In their vote of thanks, representatives of the Tinubu family, Alhaji Rafiu Tinubu and Alhaja Iyalode Bintu Tinubu, thanked God for the family’s blessings and urged Nigerians to support the president’s efforts to deliver the dividends of democracy.

Chairman of the Lagos State Government House Chapel, Rev. Honsu Oluremi, referenced the Bible and urged Nigerians to pray for the success of their leaders.

The coordinator of the prayer session, Abou-Nolla, also read from the Qur’an and prayed for Allah to grant the president success and good health.

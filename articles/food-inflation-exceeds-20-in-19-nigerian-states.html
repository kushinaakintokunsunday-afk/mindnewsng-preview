Food inflation exceeds 20% in 19 Nigerian states

By Punch Punch 2d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Food inflation exceeded 20 per cent in 19 Nigerian states in October 2026, despite a decline in the national annual food inflation rate, according to an analysi

The affected states represent 52.8 per cent of Nigeria’s 36 states, indicating that more than half experienced annual food price increases exceeding 20 per cent, even as the national rate moderated from 19.59 per cent in September to 19.13 per cent in October.

The findings were contained in the September and October 2026 CPI reports and accompanying statistical tables obtained by The PUNCH from the National Bureau of Statistics website on Friday.

Ondo recorded the highest annual food inflation at 31.74 per cent, up from 31.49 per cent in September, while Lagos recorded 31.43 per cent, down from 34.40 per cent.

Imo followed with 29.75 per cent, declining from 33.79 per cent, while Oyo recorded 28.81 per cent against 24.50 per cent in September.

According to the October report, “Food inflation on a Year-on-Year basis was highest in Ondo (31.74 per cent), Lagos (31.43 per cent), and Imo (29.75 per cent).”

Ebonyi’s rate increased from approximately 22.0 per cent to 27.96 per cent, while Rivers recorded 25.33 per cent, compared with about 28 per cent previously.

Other affected states included Abia at 24.22 per cent, Delta at 23.91 per cent, Enugu at 23.48 per cent, Zamfara at 22.88 per cent, Ogun at 22.47 per cent, Yobe at 22.26 per cent, Anambra at 22.23 per cent and Kebbi at 22.21 per cent.

Akwa Ibom recorded 21.56 per cent; Edo, 21.52 per cent; Sokoto, 21.40 per cent; Ekiti, 20.61 per cent, and Kaduna, 20.32 per cent.

The figures showed that two states recorded food inflation above 30 per cent, while six exceeded 25 per cent.

However, the monthly figures revealed a different pattern, with food prices declining in several states despite their high annual inflation rates.

Nationally, food prices declined by 0.75 per cent in October, following a steeper 1.55 per cent reduction in September.

This represented a 0.80 percentage-point increase in the monthly inflation rate, indicating that food prices were still falling nationally, but at a slower pace.

The NBS explained, “On a month-on-month basis, the Food inflation rate in October 2026 was -0.75 per cent, up by 0.80 percentage points from September 2026 (-1.55 per cent).”

The September report showed that monthly food inflation had fallen from a positive 1.02 per cent in August to -1.55 per cent in September, a decline of 2.57 percentage points.

Consequently, the October figures marked the second consecutive month of declining national food prices, although the pace of reduction weakened.

At the state level, Yobe recorded the most significant reversal, with monthly food inflation rising to 18.41 per cent in October from -7.0 per cent in September, a 25.41 percentage-point increase.

Kebbi followed with 16.45 per cent, compared with minus 4.9 per cent previously, while Sokoto rose to 13.0 per cent from minus 1.7 per cent.

Zamfara’s monthly rate increased to 8.7 per cent from 6.0 per cent, while Anambra rose to 4.3 per cent from minus 0.3 per cent.

Enugu recorded 4.9 per cent against 14.5 per cent previously, Abia increased to 2.6 per cent from 0.4 per cent, and Ebonyi rose to 2.4 per cent from -2.1 per cent.

Ogun’s monthly food inflation increased to 1.5 per cent from -0.4 per cent, Edo rose to 0.9 per cent from -2.8 per cent, and Imo slowed to 0.6 per cent from 5.2 per cent.

Ondo moderated to 0.6 per cent from 2.6 per cent, while Kaduna’s food prices declined by 4.7 per cent, reversing September’s 3.2 per cent increase.

In contrast, Lagos recorded a monthly decline of 5.93 per cent, compared with a 0.4 per cent increase in September. Rivers recorded -4.3 per cent against -2.0 per cent previously.

Delta recorded minus 3.8 per cent compared with a 2.2 per cent increase, Oyo recorded -2.6 per cent against 3.8 per cent, and Akwa Ibom recorded -1.5 per cent against -0.4 per cent.

Ekiti’s monthly food inflation improved to -0.4 per cent from -1.5 per cent.

Overall, 11 of the 19 states recorded monthly food price increases in October, while eight experienced declines, highlighting the uneven distribution of food price pressures.

The NBS attributed national food price movements to changes in the prices of vegetable oil, sardines, green peas, frozen fish, local rice, fresh catfish, beef tripe, palm oil and millet flour.

However, the NBS cautioned against drawing direct conclusions from comparisons between states because consumption patterns and the weights assigned to food items differ across locations.

N582m fraud: Court threatens ex-NNPC cooperative president with arrest

By Punch Punch 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Capital Territory High Court in Gwarinpa, Abuja, on Monday threatened to issue a warrant of arrest against a former President of the NNPC Staff Mult

Justice Leslie Wike gave the warning while adjourning the case until December 15, 2026, for the arraignment of Ogunbayo and his co-defendant, Abolade Nurudeen.

This was contained in a statement on the anti-graft agency’s social media handle on Monday.

“Justice Leslie Wike of the Federal Capital Territory, FCT, High Court in Gwarinpa, Abuja, on Monday, November 23, 2026, ordered the former President of the NNPC Staff Multi-Purpose Cooperative Society, Olalekan Ogunbayo, to appear in court on December 15, 2026, for his arraignment over an alleged N582m.

“The judge warned that Ogunbayo risked a warrant of arrest if he failed to appear on the next adjourned date,” the statement partly read.

At Monday’s proceedings, prosecution counsel, R.U. Adagba, informed the court that the matter was scheduled for arraignment but Ogunbayo was absent.

Adagba said the defendant was also absent at the previous sitting on October 26, despite an undertaking by his counsel to produce him in court.

She urged the court to issue a warrant of arrest against Ogunbayo pursuant to Sections 35, 114 and 115 of the Administration of Criminal Justice Act, 2015, citing his repeated failure to attend proceedings without explanation.

The prosecution counsel said efforts to secure his appearance, including written invitations and attempts to reach his surety, had proved unsuccessful.

She further argued that Ogunbayo was aware of the criminal proceedings, having personally deposed to an affidavit in support of a bail application before the court on October 13.

However, counsel to the first defendant, Innocent Adoo, acknowledged that he had undertaken to produce his client but explained that the prosecution had yet to serve him with a copy of the charge.

Adoo told the court that Ogunbayo contacted his head of chambers earlier on Monday, claiming he had received invitations from the Inspector-General of Police and the Code of Conduct Bureau over the same subject matter.

He said a copy of the charge would enable his client to establish that the matter was already before the court when responding to the invitations.

In her response, Adagba described the defence counsel’s position on service as a misrepresentation, insisting that the court had not made any order authorising service of the charge on him on behalf of the first defendant.

She said the prosecution had written to Ogunbayo’s office on October 22 and made efforts to reach his surety to ensure his appearance.

Adagba also clarified that the EFCC was prosecuting the charge before the court and was not a party to any separate proceedings involving the police or the Code of Conduct Bureau.

In her ruling, Justice Wike directed that the charge be served on Ogunbayo through his counsel, Adoo.

The judge warned that the defendant risked a warrant of arrest if he failed to appear at the next sitting.

The matter was subsequently adjourned until December 15, 2026 for arraignment.

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission is prosecuting the defendants on an eight-count charge bordering on criminal misappropriation, conspiracy and corruption over the alleged diversion of N582m realised from the sale of a property belonging to the cooperative society.

The Governorship candidate of the People’s Democratic Party (PDP) in Lagos State, Adedeji Doherty, has vowed that doctors and teachers will not pay tax if he emerges governor of the State.

Deji pledged while appearing as a guest on Channels Television’s Sunrise Daily on Monday.

“When I am governor, I will definitely transfer these palliatives, these subsidies to my people. For instance, the doctors will not pay tax. For instance, the teachers will not pay tax. Those two people will not pay tax.”

He promised to employ measures such as a tax holiday and transport subsidy funded from the Federal Government palliative to make life easy for the people of the state.

“You will get a subsidy on your drugs, you will get a subsidy on your transportation. This is giving money back to the people of Lagos State because I have it.”

The governorship candidate applauded President Bola Tinubu’s economic reforms, including increased Federation Account Allocation Committee (FAAC) allocation and lower import duties.


He also noted that private organisations can now plan their operation as a result of the economic stability brought by the President’s economic reforms.

“I support fully what my national leader is doing, the renewed hope agenda which is the agenda for the entire country.”

Commenting on the recent opposition attack in Lagos and across the country, he linked the incidents to the poor performance of state governors, including Lagos State.

He, however, argued that Lagos debt burden remains very high, alleging that Lagos has failed to implement the renewed hope agenda of the President at the state level.

“I do not support the agenda being materialised and crystallised in Lagos state.”

“All these fallouts of thuggery and removing of banners and tearing of posters and things like that definitely is what is going to happen because you are trying to cover up your inadequacies in government.”

The PDP governorship candidate urged the ruling party to create an enabling democratic environment for political activities to thrive. “The ruling party has to take responsibility,” he said.

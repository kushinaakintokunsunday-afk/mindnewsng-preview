Two death-row inmates escape Kwara prison

By Punch Punch 5d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Two inmates sentenced to death have escaped from the Oke-Kura Custodial Centre in Ilorin, Kwara State, after reportedly breaking through and scaling the facilit

The escapees, identified as Segun and Abraham, were said to have exploited security lapses at the correctional facility to make their way out in the early hours of Wednesday, prompting the authorities to launch a manhunt for their recapture.

The PUNCH gathered that Abraham was reportedly a repeat escapee, having earlier fled from the Mandala Custodial Centre before he was rearrested and transferred to Oke-Kura, where he subsequently escaped again.

Following the incident, the custodial centre was placed under heightened security, with officers reportedly put on red alert to prevent further breaches and forestall any untoward development.

Reacting to the incident while addressing journalists at the facility in Ilorin on Thursday, the Controller-General of the Nigerian Correctional Service, Sylvester Mwakuche, admitted that there were indications of negligence in the circumstances surrounding the escape.

Mwakuche, who spoke on the need to establish the level of culpability of both officers and inmates, described the incident as unfortunate and unacceptable, promising that those found responsible would face disciplinary action.

He said, “Naturally, they will face the music.”

The Controller-General disclosed that the incident had attracted the displeasure of the Minister of Interior, adding that the President would equally be unhappy with the development, considering the Federal Government’s efforts to improve the welfare of correctional officers.

“The Honourable Minister is not happy. Mr President won’t be happy. Because the President of this great nation has done a lot for staff,” he said.

He listed improved salaries, regular promotions and the payment of outstanding promotional arrears among the measures taken to enhance the welfare of personnel, stressing that officers had been warned against conduct capable of undermining security in custodial facilities.

“I’ve admonished the staff. I’ve also admonished the inmates. And I know that it will not occur again,” Mwakuche added.

He, however, assured that efforts were underway to recapture the fleeing inmates, saying the authorities had spread their dragnet to ensure their rearrest.

The Controller-General also pledged that investigations would establish the circumstances that enabled the inmates to escape and ensure that anyone found culpable was appropriately sanctioned.

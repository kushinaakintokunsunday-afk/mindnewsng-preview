2027: Adeola unveils ₦17trn Ogun development blueprint

Promises 50,000 annual jobs, 2,500km roads, 200MW additional power

By James Ogunnaike

ABEOKUTA — The governorship candidate of the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Ogun State, Senator Solomon Olamilekan Adeola, has unveiled a development blueprint featuring plans to generate more than 50,000 formal jobs annually, construct and rehabilitate 2,500 kilometres of roads and provide an additional 200 megawatts of electricity if elected governor in 2027.

Adeola unveiled the blueprint, tagged ITESIWAJU: Governance That Works for Everyone, at its formal launch at the June 12 Cultural Centre, Abeokuta, on Friday.

The manifesto, which outlines his proposed development agenda up to 2031, is anchored on nine policy pillars covering infrastructure, tourism, education, security, healthcare, women and youth empowerment, agriculture, industrialisation and urban renewal.

Addressing political leaders, traditional rulers and other stakeholders at the event, the senator said the document was developed following consultations with communities across the state’s 20 local government areas.

He described the manifesto as a social contract with the people, urging residents to scrutinise its contents and hold him accountable for the commitments should he emerge governor.

“Today, I place that agenda, built on your voices, back in your hands. This agenda is a plan, not a performance. Read it as you would read a contract, because that is exactly what it is,” he said.

Adeola pledged to sustain and expand the infrastructure and economic development initiatives of Governor Dapo Abiodun’s administration, stressing that he would not abandon ongoing projects or policies yielding positive results.

He commended successive administrations in the state, including those of former governors Olabisi Onabanjo, Olusegun Osoba, Gbenga Daniel and Ibikunle Amosun, for their contributions to Ogun’s development.

He particularly acknowledged Abiodun’s ISEYA Development Agenda, citing the Gateway International Agro-Cargo Airport, proposed Kajola and Ijebu-Ode dry ports, planned Gateway Deep Sea Port, road construction and housing projects as investments capable of strengthening the state’s economy.

According to Adeola, Ogun’s economy is currently valued at approximately ₦17 trillion, while its internally generated revenue has increased significantly.

“ISEYA laid the foundation for economic growth. ITESIWAJU will build the foundation for more economic growth and prosperity for our people,” he said.

He described Ogun’s strategic location, bordering Lagos State, the Republic of Benin and other Nigerian states, as a major advantage for industrialisation, trade, logistics and regional economic integration.

The senator proposed positioning Ogun as the “New Jersey” to Lagos, arguing that improved transportation, reliable electricity, planned residential communities and an investor-friendly environment would enable the state to accommodate businesses and workers seeking opportunities around Lagos.

However, he acknowledged that the benefits of economic growth had yet to reach many households, citing unemployment, unreliable electricity, inadequate agricultural storage facilities and pressure on healthcare services.

On employment and empowerment, Adeola promised to establish a ₦10 billion Enterprise and Empowerment Fund to support 60,000 businesses owned by women and young people.

He also pledged to train 100,000 youths in digital skills, including coding, data analysis, graphic design and digital marketing, as well as another 100,000 women in practical and entrepreneurial skills.

The manifesto further proposes reserving at least 30 per cent of senior government appointments for women.

He promised to establish sports hubs, youth academies and an annual Ogun State Sports Festival to identify and develop sporting talents.

A social safety net would also be introduced to support 20,000 widows, elderly citizens, persons living with disabilities and low-income households.

On infrastructure, Adeola pledged to construct and rehabilitate 2,500 kilometres of roads by 2031, with emphasis on rural, urban and border communities.

He said the programme would improve connectivity between farming communities and markets, facilitate access to essential services and stimulate economic activities across the three senatorial districts.

On electricity, he identified an estimated 600-megawatt power deficit in the state and promised to address it initially through 200 megawatts of embedded and renewable energy generation.

The proposed projects include the Oke-Mosan Independent Power Project, renewable energy investments around Oyan Dam, power development around Ewekoro and solar mini-grids for underserved communities.

He also pledged to advance the development of the Gateway International Agro-Cargo Airport, Kajola and Ijebu-Ode dry ports, the proposed Gateway Deep Sea Port and Blue Marine Special Economic Zone in Ogun Waterside.

On transportation, Adeola promised to introduce between 50 and 100 compressed natural gas-powered buses within 12 months of assuming office.

The buses, he said, would operate along priority routes, including Abeokuta, Sango-Ota to Lagos, Mowe-Ibafo-Berger, Sagamu to Abeokuta and Sagamu to Lagos.

He also proposed establishing a Gateway Metropolitan Area Transport Authority to coordinate public transportation and introducing OgunRide, a regulated ride-hailing service covering cars, tricycles and licensed motorcycles.

“No one in Ogun State should wait under a tree for a bus again,” he said.

On industrialisation, the APC candidate promised to strengthen industrial estates, establish export-oriented industrial clusters and transform border communities such as Idiroko, Imeko and Ipokia into commercial and logistics centres.

He said his administration would introduce an Ogun State Local Content and Skills Development Law to encourage businesses operating in the state to employ and develop local talent without imposing additional taxes or levies.

He also promised to create 12,000 apprenticeship and internship opportunities by his fourth year in office, including placements in the energy, minerals and petroleum supply chain sectors.

On agriculture, Adeola said only about 30 per cent of Ogun’s estimated 1.2 million hectares of arable land was currently cultivated.

He promised to increase the proportion to 60 per cent through a programme tagged “Grow Ogun, Feed Nigeria”, which would focus on mechanisation, irrigation, improved extension services, agricultural financing and market access.

He also proposed developing cocoa production hubs in Ogun East, cashew hubs in Ogun West and oil palm hubs in Ogun Central, linking them to Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones.

In education, the senator pledged to rehabilitate 2,000 classrooms, construct 500 new educational facilities and train more than 35,000 public-school teachers.

He also set a target of achieving 95 per cent primary school completion through improved learning facilities, technology integration, curriculum modernisation and better teacher welfare.

On healthcare, he promised to equip and adequately staff primary healthcare centres across the state’s wards and establish a Centre of Medical Excellence in each senatorial district.

He further pledged to expand the Ilera D’ero health insurance programme to cover two million residents by 2031 and reduce maternal mortality to below 200 deaths per 100,000 live births.

On security, Adeola promised to strengthen the Amotekun Corps, reposition the State Security Trust Fund and establish a Joint Intelligence Fusion Centre.

He also proposed deploying CCTV surveillance and drones in major towns and border communities within three years, while preparing the state for a possible transition to state policing, subject to the necessary national legislation.

The APC candidate identified tourism and the creative economy as additional sources of revenue and employment, promising to develop 12 flagship tourism destinations to attract 500,000 visitors annually and generate a projected ₦15 billion in revenue by 2031.

Among the attractions he highlighted were Olumo Rock, Bilikisu Sungbo Grove, the Ojude Oba Festival, Lisabi Festival, Oronna Festival and the coastal attractions of Ogun Waterside.

He also proposed establishing an Ogun Film Village to promote film production, fashion, music and the state’s indigenous Adire textile industry.

On natural resources, Adeola promised to maximise opportunities in oil, gas and solid minerals, expressing optimism about prospective commercial oil production around Eba Island.

He said his administration would pursue the state’s constitutional entitlement to 13 per cent derivation when applicable and proposed establishing a naval base on Tongeji Island to strengthen maritime security.

He also identified gold, tantalite, kaolin, limestone and bitumen as mineral resources that should be processed locally to create employment and generate greater economic value.

Under urban renewal, the senator promised to attract ₦500 billion in private investment into urban development, identifying the Ota-Agbara, Mowe, Sagamu, Lagos-Ogun border, Sagamu-Ijebu and Abeokuta-Owode corridors as priority areas.

He also proposed preparing physical development master plans for all 20 local government areas and establishing physical planning offices in each council.

On environmental management, he pledged to strengthen the Ogun State Waste Management Agency, introduce performance-based contracts for private waste operators, establish waste-to-energy facilities and expand recycling programmes.

He also promised to eliminate open defecation by 2031.

Beyond the nine policy pillars, Adeola identified six strategic enablers for implementing the manifesto. They include fiscal discipline and revenue mobilisation, justice sector reform, legislative reform, civil service reform, optimisation of government assets and local government autonomy.

He proposed a Single Window Revenue Collection System to improve transparency, reduce revenue leakages and eliminate multiple taxation.

According to him, the state’s revenue increased by 136 per cent to ₦238.5 billion in 2025, while federal allocations still account for approximately 40 per cent of government expenditure.

He promised to expand the state’s revenue base without imposing additional taxes or double taxation on investors.

On justice administration, he proposed mobile courts, fast-track courts and electronic justice systems to reduce delays and improve access to justice.

He also pledged merit-based recruitment, improved pension administration, digital workflows and sustained implementation of the minimum wage in the civil service.

Adeola further promised to ensure that statutory allocations reach the 20 local government areas directly, fully and promptly, in accordance with constitutional provisions.

To ensure accountability, he pledged to establish a Governor’s Delivery Unit within 30 days of assuming office.

He said commissioners and heads of government agencies would sign performance agreements tied to specific targets, while a public performance dashboard would track progress quarterly.

The indicators would include secondary school completion, household electricity access, youth unemployment, maternal mortality, internally generated revenue as a proportion of total revenue and access to primary healthcare facilities within five kilometres.

He also promised that citizens would be able to report service delivery failures and receive responses within 21 working days.

An independent panel, he said, would assess government performance twice yearly, while an annual State of Ogun Report would be presented to the public.

“We will measure progress not by the ribbons we cut, but by the lives we change,” he said.

Adeola outlined four major economic and social targets for 2031, promising to increase per capita income from approximately $1,740 to $2,500, raise annual economic growth from about four per cent to between six and seven per cent, and increase revenue per resident from ₦39,600 to ₦66,000.

He also pledged to reduce the proportion of households living in multidimensional poverty from 68 per cent to below 45 per cent.

The senator acknowledged that some proposals, including state policing, the extension of Lagos rail lines into Ogun and the realisation of oil derivation benefits, would require cooperation with the Federal Government.

He nevertheless promised to pursue the necessary partnerships and keep residents informed about their progress.

Adeola urged residents to scrutinise the manifesto and hold him accountable for its implementation if elected, saying the document should serve as a basis for assessing his performance in 2028, 2029 and 2031.

“I do not ask you to believe me. I ask you to hold me to this covenant,” he said.

He added: “We are not coming to rule over you. We are coming to work beside you. We will listen closely. We will learn with humility. We will act with the fear of God and with decisive resolve.”

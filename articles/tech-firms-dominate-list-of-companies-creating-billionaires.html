Tech firms dominate list of companies creating billionaires

By Punch Punch 17h ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Technology organisations are emerging as major engines of billionaire wealth, from the artificial intelligence firms reshaping the industry to social media plat

A study by trading firm Atmos found that seven of the 12 companies with at least five billionaire founders and employees were technology or technology-related businesses. The list was led by Anthropic, the Claude AI maker, which had seven billionaires, all co-founders, with a combined estimated fortune of $95.4bn.

The findings created a picture of how wealth is being created in businesses whose valuations can rise significantly as their products attract users, customers and investment. The report also drew attention to the role of company ownership in building fortunes, as founders and early employees benefit from shares and stock options when businesses expand.

The ranking suggests that technology’s contribution to billionaire wealth extends beyond the founders of household-name platforms. It includes employees who joined businesses, acquired equity and benefited as those companies grew. In the case of Meta, for example, three of the six billionaires identified by the study were employees rather than founders. At Alphabet, the parent company of Google, three of the five were also non-founders.

The distinction matters because it shows that the wealth generated by a successful technology company can spread beyond the people who started it. Employees who receive shares or stock options can build substantial fortunes if the company’s value rises, although the size of those gains depends on the equity they hold and the terms attached to it.

Anthropic illustrates the founder-led side of the trend. Established in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers, the artificial intelligence company develops Claude, a family of AI models and products. Its seven billionaire founders had an estimated combined fortune of $95.4bn, according to the Atmos study.

“Billionaires are rarely made with paychecks. Anthropic’s CEO earned about $18M in total pay in 2025, and only $654K of that was salary,” Nick Cooke, CEO of Atmos, commented on the study.

Meta, Facebook’s parent company, provides another example of how wealth can extend to employees. Founded in 2004 by Mark Zuckerberg and his co-founders, the US social media company has since expanded through acquisitions and the development of platforms, including Instagram and WhatsApp. The study identified six billionaires linked to the company, with a combined estimated fortune of $262.9bn. Three were founders, while the other three were former employees, including former Chief Operating Officer Sheryl Sandberg and former President Sean Parker.

Alphabet, the parent company of Google, had five billionaires with a combined estimated fortune of $553bn, the largest total among the businesses in the ranking. They included Google co-founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin, as well as former Chief Executive Officer Eric Schmidt, Alphabet Chief Executive Sundar Pichai and former Google executive Marissa Mayer.

Google was founded in 1998 by Page and Brin as an internet search engine before expanding into advertising, mobile operating systems, cloud computing, video and other digital services. Alphabet was established in 2015 as its parent holding company. The fortunes attributed to the group demonstrate how the growth of a technology business can create substantial wealth for founders and senior employees over time.

Chinese smartphone maker Xiaomi had five billionaires, all founders, with a combined fortune of $36bn. Its chairman and chief executive, Lei Jun, accounted for an estimated $20.9bn.

Contemporary Amperex Technology Company Limited, known as CATL, also had five billionaires, with a combined estimated wealth of $63bn. The Chinese company, established in 2011, manufactures batteries for electric vehicles and energy storage systems, placing it at the intersection of technology and the global transition towards electric mobility.

India’s Infosys, an information technology services and consulting company, had five billionaire founders with a combined estimated fortune of $13.3bn. Founded in 1981, the company is the only Indian business among the 12 firms highlighted in the study.

Snowflake, an American cloud data platform company, also had five billionaires, comprising two founders and three other employees. Their combined estimated fortune was $11.3bn. The company’s inclusion points to the wealth created not only by consumer-facing technology brands but also by businesses providing the data infrastructure used by other organisations.

Beyond technology founders

Although technology companies accounted for seven of the 12 businesses, the study found that other sectors have also produced billionaires through company ownership and employment.

Alfa Group, a Russian banking and investment group, had six billionaires, split equally between founders and other employees, with a combined estimated fortune of $44.4bn. Glencore, the Switzerland-based commodities trading and mining company, also had six, all of whom were employees rather than founders. Their combined wealth was estimated at $29bn.

Private equity firms Thoma Bravo and EQT each had five billionaires, while Chinese food manufacturer Foshan Haitian Flavouring and Food also recorded five. The figures show that the accumulation of substantial wealth through company ownership is not exclusive to technology, even if technology businesses feature prominently in the ranking.

Value of equity

The study’s central argument is that company ownership, rather than salary alone, is a major route through which founders and employees build billionaire fortunes.

“Many founders and early employees accept lower pay in the early years in exchange for stock or options, then wait. Even after a company lists, a 180-day lockup usually keeps insiders from selling right away, so most of the wealth stays on paper for a while longer,” Cooke added.

The explanation is particularly relevant to technology businesses, where founders and early employees may receive equity as part of their compensation. As a company attracts investment, expands its operations or lists on a stock exchange, the value of those holdings can increase. However, the wealth represented by shares is not necessarily cash available to the individuals, particularly where shares are subject to restrictions on sale.

The methodology also sets boundaries on what the ranking measures. Atmos examined nearly 100 companies and identified self-made billionaires on Forbes’ real-time list whose wealth was primarily linked to a company they founded or worked for. It excluded investors, board members and heirs, and counted each individual against only one company. The 12 businesses highlighted had at least five qualifying billionaires, although the wider study included companies with at least three.

The combined fortunes are estimates of the individuals’ net worth, not the market value of the companies or the value of their holdings alone. They are also tied to the date on which the estimates were compiled and may change as share prices and private-company valuations move.

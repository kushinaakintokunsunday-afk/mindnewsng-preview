Borno youths develop affordable local foods to tackle child malnutrition

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 2d ago 3 min read

From Timothy Olanrewaju, Maiduguri

Young innovators in Borno State have developed locally produced, ready-to-use nutritious foods aimed at combating child malnutrition, offering a potentially affordable and accessible alternative for vulnerable children suffering from nutritional deficiencies.

The food products, made from locally sourced ingredients, were unveiled at a youth-led nutrition challenge organised by the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) in Maiduguri at the weekend to commemorate the 2026 World Children’s Day.

Health experts at the event described the innovations as nutritious and affordable, while UNICEF said the initiative demonstrated the potential of young people to develop practical solutions to malnutrition using resources available within their communities.

UNICEF Chief of Maiduguri Field Office, Francis Butichi, said the competition was designed not only to address acute malnutrition but also to encourage young people to collaborate, innovate and contribute directly to improving child nutrition.

“The youth-led nutrition challenge is more than an effort to address acute malnutrition. It is a platform for young people to collaborate and create local solutions to a global problem.”

Butichi explained that the participants sourced nutritious ingredients locally to develop food products that could be affordable and accessible to children affected by malnutrition, particularly in vulnerable communities.

He said the initiative reflected UNICEF’s commitment to supporting homegrown innovations capable of improving children’s health and wellbeing.

According to him, the commemoration of World Children’s Day provided an opportunity to celebrate children while renewing commitments to their rights, survival, development and protection from preventable illnesses.

He stressed that every child deserved the opportunity to grow up healthy and fulfil their potential, adding that tackling acute malnutrition remained essential to achieving that objective.

“We urge our young people to keep going. The world is waiting for your creative ideas for the nutrition of children right here in Borno. UNICEF will continue to support you,” Butichi said.

Director of Community and Family Health at the Borno State Primary Health Care Development Board, Abdulwahab Mala, commended UNICEF for its interventions in child health and nutrition across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states.

Mala, who represented the Borno State Government at the event, said the state recognised the progress recorded in efforts to prevent and manage malnutrition, describing the youth-led initiative as a demonstration of collective commitment to improving child health.

“Malnutrition is a scourge and it is possible to deal with it. This collective initiative is a reflection of our bold commitment to child health,” he said.

He also commended the state government for expanding access to healthcare services across several local government areas, maintaining that improved healthcare delivery and collaborative interventions were essential to addressing the nutritional needs of vulnerable children.

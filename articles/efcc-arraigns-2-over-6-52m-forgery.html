EFCC arraigns 2 over $6.52m forgery

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has arraigned two defendants, Olasubomi Osinusi and Emenike Mgbemena, over an alleged $6.52 million forgery

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has arraigned two defendants, Olasubomi Osinusi and Emenike Mgbemena, over an alleged $6.52 million forgery before Justice Mojisola Dada of the Special Offences Court sitting in Ikeja, Lagos.

The defendants were arraigned yesterday on an amended eight-count charge bordering on conspiracy, possession of fraudulent documents and the alleged use of fabricated evidence to procure a judgment of $6,520,190 against the Federal Government of Nigeria.

They were accused of conspiring to fabricate and use written statements on oath purportedly made by one Dan Mohammed Jafaru to mislead the High Court of Justice of England and Wales in proceedings involving Louis Emovbira Williams (at large), the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) and the Federal Government.

Count one of the charge alleged that Osinusi, Mgbemena and Williams, on or about March 25, 2013, in Lagos, conspired to fabricate and use a written statement on oath purportedly made by Jafaru as evidence in a claim before the High Court of Justice of England and Wales involving Williams and the CBN.

The alleged offence was said to be contrary to Sections 409 and 88(1) of the Criminal Law of Lagos State, 2011, and punishable under Section 88(1) of the same law.

Count three similarly accused the defendants of conspiring to fabricate and use the purported statement on oath to procure a judgment sum of $6,520,190 against the Federal Government of Nigeria in a case involving Williams, the Federal Government and the Attorney-General of the Federation.

The defendants, however, pleaded not guilty to all the charges.

Following their pleas, prosecution counsel, Muhammed Abbah, SAN, urged the court to remand them in a correctional centre pending the continuation of trial.

Abbah, however, said the prosecution had no objection to the first defendant, Osinusi, who was initially arraigned on February 25, 2026, on a two-count charge bordering on forgery, continuing to enjoy the bail earlier granted to him.

He explained that Osinusi had consistently attended court since the commencement of proceedings.

On the second defendant, Mgbemena, the prosecution counsel told the court that he was a dual citizen of Nigeria and the United Kingdom and had allegedly failed to honour several invitations extended to him by the EFCC since 2024 and subsequently in 2025.

Abbah said the Commission had evidence that an invitation letter was dispatched to Mgbemena’s UK address, adding that the defendant also responded electronically to an email from one of the EFCC investigators.

According to him, the invitation was also scanned and forwarded to Mgbemena through email and WhatsApp, but he failed to honour it at the time.

The prosecution counsel said Mgbemena was eventually traced to a location in Nigeria following his return to the country, after which he reported to the EFCC and was granted administrative bail.

He, however, left the decision on whether the second defendant should be remanded in EFCC custody or a correctional centre to the discretion of the court.

Earlier, counsel to Mgbemena, Obinna S. Nwosu, told the court that his client was served with the amended charges on the afternoon of Friday, November 20, 2026.

He explained that he could not file a bail application immediately because he was in Abuja at the time.

Nwosu said the defence would file a formal bail application for his client, whom he described as a senior member of the Bar.

He also informed the court that Mgbemena had earlier been granted administrative bail by the EFCC and that his Nigerian and British passports were still in the custody of the Commission.

In her remarks, Justice Dada observed that the submissions before the court showed that Mgbemena had been invited by the EFCC but failed to honour the invitation at the time.

In her ruling, the judge ordered that Mgbemena be remanded in a correctional centre pending the determination of his bail application.

She, however, allowed Osinusi to continue enjoying the bail earlier granted to him.

Justice Dada adjourned the hearing of Mgbemena’s bail application to December 7, 2026, while February 8 and 9, 2027, were fixed for the commencement of trial.

Court freezes Governors’ Forum account over $100m debt, restrains 29 banks

By Ikechukwu Nnochiri

ABUJA –A Federal Capital Territory (FCT) High Court in Abuja has issued an interim order freezing all accounts operated by the Nigerian Governors’ Forum (NGF), following an alleged failure to release the sum of $100 million that was paid in by the Federal Government.

In an ex-parte order, Justice Charles Agbaza granted an interim Mareva injunction restraining the Incorporated Trustees of the NGF from withdrawing, transferring or dissipating the funds.

It further restrained 29 commercial banks from releasing, transferring, converting or otherwise dealing with funds belonging to the NGF that are in their custody.

The ruling followed a legal action relating to the Paris Club refunds, which were payments for over-deductions made from the Federation Account Allocation Committee (FAAC) for external debt servicing between 1995 and 2002.

The court had affirmed that the claimant, Dr. Ted Iseghohi-Edwards, rendered legal services in connection with the London and Paris Club refunds owed to Nigeria’s 774 Local Government Areas.

He subsequently secured a judgment in respect of those services, which remains unsatisfied.

Justice Agbaza directed the listed banks to disclose, within 48 hours of being served with the order, whether the NGF maintains accounts containing the said funds.

The banks are required to identify such accounts, their currencies, available balances and the amounts preserved under the court’s order.

The amount represents $100 million, equivalent to N135.55 billion at an exchange rate of N1,355.80 to the dollar.

The orders, dated November 19, were made after the court heard an application moved by the claimant’s counsel, Emonye Adekwu, SAN, and Damilola Odesola.

The claimant approached the court seeking recovery of the judgment debt, pursuant to an indemnity he said was issued by the NGF on December 20, 2018.

The indemnity reportedly arose from an arrangement under which the Federal Government paid $100 million to the NGF as stakeholder.

The claimant had sued the FG over non-payment of his consultancy fees relating to the Paris Club refunds.

In suit number FHC/ABJ/CS/856/2016, the Federal High Court in Abuja, on February 16, 2018, delivered judgment establishing the government’s indebtedness of $318 million.

The claimant said the government subsequently made a part-payment of $159 million through promissory notes.

He contended that the judgment fulfilled the condition contained in the NGF’s indemnity, thereby making the $100 million payable directly to him.

Iseghohi-Edwards said he formally demanded payment from the NGF in a letter dated October 8, 2026, but the forum allegedly failed to honour the undertaking.

Consequently, he approached the FCT High Court, seeking to preserve the money pending the determination of his substantive claim.

In the application, he expressed apprehension that the NGF might transfer, disburse or dissipate the funds, thereby frustrating enforcement of any eventual judgment.

He also told the court that he could not identify the specific bank accounts holding the funds without judicial intervention.

The claimant argued that the injunction was necessary to preserve the subject matter of the dispute and prevent the proceedings from becoming futile.

He maintained that the restriction was limited to one aggregate sum of $100 million across the NGF and all participating banks.

The application was brought pursuant to Orders 30(3) and 32(1) of the FCT High Court Civil Procedure Rules and the court’s inherent jurisdiction.

The claimant also undertook to abide by any order for damages should the court subsequently determine that the interim injunction ought not to have been granted.

Justice Agbaza adjourned the matter until December 1 and ordered service of the order on all the respondents.

The Incorporated Trustees of the Nigerian Governors’ Forum is listed as the 1st respondent in the suit, while 29 banks are joined as co-respondents.

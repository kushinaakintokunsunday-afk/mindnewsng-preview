I nearly quit football after 18-month injury ordeal – Kulusevski

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Tottenham Hotspur and Sweden winger Dejan Kulusevski has revealed that he considered walking away from football during an 18-month injury nightmare that left hi

The 26-year-old admitted that his prolonged battle with a serious knee injury became so emotionally draining that he eventually told his mother he no longer believed he could continue playing football.

In an interview with BBC Sport on Friday, the Swede was quoted as saying, “I told my mother. I sat with her and I told her: ‘Listen, I don’t think I can play any more,'” Kulusevski revealed in an emotional interview with Tottenham’s in-house media.

The Swedish international has not started a competitive match since May 11, 2025, when he suffered a serious right knee injury during Tottenham’s Premier League encounter against Crystal Palace.

Initially expected to spend only a few months on the sidelines, complications prolonged his recovery, forcing him to undergo multiple operations and miss the entire 2025-26 campaign, including the 2026 FIFA World Cup.

Reflecting on the emotional impact of his absence, Kulusevski admitted that watching his teammates celebrate Tottenham’s Europa League triumph against Manchester United while he struggled with crutches was particularly painful.

“That was one of the hardest days of my life,” he said.

“It was so beautiful to see all the people happy, but for me I was going there with crutches. I couldn’t work.

“I couldn’t even go and get my medal because it was too slippy to go on that thing [platform].”

The former Juventus player also described the loneliness of watching his teammates enjoy their success while he remained sidelined.

“Seeing the team celebrating on the bus [outside Tottenham Hotspur Stadium] was probably one of the most difficult parts of this journey because you feel so alone.

“You feel like the whole world has forgotten about you and what you put in.”

Kulusevski’s recovery was further complicated by repeated setbacks, with the midfielder revealing that he was even offered a knee transplant as a possible treatment option, which he rejected.

“You get stressed because nothing works,” he added.

Another major disappointment came when Sweden manager Graham Potter informed him that he would not recover in time to represent his country at the 2026 World Cup.

Despite remaining hopeful of making the tournament, the news left him devastated.

“I just stared at the wall for three hours because I really believed I would do it,” he recalled.

His wife, Eldina Ahmic, also spoke about the difficulties of watching her husband endure such a challenging period, particularly as he missed Tottenham’s European triumph and every Premier League game during the following campaign.

Kulusevski has now made a full recovery and is closing in on his first Tottenham start since May 2025, offering a positive end to a difficult chapter in his career.

His story highlights the psychological challenges that accompany long-term injuries in professional football, with isolation, uncertainty and frustration often making rehabilitation as difficult mentally as it is physically.

For Kulusevski, the road back has been about more than recovering his fitness. It has been a fight to rediscover his confidence, belief and love for the game.

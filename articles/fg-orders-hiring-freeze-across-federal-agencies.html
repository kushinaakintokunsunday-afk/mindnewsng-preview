FG orders hiring freeze across federal agencies

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG

The government also prohibited federal institutions from creating additional positions or expanding their organisational structures without approval from the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation.

The directives were contained in a November 9, 2026, circular titled ‘2027 Workforce Budget Call Circular,’ issued by the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation and signed by the Permanent Secretary, Service Policies and Strategies Office, Ishiyaku Musa Mohammed.

The circular, addressed to the Chief of Staff to the President, Deputy Chief of Staff to the Vice President, Secretary to the Government of the Federation, ministers, permanent secretaries, service chiefs, heads of federal institutions and chief executives of government agencies, directed all Ministries, Departments and Agencies to submit their workforce budget proposals for the 2027 fiscal year for consideration and approval.

It said the directive was aimed at consolidating the gains of the Federal Government’s public service reform programmes, particularly in human capacity development, while ensuring that personnel requirements were properly captured in the next budget.

The Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission assessed 344 MDAs in its 2025 Ethics and Integrity Compliance Scorecard, according to reports, indicating that Nigeria has over 300 federal MDAs.

The circular stated, “MDAs are advised to strictly maintain their current staff position for the 2027 fiscal year.”

It, however, acknowledged that some institutions might have genuine manpower needs but insisted that requests for additional personnel must be properly justified and supported by evidence of funding.

“However, where it is absolutely necessary to seek an increase, such requests should be supported with justifications attaching relevant information, including financial provision in the budget to meet additional emolument commitment; otherwise the existing workforce ceiling should not be exceeded,” it stated.

Additional departments banned

The government further warned against creating additional departments, units and other organisational structures without clearance from the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation.

It directed that every workforce budget proposal must be accompanied by a copy of the institution’s approved organisational structure, stressing that vacancies must not be created for positions in structures that had not received official approval.

The circular specifically cautioned MDAs against creating unauthorised vacancies, particularly for positions at Salary Grade Levels 15 to 17 or their equivalents, which cover senior and top-level officers in the federal public service.

The directive effectively places restrictions on workforce expansion across federal institutions, requiring agencies seeking additional personnel to demonstrate that their requests are necessary, approved, and financially sustainable.

The circular further mandated MDAs to submit detailed nominal rolls of their employees as of October 31, 2026, alongside their workforce budget proposals, in what appears to be an effort to establish the actual number of workers across federal institutions and verify their personnel records.

It prescribed three categories of nominal rolls: an omnibus nominal roll arranged according to grade levels, a department-by-department record, and a cadre-by-cadre record.

Under the omnibus arrangement, institutions are required to list officers from the highest to the lowest grade levels, irrespective of their cadres.

For departmental records, employees must be listed according to their departments, beginning with the director or most senior officer, while the cadre-by-cadre nominal roll must capture personnel in each cadre according to seniority.

Requirements for promotion

The government also directed MDAs to submit the names of all officers due for promotion during the 2027 budget year, indicating their cadres and salary grade levels.

Other documents required include a summary of promotion cases according to cadres and salary grade levels, a breakdown of vacancies available for recruitment, and a summary of the number of officers in each cadre.

The circular stated that the information must be submitted using the prescribed formats, including personnel cost summaries, workforce budget summaries and details of existing staff positions.

It also directed institutions operating different salary structures to harmonise them into either the Salary Grade Level 03–17 or 02–15 structure, as appropriate.

The government fixed November 20, 2026, as the deadline for submitting the workforce budget proposals, which must be endorsed by the Permanent Secretary or Chief Executive/Accounting Officer of each institution.

The submissions are to be forwarded electronically in Excel spreadsheet format to the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation through designated email addresses.

Budget consequences

The government warned that failure to comply with the November 20 deadline could have serious consequences for the affected institutions’ personnel budget provisions.

According to the circular, bilateral discussions with ministries, departments and agencies to examine their workforce budget proposals would commence on November 23, 2026.

It directed institutions to adhere strictly to the meeting schedule, warning that requests to reschedule the discussions would not be entertained.

More significantly, the government threatened to exclude defaulting institutions from the 2027 authorised establishment process, potentially leaving them without personnel provisions in the next Appropriation.

It stated, “Any MDA that fails to submit its workforce budget by Friday, 20th November, 2026 or fails to attend the meetings to defend its proposal may not have Authorised Establishments for the year 2027.

“This would imply that provisions may not be made in the Appropriation for the year under consideration.”

The implication is that institutions that fail to submit their proposals or defend their manpower requirements could be left without approved staffing establishments for the 2027 fiscal year, potentially affecting their ability to secure budgetary provisions for personnel requirements.

The government also directed every MDA to attend the bilateral discussions with copies of its approved organisational structure or organogram and the current year’s staff-in-post records, showing established positions and vacancies.

It further instructed ministries to submit lists of all agencies under their supervision and ensure that the agencies participate in the 2027 manpower budget exercise. The circular warned that inaccuracies or shortcomings in the records submitted would attract sanctions under the Public Service Rules.

“Inaccuracies and shortcomings in records submitted shall be treated in accordance with the Public Service Rule 100402,” it stated.

The meetings are scheduled to be held at the Conference Room of the Permanent Secretary, Service Policies and Strategies Office, on the third floor of Block A, Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation, Federal Secretariat Complex, Abuja.

The circular concluded by directing all concerned institutions to ensure strict compliance with its provisions.

The latest directive comes amid a series of measures by the Federal Government to strengthen personnel management, curb irregularities in the public service payroll and ensure that government institutions operate within approved staffing and expenditure limits.

It also represents another layer of scrutiny in the preparation of the 2027 budget, following earlier measures introduced by the Budget Office of the Federation to tighten controls over personnel expenditure and the eligibility of government agencies to receive public funding.

In September 2026, the Budget Office issued a separate 2027 Personnel Costs Budget Call Circular, directing MDAs to submit their personnel cost proposals alongside their establishment Acts to establish their legal status.

The circular, signed by the Director-General of the Budget Office, Tanimu Yakubu, warned that failure to submit the required establishment Acts could lead to the rejection of an agency’s budget proposal.

The Budget Office also directed that no personnel cost provision should be made in the 2027 budget for serving federal employees who were not captured on the Integrated Personnel and Payroll Information System or enrolled on the Government Integrated Financial Management Information System, except where specific exemptions had been granted.

The measures followed concerns over the existence of fictitious government agencies and questionable personnel expenditure, with the government seeking to prevent unauthorised institutions and workers from accessing public funds.

The new directive by the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation, however, places additional emphasis on the actual workforce strength, approved organisational structures, vacancies, promotions and manpower requirements of individual institutions.

It comes against the backdrop of the Federal Government’s ongoing Personnel Audit and Skills Gap Analysis, initiated in August 2025 to determine the actual size, structure and skills composition of the federal civil service.

The exercise, coordinated by the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation, was designed to identify the competencies required across government institutions and establish a more efficient and fit-for-purpose public service.

The government said the audit would help identify existing workforce gaps and determine the skills required to improve service delivery.

FG backs Abuja aviation school as regional training hub

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria is targeting a reduction in its annual foreign aviation training expenditure as the Federal Government backs the development of Baze Aviation School int

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, stated this on Friday, during a familiarisation and inspection tour of the aviation training facility in Bwari, Abuja, where he described the project as a major investment in local capacity development and the future of the aviation industry.

He noted that the facility would enable Nigeria to retain a significant portion of the money currently spent sending aviation personnel abroad for specialised training, while positioning the country as a destination for aviation education.

“We spend a lot of money every year sending our pilots and other aviation personnel to different parts of the world for training. With this facility, we expect that a significant part of that expenditure will be retained within Nigeria,” Keyamo said.

The minister noted that Nigerian airline captains and other aviation professionals currently travelled to countries such as South Africa and the United States for specialised training and simulator services, with airlines and employers bearing substantial costs.

He expressed optimism that the proposed simulator centre, equipped with facilities associated with major aircraft manufacturers such as Boeing and Airbus, would reduce the country’s dependence on foreign training institutions.

Beyond serving Nigerian aviation professionals, Keyamo said the institution was expected to attract trainees from neighbouring countries and become one of the leading aviation training centres in West and Central Africa.

He, however, warned government officials against frustrating the project through bureaucratic delays, assuring its promoters of the Federal Government’s support.

“Nobody should frustrate this project. No bureaucracy or official should keep your files or hide them somewhere, forcing you to run from one office to another. Don’t go around looking for anybody. Don’t even look for me. Let this project come to fruition,” he said.

Keyamo described the initiative as a national development project that should enjoy support irrespective of ethnic, religious or political differences. He urged the promoters to report any official attempting to obstruct its progress, promising to intervene within his supervisory and regulatory responsibilities.

Earlier, the founder of Baze University, Datti Baba-Ahmed, disclosed that the aviation school was being developed as a comprehensive aviation ecosystem, comprising a runway, hangars, terminal, academic facilities, accommodation and emergency response infrastructure.

He said the master plan included four hangars, a heliport, airside facilities, a runway safety area, an air traffic control building, airline management offices, aviation security facilities, a clinic and an emergency response centre.

The project would also accommodate original equipment manufacturers, with provisions for maintenance, repair and overhaul facilities, hotels and other hospitality services.

Giving an update on construction, Baba-Ahmed said the apron was being developed to cover 500 metres, with 155 metres already completed.

He added that work on the granular sub-base for the first 1,000 metres of the runway was about 95 per cent completed, while the remaining section of the initial kilometre was expected to be finished by the end of November.

According to him, the first phase of runway pavement construction would begin in December, followed by asphalt surfacing, after which the institution would seek approval from the aviation minister to commence flight training.

Baba-Ahmed said the first 1,000 metres of the runway was expected to be ready by January, providing capacity to accommodate virtually all categories of light aircraft.

He also disclosed that the school’s two Grand Caravan aircraft had arrived in Nigeria, adding that the aircraft and ongoing infrastructure development had generated interest among prospective trainees and parents seeking aviation careers for their children.

He said the institution was undertaking much of the construction itself to ensure quality, timely delivery and cost efficiency, while engaging specialised partners for technical aspects of the project.

The development has also generated expectations among host communities, with local leaders anticipating employment opportunities, improved economic activities and access to aviation careers for their children.

A representative of Gaba community and retired police officer, Mr Bulus Luka, said the institution would transform the economic and social fortunes of surrounding communities.

He expressed optimism that young people from Gaba, Nako and Tokolo villages could eventually become pilots and other aviation professionals through opportunities created by the school.

Luka said businesses had already begun springing up around the facility, adding that economic activities would increase as construction progressed and the institution commenced operations.

He, however, called for cooperation among traditional rulers and community leaders in the three communities to ensure residents maximised the benefits of the development.

The aviation school is expected to create a new training pathway for Nigerian and regional aviation professionals, while opening opportunities for investment, employment and the development of supporting aviation services.

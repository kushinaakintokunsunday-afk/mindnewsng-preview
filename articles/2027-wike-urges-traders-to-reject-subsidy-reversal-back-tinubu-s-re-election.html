2027: Wike urges traders to reject subsidy reversal, back Tinubu’s re-election

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

• says tough reforms will deliver prosperity, cites increased allocations, pension payments, end to ASUU strikes Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT)

• says tough reforms will deliver prosperity, cites increased allocations, pension payments, end to ASUU strikes

Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Nyesom Wike, hasurged Nigerians to reject opposition politicians promising to reverse the removal of fuel subsidy, insisting that President Bola Tinubu’s difficult economic reforms are necessary to secure the country’s long-term prosperity.

Wike made the appeal on Wednesday at an interactive town hall meeting with market traders in Abuja. He also urged them to mobilise support for Tinubu’s re-election in 2027, arguing that previous administrations failed to address the subsidy regime because they lacked the courage to take difficult decisions.

Acknowledging the economic hardship occasioned by the subsidy removal, the minister described the former arrangement as a scam that nearly crippled the country, insisting that the reforms’ gains would become more evident if the policies were allowed to mature.

“This country was nearly gone. To try to change what is happening means you must lay a foundation first, and when you lay a foundation, you give it time to grow,” he said.

Wike argued that successive administrations recognised the problems associated with fuel subsidy but failed to address them due to political considerations.

“There’s no government has not identified that this oil subsidy is a scam, why university teachers cannot work, no payment of salaries, no payment of pensioners. Everybody is afraid, they lacked courage. If you don’t have good leaders who have courage to take decisions, that country will not grow,” he said.

The minister listed increased revenue allocations to states and local governments, clearance of pension arrears and the absence of strikes by the Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU) for more than three and a half years among the benefits of the reforms.

He warned Nigerians against politicians campaigning on promises to reverse the policies, saying such assurances could derail the country’s recovery.

“If we allow these policies that the government has put in place to mature, what is going to happen in the next four years will be good. Forget about people coming to tell you stories,” he said.

Turning to his administration’s performance in the FCT, Wike said ongoing and completed road projects across satellite towns and districts, including Gwarinpa and Kubwa, were designed to improve commercial activities and reduce transportation costs for traders.

He also promised to facilitate further development of markets in the territory and institutionalise regular engagements with market associations at least twice a year.

The minister highlighted what he described as significant institutional changes in the FCT under Tinubu, including the appointment of an indigene of the territory to represent it in the Federal Executive Council for the first time since its creation in 1976.

He also cited the establishment of the FCT Civil Service Commission, which provides career progression opportunities for civil servants to attain the positions of Permanent Secretary and Head of Service, as well as the creation of Mandate Secretariats for Women and Youth.

Wike urged the traders to assess political leadership based on performance rather than ethnic or regional considerations.

“Governance has nothing to do with where I come from. It is not about Hausa, Yoruba, or Igbo; it is about ‘If I do my business here, am I happy?'” he said.

He also criticised opposition figures, questioning former Vice President Atiku Abubakar’s handling of past privatisation exercises and faulting former Rivers State Governor Rotimi Amaechi over projects he said were left uncompleted, including the Port Harcourt monorail and a proposed 1,000-bed specialist hospital.

The minister urged the traders to become actively involved in the 2027 campaign structure, asking market associations to nominate two representatives from each state chapter to serve on the campaign committee. He promised logistical support for grassroots mobilisation.

Responding, market leaders pledged to mobilise support for Tinubu and other candidates aligned with the administration in the 2027 elections, citing infrastructure development and market reforms in the FCT.

The Group Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of Abuja Investments Company Limited (AICL), Ambassador Maureen Tamuno, described the meeting as a landmark engagement, saying it demonstrated Wike’s willingness to interact directly with traders and address issues affecting their businesses.

She commended the minister’s road construction and rehabilitation projects across the city centre and satellite towns, particularly the Saburi Road corridor, which she said had improved commercial movement.

Tamuno also disclosed that Wike had approved the procurement of a dedicated fire truck and establishment of a fire station within Wuse Market, describing the intervention as a major step towards improving safety.

She urged traders to reciprocate government interventions by complying with market rules and regulations.

Chairman of the Wuse Market Traders Association, Okorie Ikechukwu-Raphael, commended the minister for the rehabilitation of internal market roads, redevelopment of shops and opening up of communities across the FCT.

He appealed for approval for AICL to develop new shops and malls in emerging districts, including Katampe and Kaura, to provide traders with opportunities to expand their businesses.

He also declared the readiness of traders to commence grassroots political mobilisation through trade unions, cooperatives and community groups.

“Today, Your Excellency, we want to confirm to you that we are going into cluster mobilisation. Whoever follows you knows the road,” he said.

Similarly, Chairman of the Arewa Traders Association, Alhaji Adamu Ibrahim, pledged the support of northern traders for Tinubu, Vice President Kashim Shettima and other candidates aligned with the administration in the FCT.

He urged the minister to give market leaders a stronger voice in the formulation of policies affecting their businesses.

Also speaking, Chairperson of the National Assembly Quarters (Zone A) Market, Mrs Abiola Adekanbi, who said she had lived in Abuja for 26 years, described the pace of road construction under Wike as unprecedented.

She recalled the rehabilitation of previously deplorable roads around Sunnyvale and pledged the support of market women for Tinubu’s re-election.

Adekanbi said the traders would not only vote for the President but also remain at polling units to protect their votes.

“It is very simple: we are going to follow who knows the road. We are going to follow the ‘talk-and-do,'” she said.

CAS Restores Senegal’s AFCON 2025 Title, Overturns CAF Decision

Gambiaj.com – (LAUSANNE, SWITZERLAND) – The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) has officially declared Senegal the winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON), overturning a controversial decision by the Confederation of African Football (CAF) that had stripped the Teranga Lions of their title and awarded it to Morocco.

In a ruling delivered unanimously on Tuesday, 24 November, in Lausanne, Switzerland, the sports tribunal upheld Senegal’s appeal against CAF and the Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF), confirming the original 1-0 victory secured by the Senegalese team on the pitch.

In a statement to the media, CAS said it had accepted the appeal filed by the Senegalese Football Federation (FSF), annulled the decision of CAF’s Appeals Board and reinstated the earlier ruling of the CAF Disciplinary Board.

“ The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) has upheld the appeal filed by the Senegalese Football Federation (FSF) against the Confederation of African Football (CAF) and the Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF) concerning the final of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON). The decision of the CAF Appeals Board is set aside, and the decision of the CAF Disciplinary Board is reinstated. Consequently, the result of the 2025 AFCON final is confirmed, with Senegal winning 1-0,” the tribunal said in a statement to the media.

Consequently, the result of the final, which Senegal won 1-0 after extra time, was confirmed, restoring the continental title to the West African nation.

The verdict ends nearly ten months of uncertainty and escalating tensions between Senegal and Morocco following the controversial final played in Rabat on 18 January 2026.

Senegal had initially been crowned champions after a closely contested final that descended into controversy during stoppage time. Several Senegalese players left the pitch in protest after the referee awarded Morocco a penalty, returning nearly 15 minutes later.

Morocco’s Brahim Diaz subsequently missed the penalty, and the match proceeded into extra time, where Senegal secured a 1-0 victory to lift the trophy.

However, the celebrations were overshadowed when CAF’s Appeals Board, acting on a complaint from the Moroccan federation, ruled on 17 March 2026 that Senegal had forfeited the final.

Invoking Articles 82 and 84 of the AFCON regulations, which prohibit teams from leaving the field before the end of a match without the referee’s authorization, CAF awarded Morocco a 3-0 victory by forfeit.

The decision triggered strong protests from the Senegalese federation, which described it as “the grossest administrative robbery in the history of our sport” and subsequently took the matter to CAS.

The tribunal held a closed-door hearing in Lausanne on 8 October, during which it heard arguments from the parties for more than five hours. It had subsequently indicated that its decision would be announced towards the end of November.

Morocco Reports Alleged Contact With CAS Arbitrator

The ruling comes amid fresh controversy surrounding an alleged incident involving FSF president Abdoulaye Fall.

The Moroccan federation had formally written to CAS reporting an alleged telephone conversation between Fall and one of the three arbitrators appointed to decide the case.

However, Fall firmly denied the allegation when contacted by RFI, saying he was “surprised” by the report and insisting that he had not had any telephone communication with any of the arbitrators.

The allegation added another layer of tension to an already contentious dispute between the two football federations.

Meanwhile, CAS’s official website experienced technical disruptions as news of the verdict emerged, reportedly due to an exceptional surge in visitors seeking access to the ruling.

The decision restores Senegal’s status as African champions and allows the country to officially add a second star to its national team’s jersey, following its first AFCON triumph in 2021.

Police deploy across Ibadan as NURTW threatens park takeover

By Punch Punch 17h ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Men of the Nigeria Police Force and the Nigerian Army on Monday embarked on patrols across parts of Ibadan, the Oyo State capital, in a show of force amid threa

The PUNCH sighted the patrol vehicles, with sirens blaring, moving from Challenge through Iwo Road to the Ojoo area of the state capital.

Combined teams of the police, the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps and volunteer policemen were also stationed at bus terminals in Challenge, Iwo Road, Ojoo, the Iwo Road underbridge and the area before World Oil motor park.

The security teams had arrived at the parks early on Monday morning. One of the stationed policemen was heard daring NURTW members to show up, adding that security agencies were on the ground.

There was palpable fear on Monday morning as traders at the parks made calls to ascertain the situation before leaving their homes.

A woman who spoke with The PUNCH at the Iwo Road bus terminal, a POS operator, said, “I had to call before coming out as we have been told that the NURTW members are coming to take over the parks. But so far, it is still the PMS members that are in charge.”

The tension was fuelled by a viral notice circulated on Sunday, asking residents of the state capital to avoid the Iwo Road, Ojoo and Challenge areas and warning of a possible clash between NURTW members and park managers over control of motor parks on Monday.

The notice, sighted by The PUNCH, added, “If possible, stay indoors throughout the day.”

The Oyo State Police Public Relations Officer, Ayanlade Olayinka, had in a statement allayed fears, saying the command had deployed adequate personnel and enhanced surveillance and intelligence-gathering mechanisms across strategic locations in the state.

The police urged the two parties to embrace dialogue and explore legitimate and appropriate legal channels for resolving their differences.

“No individual or group should resort to self-help, violence, intimidation or any action capable of threatening public peace,” the police urged.

The command, however, vowed to deal decisively with anyone found engaging in conduct capable of disturbing public peace.

The NURTW had threatened to take over motor parks across Oyo State on Monday, accusing Governor Seyi Makinde’s administration of repeatedly disobeying court judgments recognising its right to manage the parks.

The union said it would begin enforcing the judgments if the state government failed to withdraw its Park Management System from motor parks in the state’s 33 local government areas.

It described its exclusion from managing the parks as seven and a half years of unlawful exclusion, hardship and humiliation under the state government’s management arrangement.

In a joint statement signed by its state leaders, Abideen Olajide and Lekan Alesinloye, the union accused the Makinde administration of disregarding the rule of law by continuing to operate through the PMS despite several court rulings in its favour.

The NURTW said it had repeatedly appealed to the government to comply with the judgments recognising its status as a constitutionally registered labour union and its rights over the management of motor parks and the activities of its members across the state.

It alleged that PMS officials remained in control of the parks despite the judicial pronouncements.

The union said it had notified the Federal Government, the General Officer Commanding, 2 Division of the Nigerian Army, the Department of State Services, the Oyo State Commissioner of Police and the Nigeria Labour Congress of its intention to enforce the judgments without waiting any longer for the state government.

“We are alerting the Federal Government, GOC, Director of DSS and the Commissioner of Police that we will implement the court judgment by ourselves because the governor has failed us,” the union leaders said.

The NURTW also alleged that its prolonged exclusion from managing the parks had resulted in hunger, humiliation and the deaths of several members, warning that it could no longer tolerate the situation.

The Makinde government replaced the NURTW system with the Park Management System upon assuming office in 2019.

The Oyo State chapter of the NURTW is relying on the unanimous judgment of the Court of Appeal, Ibadan Division, delivered on September 26, 2025, in Appeal No. CA/IB/263/2022.

The lead judgment was delivered by Justice Kenneth Ikechukwu Amadi in an appeal brought by the NURTW against the Governor of Oyo State, the Attorney General of Oyo State, the Commissioner for Public Works, Infrastructure and Transportation, and the Commissioner of Police, Oyo State.

The appellate court overturned an earlier National Industrial Court decision, ruled that the state government had failed to justify the union’s suspension and set aside Governor Seyi Makinde’s May 31, 2019, proscription and suspension of NURTW operations in Oyo State.

The union leadership, led by Chairman Alhaji Abideen Olajide and Secretary Lekan Alesinloye, contends that setting aside the 2019 suspension restores its right to operate in motor parks across the state. It is relying on the judgment as the basis for its ultimatum to displace Park Management System officials.

However, the state government disputes that interpretation, maintaining that the judgment merely affirmed freedom of association and did not order the handover of public motor parks or grant ownership, control or management rights over local government-owned facilities to the NURTW.

Meanwhile, the Oyo State Government said security agencies across the state had been placed on red alert to bring the full weight of the law against troublemakers and anyone who might attempt to disrupt the peace and harmony of the state.

In a statement on Monday made available to The PUNCH, the Special Adviser on Security to the Oyo State Governor, Abayomi Fagbenro, advised the NURTW leadership to immediately withdraw its threats of unleashing mayhem on the state.

Fagbenro urged NURTW members to embrace dialogue, amicability, moral suasion, sacrifice, and give-and-take to settle the dispute.

The statement read, “The reported decision of the NURTW leaders to resort to self-help in their vaunted ambition for the control of the motor parks and garages will bode ill for them and those backing them.

“The government is not unmindful of the unseen hand behind the sudden boldness of the NURTW issuing threats of mayhem in the thick of electioneering ahead of the forthcoming crucial elections.

“Let me state unequivocally that no individual, group, or union has the right to take the law into its own hands, and any such attempt will be resisted.”

Fagbenro argued that the management of motor parks in Oyo State was regulated by law and government policy, stressing that any change in leadership or administration must follow due process.

He added, “Any attempt to cause a breakdown of law and order, foment crisis, or unleash violence on innocent citizens and park users under the guise of union rivalry will not be tolerated by this government.

“I am by this statement warning all those involved in this plot to desist forthwith, because the state has put behind it the past spectre of thuggery and brigandage synonymous with drivers’ union activities.

“Anyone caught with dangerous weapons, inciting violence, or leading thugs to attack any park will be arrested and prosecuted to the full extent of the law. There will be no sacred cows.”

While urging all law-abiding drivers and park managers to go about their lawful duties without fear, Fagbenro also urged residents of the state to remain vigilant.

“All grievances should be channelled through legitimate and lawful means and any action considered an affront to the constituted authorities will have lawful and legal consequences,” Fagbenro added.

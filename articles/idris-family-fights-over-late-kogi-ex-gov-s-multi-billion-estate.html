Idris’ family fights over late Kogi ex-gov’s multi-billion estate

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 3 min read

From Godwin Tsa, Abuja

A battle over the estate of the late former Governor of Kogi State, Alhaji Ibrahim Idris, has opened at an Upper Area Court in Abuja. Four of his children are asking the court to freeze his properties and appoint a receiver/manager to take charge of them.

In suit CV/101/2026, Asma’u, Fatima, Zainab and Jamila Ibrahim Idris are asking the court to restrain 13 other family members from selling, leasing, mortgaging, gifting, transferring or otherwise dealing with any part of the estate.

The respondents include Hajiya Aisha, Abubakar, Abdullahi, Sulaiman, Hussaini, Abdurahman, Idris (Atayi), Ibrahim, Hassan, Iman, Iram and Ilan Ibrahim Idris.

In a motion marked M/46/2026, filed by their lawyer, I. H. Nalaraba, and brought under sections 6(6)(b) and 272(1) of the 1999 Constitution and the court’s inherent jurisdiction, the applicants want an interlocutory injunction pending the determination of the substantive suit.

Properties in dispute

The properties listed include:

Grand Ibro Hotel and its Annex, both in Wuse Zone 5, Abuja; A house on Kwame Nkrumah Street, Asokoro, Abuja;

A house behind Government House, Lokoja; A house in Idah; Landed properties in Lokoja, Idah, Ayingba and Abejukolo.

Others are; a one-bedroom apartment in Dubai; Land behind SETRACO, Jabi, Abuja; Bank accounts in several Nigerian banks; The late governor’s personal effects, including cars, clothes, wristwatches, footwear and furniture.

The applicants want a receiver/manager to take over, preserve and manage the estate and collect its rents and income. They want the receiver to file a full inventory of the properties, within and outside Abuja, within 21 days of appointment. He would open a dedicated estate account in a reputable bank and file accounts of his stewardship every three months. He would also be barred from disposing of any property without the court’s leave, and paid a remuneration fixed by the court out of the estate’s income.

They also want the respondents, and any other heir who has collected rent or income or taken possession of any item since the governor’s death on September 20, 2026, to account for it and return it to the receiver within 14 days.

The applicants further want tenants, managers, staff and bankers of the estate properties, including the management of Grand Ibro Hotel, to deal only with the receiver.

They want the respondents ordered to preserve all title documents, bank statements, receipts and tenancy agreements, and an accelerated hearing of the main suit.

In a 16-paragraph affidavit, Sani Sambo, a lawyer in the applicants’ counsel’s firm, said the estate has not been shared among the heirs according to Islamic law, although the deceased was a practising Muslim.

He said some respondents and other family members have been collecting rents from the hotel and other properties. They have also allegedly taken the late governor’s cars, wristwatches and other belongings, to the exclusion of the other heirs.

Sambo also alleged that a mosque at the hotel was demolished after the governor’s death and new construction begun by some family members. He added that the hotel business continues to run without any accounting to the family.

The applicants have also applied for a plaint seeking a declaration of the deceased’s heirs and an order sharing the estate under Islamic law.

Judge Ado Muktar has directed all the family members to appear in court on November 23, the next adjourned date, when an inventory of the estate is also to be taken.

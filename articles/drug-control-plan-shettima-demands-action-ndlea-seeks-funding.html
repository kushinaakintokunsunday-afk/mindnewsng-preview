Drug control plan: Shettima demands action, NDLEA seeks funding

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 15h ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG

From Godwin Tsa, Abuja

Vice President Kashim Shettima has called for urgent implementation of the National Drug Control Master Plan (NDCMP) 2026–2030, directing that its priorities be incorporated into institutional work plans, budget proposals and implementation schedules.

Shettima, who spoke at the launch of the plan in Abuja on Monday, warned that inadequate financing could undermine its objectives, as the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) sought sustained funding from government and support from the private sector.

“A national plan without a credible financing pathway risks becoming a declaration of good intentions,” the Vice President said.

Represented by the Special Adviser to the President on Special Duties, Office of the Vice President, Modibbo Aliyu Umar, Shettima urged federal agencies, state and local governments, the National Assembly, businesses, traditional and religious institutions, and international partners to implement the plan with urgency, discipline and accountability.

He described the drug problem as a public health concern, security threat, development challenge and economic issue, warning that enforcement alone would not succeed without prevention, treatment and action against the illicit financial flows sustaining drug markets.

“A nation cannot secure its future if it fails to protect the people who must build that future,” he said.

The plan rests on six pillars: supply reduction; drug demand reduction; access to controlled medicines for scientific and medical purposes; alternative development and sustainable livelihoods; disruption of the illicit drug economy; and governance, financing and coordination.

Shettima commended the NDLEA, under its Chairman, Mohamed Marwa, for what he described as its pioneering alternative development programme in Africa. He said the pilot was already transforming three communities in Ondo State and urged state and local governments to take ownership of the initiative.

In his welcome address, Marwa said the new plan drew on the achievements and shortcomings of its predecessor and was designed “to keep what worked, fix what did not, and confront what is emerging.”

He disclosed that an evaluation of the 2021–2025 plan recorded 77,736 arrests, seizures of more than 14.8 million kilograms of drugs and over 14,200 convictions, while more than 32,400 people received treatment, counselling and rehabilitation.

However, the evaluation identified gaps in coordination, financing, service delivery, data, monitoring and accountability, alongside weak capacity at state and local government levels.

Marwa said the new framework would address those weaknesses and strengthen the response to trafficking networks, synthetic drugs, new psychoactive substances and syndicates exploiting encrypted communications and cryptocurrency.

Describing sustainable financing as “perhaps the single most important condition” for success, he urged federal budget authorities to reflect the plan’s priorities in annual budgets and the Medium-Term Expenditure Framework.

He also asked states and local governments to provide dedicated funding for their drug control committees, while inviting banks, telecommunications companies, technology firms, agribusinesses and philanthropists to support prevention, treatment and livelihood programmes.

“A policy priority without resources is merely an aspiration,” he said.

Highlighting the international reach of trafficking networks, Marwa cited a joint operation conducted on April 28, 2026, with the United States Drug Enforcement Administration and law enforcement partners in Switzerland, France and Greece.

He said the operation led to the arrest of an alleged drug baron, Amadi Simon, in Switzerland, as well as Jecinta Amara Ikechi in Anambra State and Blessing Ngozi Amadi in Delta State.

According to him, several bank accounts and cryptocurrency wallets were blocked, while the agency secured interim forfeiture of properties linked to Simon, including two hotels in Delta State and an apartment building in Mabushi, Abuja.

Marwa also recalled the dismantling of a Nigerian-Mexican methamphetamine network and a clandestine laboratory in Ogun State, involving drugs and chemicals valued at about $362.9 million.

He said four additional Mexican-linked laboratories were subsequently uncovered in Oyo, Ebonyi and Enugu states, leading to the arrest of 10 Mexicans and 14 local collaborators who were facing criminal charges.

The NDLEA chairman welcomed President Bola Tinubu’s transmission of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency Bill, 2026, to the National Assembly, where he said it had passed second reading, and assured that enforcement would remain guided by the rule of law, due process and respect for human rights.

Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, Lateef Fagbemi, described the NDLEA as “the bedrock of our national drug response,” saying the plan linked law enforcement with public health, education, social development and financial investigation.

He said the Ministry of Justice would work with the Judiciary to accelerate the disposal of drug cases under the Administration of Criminal Justice Act, pursue confiscation of illicit assets alongside convictions and strengthen mutual legal assistance, extradition and cross-border asset recovery.

Fagbemi stressed the distinction between trafficking and drug dependence.

“While traffickers must face the full weight of the law, those experiencing drug use disorders require evidence-based care, counseling, treatment, and community reintegration,” he said.

The ECOWAS Commissioner for Human Development and Social Affairs, Nassirou Bako-Arifari, said the plan aligned with regional commitments and pledged the commission’s support.

ECOWAS would “stand with Nigeria at every step,” he said, adding that “no country can win this fight alone.”

Chairman of the House of Representatives Committee on Drugs and Narcotics, Abass Adigun, represented by Dominic Okafor, pledged legislative support and oversight, stressing the need for adequate funding and coordination among agencies. Yobe State First Lady, Hafsat Kollere Buni, also pledged support.

Marwa acknowledged the European Union’s sponsorship of the previous plan and the support of the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime and ECOWAS.

The launch, themed “Strategic Action for a Drug-Free Nation,” brought together government officials, lawmakers, diplomats, traditional and religious leaders, civil society organisations, businesses and youth groups.

“Five years from now, the significance of today’s launch will not be judged by the ceremony,” Shettima said. “Let this be the moment Nigeria moves from policy to action.”

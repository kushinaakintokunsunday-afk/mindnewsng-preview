Kenyan, Ivorian Cinema Take Spotlight As 2026 AMAA Delivers Stunning, Historic Sweeps

The Africa Movie Academy Awards (AMAA) delivered a night of stunning upsets and historic sweeps, with Kenyan and Ivorian cinema firmly taking the spotlight at this year’s ceremony.

​While the night was a celebration of cinematic excellence across the continent and the diaspora, two films emerged as the undeniable titans of the evening: Kenya’s One Woman, One Bra and Côte d’Ivoire’s Colours of Exile.

In terms of qualitative prestige, the night belonged to Kenya. One Woman, One Bra was crowned the biggest winner in quality, taking home the highly coveted Best Film award. The cinematic masterpiece didn’t stop there; it also captured Best Film in an African Language, while its visionary creator, Vincho Nchogu, was honored with the award for Best Debut Feature Film by a Director. The triple victory cemented the film’s status as a critical darling and a landmark achievement in contemporary East African cinema.

If Kenya took the night’s biggest prize, Côte d’Ivoire dominated the sheer volume of accolades. Colours of Exile proved to be an unstoppable force, securing a leading four awards to become the biggest winner in numbers. The Ivorian powerhouse swept major categories, with Azlorabe Alaoui winning Best Director and Naku Sy Savane taking home Best Actress in a Leading Role. The film’s immersive technical execution was also recognized with wins for Achievement in Cinematography and Achievement in Sound.

Nigeria maintained a formidable presence throughout the ceremony, demonstrating its continued technical and narrative prowess with a total of eight wins. The Herd was a standout, securing three awards: Achievement in Production Design, Achievement in Make-up, and a Best Actress in a Supporting Role win for Linda Ejiofor.

​Wale Ojo delivered a major acting victory for Nigeria, winning Best Actor in a Leading Role for Light Out.

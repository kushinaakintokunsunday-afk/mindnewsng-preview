Igbo presidency must be eight full years – Oshiomhole

By Punch Punch 5d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Senator Adams Oshiomhole has said any future Nigerian president of Igbo extraction must serve a full eight-year tenure, declaring that the South-East should not

Oshiomhole stated this on Thursday at the All Progressives Congress presidential campaign rally in Anambra State, where he praised Governor Chukwuma Soludo’s administration and President Bola Tinubu’s policies on education and university funding.

“When Ndi Igbo will become president, it must be eight full years. No half cup. It will be full cup by those with brain, with courage, and fill the gaps,” the former Edo State governor said.

He also described Anambra as “the headquarters of intellectuals”, saying the state had historically produced prominent figures, including Nigeria’s first president, Dr Nnamdi Azikiwe.

“Historically, it has been so. You produce the Zik of Africa, not just Zik of Nigeria,” he said.

Oshiomhole, who spoke about growing up in a village without a hospital or school, praised Tinubu’s education initiatives, particularly the provision of financial support and loans for students.

He recalled having to travel several kilometres to attend primary school, saying he appreciated a President who recognised the difficulties faced by people from disadvantaged backgrounds.

“When I find a president who remembers people of my background, and say you shall not lack education; I will give you money. I will give you loan, which you can only pay when you get job,” he said.

The senator also praised the Federal Government’s handling of issues affecting universities, particularly the maintenance of academic calendars and improved remuneration for lecturers.

He said, “Now, for four years, there has been no strike. This is a threat to those who live on primary ownership of a public university, because the only reason people say they are children there is because of the integrity of academic calendar.”

He attributed the previous disruption of university education to what he described as poor leadership in Abuja, saying, “Because of poor brains in Abuja in the past, the best brains were leaving the university system.”

Oshiomhole said Tinubu was addressing the challenges confronting the university system and working to attract experienced academics back to the sector through improved remuneration.

“President Bola Tinubu is not only respecting that calendar by dealing with issues confronting us, but also ensuring that by proper remuneration, we bring back to academia the finest brains in our society,” he said.

He urged Anambra residents to support the President, expressing confidence that they would benefit from the investment they were being asked to make.

The rally was also attended by Soludo, who said Tinubu did not need to rig the 2027 presidential election to win a second term, claiming that the APC ticket was better positioned than those of opposition parties.

PUNCH reported that the governor argued that opposition candidates lacked nationwide reach compared with Tinubu and Vice-President Kashim Shettima. 

He also praised the administration’s economic and security policies and cited the Nigerian Education Loan Fund as one of its initiatives.

Soludo said, “This election, to the best of our knowledge, is already won and lost. The President doesn’t need to rig any election. President Tinubu doesn’t need to rig any election.”

Oshiomhole’s comments come amid debate over the South-East’s quest for the presidency and the proposed single-term arrangement involving former Anambra State Governor Peter Obi ahead of the 2027 election.

Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, has repeatedly pledged to serve only one four-year term if elected, saying the decision is intended to promote stability.

“I want to be a one-term president because of stability. I would not stay a day longer than four years, even with a gun to my head,” Obi said in an interview with News Central TV in May.

His running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso, later confirmed that they signed separate agreements committing Obi to a single four-year tenure, after which the presidency would return to the North.

Kwankwaso said the arrangement provided that “after his term of four years (2027-2031), it comes back to the North.” Obi subsequently described the arrangement as an agreement that should be respected.

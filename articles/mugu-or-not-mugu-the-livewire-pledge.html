Mugu or not mugu: The livewire pledge

By Punch Punch 2d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Suddenly, the Nigerian government is defending electricity: as supplied—or not—to the Nigerian people. Because it is another election season, featuring a ballot

Because it is another election season, featuring a ballot box noose developed by the ruling party’s candidate exactly four years ago.

Officials and political party relatives are scrambling to rework the words and intentions of Mr Bola Tinubu, the APC flag-bearer, deployed in 2023 to take advantage of Nigerians’ desperation for electricity.

To the ruling party, the matter did not appear to be an important issue at the time. Its institutional promise on the matter, outlined on page 22 of the 2014 APC Manifesto, was simply to “Ensure Nigeria meets all its power needs within the next ten years.”

It was closing in on that 10 years later when candidate Tinubu arrived with his Emilokan (My Turn) philosophy.  It was not the most popular approach, and he knew it.

Perhaps that was why he looked for any dimensions that would haul in any hesitant votes. Electricity was one such fishing trip.

While his Renewed Hope agenda recognised that “the nation’s power problems cannot be solved overnight,” Tinubu wanted more.

And so, speaking during “Business Lunch with Asiwaju,” a meeting with the Organised Private Sector in Lagos in late 2022, he grabbed the national audience by the neck.

Here is the video of the event.  He commences his speech at about 1:43:43 on the video and gets to the defining BAMN promise at 2:09:50. I call it B-A-M-N—as in DAMN—By Any Means Necessary.

Here is the transcript: “Whichever way, by all means necessary, you must have electricity. And you will not pay for estimated billing anymore. A promise made will be a promise kept. If I couldn’t keep the promise and I come back for a second term, don’t vote for me. That’s the truth, unless I give you adequate reasons why I couldn’t deliver, and it is convincing enough.”

It was seductive. Powerful. Unforgettable.

An uncomplicated quid pro quo: A promise to be kept by all means, unless I give you convincing reasons. If not, please vote for someone else.

With the presidential contest just two months away, it is not surprising that this has become controversial. But it is a false controversy. Because while this matter masquerades as an election issue, what is really at stake is bigger: it is about who or what Nigeria is.

Did Candidate Tinubu make the B-A-M-N promise? I have quoted him in full. There is no hidden language in it. He provided a qualifier, to be sure: “…adequate reasons [that are] convincing enough.”

“You must have electricity.”

A direct, uncomplicated assertion with a three-year lifespan. Because the “second term” is campaigned for in the last year of the first. That is where we are now.

“You must have electricity”: a pledge that has now been breached, without the “convincing reasons” being tendered.

The truth is that it is impossible to manufacture an intelligent argument here in support of the man who is now President Tinubu.

That is attributable to who he has been as president, never matching his performance with the urgency and seriousness required of his office, even if he did not intend to run again.

Instead, he has acted like a man who arrived at the buffet and forgot that others were equally hungry, treating the public interest as background noise. In his first year, he advanced the rules for himself and his own:

  • A presidential jet for N150 billion
  • N40.6bn for the renovation and maintenance of the presidential palace
  • Foreign Exchange purchases of over N23bn in 2024 alone for international travel, and about N34bn in two years
  • A N5bn presidential yacht.
  • Acquisition, renovation, and rehabilitation of four EFCC fortified quarters in Mabushi and Guzape in the FCT, as “State House complexes,” for N3bn
  • Extensive acquisition of expensive SUVs and bulletproof tyres
  • Renovation of his official residence in Dodan Barracks, Lagos, for N4bn
  • Construction of an office complex within Aso Rock for N4bn;
  • N1.5 billion on cars for the nonexistent Office of the First Lady
  • Instituted the rock-star travel of his son, Seyi, and paid the private hotel bills of his daughter

He was only just the beginning.  Tinubu abandoned the elite State House Medical Centre—renovated and upgraded for over N21bn just days before he took office so it could care even for foreign leaders—for medical tourism abroad.

He commenced travelling in 100-car convoys, sometimes tying up roads for hours.  That was despite directing government officials in October 2024 to reduce their official convoys to three or less, citing the need to reduce the cost of governance. Most of all, that was despite his asking Nigerians to accept the hardship his “reforms” had emptied all over them.

The point, then, is that all of those who argue in favour of Mr Tinubu’s BAMN promise do not do him a favour.  He made the pledge to win an election but then ignored what was literally a live wire for a lifestyle that suggests it was a ruse.

What is repeatedly demonstrated to Nigerians is that the political elite is in it for itself.  Tinubu’s abandonment of his pledge is not accidental; it has been part of the APC game since taking office.  Remember: in the 2014 Manifesto, the party promised Nigerians not merely a change of government but a fundamental transformation of the country’s economic and social conditions. It expressly described the manifesto as a social contract and invited Nigerians to hold the party accountable for its delivery, a commitment from which it has continuously backed away since assuming control.

That manifesto was organised around three themes: Relief, Recovery and Reform, intended to produce immediate improvements in citizens’ lives, the rehabilitation of national institutions and a structural transformation of Nigeria’s political economy. In the Tinubu Years, APC has shifted the goalposts and diminished Nigerians by subverting those advertised goals.

They were going to conquer corruption, but they became corruption.  They were going to bring CHANGE, but they resisted change.  They were going to combat insecurity, but they became insecurity. They were going to lift 100m out of poverty, but they made us far poorer.

The mechanism of setting traps for the Nigerian voter reveals a self-centred leadership with no commitment to accountability, service, excellence, decency, compassion or merit.  Every “reform” emerges as a cover for official excess, and tokenism and speeches are substituted for performance.

No, this chicanery was always about power, not electricity, or insecurity, or education, or healthcare.  The government is spending billions not on programme delivery, but to win an election I have described as a survey where we learn who Nigerians really are.

Our country is poorer, hungrier, more insecure, less livable and less respected since APC arrived.  We have lost our way so thoroughly that only masochists and haters of true national progress can revel in this shame.

That is why the way we vote in 2027 will tell the world whether we are anything but mugus (pathetic fools).

BAMN and DAMN!

No Wedding Until You Pass These 3 Rules: What the French Foreign Legion Demands Before Soldiers Can Marry

By Mindnewsng Mindnewsng 10 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Mindnewsng

Inside the French Foreign Legion, even marriage is a military operation.

The world's most famous foreign volunteer force - where recruits from some 150 countries, including Nigeria, enlist for a non-negotiable five-year contract - treats tying the knot as an administrative achievement, not a personal choice. Its own official recruitment site, legion-recrute.com, states it plainly: a legionnaire is only allowed to marry after clearing three conditions.

The three rules before the aisle

Rule 1 - Serve under your true identity. New legionnaires traditionally enlist under an assumed name, and some spend their first years under it. A soldier must have completed the formal process known as "régularisation de situation militaire" - recovering his real identity through official documents from his country of origin - before marriage can even be considered. Rule 2 - The command must know. The legionnaire must have officially informed his chain of command. In the Legion, family milestones are reported like mission reports. Rule 3 - Under 5 years of service? Write to the Minister. A legionnaire with fewer than five years of service needs the personal authorization of the Minister of the Armed Forces in Paris. After five years, the ministerial step falls away - but the duty to keep the command informed never does.

Why the rules are this strict

The Legion's logic is availability. For the first five years of service, a legionnaire lives inside the barracks - the site describes this lifestyle as "corresponding to that of a single person" - and is ready to deploy wherever France sends him. He gets 45 days of leave a year (by agreement of the command), must wear his uniform during free time, and cannot even buy a car without command authorization until later in his career.

As the Legion puts it: after a number of years, as he progresses in his career, he "can legitimately start a family." Marriage is treated as the reward of a settled, regularized soldier - not the right of a new recruit.

What else legionnaires earn over time

  • French citizenship: after serving well, a foreign legionnaire can apply for French nationality - and a wound sustained on the battlefield can earn citizenship through the famous "French by spilled blood" provision.
  • A fresh start: the Legion reviews each criminal record individually and does not recruit violent offenders - but it does offer men a second chance.
  • Starting pay: around €1,380 a month, with room, food, clothing and free medical care - plus bonuses for missions.

And one more quirk: married men may join the Legion - but they enlist as single men, because the barracks life of the first five years leaves no place for a spouse.

For Nigerians and other West Africans considering the Legion as a career or a route to French papers, the marriage rules are a window into the real deal: five years of discipline first, family life after.

Sources: legion-recrute.com (official recruitment site of the French Foreign Legion); Forces News. This article is general information only - always verify enlistment details on the official Legion website.

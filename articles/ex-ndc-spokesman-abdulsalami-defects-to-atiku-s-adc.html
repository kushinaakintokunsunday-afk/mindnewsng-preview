Ex-NDC spokesman Abdulsalami defects to Atiku’s ADC

By Punch Punch 1d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former Deputy National Publicity Secretary of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, Abdulmumin Abdulsalami, has defected to the African Democratic Congress, the poli

Abdulsalami confirmed his defection in an exclusive interview with The PUNCH, saying he had been warmly received by the ADC and was ready to contribute to the party.

 Asked whether he had decided on his next political destination after quitting the NDC, the former spokesman said he had already joined the ADC.

 “No, there is no going back to the NDC. I have joined the ADC, where I have been welcomed with open arms. Now, I feel at home, and I am willing to put in my best, knowing that my efforts will be appreciated,” he said.

 When asked if he had been assigned a new responsibility, particularly in the ADC Campaign Council ahead of the 2027 general elections, Abdulsalami confirmed that he had taken up a new role but declined to disclose details.

 “Yes, I have a new role now. But I have not been cleared to release it for media consumption,” he said.

 His defection comes days after he announced his resignation from the NDC, accusing some influential party officials of frustrating his contributions and sidelining him despite his role in building the party.

 Abdulsalami, a foundation member and pioneer spokesperson of the NDC, announced his departure in a letter addressed to the National Chairman, Senator Moses Cleopas, through his former ward chairman in Nagazi Ward, Adavi Local Government Area of Kogi State.

 In the letter, titled Letter of Resignation from the Nigeria Democratic Congress: Withdrawal from All Partisan Commitments to the Party, he said his decision followed a growing disagreement between his contributions to the party and the interests of some powerful officials.

 “As a foundation member of the NDC, and a critical formative leader in the party, I am compelled to voluntarily take the exit having realised that my sincere and positive contributions to the meteoric rise and success of the party are at odds with the vested interests of a few powerful officers who call the shots.

 “More so, this painstaking decision serves my best interest as I prioritise my peace of mind rather than recurrent complaints at the sight of a series of injustices meted out to me,” he stated.

 Abdulsalami served as the NDC Protem Chairman in Kogi State before becoming Deputy National Publicity Secretary and later Deputy National Director for Labour and Civil Society.

 He also thanked the party’s National Leader, Senator Henry Seriake Dickson, for the opportunities given to him, while acknowledging the support of colleagues, civil society activists and media practitioners who worked with him during the party’s formative period.

 Before confirming his defection to the ADC, Abdulsalami had indicated that he was withdrawing from partisan politics, saying he was no longer willing to remain in a party where he felt excluded.

 “I am calling it quits and heading back home to avoid orchestrated melodrama of exclusion from party affairs. I love you all, but I love myself and my mental sanity most,” he said.

 He, however, declined to identify the party officials he accused of frustrating his contributions.

 An NDC source, who spoke on condition of anonymity because of the sensitivity of the matter, described his resignation as the culmination of a troubled relationship with the party’s leadership.

 “Abdul’s case is really a sad one. He has survived what I can call a toxic relationship. Despite all his efforts since the start of this NDC, he has never been paid a dime as salary or in the working budgets.

 “Secondly, he was subtly eased out of the National Working Committee despite being a foundational member by high-ranking leaders of the party. His leaving the party, to many of us, is the only choice.

 “He has been trying to do that since October. But the national leader of our party (Senator Seriake Dickson) kept urging him to stay back,” the source said.

 However, the NDC National Publicity Secretary, Osa Director, dismissed suggestions that Abdulsalami’s exit reflected a deeper crisis within the party.

 He told our correspondent, “There is no internal wrangling in the party. The exit of one person is not enough to conclude, especially as we also witness an influx of a lot of people into the party every day.”

 Abdulsalami’s defection comes amid a series of disagreements and political realignments involving the NDC and opposition figures ahead of the 2027 general elections.

 The party has faced disagreements over the relationship between its structures and the Obi-Kwankwaso Movement, a political platform supporting its presidential candidate, Peter Obi, and his prospective running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso.

 Barely two months ago, the Director-General of the OK Movement, John Ughulu, rejected suggestions that the group should surrender its national, state, local government and ward structures to the NDC.

 Dickson had argued that support groups should complement political parties rather than compete with them or operate parallel structures. He also described the movement’s decision to establish a presidential campaign council as “unheard of” and “nonsensical”.

 Ughulu, however, maintained that the movement was an independent political organisation and not merely a conventional support group.

…forces 26 terrorists, family members to surrender

The Armed Forces of Nigeria (AFN) have intensified offensives against terrorist networks, killing 21 ISWAP fighters, including senior commanders, and recording the surrender of 26 fighters and their family members in the Northeast.

The Director, Defence Media Operations, Maj.-Gen. Michael Onoja, disclosed this in a statement on ongoing military operations covering Nov. 13 to Nov. 19.

Onoja said precision air strikes conducted between Monday and Tuesday around Monguno and Arinna in the South Lake Chad area of Borno neutralised 21 ISWAP fighters and severely injured 10 others.

He said the first strike killed seven terrorists, including members of the group’s Improvised Explosive Device (IED) network, and incapacitated a key IED expert.

According to him, the second engagement at Arinna neutralised a member of ISWAP’s Shura Council, who served as its Provost Commander, a Qaid of Sabon Tumbu and 12 other fighters.

The director said the sustained military pressure also prompted 26 terrorists and their family members, comprising eight fighters and 18 family members, to surrender between Nov. 13 and Nov. 18.

He said the surrender took place in Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Kukawa and Monguno Local Government Areas of Borno.

Among those who surrendered were 12 women and children who fled Bula Daloye on Nov. 13, as well as a terrorist who surrendered along Gwoza-Pulka Road on Nov. 14.

“The latter reportedly handed over an AK-47 rifle, five magazines, 34 rounds of ammunition and two hand grenades,” he said.

Onoja said the surrendering terrorists cited intense military operations and internal clashes between rival factions as reasons for abandoning the group.

He added that troops also rescued four internally displaced persons who escaped from terrorist captivity at Zaha Village in the Mandara Mountains, Gwoza LGA, on Tuesday.

Onoja further disclosed that troops arrested suspected terrorist collaborators and logistics suppliers in Borno, Yobe and Kano, including individuals allegedly conveying combat materials and supplies to terrorist enclaves.

In the Northwest, he said the troops of Operation Fansan Yamma rescued three kidnapped victims along Anka-Gada Mayya Road in Zamfara on Nov. 12 and captured a vehicle belonging to the terrorists.

According to him, troops also neutralised one terrorist during an engagement at Aguba Village in Kajuru LGA of Kaduna on Nov. 14, recovering two fabricated rifles and a mobile phone.

Onoja said troops arrested a principal suspect in Ribah, whose confession reportedly led to the arrest of three other persons linked to an illegal supply network serving bandits along the Bena axis.

“In the North-Central, troops of Operation Enduring Peace recovered a PKT machine gun, three AK-47 rifles and three magazines surrendered by fleeing terrorists following sustained offensives around Odare Forest in Kanam LGA of Plateau.

“Troops of Operation Whirl Stroke also arrested suspected arms and logistics couriers in Taraba and Kogi, including individuals allegedly transporting weapons across the River Benue and River Niger corridors.

“In Niger State, troops arrested two suspected kidnappers at Warari Village in Rijau LGA while attempting to collect a N3.5 million ransom from a resident,” he said.

The director said troops of Operation Savannah Shield also recovered 12 rifles and rescued 10 kidnapped persons during the initial stage of negotiations with three prominent bandit leaders operating around Gbugbu Forest in Edu LGA of Kwara.

He added that four additional kidnapped victims were released to troops on Nov. 18 following further engagements.

He said troops subsequently intercepted four suspected terrorist logistics suppliers at Kanikoko in Kaiama Local Government Area.

According to him, troops recovered detonating cords, cartons of blasting explosives and fuses allegedly being conveyed to Sabon Fegi in Borgu Local Government Area of Niger.

Onoja said troops, in another operation at Kara’a Village in Mariga LGA of Niger on Tuesday, neutralised three terrorists and rescued three farmers abducted from Warari town.

He said troops of Operation Udo Ka discovered three command-detonated improvised explosive devices at an IPOB/ESN camp in Amankwu Village, Arochukwu LGA of Abia, alongside solar panels, a Biafran flag and other items.

“Troops also neutralised two suspected IPOB/ESN fighters during operations around Njaba in Mbaitoli LGA of Imo, recovering an AK-47 rifle, more than 110 rounds of ammunition and other items.

“In the South-South, troops of Operation Delta Safe uncovered seven illegal bunkering camps around Ndoni in Ogba/Egbema/Ndoni LGA of Rivers, with about 85,000 litres of stolen crude oil.

“They also discovered an illegal refining site at Apani Community in Ikwerre LGA, containing an estimated 2,100 litres of illegally refined products,” he added.

Onoja said troops, working with the police and local security groups, also arrested two suspected kidnappers at Iguelaba Community along the Delta-Edo boundary.

He said the Chief of Defence Staff, Gen. Olufemi Oluyede, commended troops and the Air Component for their resilience and sacrifices.

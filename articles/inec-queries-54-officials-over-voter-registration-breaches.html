INEC queries 54 officials over voter registration breaches

By Punch Punch 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Independent National Electoral Commission said it had issued queries to 54 of its personnel allegedly involved in voter registration irregularities across 1

INEC National Commissioner, Mohammed Haruna, disclosed this in an exclusive chat with The PUNCH on Monday in Abuja, explaining that the commission had commenced the process of investigating the officials but would not rush to disciplinary decisions without observing due process.

Haruna said the affected personnel had been issued queries and were expected to respond, after which they would be invited for further investigation.

He stressed that the commission was committed to ensuring fairness and allowing the officials to defend themselves before any decision was taken.

“There is a due process. They have to be queried. They’ve been queried. When they respond, they are invited, and the whole matter is investigated. You have to give people a fair hearing. You can’t just take a decision within a year.

“When did this happen? It’s less than two weeks. These disciplinary procedures, they take weeks. You have to query them.

“They have to give you an answer within 72 hours. After that, then you invite them. These persons involved are from the far places. They come; you interview them. So the process is elaborate. We have to follow every step of the way, and you know they are in other states. So across 10 states. So it will take a while for their reply to reach us,” he said.

The national commissioner explained that investigations would begin at the state level through committees to be constituted by the respective Resident Electoral Commissioners before a panel would be established at the commission’s headquarters to review the findings.

He said the geographical spread of the affected personnel across 10 states and the administrative procedures involved meant that the investigation would take time.

“The investigation will start from their own place because, of course, there is a Resident Electoral Commission there. They will set up a committee to look into it, before a panel will be set up in headquarters to look into the matter. So there are steps that we have to take.

“Every step of the way, we have to follow those processes,” Haruna added.

INEC had earlier disclosed that it had identified 54 personnel across 10 states allegedly involved in the manipulation of the voter registration exercise.

On November 9, the INEC Chairman, Prof. Joash Amupitan, while addressing journalists in Abuja on the outcome of the commission’s review of the Preliminary Register of Voters ahead of the 2027 general elections, warned that those found culpable would face administrative disciplinary measures and possible prosecution.

The queries issued to the INEC officials followed public complaints about irregularities in the preliminary register of voters displayed at registration areas between October 9 and 15, 2026.

After the register was published online on October 26, there was an outcry among Nigerians over the inclusion of suspicious profiles, including those suspected to be AI-generated, in the voters’ register.

The complaints included allegations of multiple registrations, unusual names, artificial or manipulated photographs, and deceased persons remaining on the register.

The commission, however, clarified that it had deleted over 1,608,660 confirmed invalid registrations from the preliminary register generated from the 2025/2026 Continuous Voter Registration exercise.

The clean-up comes amid the commission’s efforts to sanitise the voters’ register and prevent irregularities that could undermine the credibility of the 2027 general elections.

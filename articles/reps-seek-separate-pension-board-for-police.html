Reps seek separate pension board for police

By Punch Punch 4d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The House of Representatives is considering a bill to exempt the Nigeria Police Force from the Contributory Pension Scheme and establish a separate pension boar

 The proposed legislation, titled “A Bill for an Act to Establish the Nigeria Police Pension Board, to Provide for a Special Pension Scheme for Officers, Inspectors, Rank and File of the Nigeria Police Force; and for Related Matters,” seeks to create a dedicated institutional framework for the administration of police pensions and gratuities.

 Sponsored by the member representing Oshodi-Isolo II Federal Constituency of Lagos State, Jesse Onuakalusi, the bill was introduced on the floor of the Green Chamber on Wednesday and now awaits a date in the coming weeks for the debate of its general principles.

 A copy of the bill, exclusively obtained by The PUNCH, shows that the proposed Nigeria Police Pension Board would assume responsibility for “managing the pensions, gratuities and other retirement benefits of all categories of police personnel, replacing the existing arrangement under the Contributory Pension Scheme established by the Pension Reform Act, 2014.”

 Specifically, Section 6 of the bill provides that “notwithstanding the provisions of the Pension Reform Act, 2014, the Nigeria Police Force shall, from the commencement of the proposed law, be exempted from the Contributory Pension Scheme.”

 It further provides for the transfer of the accrued rights and benefits of serving police officers under the existing scheme to the new board, in accordance with regulations to be issued by the Minister of Finance.

 The bill also gives the National Pension Commission “six months from the commencement of the Act to ensure the seamless transfer of all police pension assets and liabilities to the proposed board.”

 Upon completion of the transfer, the board would assume full responsibility for the administration of all police pension matters.

 Explaining the rationale for the proposed legislation, its explanatory memorandum states that the initiative is intended to address “the peculiar, hazardous and essential nature of policing duties in Nigeria.”

 It further states that the proposed special pension arrangement would ensure “timely, dignified and adequate post-service welfare for police personnel while providing a specialised management and investment framework for the sustainability of their retirement benefits.”

 The proposed board would be headquartered in Abuja and operate as a corporate body with perpetual succession, a common seal and the legal capacity to sue and be sued in its corporate name.

 Under Section 3, its governing board would be chaired by a retired senior police officer not below the rank of Deputy Inspector-General of Police.

 Other members would include “the Permanent Secretary of the Ministry of Police Affairs, a representative of the Police Service Commission, the Inspector-General of Police or his representative, and representatives of the Budget Office of the Federation and the Office of the Accountant-General of the Federation.”

 The governing board would also include two representatives of retired police officers, one from the senior cadre and the other from the rank and file, as well as the Executive Secretary, who would serve as the chief executive officer.

 Members of the board would be appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Minister of Police Affairs.

 In terms of its responsibilities, the proposed board would formulate policies and guidelines for police pensions and gratuities, supervise pension fund management, authorise payments to eligible retirees and conduct periodic verification of pensioners.

 It would also be empowered to enter into contracts, acquire and dispose of property, employ personnel and collaborate with relevant government agencies to improve pension administration.

 On funding, the bill provides that the board’s resources would come from “annual budgetary allocations by the Federal Government, pension funds transferred from PenCom, investment income and grants or donations approved by the government.”

 It also provides for the establishment of a dedicated Police Pension Fund Account into which appropriated funds, transferred pension rights and contributions, investment proceeds and other lawful revenues would be paid.

 The fund would be applied to the payment of pensions, gratuities and other benefits, administrative and operational expenses, staff training and welfare, as well as procurement and logistics.

 To strengthen accountability, the proposed legislation requires the board to maintain proper financial records and subject its accounts to annual audits by the Auditor-General for the Federation or an external auditor approved by the Auditor-General.

 The audited accounts and reports would be submitted to the National Assembly not later than six months after the end of each financial year.

 The board would also be required to submit an annual report to the President and the National Assembly not later than June 30, detailing its activities during the preceding year and the financial position of the Police Pension Fund.

 The proposed management structure provides for an Executive Secretary, who would serve as the board’s chief executive and accounting officer.

 The Executive Secretary would be appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Minister of Police Affairs, subject to confirmation by the Senate, for a five-year term renewable once.

 The position would be open to a retired or serving police officer not below the rank of Assistant Inspector-General of Police, or a public administrator or pension management professional with at least 20 years of relevant experience.

 The bill further provides for seven core directorates covering finance and accounts, pension administration, human resources and administration, information and communication technology, investment and treasury management, legal services and board secretariat, as well as internal audit and compliance.

 To bring pension administration closer to retirees, the proposed board would establish zonal pension offices in the six geopolitical zones of the country. The offices would coordinate pension operations, facilitate retiree verification and enrolment, and submit periodic reports to the headquarters.

 The legislation also empowers the Minister of Police Affairs, on the recommendation of the board and with the President’s approval, to make regulations necessary for its implementation.

 If passed by the National Assembly and assented to by the President, the legislation would be cited as the Nigeria Police Pension Board (Establishment) Act, 2025.

 The proposal comes against the backdrop of longstanding concerns over the welfare and retirement conditions of police personnel, particularly the need for an effective pension arrangement that recognises the peculiar demands and risks associated with policing.

 By seeking a dedicated pension institution outside the existing contributory framework, the bill proposes a fundamental restructuring of how the retirement benefits of police officers are to be administered and funded in Nigeria.

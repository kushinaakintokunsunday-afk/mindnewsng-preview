Create waste ministry, group urges next Lagos govt

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

An advocacy group, the Lagos Waste Forum, has called on the next administration in Lagos State to establish a dedicated Ministry of Waste Resources to provide “

The forum said the proposed ministry would coordinate waste management activities, strengthen accountability and provide long-term policy direction for the sector.

According to a statement made available to our correspondent, the call was made during a virtual policy dialogue titled The Governor’s Brief: Setting a Waste Policy Agenda for the Next Lagos, organised by the Lagos Waste Forum in collaboration with the SWEEP Foundation NG last Thursday.

The dialogue brought together stakeholders from the media, private sector, civil society, environmental advocacy groups and the informal waste economy, including waste pickers, recyclers, waste operators and policy experts.

The stakeholders said Lagos’ waste management challenges had become structural and could no longer be addressed through periodic changes in agencies, contractors and operational arrangements.

They noted that the state’s population had increased from about 5.7 million in 1991 to more than 22 million, putting increasing pressure on its waste management infrastructure.

The forum also cited an acknowledgement by the state Deputy Governor, Obafemi Hamzat, that Lagos generates about 17,000 tonnes of waste daily, compared with the previously cited 13,000 tonnes.

It said only about 60 per cent of the waste generated was collected, while approximately 13 per cent was recycled, leaving substantial quantities inadequately managed.

According to the stakeholders, the situation contributes to environmental pollution, blocked drainage channels, flooding and public health risks.

They called for a comprehensive waste governance framework that would integrate waste management with public health, flood prevention, infrastructure development and economic planning.

The forum also urged the incoming administration to improve waste data collection and public reporting, maintain waste infrastructure, increase disposal capacity and reduce dependence on the monthly environmental sanitation exercise by promoting everyday environmental responsibility.

The Convener of the Lagos Waste Forum, Obuesi Phillips, said the policy dialogue was aimed at moving beyond recurring complaints about waste management failures to developing a coordinated reform agenda.

Phillips said the recommendations from the engagement would be consolidated into a policy compendium to be presented to the incoming governor and other relevant government stakeholders.

The panel at the dialogue included the Publisher of Prime Business Africa, Dr Marcel Mbamalu; Metro Editor of PUNCH Newspapers, Joel Nwokeoma; Phillips; BBC Senior Journalist, Esther Omopariola; and City Scope Africa’s John Fatanmi.

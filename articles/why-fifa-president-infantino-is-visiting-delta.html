Why FIFA President Infantino is visiting Delta

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The arrival of FIFA President Gianni Infantino in Warri, Delta State on Friday has drawn attention to the city’s football heritage, grassroots sporting culture

The arrival of FIFA President Gianni Infantino in Warri, Delta State on Friday has drawn attention to the city’s football heritage, grassroots sporting culture and ongoing efforts to upgrade its sporting facilities.

Beyond the inspection of the renovated Warri City Stadium, Infantino’s visit is also linked to his interest in grassroots football development and the history of Hussey College, an institution said to have produced several players for Nigeria’s senior national team.

Pinnick’s role in bringing Infantino to Warri

Former Nigeria Football Federation President and Deputy Chairperson of the FIFA Men’s Teams Competition Committee, Amaju Pinnick, had explained that Infantino’s visit was a reflection of his passion for grassroots football.

Speaking on the Morning Show, an Arise Television programme, on Tuesday, Pinnick said he had discussed Warri’s football history with the FIFA president and explained why the city deserved attention.

“On Saturday, by God’s grace, Gianni will arrive in Warri. Who will ever believe the president of FIFA will go to Warri, a subnational? Gianni loves grassroots football development,” he said.

Pinnick recalled that during one of their journeys from the airport, Infantino observed the number of people playing football along the road.

“The first time I picked him from the airport, as we were driving, he counted the number of people playing football, and I told him about Hussey College. This idea was also crystallised… He loves football, and he has seen everything,” he said.

The Hussey College connection

One of the places expected to feature prominently during the visit is Hussey College, Warri, an institution with a long history of producing football talents.

Pinnick drew attention to the school’s contribution to Nigerian football, claiming that it had produced 17 players who went on to represent the country with the Super Eagles.

“Hussey College produced 17 Super Eagles. Can you hear that? So, Gianni believes in these legends,” he said.

The school, founded in 1947, is among Warri’s historic educational institutions and has produced notable personalities across different fields.

The upgraded sporting facilities at the college are also part of efforts to provide opportunities for young people to develop their sporting abilities.

Among the former students associated with the national team are Clement Temile, Thompson Usiyan (Usiyen), Jossy Dombraye, Mike Obiku and Morten Owolo.

Why the Warri City Stadium matters

Infantino’s visit also brings attention to the Warri City Stadium, which the Delta State Government is rehabilitating under Governor Sheriff Oborevwori’s administration.

The 15,000-capacity facility is undergoing extensive improvements, including upgrades to its football pitch, athletics track, swimming pool, indoor sporting facilities, dressing rooms and other supporting infrastructure.

The project is intended to improve access to sporting facilities in Delta South and Central and strengthen the state’s capacity to host sporting competitions.

The state government has maintained that the project must meet the required standards before it is commissioned.

Infantino’s message about grassroots football

Speaking in an interview with Arise News in Warri, Infantino reiterated FIFA’s commitment to making football accessible to everyone, regardless of their location.

“Yeah, that’s what football is about, isn’t it? It’s for everyone, everywhere,” he said.

He explained that beyond major stadiums and the World Cup, smaller sporting facilities were equally important in bringing football closer to local communities.

“It’s big stadiums, it’s the World Cup, but it’s also smaller stadiums. It’s the proximity, and to be here in Warri for me is a fantastic occasion to showcase what we are about in FIFA, what we are about in football,” he said.

The FIFA president also expressed appreciation for the reception he received in Nigeria, citing the country’s recent victory at the Under-17 Women’s World Cup in Morocco as an example of the opportunities football could create for young people.

Infantino said FIFA wanted to extend similar life-changing experiences to more girls and boys in Nigeria and across Africa.

Describing his experience in the country, he said he was happy to be in Nigeria and was encouraged by the warm reception.

“I’m very well, I’m very good, I’m here, I’m with family in Nigeria, in Africa, and we are working, we are continuing to work, we’ve been working a lot, and I’m just happy, I’m just enjoying and taking on all this positive energy here,” he said.

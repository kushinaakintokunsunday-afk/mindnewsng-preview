N220bn solar push: ECN boss rallies youths behind Tinubu

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 3 min read

From Charity Nwakaudu, Abuja

The Director-General of the Energy Commission of Nigeria (ECN), Dr Mustapha Abdullahi, has urged Nigerian youths to support President Bola Tinubu’s second-term ambition, citing the administration’s investments in solar energy and renewable power development.

Abdullahi said continuity in leadership would enable the Federal Government to consolidate ongoing reforms, expand renewable energy projects and accelerate Nigeria’s quest for energy security.

The ECN boss, who highlighted the administration’s achievements in the energy sector, said the government had made significant investments in solar power as part of efforts to reduce dependence on the national grid and expensive diesel-powered generators.

He identified the National Public Sector Solarisation Initiative, backed by a reported N220 billion commitment, as one of the major interventions designed to provide alternative electricity to public institutions.

The programme targets the delivery of more than 100 megawatts of alternative energy to 72 primary healthcare centres and tertiary institutions nationwide.

The Energy Commission, he said, had provided technical support through energy audits, load assessments, project designs and coordination.

Among the flagship projects is the 4MWp solar photovoltaic mini-grid at Aminu Kano Teaching Hospital, Kano, which had reached 85 per cent completion as of May 2026.

Other beneficiary institutions include the University of Abuja, Bayero University Kano, Enugu Research Centre and Aliko Dangote University of Science and Technology, Wudil.

Abdullahi said the projects would reduce electricity costs and guarantee more reliable power for healthcare delivery, teaching and research.

He also listed the $750 million Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up (DARES) programme, supported by the World Bank, as another major intervention to expand electricity access in underserved and off-grid communities.

On the achievements of the Commission, the ECN boss highlighted its strategic partnership with LONGi, an international solar technology company, to promote local solar manufacturing, technology transfer and the development of Nigeria’s renewable energy value chain.

He said the partnership would reduce dependence on imported solar technologies while creating opportunities for local engineers, technicians and entrepreneurs.

The Commission has also strengthened collaboration with China Energy on renewable energy development, grid modernisation and technical cooperation.

Another major initiative is the establishment of the Barefoot Renewable Energy College at Osara, Kogi State, to train young Nigerians in solar installation, maintenance and other clean energy technologies.

Abdullahi said the initiative would equip youths with practical skills to benefit from emerging employment opportunities in the renewable energy industry.

The Commission has equally advanced the country’s clean mobility agenda with the commissioning of an electric vehicle charging station at its Abuja headquarters.

It has also established the National Committee on Renewable Energy, Energy Efficiency, Innovation and Certification to strengthen research, technical standards and collaboration among stakeholders.

Abdullahi further identified the promotion of Minimum Energy Performance Standards for cooling and refrigeration equipment as part of efforts to reduce energy consumption and electricity costs.

He maintained that renewable energy development offered enormous opportunities for Nigerian youths to become manufacturers, installers, researchers, engineers and entrepreneurs.

He urged young Nigerians to support the Tinubu administration’s second-term bid, insisting that policy consistency and sustained investment were necessary to deepen ongoing reforms and transform the country’s energy sector.

According to him, sustaining the momentum would help deliver affordable electricity, create jobs and strengthen Nigeria’s industrial capacity.

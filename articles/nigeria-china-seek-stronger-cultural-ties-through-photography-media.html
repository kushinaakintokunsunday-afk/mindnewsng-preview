Nigeria, China seek stronger cultural ties through photography, media

By Punch Punch 5d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria and China have called for stronger cultural exchanges and creative partnerships, with officials from both countries identifying photography, broadcastin

The call was made on Wednesday at the opening of the China Stories Photography Exhibition, organised by the China Cultural Centre in Nigeria in collaboration with the China Photographers Association in Abuja.

The exhibition featured 40 photographs showcasing China’s landscapes, cultural heritage and development. It brought together Chinese photographers, Nigerian government officials, diplomats and representatives of the creative industry as the countries marked 55 years of diplomatic relations.

Speaking at the event, the Chinese Cultural Counsellor, Yang Jianxing, said the exhibition was intended to promote mutual understanding and strengthen ties between the artistic communities of both countries, particularly photographers.

He described photography as a medium that could connect people across national and linguistic boundaries.

“Through its unique visual language, it captures the spirit of an era, conveys human emotions and connects hearts and minds. It serves as an important bridge for promoting mutual understanding and friendship among people from different countries,” Yang said.

He said the exhibition was also part of efforts to implement the outcomes of the 2024 Beijing Summit of the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation and advance the Global Civilisation Initiative.

Reflecting on the countries’ 55-year relationship, Yang said Nigeria and China had supported each other politically, pursued economic cooperation and enriched each other’s cultures.

“Over the past 55 years, our two peoples have supported each other politically, pursued mutual benefit and win-win cooperation economically, and learned from and enriched each other culturally. Our relationship has become a fine example of friendly exchanges among countries of the Global South,” he said.

The director of the China Cultural Centre urged cultural institutions and artists in both countries to deepen their cooperation and contribute to stronger people-to-people relations.

In his goodwill message, the Permanent Secretary of the Federal Ministry of Arts, Culture, Tourism and Creative Economy, Abdulkarim Ibrahim, assured the Chinese delegation of Nigeria’s support for joint exhibitions, training and other creative collaborations.

He said photography offered countries a way to document their histories and communicate their identities to the world.

“Photography is more than pictures. Photography is memory. It is history. It is how a nation tells its story to the world without speaking a word,” he said.

He described the visit by the Chinese photographers as an opportunity for artists from both countries to build relationships through shared creative experiences.

Ibrahim called for partnerships that would allow photographers to work together, organise exhibitions and tell stories about their respective societies.

Also speaking, the Director-General of the National Broadcasting Commission, Charles Ebuebu, urged Nigerian and Chinese broadcasters, filmmakers, photographers and digital content creators to develop partnerships that would promote cultural understanding.

Ebuebu said the exhibition was significant as the countries marked 55 years of diplomatic relations and the 2026 China-Africa Year of People-to-People Exchanges.

He said the photographs offered Nigerians an opportunity to experience aspects of China’s landscapes, heritage and cultural traditions.

According to him, advances in broadcasting and digital technology had made it easier for countries to share their cultures with international audiences, but greater connectivity alone did not guarantee mutual understanding.

“What matters is the quality, authenticity and diversity of the stories we share,” he said.

Ebuebu said cultural exchanges should be reciprocal and should preserve the identities of participating countries.

“The objective should be mutual appreciation, learning and enrichment. Nigerians should have opportunities to experience Chinese culture, just as Chinese audiences should have greater access to Nigeria’s rich cultural heritage, music, film, literature, festivals and creative expressions,” he said.

He identified joint documentary productions, photography projects, cultural programming, exchange visits and creative industry partnerships as areas where both countries could expand cooperation.

Such partnerships, he added, could promote cultural tourism, support knowledge transfer and create economic opportunities for their creative industries.

“The future of international friendship will depend not only on diplomatic engagements and economic partnerships but also on how well our peoples understand, appreciate and connect with one another,” he said.

He urged the organisers to sustain the initiative and pursue further opportunities for cultural and media exchanges between Nigeria and China.

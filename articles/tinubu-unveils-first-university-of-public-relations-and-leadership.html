Tinubu unveils first University of Public Relations and Leadership

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 5d ago 4 min read

From Juliana Taiwo-Obalonye, Abuja

President Bola Tinubu has unveiled Nigeria’s first University of Public Relations and Leadership, approving the specialised institution to advance professional training, research and leadership development amid growing concerns over misinformation, disinformation and declining public trust.

Tinubu, represented by Vice-President Kashim Shettima at the opening of the 38th World Public Relations Forum (WPRF) and fifth African Public Relations Association Conference in Abuja on Wednesday, said the institution would equip communication professionals to confront the challenges of artificial intelligence and rapidly changing digital technologies.

Tinubu described the university, located in Nasarawa State, as a lasting legacy of Nigeria’s hosting of the global forum and an investment in the future of responsible communication.

“I am pleased to formally approve and support the establishment of the University of Public Relations and Leadership, a pioneering initiative designed to advance specialised education, research, professional development and leadership training in public relations, strategic communications and related disciplines.”

He commended the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations (NIPR), Federal Ministry of Information and National Orientation and Nasarawa State Government for collaborating on the initiative, particularly acknowledging Governor Abdullahi Sule’s commitment to the project.

Speaking on the forum’s theme, “Responsible Communication: The Voice of the World,” Tinubu challenged practitioners to move beyond image and reputation management to promote truth, accountability, transparency and constructive engagement between governments and citizens.

He said responsible communication was essential to democratic governance, economic development and public confidence in institutions, urging practitioners to uphold the highest ethical and professional standards.

The President also highlighted Nigeria’s economic and demographic potential, projecting that its population would reach 440 million by 2050, making it the world’s third most populous country.

He said six of Africa’s 10 unicorn companies were Nigerian, citing Flutterwave, OPay, Interswitch, Andela, Moniepoint and Moove as examples of the country’s growing technology ecosystem.

Tinubu urged international delegates to explore investment opportunities in Nigeria’s technology, creative industries, tourism and other sectors.

“Nigeria welcomes you. Nigeria is open for partnership, and Nigeria is ready to engage with the world,” he said.

Kenyan President William Ruto, represented at the forum, urged African governments to make communication central to policymaking, warning that public trust could not be manufactured through publicity but must be earned through honesty, transparency and accountability.

He disclosed that Kenya adopted a National Communication Policy in August 2026 to ensure that government institutions provided citizens with timely, accurate and accessible information.

Minister of Information and National Orientation, Mohammed Idris, called for stronger professional ethics and international cooperation against misinformation, warning that the misuse of digital platforms could deepen divisions and threaten security.

“Our communication should build bridges, not walls,” he said, urging governments to communicate openly, journalists to uphold professional standards and public relations practitioners to embrace transparency.

NIPR President, Ike Neliaku, disclosed that 2,837 delegates had attended the forum as of yesterday, describing it as the largest gathering in the event’s history.

He said the conference would address professional development, youth participation, women’s leadership, community relations and peacebuilding, alongside a cultural festival and a social responsibility initiative to feed 10,000 people.

“Public relations is not propaganda. Public relations is a business of trust,” Neliaku declared.

President of the Global Alliance for Public Relations and Communication Management, Justin Green, commended Nigeria for securing the hosting rights, saying the country’s determination had attracted delegates from 126 countries.

Green also described the University of Public Relations and Leadership as the world’s first university dedicated to the profession and praised the Nasarawa State Government for supporting the initiative.

African Public Relations Association President, Arik Karani, commended Nigeria’s leadership in professional regulation, noting that its public relations legislation had provided a model for other African countries.

“Nigeria is the table when we are discussing public relations,” he said, urging Africa to take a more active role in shaping global communication standards.

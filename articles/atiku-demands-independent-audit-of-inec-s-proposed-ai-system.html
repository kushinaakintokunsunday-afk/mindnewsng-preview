Atiku demands independent audit of INEC’s proposed AI system

By Punch Punch 5d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has called on the Independent National Electoral Commission to open its proposed Artificial Intelligence system to independ

 Atiku warned that the use of AI in election result management without adequate transparency and safeguards could undermine public confidence in the electoral process, particularly if algorithms were allowed to delay, reject or interfere with the transmission of polling unit results.

 He asked the electoral commission to disclose the technical and operational details of the system, including its source code, training data, discrepancy-detection criteria and the identities of its developers and third-party vendors.

 The former vice president made the demands in a petition addressed to the INEC Chairman on behalf of the African Democratic Congress and allied democratic stakeholders.

 In a statement issued on Thursday by the Director of Strategic Communication, African Democratic Congress Presidential Campaign Council, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku said independent cybersecurity experts, representatives of political parties and civil society technologists should be granted access to the system for a comprehensive audit before the nationwide mock accreditation exercise scheduled for November 28, 2026.

 He expressed concern over INEC’s announced integration of AI and intelligent document processing into the process linking its Data Verification and Authentication System (DIVAS) with the INEC Result Viewing portal (IReV).

 According to him, Nigerians must be allowed to understand how the technology works and the extent of its influence on the handling of election results.

 “INEC cannot ask Nigerians to trust a system whose rules they are forbidden to see. If an algorithm can flag a polling unit result sheet, the public deserves to know what triggers that flag, who set the threshold, who can change it and what happens to the result afterward,” he said.

 Atiku cautioned that technical limitations could cause legitimate result sheets to be flagged or delayed, citing poor lighting, unfamiliar handwriting and worn-out paper as possible challenges that could affect the system’s ability to recognise uploaded documents.

 He argued that such technical difficulties should not become grounds for withholding valid election results, insisting that similar discrepancies must be treated consistently across polling units.

 The former presidential candidate also demanded that INEC establish and publicise a clear procedure for reviewing results flagged by the AI system.

 He said the commission must explain who would review such results, how decisions would be documented, how political parties would be notified and the mechanism available for promptly challenging erroneous assessments.

 Atiku maintained that AI should serve only as an administrative tool under human supervision and must not be empowered to delete, alter or permanently block election results.

 He further insisted that the commission must reaffirm the legal status of Form EC8A, the polling unit result sheet completed and signed by presiding officers and party agents.

 “The polling unit result sheet, Form EC8A, records what happened where the people voted. INEC must publicly reaffirm the legal status of the physical form completed and signed at the polling unit, and explain how it will resolve any conflict between that document and an AI assessment.

 “A machine’s verdict cannot be allowed to quietly displace the evidence witnessed by voters and party agents,” he added.

 The ADC presidential candidate also called for real-time monitoring access for political parties through a dedicated dashboard or observer interface to enable them to identify unusual patterns of result flagging, upload delays and blocked submissions during elections.

 He urged the National Assembly to summon INEC’s leadership and its Information and Communication Technology Directorate to explain the system’s design, safeguards and independent audit arrangements.

 Atiku also called on election observers and international partners to extend their monitoring activities beyond polling unit activities to include the digital handling and transmission of results.

 He advised voters to lawfully document results as announced and displayed at their polling units to provide a public record against which electronically uploaded results could be verified.

 The former vice president linked the demand for transparency to his campaign’s promise of an affordable life, arguing that Nigerians must be allowed to freely determine their political future without technological interference.

 “Voters must be free to accept or reject that promise at the ballot box. No hidden algorithm should stand between a struggling family’s vote and the change that family hopes to see,” he said.

 He urged INEC to address the concerns before public mistrust over the technology escalates into an electoral crisis.

 The demand comes amid growing attention to the role of technology in Nigeria’s electoral process, particularly following controversies surrounding the electronic transmission and public viewing of polling unit results in previous elections.

 INEC’s IReV portal was introduced to provide public access to scanned copies of polling unit result sheets, although its role is distinct from the statutory collation and declaration of election results.

 The proposed use of AI in processing election documents has raised questions about transparency, accountability, human intervention and the safeguards required to ensure that technological tools do not compromise the integrity of votes cast by Nigerians.

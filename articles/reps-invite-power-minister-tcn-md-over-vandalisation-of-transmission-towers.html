Reps invite power minister, TCN MD over vandalisation of transmission towers

By Punch Punch 6d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The House of Representatives has resolved to investigate the recurrent vandalisation of electricity transmission towers across the country, summoning the Minist

The House also directed its Committees on Public Assets and Power to investigate the destruction of 10 transmission towers along the Kano-Bauchi, Kano-Katsina and Kano-Kaduna 330kV transmission lines within a five-month period.

The resolution followed the adoption of a motion sponsored by the member representing Surulere II Federal Constituency of Lagos State, Lanre Okunlola, titled, “Need to investigate the recurrent vandalism of Transmission Company of Nigeria and Niger Delta Power Holding Company Transmission towers and protect the national electricity infrastructure.”

Moving the motion, the lawmaker drew attention to a report published by THISDAY newspaper on October 29, 2026, which disclosed that 10 transmission towers had been vandalised within five months along the three transmission corridors.

He warned that the destruction of transmission towers and the theft of their components posed “a serious threat to the stability of the national grid, disrupts electricity supply and exposes members of the public to safety hazards.”

The lawmaker further expressed concern that the incidents imposed substantial and unbudgeted repair costs on the Federal Government, while raising questions about the effectiveness of existing security arrangements around critical electricity infrastructure.

The House noted that TCN and NDPHC operate critical components of Nigeria’s national electricity transmission network, describing the facilities as “vital national assets essential to electricity supply, economic development and national security.”

It expressed concern that the recurrence of such attacks pointed to possible weaknesses in surveillance, security architecture, intelligence gathering, asset management and the enforcement of existing laws.

Following the adoption of the motion, the House mandated its Committees on Public Assets and Power to invite the Minister of Power, the Managing Director of TCN and the Managing Director of NDPHC to brief them on the circumstances surrounding the reported incidents.

They are expected to provide “details of the extent of damage, estimated financial losses, the status of repairs and preventive measures put in place to forestall further attacks.”

The committees are also to request both companies to submit comprehensive reports covering all recorded incidents of transmission infrastructure vandalism from January 2024 to date.

The reports are expected to identify affected locations, damaged assets, repair and replacement costs, emergency procurements and the status of remedial measures taken by the companies.

Beyond the immediate investigation, the House directed the committees to ascertain the total financial implications of the vandalism, including repair costs, replacement expenditure, emergency procurement and demonstrable economic losses resulting from disruptions to electricity transmission.

They are also to assess the adequacy of existing security arrangements, surveillance systems, maintenance procedures and risk assessments for transmission towers and associated facilities.

The House further called for the involvement of relevant security and law enforcement agencies “to provide updates on investigations, arrests and prosecutions connected with the reported incidents, subject to applicable laws and the independence of judicial proceedings.”

The agencies are also expected to examine the effectiveness of existing regulatory and enforcement controls against the purchase, sale and recycling of stolen transmission components, with a view to dismantling the illicit market for materials taken from public infrastructure.

To address the problem, the lawmakers recommended stronger surveillance, routine inspections, intelligence sharing, community engagement and improved coordination among TCN, NDPHC, security agencies and host communities.

The committees were given four weeks to conclude the investigation and report back to the House for further legislative action.

The investigation comes against the backdrop of persistent challenges confronting Nigeria’s electricity sector, where the transmission network remains a critical link between power generation companies and distribution companies.

Transmission towers carry high-voltage electricity across long distances, and damage to the structures can disrupt power supply to several communities and place additional pressure on other parts of the network.

Repeated attacks on transmission infrastructure also impose additional costs on the electricity industry, as damaged towers require specialised equipment, materials and technical personnel to restore them.

The House said the investigation would help establish accountability for public funds spent on repairs, identify weaknesses in the protection of electricity assets and develop measures to prevent further attacks.

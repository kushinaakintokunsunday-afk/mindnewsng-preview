FG, UN disappointed as COP31 climate talks collapse

By Punch Punch 1d ago 5 min read

The FG maintained that despite the lack of a finalised deal, Nigeria would not stop its domestic climate programmes.

In a statement released on Sunday after the fallout, the Director-General, National Council on Climate Change, Omotenioye Majekodunmi, said Nigeria would continue implementing its climate commitments under its Nationally Determined Contribution 3.0, strengthening climate governance and attracting investment into the energy transition.

She said, “COP31’s inability to reach consensus on an overarching political outcome is disappointing and underscores the continuing challenges facing international climate cooperation. However, the absence of a new political agreement does not diminish the urgency of climate action, nor does it invalidate the commitments already undertaken under the Paris Agreement.”

She added, “For Nigeria, our focus remains firmly on implementation. We will continue advancing our NDC 3.0, strengthening climate governance, mobilising investment and pursuing an orderly, just and nationally determined energy transition.”

Majekodunmi said the negotiations had reinforced the need to match climate ambition with adequate financing and practical implementation plans, particularly for developing countries with different economic circumstances.

She said Nigeria’s climate progress would ultimately be measured by its impact on employment, businesses, energy access and livelihoods.

This year’s climate conference, tagged COP31, is the second time in the history of global climate talks that a summit has ended without an agreement and the first in 17 years, The PUNCH reports.

The summit, which opened on November 9, 2026, and was scheduled to close on November 20, 2026, ended on Sunday without adopting a final decision, exposing deep divisions over fossil fuel phase-out, climate finance, adaptation funding and a just energy transition.

The conference became the longest United Nations climate summit on record, surpassing the previous record held by COP25 in Madrid, Spain, in 2019, which ran about 44 hours past its scheduled closing time.

COP31 President Murat Kurum confirmed during the closing plenary on Sunday that the final draft agreement would not be adopted after countries failed to bridge their differences over the proposed text.

The breakdown followed disagreements over the inclusion of language on transitioning away from fossil fuels, adaptation finance and the role of carbon capture technologies in tackling climate change.

He said, “As the Presidency, we have worked with a focus on results from day one. Getting hung up on a single word and stalling the process means dashing the hopes of people expecting good news from us. A system that cannot make decisions benefits no one.

“If we are talking about sincerity, we must all act accordingly and work together towards our shared goals.”

COP31 negotiations chair Chris Bowen, who unsuccessfully sought to broker consensus among nearly 200 nations, conceded he could not overcome what he called “unbridgeable red lines.”

“Unfortunately, we were unable to reach agreement on a cover decision… And that’s disappointing, very disappointing,” Bowen, who is Australia’s climate minister, said.

The breakdown followed nearly two weeks of negotiations and disagreements over the language of the proposed agreement, particularly the demand for a clear commitment memories of the failed Copenhagen summit of 2009 and raised fresh doubts about the effectiveness of climate diplomacy.

The failure also attracted criticism from the UN Secretary-General, António Guterres, who expressed deep disappointment over the inability of governments to agree on a common political outcome.

In a statement following the conclusion of the summit, Guterres said, “I am deeply disappointed that COP31 has ended without consensus on an overarching outcome. I share the very profound frustration of all those who expected governments to rise to the moment the climate crisis demands.”

He warned that the failure to reach an agreement would not change the urgency of the climate crisis, noting that global temperatures continued to break records while emissions remained dangerously high.

“There may be no consensus in the negotiating room. There is no ambiguity in the science. Temperatures keep shattering records. Climate extremes keep intensifying. Emissions remain far too high. And every delay makes our task harder and more costly,” he said.

The UN chief also criticised the continued dependence on fossil fuels, saying, “Fossil fuels are driving the climate crisis. The continued addiction to coal, oil and gas is fueling climate chaos, exposing economies to price shocks, and undermining energy security. We can’t put out the fire while pouring fuel on it.”

“It certainly fell short today,” said UN climate chief Simon Stiell, describing progress over the marathon fortnight of negotiations in Antalya as “limited. The world cannot afford a repeat.”

The disagreements also involved the proposed adaptation finance target of $120bn, which disappeared from the final draft, while countries failed to agree on a concrete plan to mobilise the resources needed by vulnerable economies.

The breakdown has shifted attention to COP32, scheduled to take place in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, next year, where countries are expected to continue discussions on unresolved climate commitments.

The failure comes after COP28 in Dubai in 2023, when countries agreed to transition away from fossil fuels in a just, orderly and equitable manner. COP30 in Belém, Brazil, also provided a mandate for developing a just transition mechanism, which campaigners had hoped would receive concrete institutional backing in Antalya.

However, the Climate Action Network maintained that the fight for climate justice would continue, insisting that the next summit must deliver financing, adaptation support and a fair transition for developing countries.

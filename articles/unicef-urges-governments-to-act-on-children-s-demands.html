UNICEF urges governments to act on children’s demands

By Vanguard Vanguard 2d ago 2 min read

By Hadiza Yusuf

The United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) has urged governments to translate children’s recommendations into concrete actions through policies, budgetary allocations and programmes that protect their rights and improve their welfare.

The Chief of UNICEF Field Office, Kano, Shafeeq Ur-Rehman, made the call at the Zonal Children’s Summit organised to mark the 2026 World Children’s Day in Kano.

Ur-Rehman, who was represented by the Officer-in-Charge of the field office, Dr. Serekeberehan Seyoum Deres, said children from Kano, Jigawa and Katsina states had identified critical issues affecting their lives and well-being.

He urged commissioners and senior government officials from the three states to give serious consideration to the children’s concerns and ensure their recommendations informed decisions in their respective ministries and agencies.

“But I urge you to do more than listen today: Take the children’s recommendations back to your ministries and agencies, and let them shape your budgets, policies and programmes,” he said.

He added that the children would hold government officials accountable for commitments made at the summit.

The UNICEF official stressed that children’s rights must be protected at all times, noting that every child deserved equal opportunities to survive, learn, live in safety and have their voices heard.

“Every child deserves the right to survive, learn, be protected and have their voice heard, regardless of gender, ability or circumstance,” he said.

He said UNICEF would sustain its collaboration with governments, communities and young people in Kano, Jigawa and Katsina to ensure that no child was left behind, particularly girls, children with disabilities, out-of-school children and those living in hard-to-reach communities.

Also speaking, Hajiya Aisha Albasu, who represented the Chairman of the State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB), Alhaji Yusuf Kabir, said the Kano State Government remained committed to promoting and protecting children’s rights.

She said the government would continue to work with relevant agencies and development partners to ensure children had access to quality education, healthcare, protection and other essential services needed for their growth and development.

Speaking on behalf of the children, Alamin Umar and Asma’u Anka raised concerns over alleged cases of abuse involving pupils in schools.

They called for stronger measures to protect students from all forms of violence, urging the government and relevant authorities to provide safe learning environments where children could study without fear of abuse or intimidation.

The children also appealed to the government to provide school buses to ease transportation challenges faced by students, as well as more classroom seats and improved learning facilities to create a conducive environment for effective teaching and learning.

Women groups push 35% leadership quota for firms

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Women in Successful Careers is advocating reforms to raise female representation on corporate boards and executive management teams to 35 per cent. The push for

The push forms the core agenda at a press conference hearalding the organisation’s 18th Annual Leadership and Mentoring Conference scheduled to hold on Saturday, 28 November, 2026, in Lagos, under the theme, ‘Courage in Action: Shaping Policies and Structures for Inclusive Progress.’

According to the organisers, the gathering will examine how policies, institutional frameworks and governance structures can create more equitable pathways for women to access leadership, influence decision-making and contribute meaningfully to national economic development.

WISCAR noted that conversations around women’s representation must move beyond advocacy and commitments to tangible institutional change. While developing capable women through mentoring and professional development remains essential, the group maintained that sustainable economic progress depends on institutions creating the enabling environment for women’s capabilities to translate into leadership and impact.

Speaking at a pre-conference press briefing in Lagos, the Founder and Chairperson of WISCAR, Mrs Amina Oyagbola, stated that the organisation’s experience over nearly two decades had reinforced the urgency of dismantling institutional barriers alongside individual capacity building.

“Developing capable women is essential, but institutions must also create the conditions and environment in which the capabilities of these talented women translate into leadership, influence and impact. We will not be able to move the needle until the systems and institutions within which women operate are redesigned to enable their full participation and contribution,” she said.

As part of its policy drive, WISCAR is collaborating through the Women in Leadership Coalition alongside the Women in Leadership Advancement Network, Women in Management, Business and Public Service, and the Nigeria Governors’ Forum to advance workplace and governance reforms.

Beyond the 35 per cent private-sector board and executive target, the coalition is pushing for improved female representation in public-sector leadership and family-friendly workplace policies, including 16 weeks of fully paid maternity leave and 14 days of paid paternity leave for new fathers.

The initiatives align with WISCAR’s 2026–2030 strategic plan to consolidate impact and scale for systemic change by prioritising leadership pipelines, institutional partnerships and policy influence.

A major highlight of the conference will be the conferment of the Distinguished WISCAR Award on a social impact and leadership executive, Dr Ndidi Nwuneli, who will also deliver the keynote address. Nwuneli is the former President and Chief Executive Officer of the ONE Campaign, Founder of LEAP Africa and African Food Changemakers, and Co-Founder of Sahel Consulting, AACE Foods and Wealth4Impact.

The conference, the organisers said, would convene leaders from government, the private sector, professional bodies and civil society. Expected speakers include the President of the Nigerian Bar Association, Mrs Oyinkansola Badejo-Okusanya, SAN; Group Chairman of the Nigerian Exchange Group, Alhaji Umaru Kwairanga; Director-General of the Maryam Babangida National Centre for Women Development, Dr Adedayo Benjamins-Laniyi; President of the Chartered Institute of Personnel Management, Mallam Ahmed Gobir; and Partner at Templars and Chairperson of the Chartered Institute of Directors Women’s Group, Mrs Ronke Sokefun.

2027: LP factional presidential candidate quits race, backs Tinubu

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A factional presidential candidate of the Labour Party, Kennedy Ahanotu, has withdrawn from the 2027 presidential race and declared support for President Bola T

Ahanotu announced this at a press briefing in Abuja on Monday.

The former National Youth Leader of the party and an ally of LP’s 2023 presidential candidate, Peter Obi, said he has agreed to work with the City Boy Movement to mobilise support for Tinubu ahead of the 2027 general election.

In May 2026, The PUNCH reported that Ahanotu emerged as the consensus presidential candidate of the Julius Abure-led faction of the Labour Party at its presidential, governorship and House of Representatives primaries held in Abuja.

The faction adopted the 42-year-old party stalwart amid the protracted leadership crisis that had divided the opposition party.

However, he disclosed his withdrawal from the race on Monday, explaining that his decision followed a meeting with youth leaders across the country, who mandated him to engage with the President’s support group and galvanise support for his re-election because of the lingering crisis in the party.

He said, “I took the decision to withdraw my presidential ambition after a meeting with the Labour Party youth structure we deployed in 2023 across the country, where all the state leaders, having witnessed and seen what the party has become, urged me to align with City Boy Movement to back President Bola Tinubu’s re-election.

“Nobody is happy with what the party has become. It has spent the last few years in crisis. So, when your followers mandate you as a leader to make a bold move, you have to do it. So I heeded that call.

“Of course, Seyi Tinubu has been my friend since 2022, and we have a good relationship. Most people don’t know this. He’s a great guy, very intelligent and brilliant.”

Ahanotu said the youth leaders, who represented structures across the 774 local government areas, believed the persistent crisis in the Labour Party had made it necessary to explore alternative political alignments.

He added, “We are currently partnering to support the re-election of President Bola Tinubu in 2027.”

Defending his decision against allegations of betraying the Labour Party, Ahanotu said he had invested 10 years in the party, having joined in 2016 after studying the manifestos of several political parties.

He said he actively campaigned for Obi during the 2023 presidential election, mobilising young Nigerians to support the Labour Party candidate.

However, he accused politicians who joined the party shortly before the 2023 elections of sidelining its longstanding members after gaining political prominence, alleging that the party’s legacy members were eventually pushed out of its leadership structure.

Asked whether his support for Tinubu would be regarded as a betrayal by Labour Party stakeholders, Ahanotu said the party had betrayed him instead.

He said, “I can’t say I have betrayed anybody. I’d rather say that the party has betrayed me because the party I’ve worked for, the party I gave all, I gave 10 years to the Labour Party.”

But the Labour Party dismissed Ahanotu’s claim to its membership and presidential candidacy, insisting that neither was recognised by the Senator Nenadi Usman-led leadership.

The party’s National Publicity Secretary, Ken Asogwa, said Ahanotu was free to support any presidential candidate but accused him of dragging the party’s name into his political realignment.

He said, “The young man, who used to be a member of the Labour Party, is free to root for any candidate of his choice. After all, the Constitution guarantees him that freedom to make his choice. However, the Labour Party feels particularly embarrassed that its name is being dragged into his new undertaking.

“First, the fellow was expelled by the Party as far back as 14th March 2026 and is, therefore, no longer a member of the Labour Party. He is only dragging the Party’s name along because he knows that his faceless name cannot land him a job in the APC.

“Secondly, it is a crime and an offence against the Electoral Act for anyone to refer to this individual as a former or factional presidential candidate of the Labour Party. He was not even a member of the Labour Party when the Party commenced the sale of its presidential nomination forms and, therefore, could not have laid claim to being a presidential candidate of the Party, or a factional one.”

Asogwa also dismissed Ahanotu’s claim that the party’s internal crisis informed his decision, describing the alleged factionalisation as imaginary.

He said, “There is no crisis in the Party to warrant the mention of an imaginary faction as an excuse for his defection to the APC.

“Finally, the Labour Party notes with enthusiasm the recent upsurge in the penchant for dropping the name of the Labour Party by politicians seeking refuge in other political platforms. It evidently demonstrates that the soaring image of the Party remains unmatched.

“However, as much as the party recognises that these pockets of name-dropping are a testament to its towering image, the Labour Party wishes to enjoin displaced politicians to desist from needlessly dragging the name of the Party into their political activities. The party will soon begin to take legal action against some of these acts of impersonation.”

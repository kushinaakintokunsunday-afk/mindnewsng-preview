Lagos moves to strengthen law against HIV stigma, discrimination

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 4d ago 4 min read

Photo: The Sun Nigeria

By Lukman Olabiyi

Lagos State Government is working towards strengthening its legal framework to combat HIV-related stigma and discrimination, as part of efforts to remove barriers to prevention, testing and treatment of the disease.

The Chief Executive Officer of the Lagos State AIDS Control Agency (LSACA), Dr Folakemi Animashaun, disclosed this on Thursday at a media briefing at the Bagauda Kaltho Press Centre, Alausa, Ikeja, to flag off activities marking the 2026 World AIDS Day.

Animashaun said the state was working towards introducing a more effective legal framework to address emerging challenges associated with HIV and provide stronger protection for people living with the virus.

She explained that the existing legal framework being relied upon to address HIV-related discrimination originated in 2001, during the administration of President Bola Tinubu, then governor of Lagos State.

According to her, the proposed reforms are intended to bring the legal framework in line with contemporary realities and strengthen the protection of the rights and dignity of people living with HIV.

She identified stigma and discrimination as major obstacles to the state’s HIV response, noting that fear of being discriminated against could discourage people from undergoing HIV tests, disclosing their status or seeking medical attention.

The LSACA boss said the agency was collaborating with the State Gender and Human Rights Response Team and other relevant institutions to promote confidentiality, protect the dignity of people living with HIV and ensure appropriate responses to discriminatory practices.
She insisted that nobody should be denied healthcare or treated with indignity because of their HIV status.

Animashaun, who spoke on the theme, “Ending AIDS Starts with Access: Equity, Ownership and Sustainability,” said Lagos was also intensifying efforts to protect the gains recorded in the fight against HIV through increased domestic funding and stronger institutional capacity.

She disclosed that the state’s approved HIV budget allocation increased from approximately N791 million to N1.065 billion in 2026, representing an increase of about 35 per cent.
She said the state government had also procured antiretroviral medicines and that LSACA had commenced their distribution to people who depend on the drugs for treatment.

According to her, the intervention is designed to complement existing national and development-partner arrangements and strengthen the state’s capacity to ensure uninterrupted access to essential HIV medicines.

She, however, stressed that the success of the initiative would depend not merely on the procurement of medicines but also on effective forecasting, quality assurance, distribution and stock monitoring.

” The true measure of treatment security is not simply the purchase of medicines; it is whether the person who needs those medicines can obtain them consistently, without interruption,” she said.

The agency’s CEO further disclosed that Lagos was developing a 10-year HIV Response Transition and Sustainability Roadmap covering 2027 to 2036.

She said the roadmap would provide a framework for strengthening domestic financing, healthcare delivery, human resources, medicines and diagnostics, programme information, community participation and institutional accountability.

The roadmap, she added, was expected to be formally launched by Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu at the World AIDS Day Symposium scheduled for December 1, 2026, subject to the necessary approvals.

Providing an update on the state’s HIV response, Animashaun said consolidated programme records showed 387,558 HIV-negative test results and 7,584 HIV-positive results.

She clarified that the figures represented reported test results and should not be interpreted as the number of new HIV infections or necessarily as unique individuals.

She also disclosed that the maternal health programme recorded 156,122 new antenatal care clients and 156,109 HIV-negative test results among pregnant and breastfeeding women.
In community-based interventions, she said, 25,167 HIV tests were conducted among people aged 15 and above between January and October 2026, producing 1,166 positive results.

Animashaun said the figures underscored the importance of expanding access to testing and ensuring that people who test positive are promptly linked to treatment and sustained care.

She added that the agency was strengthening collaboration with healthcare institutions, development partners, community organisations and relevant government ministries and agencies to improve service delivery, programme coordination and accountability.

On prevention, she urged residents to obtain accurate information about HIV, know their status and take advantage of available preventive services, including condom use, HIV testing, pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP).

She also emphasised the importance of early diagnosis and consistent treatment, explaining that people living with HIV who take effective medication and maintain an undetectable viral load cannot transmit the virus sexually, a principle known as Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U).

State police won’t be used to hound political opponents –C’River govt

From Aniekan Aniekan, Calabar

The Cross River State Government has moved to allay fears that governors could turn state police into instruments of political repression, declaring that the proposed security outfit would not be used to intimidate opposition politicians or settle political scores.

Commissioner for Information and Orientation, Ekpenyong Cobham, dismissed concerns over possible abuse of state police powers, insisting that worsening insecurity demanded decentralised policing and that the state’s experience with its Homeland Security Services had demonstrated the benefits of locally managed security operations.

Cobham maintained that the protection of lives and property must take precedence over fears of political interference, arguing that the establishment of state police would strengthen security operations by bringing law enforcement closer to communities.

He cited the state’s existing Homeland Security Services as evidence that a locally managed security outfit could operate without being deployed against political opponents.

“Cross River State already has a security system, which is the Homeland Security Services. From it, we have so far gathered the requisite experience in running a localised security apparatus,” he said.

The commissioner explained that the proposed state police system would require appropriate legislation and administrative structures before becoming operational, adding that states would need to establish recruitment procedures and provide adequate training for personnel.

According to him, each state would be expected to domesticate the relevant legislation through its House of Assembly, establish the necessary administrative framework and recruit qualified personnel for training at new or existing police facilities.

Cobham expressed confidence in Cross River’s preparedness, suggesting that the Attorney-General and Commissioner for Justice might already be considering the legal and administrative requirements for establishing the proposed police service.

He argued that decentralised policing would improve intelligence gathering and response to security threats because officers familiar with the language, culture and terrain of their communities would be better positioned to identify criminal activities and respond promptly.

Citing Calabar South, where he resides, Cobham said the area had become considerably more peaceful following the deployment of Homeland Security personnel, despite its previous reputation for insecurity.

He maintained that the experience demonstrated the advantages of community-based security arrangements and the need to strengthen local capacity to protect residents.

The commissioner insisted that the growing security challenges confronting the country made the establishment of state police imperative, stressing that concerns about possible political interference should not overshadow the need for effective policing.

His assurance comes amid renewed debate over the potential abuse of state police by governors, particularly fears that locally controlled security personnel could be deployed to intimidate opposition politicians, restrict political activities or settle political disputes.

The Senate has consequently demanded a comprehensive operational law to define the powers and responsibilities of federal and state police authorities and establish safeguards against jurisdictional conflicts.

Senate President Godswill Akpabio, while commenting on the proposed policing arrangement, stressed the importance of preventing operational clashes between the two security structures.

“The next step is to address the operationalisation of the system and ensure that there is no conflict between the State Police and the Federal Police,” Akpabio said.

The demand followed the approval of the Constitution (Sixth Alteration) Bill, 2026, by all 36 state Houses of Assembly and its transmission to President Bola Tinubu for assent.

The proposed National Policing Bill, which is expected to provide the legal and administrative framework for the decentralised policing system, is undergoing executive consideration.

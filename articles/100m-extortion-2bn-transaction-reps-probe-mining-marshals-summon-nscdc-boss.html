₦100m Extortion, ₦2bn Transaction: Reps probe mining marshals, summon NSCDC boss

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: VeryNigerian

The House of Representatives Committee on Solid Minerals has given the Commandant-General of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), Prof. Ahmed Abubakar Audi, 24 hours to appear before it over allegations of financial irregularities, extortion, unlawful arrests and abuse of power involving personnel of the Mining Marshals.

The committee is also investigating the circumstances surrounding the death of a serving NSCDC officer, Deputy Superintendent Agada Levi Agada, whose family alleged that he was shot dead by fellow officers at a mining site.

The ultimatum was issued on Monday during an investigative hearing following a motion moved by Ali Amburura, representing Sokoto State, and seconded by a lawmaker from Katsina State.

Chairman of the committee, Gaza Gbefwi, said the lawmakers would examine complaints of harassment and unlawful detention of licensed mining operators, an alleged ₦2 billion transaction linked to the bank account of a former state commander, and suspected misconduct among Mining Marshals operating across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory.

The investigation will also cover the procurement of operational vehicles and the sources of funding for the special security unit.

Gbefwi recalled that more than 2,200 Mining Marshals were inaugurated in March 2024 to tackle illegal mining and protect the country’s mineral resources.

He, however, warned that the initiative must not become an avenue for violating citizens’ fundamental rights or conducting operations outside the provisions of the law.

One of the major issues raised at the hearing was the death of Agada, whose family accused fellow NSCDC personnel of shooting him.

Presenting a petition on behalf of the family, their lawyer, Obasi-Obi Nwabueze, alleged that the deceased sustained a gunshot wound to the right side of his chest after being shot by fellow officers.

He told the lawmakers that medical affidavits from doctors who attended to Agada contradicted the NSCDC’s account that the officer died following an attack by bandits.

According to the lawyer, Agada was taken to three medical facilities before he eventually died at the Federal Medical Centre, Keffi.

Nwabueze further alleged that two NSCDC officers were indicted and three others arrested during a police investigation into the incident. He, however, claimed that the investigation was subsequently frustrated.

He urged the committee to ensure that the investigation was concluded and that anyone found culpable was prosecuted.

The lawyer also appealed for protection for the deceased officer’s widow, two children and other witnesses, alleging that some had received threats and faced intimidation.

In response, Gbefwi expressed condolences to the family and requested an executive summary of the petition to enable the committee to consider further action.

The lawmakers also heard allegations from Stone Rockers Nigeria Limited, whose Director of Communications, Rachel Ogbonna, accused the Mining Marshals of unlawfully arresting 12 workers at the company’s quarry site despite its possession of valid operating licences.

Ogbonna alleged that the company’s chairman and director were also detained after visiting an NSCDC facility along Airport Road in an attempt to secure the release of the workers.

In a separate submission, security operative Odey Cosmas Polycarp accused the Mining Marshals, allegedly led by one Onoja Attah, of arresting members of his workforce and demanding ₦100 million for their release.

Polycarp claimed that the workers were arrested in a residential area where no mining activities were taking place. He also alleged that the detainees were beaten while in custody.

The allegations have raised concerns about the operations, accountability and enforcement powers of the Mining Marshals, particularly their treatment of licensed mining operators and the conduct of personnel deployed to tackle illegal mining.

The committee is expected to hear from the NSCDC leadership and other relevant stakeholders as it investigates the alleged ₦2 billion transaction, the reported ₦100 million extortion demand, the death of Agada and questions surrounding the unit’s funding and procurement activities.

The NSCDC leadership’s response to the allegations was not contained in the submissions presented at the hearing.

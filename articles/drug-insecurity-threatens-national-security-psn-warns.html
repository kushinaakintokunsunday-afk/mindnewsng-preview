Drug insecurity threatens national security, PSN warns

By Doris Obinna

The Pharmaceutical Society of Nigeria (PSN) has warned that pharmaceutical insecurity poses a grave threat to national security, urging the Federal Government to retain control of critical aspects of the country’s drug distribution system and strengthen the fight against illicit drug activities.

The society proposed a special fund to tackle illicit financial flows in healthcare, recommending that one per cent of assets seized from drug trafficking be dedicated to strengthening the enforcement activities of the Pharmacy Council of Nigeria (PCN) and the National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC).

The resolutions were contained in a communiqué signed by the PSN President, Tanko Ayuba, and National Secretary, Gafar Madeh, issued at the end of the Society’s 99th Annual National Conference, tagged IJELE 2026, held from November 2 to 7 at the International Convention Centre, Awka, Anambra State.

The conference, themed, “One Health, One Pharmacy: Bridging Knowledge, Practice and Policy for National Impact,” also called for the integration of over 10,000 community pharmacies into Nigeria’s national security architecture and the proposed NHIA Pharmacy First initiative to strengthen early detection and reporting of suspicious drug activities.

The pharmacists warned that transferring control of public sector drug distribution to private interests could weaken regulatory oversight and undermine collaboration among the PCN, NAFDAC and the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA).

While supporting private sector involvement in financing and technology, the conference insisted that government must retain control of medicine procurement, quality assurance, storage, dispensing and the handling of controlled drugs.

It also urged the Federal Government to support the proposed Drug Revolving Fund and Federal Drug Management Agency Bill, which has passed first reading at the House of Representatives, to establish a central platform for medicine procurement and supply.

The society further called for a review of the Federal Ministry of Health’s Memorandum of Understanding with Medipool, recommending that private operators serve only as competitively selected, non-exclusive price negotiators or framework suppliers in line with the Public Procurement Act.

To drive reforms, the conference proposed an independent presidential committee on the pharmaceutical sector, led by a registered pharmacist recommended by the PSN, with representatives of relevant regulatory and research institutions.

On workers’ welfare, the conference urged organised labour to conclude negotiations on the adjustment of the Consolidated Health Salary Structure (CONHESS), which it said had remained unresolved for about 13 years. It also called for fairness to health workers in ongoing collective bargaining negotiations.

The pharmacists further advocated a specialised humanitarian pharmacy workforce to ensure access to essential medicines and continuity of treatment for displaced populations during emergencies.

The conference also endorsed stronger collaboration between the PSN and the West African Society for Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition (WASPEN), including a standardised framework for integrating advanced pharmacists into multidisciplinary clinical teams.

It approved the speedy construction of the PSN Pharmacy House at Victoria Island and directed its leadership to regularise the society’s property documents and address outstanding debenture subscribers’ entitlements.

The conference also endorsed a one-off support contribution towards the PSN’s centenary celebration in March 2027.

Anambra State Governor, Prof. Chukwuma Charles Soludo, declared the conference open, while the Minister of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Reduction, Bernard Doro, delivered the keynote address.

At the conference, Tanko Ayuba emerged as PSN president for the 2026/2027 administrative year.

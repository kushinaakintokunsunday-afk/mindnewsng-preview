INEC tightens safeguards against fake results, electoral manipulation

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 4d ago 5 min read

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has tightened safeguards against electoral manipulation ahead of the 2027 general elections, introducing automated checks to detect over-voting and discrepancies in polling unit results before their transmission to its Result Viewing Portal (IReV).

The commission has also strengthened its result-uploading and validation procedures, removed more than 1.6 million invalid entries from the voter register and procured 57,000 additional Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) devices as part of measures to address irregularities that could undermine the credibility of the polls.

INEC Chairman, Joash Amupitan, who disclosed these measures at the 2026 Annual Lecture and Awards of the Association of Corporate Online Editors (ACOE) in Abuja yesterday, said polling unit figures entered on Form EC8A would undergo automated arithmetic consistency and over-voting checks before transmission to IReV.

Amupitan, represented by his Chief Press Secretary, Adebayo Oketola, said the commission was strengthening its electoral systems against manipulation, misinformation and operational lapses, while reaffirming its neutrality in the contest between the ruling party and opposition parties.

He warned that artificial intelligence-generated misinformation, synthetic voice cloning, manipulated videos and fabricated election results posed serious threats to public confidence and electoral security, urging the media and political parties to collaborate with INEC in protecting the integrity of the electoral process.

Speaking on the theme, “Roadmap to 2027: Strengthening Electoral Security Through Collaboration between INEC, the Media, and Political Parties for Credible and Hitch-Free Elections,” the chairman said the commission would not serve as an appendage of any political party, whether in government or opposition.

He maintained that institutional neutrality, compliance with electoral laws and transparent administration would guide INEC’s preparations and conduct of the elections.

According to him, the commission has reduced the number of voters assigned to each BVAS device from 1,250 to 850 to minimise accreditation delays, while making provisions for 232,658 registered voters with disabilities.

“INEC has reduced the number of voters assigned to each BVAS device from 1,250 to 850 to minimise accreditation delays.

“The commission has also made provisions for 232,658 persons with disabilities registered to vote.”

Amupitan explained that INEC had conducted four major off-cycle elections and 16 legislative by-elections since the current leadership assumed office in October 2025, noting that the exercises provided opportunities to identify operational weaknesses, assess logistics and improve electoral procedures ahead of the general elections.

He reiterated that the presidential and National Assembly elections would hold on January 16, 2027, while the governorship and State Houses of Assembly elections were scheduled for February 6.

As part of its earlier announced preparations, INEC is scheduled to conduct a nationwide mock accreditation and electronic result transmission exercise on November 28 across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory to test its systems ahead of the polls.

On electoral security, Amupitan said the commission was collaborating with the Office of the National Security Adviser and the Nigeria Police Force through the Inter-Agency Consultative Committee on Election Security, with arrangements being extended to state, local government and registration area levels to protect electoral personnel, materials and voters.

He cautioned political parties against campaign violence, intimidation and attacks on electoral officials and facilities, reminding them that the Electoral Act 2026 prescribed penalties for abusive campaign speeches, incitement and the deployment of private armed guards and masquerades at political rallies.

The INEC chairman urged political party leaders to restrain their supporters and pursue electoral grievances through lawful channels rather than resorting to violence.

Meanwhile, the commission has endorsed a voluntary Code of Conduct for independent tabulation and publication of election results, warning that unofficial tallies, projections and independently aggregated figures must not be presented as officially declared results.

Amupitan gave the endorsement at a stakeholders’ roundtable convened by Yiaga Africa in Abuja, where he said independent verification of election figures must complement electoral accountability without creating rival centres of statutory authority.

Represented by INEC’s Director of Information and Communication Technology, Lawrence Bayode, the chairman said the proposed code would strengthen transparency, promote responsible publication of electoral figures and protect public confidence during result collation.

“Independent verification should complement electoral accountability, not create competing centres of statutory authority.”

He outlined six guiding principles for the framework, including methodological integrity, accuracy, information integrity, data security, error accountability and voluntary compliance.

Yiaga Africa’s Executive Director, Samson Itodo, who disclosed the development in a statement on Thursday, said independent tabulation remained an important mechanism for citizens to scrutinise election outcomes and assess the credibility of the electoral process.

He, however, warned that differences in methodologies, data interpretation and publication standards could generate confusion during sensitive collation periods if organisations involved failed to adopt common safeguards.

According to him, the proposed code would establish minimum standards for independent tabulation, discourage premature declarations and ensure that published findings were supported by reliable verification.

Itodo said Yiaga Africa would lead further consultations to translate the agreed principles into a practical framework before the general elections.

Participants at the roundtable, including civil society organisations, political parties, election observers and media regulatory bodies, examined different independent tabulation models, including Yiaga Africa’s Processing Results Voter Tabulation, the Election Results Analysis Dashboard and aggregations from IReV.

They agreed that organisations publishing independently collated results must clearly disclose their methodologies, data sources and margins of error to prevent public misinterpretation.

The stakeholders also emphasised human verification, audit trails, data security and transparent error-correction procedures, while media representatives stressed the responsibility of journalists to verify electoral figures and distinguish independent projections from results officially declared by INEC.

Representatives of the National Broadcasting Commission, Broadcasting Organisations of Nigeria, Nigerian Press Council and Nigeria Union of Journalists attended the meeting.

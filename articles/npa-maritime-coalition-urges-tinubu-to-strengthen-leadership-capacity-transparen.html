NPA: Maritime Coalition urges Tinubu to strengthen leadership capacity, transparency, ongoing port reforms

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 3h ago 4 min read

A Coalition of Maritime and Energy Consultants in Nigeria has called on President Bola Ahmed Tinubu to consider strengthening the leadership and administrative capacity of the Nigerian Ports Authority (NPA) to accelerate reforms and improve efficiency in the nation’s maritime sector.

The Coalition, through its spokesperson, Engr. Danjuma Yakubu, said effective leadership, transparency and accountability were essential to achieving the President’s vision of transforming Nigeria into a leading maritime and logistics hub in Africa.

In a statement issued on behalf of the coalition and other maritime groups, Yakubu noted that the NPA, under the leadership of its Managing Director, Dr. Abubakar Dantsoho, had faced several governance, procurement and operational concerns since his appointment in July 2024, which, according to the group, required attention to strengthen institutional performance.

The coalition specifically highlighted the controversy surrounding the reported $25 million emergency dredging contract for the Warri-Escravos Channel in 2025, which generated public discussions about the procurement process, the justification for the emergency procedure and compliance with established procurement regulations.

Although the NPA had defended the contract, maintaining that due process was followed in line with the Public Procurement Act, the coalition said the issues surrounding the project underscored the importance of greater transparency and public disclosure in the management of public resources.

They also called for closer scrutiny of the Authority’s multi-billion-naira budgetary allocations, particularly expenditures relating to dredging, consultancy services, environmental sanitation, conferences and administrative activities.

According to the group, independent reviews and comprehensive disclosures would help establish value for money, strengthen public confidence and promote accountability in the management of the Authority’s finances.

Another issue raised by the coalition was the status of expired port concession agreements and terminal leases, with some reportedly dating back to 2016 and 2021.

The group said the absence of publicly available terminal-by-terminal performance audits and clear timelines for concluding concession reviews had created uncertainty within the maritime industry.

The coalition observed that addressing the outstanding concession issues would provide greater regulatory certainty, encourage fresh investments, promote competition, facilitate terminal modernisation and improve government revenue.

They also called for an independent assessment of reported procurement disputes and administrative concerns involving the Authority, noting that regular institutional reviews would help identify areas requiring improvement and strengthen confidence among contractors and other industry stakeholders.

On operational efficiency, the group expressed concern over persistent challenges at Nigerian ports, including congestion, high charges, prolonged cargo clearance and infrastructure limitations.
It cited industry reports which reportedly put cargo dwell time at Apapa and Tin Can Island ports at between 18 and 21 days, compared with a global benchmark of about four days, while port charges were said to be 30 to 40 per cent higher than those of competing regional ports.

The coalition said addressing these challenges would be important to reducing the cost of doing business, improving Nigeria’s competitiveness and attracting more foreign investment.

They also referenced concerns raised by the Presidential Enabling Business Environment Council regarding the economic implications of port inefficiencies, stressing the need for sustained collaboration among relevant government agencies and maritime stakeholders to improve service delivery.

The group maintained that resolving governance, concession and operational challenges at the NPA would be critical to ensuring that ongoing investments in the maritime sector translate into tangible economic benefits.

They acknowledged the Federal Government’s commitment to expanding Nigeria’s maritime infrastructure, citing initiatives such as the approval of the Ibom Deep Seaport in Akwa Ibom State, the proposed development of the Bakassi Deep Seaport in Cross River State and the full operationalisation of the Tsamiya Inland Dry Port in Kebbi State.

They also referenced plans to modernise the Onne, Rivers, Delta and Calabar ports, alongside the modernisation programmes for Apapa and Tin Can Island ports.

According to the coalition, these projects offer significant opportunities for economic diversification, employment generation, regional trade expansion and increased government revenue.

They, however, stressed that infrastructure development must be supported by effective institutional management, investor-friendly policies, efficient concession administration and improved port operations to achieve the desired outcomes.

The group therefore urged President Tinubu to assess the current leadership structure at the NPA and consider the appointment of a maritime professional with proven administrative experience, integrity and a strong understanding of contemporary port management.

They said such leadership would be expected to prioritise accountability, tackle operational bottlenecks, accelerate digital transformation and create an enabling environment for investors and other stakeholders.

The coalition further called for stronger institutional coordination to ensure that the NPA effectively supports the Federal Government’s Renewed Hope Agenda and broader efforts to reposition Nigeria’s maritime economy.

The group said sustained attention to leadership effectiveness, transparency and operational reforms would help translate the President’s maritime development plans into measurable results for the country.

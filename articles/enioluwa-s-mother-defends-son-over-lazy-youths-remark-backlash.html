Enioluwa’s mother defends son over ‘lazy youths’ remark backlash

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The mother of social media influencer Enioluwa Adeoluwa, Dr Bolanle Adeoluwa, has defended her son following days of criticism over his remarks about Nigerian y

Bola Adeoluwa made the clarification in an Instagram post on Saturday, saying an old video of her son speaking at a youth programme in Port Harcourt had been circulated with different interpretations and edited excerpts.

The controversy followed remarks Enioluwa made at the Emergence of Stars Youth Conference in Port Harcourt, Rivers State, where he urged young people to work harder and seek mentors, but drew criticism for suggesting that they no longer liked working and expected success to come easily.

The event was held on April 1, 2025, in Port Harcourt, Rivers State.

“Young people, we don’t like to work anymore because we think that things should come easily for us.

“We need to understand that you must work hard. You can’t give up easily. You are 20, and you say, ‘I’m tired of life’. Have you even seen life? What has life shown you, really? You’ve not seen anything,” he said

The influencer also encouraged young people to identify role models, even if they had never met them physically, citing American filmmaker Tyler Perry as his own mentor.

He said he studied Perry’s career trajectory to understand the steps that had contributed to his success.

Enioluwa also argued that academic success required consistent effort, saying, “If you read every night, you are going to finish with a first class. Simple and short.”

He added, “Life is not difficult. We’re overcomplicating it,” and said people needed to put in the work that would produce their desired results.

His comments attracted criticism from Nigerians who accused him of overlooking the economic realities confronting young people, including unemployment, low wages and the difficulty of making ends meet despite working hard.

Some critics argued that his remarks presented success as a straightforward reward for effort without acknowledging the barriers many young Nigerians face.

Enioluwa subsequently apologised in a post on X on November 17, saying he did not believe Nigerian youths were lazy.

“I do not believe that Nigerian youths are lazy. That has never been my belief, nor the message I intended to communicate. We are some of the most hardworking people I know,” he wrote.

He added that his words might have appeared to overlook his privileges and the challenges faced by young Nigerians.

“I sincerely apologise for any hurt my remarks may have caused,” he said, adding that young Nigerians worked hard despite economic difficulties and deserved opportunities to thrive.

Mother speaks out

Reacting to the controversy on Saturday, his mother said she had read different opinions, interpretations and edited videos relating to the speech, which she said was delivered over a year ago.

She also referred to screenshots circulating online concerning the allegation that her son had called Nigerian youths lazy.

“I am 100 per cent sure he would never do that considering the kind of nature he has and his contributions to the development of youths in Nigeria,” she wrote.

Describing herself as an academic, she urged people to verify information and preserve context before making judgments.

“As an academic, I believe strongly in the responsibility and importance to verify information, preserve context, and uphold integrity in public discourse. Even in emotionally charged situations,” she said.

She, however, acknowledged that her reaction was also shaped by her position as a mother.

“But beyond being an academic, I am a mother. I love my son dearly, and as a family we are incredibly proud of him. Our Eni. Kind, generous, simple and loving. His siblings love him dearly too,” she wrote.

Adeoluwa said the criticism had hurt her, although she recognised that public figures were subject to scrutiny.

“Does it hurt me as a mother? Certainly. But I understand that public life comes with its own responsibilities,” she added.

She also said the family was grounded in Christian values and had supported students with school fees.

“By the grace of God, we have collectively and individually paid school fees for students, and we still do,” she wrote.

She urged Nigerians to disagree without resorting to personal attacks, adding, “We can hold people accountable without being unkind, and we can disagree without losing our humanity.”

Adeoluwa is listed on the Federal University Oye-Ekiti’s academic staff portal as a Lecturer I in its Department of Mass Communication, under the Faculty of Communication and Media Studies.

Her official profile identifies her area of specialisation as communication and public relations. It also lists a PhD in Mass Communication from Afe Babalola University, obtained in 2025.

She has qualifications in education, mass communication, public relations, and industrial and labour relations, according to the university’s profile.

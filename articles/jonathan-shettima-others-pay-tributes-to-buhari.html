Jonathan, Shettima, others pay tributes to Buhari

By Punch Punch 4d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Vice-President Kashim Shettima, former President Goodluck Jonathan and ex-Nigerien President Mahamadou Issoufou paid glowing tributes to the late President Muha

Represented by his Special Adviser on General Duties, Dr Aliyu Modibbo Umar, Shettima spoke on Thursday at the public presentation of Muhammadu Buhari: The Authorised Biography, a three-volume work authored by Dr Udu Yakubu, in Abuja.

He said Buhari’s record of public service would continue to inspire future generations, stressing the importance of leaders leaving lasting legacies.

The Vice-President stated, “His Spartan lifestyle distinguished him in a world where too many seek public office to satisfy private appetites. There was something arresting about a man who could command so much and desire so little.”

Shettima recalled that Buhari taught him that a leader’s character determines the dignity and integrity of the office he occupies.

He continued, “I learnt so much from President Buhari as a leader, as a human being who was not drawn to worldly possessions, and as a patriot whose devotion to Nigeria endured beyond the offices he held.

“He taught me that the dignity of office depends upon the character of its occupant. He also taught me that a leader’s example continues to speak long after he is out of service.”

Shettima noted that Buhari’s political journey, from military leadership to democratic rule, attracted both significant public admiration and criticism of his policies and decisions.

Speaking while unveiling the book, former President Goodluck Jonathan explained that he conceded defeat to the late President Muhammadu Buhari in the 2015 presidential election without resistance because he regarded him as a statesman who loved God and Nigeria.

Jonathan, who was represented by former Senate President Anyim Pius Anyim, had contacted him about two hours before the event to inform him of his inability to attend.

He said the former president cancelled other engagements to ensure that he was properly represented at the ceremony.

Anyim noted that many Nigerians continued to question why Jonathan conceded defeat in the 2015 presidential election, recalling that Buhari was widely recognised as the politician who defeated an incumbent president, while Jonathan earned a reputation as the African leader who peacefully relinquished power without controversy.

“President Buhari is renowned for being that politician who was able to unseat a sitting incumbent. President Goodluck is renowned to be that sitting African president who handed over power without any form of contention.

“It was easy for President Goodluck to say, ‘Look, this is a statesman that I know that will love Nigeria.’ So there was no need for any form of contention. That is the story,” he said.

He said Jonathan’s acceptance of the 2015 election results underscored the need to place national interest above party politics.

“When eventually Goodluck lost the election, there was no need for any form of opposition,” he added.

Anyim described his personal relationship with Buhari as a father-and-son bond, saying their friendship endured despite belonging to different political parties.

He recalled that their closeness remained intact even after he joined the government while Buhari was in the opposition.

Jonathan, who was then the incumbent President and PDP presidential candidate, conceded defeat to Buhari of the opposition APC before the Independent National Electoral Commission announced the final results of the 2015 election.

Similarly, Issoufou, who chaired the occasion, described Buhari as a visionary leader and a close friend of Niger, recalling their collaboration on regional security, economic integration and efforts to strengthen relations between the two countries.

He said the three-volume biography would provide future generations with documented accounts of Buhari’s life, decisions and contributions to national and continental development.

Issoufou said Buhari’s commitment to constitutional order was evident. “He respected the constitutional principle of term limits and facilitated the alternation of power,” he said.

He recalled that Buhari made Niger his first foreign destination after assuming office on June 3, 2015, adding that he honoured the late Nigerian leader with the Grand Cross of the National Order of Niger in March 2021.

Issoufou, however, stressed that the biography should not be considered the definitive account of Buhari’s life and legacy.

He added, “None of us can claim to have the final word on Muhammadu Buhari. Nor should any biography pretend to do so.”

Former military Head of State, General Ibrahim Babangida (retd.), represented by former Inspector-General of Police Mohammed D. Abubakar, paid tribute to Buhari, describing him as a “comrade-in-arms”.

He stated, “Together, we travelled the path of training in the armed forces of our country. Together, we walked the trenches of danger for the common good. We forged our nation in peace and unity, and today, Nigeria is much better for it.”

Nasarawa State Governor, Abdullahi Sule, said Buhari’s honesty, patriotism and firm stance against corruption were among the qualities that distinguished him.

He said, “One thing that has been repeated over and over is his honesty, his love for Nigeria, and his uncompromising attitude when it comes to corruption.”

Also speaking, the Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of BUA Group, Abdulsamad Isyaka Rabi’u, represented by Senator Lawal Shuaibu, described Buhari as a deeply important figure in his life.

Earlier, Chairman of the Biography Planning Committee, Engr. Suleiman Adamu, said the book chronicles the life of a man whose public career spanned over six decades of Nigeria’s history, from military service and the civil war to politics and eight years as an elected president.

Adamu stated, “There is, however, one fact about these volumes that deserves to be stated with particular emphasis. This biography was personally authorised by President Muhammadu Buhari.

“It was not a project conceived after his death. It was not an attempt to reconstruct his life when he was no longer available to speak for himself. President Buhari knew that the biography was being written. He gave his consent to it.

“He submitted himself to the process. He was interviewed repeatedly. He answered questions about the different stages of his life, including difficult and contested episodes. He assisted the author in reaching people, events and memories necessary for reconstructing the record. And he remained a participant in that process until his death.”

Author of the biography, Dr Udu Yakubu, said the three-volume work took more than two and a half years to complete, crediting former President Muhammadu Buhari for granting him access while he was alive.

Yakubu said Buhari personally authorised the project and participated in several interviews, answering questions about different stages of his life, although he did not always agree with the questions or provide the expected responses.

He also acknowledged Buhari’s nephew, Malam Mamman Daura, for helping him access relevant individuals and clarify events and timelines during the research.

The author said the biography should be open to public scrutiny and criticism, stressing that a work of such magnitude should not be shielded from critical examination.

The event was attended by former ministers who served under Buhari, alongside his friends and family members.

Buhari died on July 13, 2025, aged 82, after serving as Nigeria’s democratically elected President from 2015 to 2023.

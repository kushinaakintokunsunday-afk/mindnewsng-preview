Oil, gas operators seek credit reforms to unlock investment

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Oil and gas operators have called for a stronger and more credible credit management system to improve access to funding, unlock investment and boost energy sup

 They made the call at the National Institute of Credit Administrators’ conference in Lagos, where industry stakeholders identified inadequate access to credit, trapped receivables, weak payment structures and poor creditworthiness as major obstacles to financing projects and expanding operations across the energy value chain.

 The discussions took place during a panel session themed ‘The Energy, Oil and Gas Credit Revolution: Unlocking the Cash, Credit and Confidence to Power Nigeria’s Energy Future’, moderated by the Chief Executive Officer of BlackGold Energy Limited, Dr Oladunni Owo.

 Speaking at the session, Owo described credit as the missing link between Nigeria’s enormous energy resources and the economic benefits they should generate.

 She said that although oil and gas remain critical to the country’s foreign exchange earnings, government revenue and industrial development, operators across the sector continued to struggle with access to working capital, while distributors battled trapped receivables and consumers faced rising energy costs.

 According to her, Nigeria did not lack energy resources but lacked an effective financial bridge to convert those resources into productive economic outcomes.

 She warned that without a reliable credit management system, lenders would remain reluctant to finance projects, investors would hesitate to commit funds, and operators would find it difficult to expand their businesses.

 Owo noted that the financing challenges facing the industry have implications beyond oil and gas companies, stressing that the availability of credit ultimately affected energy affordability, industrial productivity and the welfare of ordinary Nigerians.

 She recalled spending about 15 years seeking funding for a refinery project before the emergence of the Dangote Refinery, observing that the country’s fuel supply situation could have been considerably worse without the huge investment secured for the facility.

 Owo noted that the conference was a pilot initiative ahead of a much bigger national and international summit on energy, oil and gas credit management planned for 2027.

 Speaking on how operators could improve payment cycles and restore liquidity, the Chief Operating Officer of Emadeb Energy Group, Mr Sheriff Adeeyo, identified supply chain financing as one of the most effective mechanisms for unlocking capital within the industry.

 Adeeyo explained that conventional funding arrangements had become increasingly difficult for some operators, forcing companies to explore alternative financing structures and depend more on internally generated resources to sustain and expand their businesses.

 He cited supply chain financing initiatives involving major industry players, including Renaissance Africa Energy, as examples of how contractors and vendors could access financing through their contractual relationships with oil and gas companies.

 According to him, such arrangements enabled vendors and contractors to obtain funding from commercial banks and other financial institutions using verified contracts and business transactions as the basis for financing.

 Also speaking, the Group Executive Director, Operations, Masters Energy Group, Mr Felix Eribo, said the difficulty of accessing credit was not limited to the oil and gas industry but affected businesses across different sectors of the Nigerian economy.

 Drawing from research he conducted involving 41 microfinance banks in Nigeria as part of his Doctor of Business Administration thesis, Eribo said he encountered several promising business proposals that could not secure financing because the applicants were considered insufficiently creditworthy.

 He noted that even established oil and gas companies could be denied facilities when their credit ratings failed to meet the requirements of financial institutions.

CAS To Rule On Senegal-Morocco AFCON Final Dispute Amid Fresh Allegations

Gambiaj.com – (LAUSANNE, SWITZERLAND) – The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) is expected to deliver its ruling this afternoon in the dispute over the winner of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) final between Senegal and Morocco, as fresh allegations of an alleged contact involving a member of the arbitration panel and Senegalese football officials emerge ahead of the decision.

The Moroccan Royal Football Federation (FRMF) has formally reported an alleged incident to CAS, claiming that Senegal Football Federation (FSF) president Abdoulaye Fall had a telephone conversation with one of the arbitrators handling the case shortly before the verdict was due.

The allegation, reportedly raised in a letter sent to CAS’s legal representatives on Tuesday, 24 November, has been strongly rejected by the Senegalese side.

FSF Secretary General Abdoulaye Seydou Sow dismissed the claim, describing it as an “imaginary call” and insisting that Fall does not even know the judges assigned to the case, according to statements reported by Senegalese media outlet SeneNews.

The latest development adds another layer of tension to an already contentious legal battle, with both countries awaiting a decision that will determine the official winner of the January final.

In its announcement, CAS said the parties had been informed that the ruling would be delivered during the afternoon on Tuesday.

“The parties have been notified that the CAS decision will be rendered in the course of the afternoon,” the court said, adding that only information communicated through its official channels should be considered authoritative.

Dispute Over the January Final

The legal battle stems from the final played on 18 January at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat, where Senegal defeated hosts Morocco 1-0 after extra time to claim their second Africa Cup of Nations title.

Senegal’s Pape Gueye scored the decisive goal in extra time after a controversial incident involving the referee’s decision to award Morocco a penalty.

Senegal’s players walked off the pitch in protest without the referee’s permission, forcing a suspension of nearly 15 minutes before they returned to continue the match.

Morocco had an opportunity to secure victory in regulation time when striker Brahim Diaz was awarded a penalty with virtually the final kick of the 90 minutes. However, he failed to convert, sending the match into extra time, where Senegal eventually scored the winner.

Following the final, the FRMF challenged the result, arguing that Senegal had effectively abandoned the match by leaving the pitch.

After an initial appeal was rejected by CAF’s disciplinary committee, Moroccan football authorities pursued the matter further within the continental governing body.

On 17 March 2026, CAF overturned the result, ruling that Senegal had forfeited the match and awarded Morocco a 3-0 victory, effectively transferring the continental title to the North African country.

Senegal’s football authorities subsequently appealed to CAS, challenging CAF’s decision.

Five-Hour Hearing in Lausanne

A three-member CAS panel heard arguments from lawyers representing the Senegalese and Moroccan football federations, as well as CAF, during a five-hour hearing in Lausanne, Switzerland, on 8 October.

The hearing was held behind closed doors as the independent sports tribunal considered the competing arguments over CAF’s decision and the circumstances surrounding the final.

Tuesday’s ruling will determine whether CAF’s decision stands or Senegal retains the title it won on the pitch in January.

The fresh allegations reported by Morocco have also raised questions about the circumstances surrounding the proceedings, although the Senegalese federation has categorically denied any inappropriate contact involving its president.

Both Teams Qualified for 2027 AFCON

The legal dispute has continued even as both countries have moved on to other international competitions.

At the 2026 FIFA World Cup in the United States, Mexico, Canada, and Senegal were eliminated in the round of 32, while Morocco reached the quarter-finals before losing to France.

Both nations have also secured qualification for the next Africa Cup of Nations, which will be jointly hosted by Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda in 2027.

Morocco qualified from Group A with four wins from four matches, while Senegal also secured their place from Group J under new head coach Patrick Vieira.

The 2027 AFCON finals will feature 24 teams and are scheduled to take place from 19 June to 17 July.

Tuesday’s CAS ruling is expected to settle one of the most contentious disputes in African football, determining which country will officially be recognized as the 2025 continental champion.

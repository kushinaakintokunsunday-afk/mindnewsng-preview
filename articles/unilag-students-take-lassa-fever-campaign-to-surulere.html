UNILAG students take Lassa fever campaign to Surulere

By Punch Punch 17h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Association of Medical Students, University of Lagos, has taken its Lassa fever prevention campaign to Surulere, Lagos, warning residents about the risks of

The awareness walk, organised in partnership with the Lukmon Oyewole-Lawal Foundation as part of activities marking the association’s 60th anniversary, started at the entrance of the Lagos University Teaching Hospital, Idi-Araba, and ended at Sam Shonibare Mini-Stadium, Surulere.

The students urged residents to maintain clean surroundings, observe personal hygiene and store food properly to prevent rodents from contaminating food items and spreading Lassa fever.

The AMSUL President, Adedeji Adeleke, said the walk, themed “Stop the Spread: Stride Against Lassa Fever”, was designed to educate the public on the disease and encourage practical steps to prevent infection.

He urged residents to keep food away from rodents, wash their hands regularly and maintain a clean environment, stressing that prevention remained the most effective way to reduce the spread of communicable diseases.

At the end of the walk, the students and their partners held a free medical outreach at the mini-stadium, where residents from Surulere and neighbouring communities received medical examinations, basic medications and eyeglasses.

The outreach, themed “Healthy Mothers, Healthy Future”, included screening for hypertension, diabetes and obesity, as well as awareness programmes on breast and cervical cancer.

The founder of the Lukmon Oyewole-Lawal Foundation and Special Adviser to the Speaker of the House of Representatives on Budget and Finance, Lukmon Oyewole-Lawal, said the intervention was intended to make basic healthcare more accessible to residents.

He urged beneficiaries to take their health seriously, saying community medical outreaches provided an opportunity for people to know their health status and seek medical attention before illnesses became severe.

Oyewole-Lawal also assured residents that the medical team would follow up on beneficiaries suspected of having health conditions to ensure they received further diagnosis and appropriate treatment.

Describing the Lassa fever campaign as timely, he said cases of the disease were more prevalent between December and March because of dry environmental conditions.

The Chairman of Surulere Local Government Area, Bamidele Yusuf, commended the partnership, saying it had brought medical services closer to residents.

He said his administration was upgrading and equipping primary healthcare centres across the council area to improve service delivery, while urging residents to undergo regular medical examinations to detect health conditions early.

Yusuf also acknowledged the contributions of the Chief of Staff to the President, Femi Gbajabiamila, to the development of Surulere, saying those who had worked with him had continued to bring the dividends of democracy to the people.

The outreach formed part of AMSUL’s 60th anniversary activities, through which the association said it was reaffirming its commitment to public health education, community service and improved access to healthcare.

Hundreds of Nigerian travellers stranded as US flight cancellation persists

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: VeryNigerian

Hundreds of Nigerian travellers have been affected by widespread flight cancellations in the United States following a nationwide air traffic control ground stop that began on Friday.

The disruption, which affected thousands of flights across the US, has also disrupted international aviation operations, leaving passengers travelling from Nigeria to the United States and those scheduled to return home facing delays, cancellations and uncertainty.

Findings by Very Nigerian showed that several Nigerian passengers scheduled to travel to the US over the weekend could not embark on their journeys as planned.

Some of the affected travellers were forced to temporarily abandon their trips, while others postponed their flights pending the restoration of normal airline operations.

Some perssengers temporarily spent hours within the airport terminal while some others checked into a hotel around the airport with the thought that the disruptions would only last for a day.

A passenger, Sola Aina, who spoke with our correspondent on Sunday, said he had been forced to return home after spending days waiting for his flight to be rescheduled.

“I just have to return home today. I have come for a Friday flight before we heard of the troubles in the U.S, I have to sleep in a hotel since Friday believing that the issues would be sorted before Sunday night, but this is almost 7:00pm again now.

“I have ordered a cab. I have to return to Ibadan. It’s very painful but I don’t have an option,” he said.

Another passenger, Faruq Akinwale, said the disruption had affected his plans to return home urgently, as his wife and sick child were in the US.

“I have a sick child with my wife in the U.S and I need to return home urgently, but what do I do? But I understand that no one can be blamed for this, but I will only appeal that everyone concerned should please hasten up whatever they are doing, I pray God help them,” he said.

A mother of two, Mrs Aderemi, also expressed disappointment over the situation, saying she and her family had been left with little choice but to return home.

“We will just return home, the situation is just understandable even though it’s painful,” she said.

Similarly, another passenger, Charles Adekunle, said the disruption had affected his travel plans, although he did not disclose whether he intended to reschedule his trip.

The situation has also affected passengers travelling from the US to Nigeria, particularly those booked on international services connecting major American cities to Lagos and Abuja.

Airlines operating flights between Nigeria and the US, either directly or through connecting routes, include Qatar Airways, Turkish Airlines, Ethiopian Airlines, Emirates, Virgin Atlantic and British Airways, among others.

Although the Federal Aviation Administration lifted the nationwide ground stop on Saturday evening, airlines have continued to battle operational challenges, including the repositioning of aircraft and flight crews displaced by the disruption.

An update published on Delta Air Lines’ website on Saturday indicated that the carrier was still working to restore normal operations following the FAA-mandated ground stop, which lasted more than four hours.

The airline said passengers scheduled to travel between 13 and 15 November, 2026, could change their bookings without paying additional charges.

It also encouraged customers with flexible travel arrangements to move their flights to Monday, 16 November, or later, warning that passengers travelling over the weekend should expect delays and further schedule adjustments.

Delta said, “Delta continues to restore operations following an FAA-mandated nationwide ground stop that lasted more than four hours on Friday, November 13. Customers traveling today should expect delays and additional schedule adjustments as we work to reposition flight crews and aircraft.

“Taking care of customers remains our focus, and we continue to offer flexible rebooking options to change flights at no extra cost. Customers who have flexible travel plans are encouraged to move their flights to Monday, Nov. 16 or later.

“If you are traveling on Saturday, please allow extra time and check the Delta app or delta.com for the latest flight status and travel information. Delta will provide customers with as much notice as possible about changes to flight schedules. When a cancellation does occur, Delta will automatically rebook customers to the next best itinerary.”

Meanwhile, the Donald Trump administration has warned that disruptions to air travel across the United States could persist until Monday, extending the wait for a full return to normal operations.

Hundreds more flights were cancelled on Sunday, raising concerns about the scale of the disruption in the world’s largest economy, which has one of the busiest aviation networks globally.

US officials attributed Friday’s disruption to faulty work carried out during an upgrade of air traffic control communication cables.

President Donald Trump broke his silence on the crisis on Sunday, blaming the previous administration of Joe Biden for the state of the country’s air traffic control infrastructure.

The FAA chief, Bryan Bedford, had said on Saturday that air travel for millions of Americans affected by the disruption would return to normal by the close of business on Sunday.

However, Transportation Secretary, Sean Duffy, said on Sunday that the restoration of normal operations would take longer because aircraft and flight crews had been displaced during the disruption.

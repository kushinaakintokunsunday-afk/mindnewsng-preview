‘CBN data rule requires local cloud infrastructure’

By Punch Punch 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The warning comes as CloudVantage establishes cloud availability regions in Lagos and Abuja, providing local computing, storage and networking infrastructure for Nigerian businesses seeking to host critical workloads within the country and reduce their reliance on foreign cloud services.

Ashiru said in a news release on Monday that requiring financial institutions to keep payment transaction data within Nigeria without sufficient domestic technology infrastructure could create significant operational challenges for businesses preparing to comply with the new requirements.

His comments come ahead of the CBN’s 1 January 2027 compliance deadline, which requires financial institutions and payment service providers to ensure that payment transaction data generated within Nigeria is stored and managed locally.

The development is drawing attention to the capacity of Nigeria’s domestic digital infrastructure to support financial institutions as they review their existing hosting arrangements and consider alternatives to international cloud providers.

Speaking at the Nigeria Cloud Independence Day Summit, a virtual event organised by CloudVantage under the theme ‘Securing Nigeria’s Data, Enabling Innovation and Building a Resilient Digital Economy’, Ashiru said local infrastructure needed to deliver practical business benefits alongside regulatory compliance.

“The CBN’s new rule is a major step towards taking control of Nigeria’s digital future. But requiring businesses to keep data local without providing reliable domestic technology would create significant challenges,” he said.

“Through our partnership with Rack Centre, CloudVantage is building a local cloud platform that helps businesses strengthen data control, improve resilience and manage their infrastructure costs. We want local cloud adoption to be a smart business decision, not just a compliance exercise.”

The Lagos region was initially announced at the summit, which brought together technology leaders, risk managers and industry stakeholders to discuss data sovereignty, infrastructure readiness and the future of Nigeria’s digital economy. The company has now established availability regions in both Lagos and Abuja.

However, moving workloads from international cloud providers to domestic infrastructure could present technical challenges for businesses, particularly those operating complex databases, legacy applications, interconnected systems and sensitive transaction records.

Such migrations require organisations to protect data integrity and maintain service availability while transferring critical workloads. Ashiru said regulatory compliance involved more than securing physical space in a domestic data centre, noting that businesses also needed to address data protection during migration, system configuration, backup and recovery.

“True regulatory compliance goes beyond securing physical rack space in a data centre. Businesses must also consider data protection during migration, system configuration, backup and recovery. Our automation tools help organisations manage these operational requirements while their teams focus on their core business,” he said.

CloudVantage said its platform combines cloud automation, workload management, data replication and backup capabilities to simplify migration and ongoing infrastructure management. The services are designed to reduce manual intervention, support recovery planning and help organisations maintain business continuity.

Both availability regions are backed by a 99.99 per cent availability service-level agreement, according to the company.

Beyond regulatory compliance, the shift towards local cloud hosting could give businesses an opportunity to review their technology expenditure, particularly where international cloud contracts expose them to foreign exchange fluctuations.

CloudVantage said its pricing was tailored to the markets in which its customers operate, with naira payment options intended to reduce exposure to foreign currency-denominated cloud charges.

Depending on their workloads and existing contracts, businesses could also reduce cross-border data transfers and improve response times for local users by hosting infrastructure closer to their customers.

The company said its infrastructure was supported by security and business continuity standards, including ISO 27001, ISO 22301 and PCI DSS compliance credentials.

Speaking at the summit, Kelechukwu Nsofor, Head of Business Development at Digital Realty, encouraged Nigerian organisations using international hyperscale cloud providers to explore domestic alternatives and communicate their infrastructure challenges to local providers.

“This is the perfect opportunity for organisations using foreign hyperscalers to look inward and share their pain points with Nigerian providers like CloudVantage,” Nsofor said.

He also highlighted the importance of developing clear migration plans as organisations prepare for the CBN’s data localisation requirements.

CloudVantage said its Lagos and Abuja regions would give banks, fintech companies, payment service providers and other businesses additional options for hosting and managing critical workloads locally while maintaining control over where their data is stored.

The company added that its services were designed to support secure operations, simplify migration and strengthen infrastructure resilience through automation-backed management and technical support.

Lumumba faults African leaders seeking medical treatment abroad

By Punch Punch 5d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Renowned Kenyan scholar and Pan-Africanist, Prof. Patrick Lumumba, has urged African leaders to reduce dependence on foreign expertise and develop indigenous ca

Lumumba said the continued reliance on foreign countries, despite the availability of trained African professionals and local resources, was reinforcing neo-colonialism and undermining the continent’s development.

He spoke on Thursday in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State, while delivering the keynote address at the maiden Pan-African Awakening Conference organised by the Social Engagement and Reorientation Initiative.

The scholar called for a change in mindset among African leaders, saying the continent must develop home-grown solutions to bridge the technological and economic gap between it and developed countries.

“They teach us engineering, but largely, when we want to do our roads, we still call the Chinese to do them for us,” he said.

Lumumba also criticised the practice of African leaders seeking medical treatment abroad despite the training and qualifications of healthcare professionals on the continent.

“Why is it that we train our doctors in universities, complete with PhDs and other specialisations, but even our ministers for health in African countries, when they are sick, they don’t go to hospitals in Abuja, or in Nairobi, or in Senegal? They are in London, and in Paris, and in Germany.

“Even our presidents don’t trust our ministries of health, yet they have allocations for them. Why is it?” he asked.

He also faulted the continent’s dependence on imported goods despite its capacity to produce raw materials and manufacture finished products.

“We produce what we do not consume, and consume what we do not produce,” he said.

“Why is it that our cows produce milk, and when we want butter, we still go to the Netherlands? Why is it that we produce cotton, but once we have produced cotton, we are still importing bedsheets from Vietnam and China?

“Why is it that we have leather, and we have skin, and you sell it in Kano, and in Kaduna, and in other places, but when we want bags for our ladies, we still go to Italy to get Gucci and Dolce & Gabbana?” he added.

Lumumba urged African countries to learn from the development experiences of nations such as China, India and South Korea, but adapt the lessons to their local circumstances.

“We can learn from the Indians. We can learn from the Caucasians. We can learn from the Chinese. We can learn from the Koreans, but once we have done so, let us give it an African flavour. Because when we give it an African flavour, that is when it becomes African,” he said.

He disclosed that 22 African and Caribbean countries had united to establish a common front aimed at changing what he described as the negative and colonial mindset of leaders across the continent.

The scholar also urged Nigeria to take the lead in the campaign against neo-colonialism, citing its human and natural resources.

He called on Nigerian youths to rise above ethnic and religious divisions and embrace citizenship as a basis for national development.

“You know, this Nigeria, one out of every five Africans is Nigerian. If you want to know how great this Nigeria could be, travel the world. Go to Costa Rica, you’ll find Nigerian doctors, Nigerian businessmen. Go to Saint Kitts and Nevis, go to Tonga, go to Europe, go to the United States of America.

“You are Nigerians. You are the ones who are going to change Nigeria. And you can only change Nigeria if you are a Nigerian citizen, not if you are a tribalist and an idiot. So be citizens,” Lumumba said.

He commended the Akwa Ibom State Government for its efforts to develop infrastructure, particularly roads, saying the state’s progress demonstrated what could be achieved through investment in local development.

“Akwa Ibom has some of the best roads in the continent of Africa, and I have travelled Africa. At least, when you look at the roads in Akwa Ibom, it tells you what can be done.

“You know, Akwa Ibom has oil. You know, Akwa Ibom has a GDP that is larger than a number of African countries. So when Nigeria tells me, ‘Our economy is improving, we are now 500 billion,’ I don’t even clap my hands. Nigeria should be talking two trillion, then I’ll begin to clap my hands,” he said.

Earlier, the Founder and Executive Director of the Social Engagement and Reorientation Initiative, Engr. Ekemini Amos, said the organisation was established to raise a new generation of Nigerian youths from different ethnic backgrounds but united by a common purpose of contributing to national and continental development.

“This gathering is not just another conference! This is the resurrection of a cause! The cause of African liberation was buried with Nkrumah! It was buried with Sankara! It was buried with Murtala Muhammed! For many years, we buried it with our silence, with our japa syndrome, with our love for foreign things! But today, in Uyo, we are resurrecting that cause!” Amos said.

He urged stakeholders to partner with the organisation in building a better future for Africa and supporting its mentorship initiatives, including the Youth Membership Hub for people aged 18 to 35 and the Nation Builders Club for secondary school students aged 10 to 18.

The highlight of the conference was the unveiling of the SERI Mentorship Programme, the organisation’s flagship initiative. Lumumba, who serves as its Chief Adviser and Grand Patron, performed the unveiling.

Lumumba and his wife were also decorated with the titles Akwa Ujo Africa, meaning the biggest voice in Africa, and Mkpouto Africa, meaning the jewel of Africa, respectively.

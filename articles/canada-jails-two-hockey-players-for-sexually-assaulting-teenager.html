Canada jails two hockey players for sexually assaulting teenager

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Two young hockey players in Canada have each been sentenced to 40 months in prison for sexually assaulting a 17-year-old girl during an unwanted threesome at a

The two men, who were 18 at the time of the incident in 2023, were convicted of jointly sexually assaulting the teenager at a private residence.

According to a report by the National Post on Friday, the victim’s identity and those of the offenders were protected, with the court identifying the men only by their initials.

In a sentencing decision delivered on November 3, Ontario Superior Court Justice J. Scott Cowan described the case as the joint sexual violation of a young girl by two youthful offenders who otherwise had excellent backgrounds.

The court heard that the teenager initially went into a bedroom with one of the men, where they engaged in consensual kissing before the encounter escalated.

The judge said he had significant reservations about whether she genuinely consented to the sexual activity that followed but was not satisfied beyond a reasonable doubt that a criminal offence had occurred during that initial encounter.

The situation changed when the second man joined them after receiving a telephone call from his friend, who told him, “It’s happening.”

The judge said the second man asked the teenager to perform oral sex on him, but the court was not satisfied beyond a reasonable doubt that the act constituted a criminal assault.

However, the court found that the criminal conduct began when the second man instructed his friend to “hit that from behind” while the teenager was performing oral s#x on him.

Cowan said the girl had not been consulted about the proposed sexual activity and had testified that she did not want to participate in a threesome.

The judge found that, despite her lack of consent, the men proceeded to subject her to simultaneous oral and vaginal penetration.

“For several minutes, the offenders alternated positions and each penetrated her orally and vaginally without her consent,” the judge said.

He added that the sexual activity was, at times, rough and degrading, noting that the teenager gagged and regurgitated during the encounter, while one of the men digitally scratched her inside her vagina and she sustained a visible bruise to her face.

The encounter ended “when one of the men asked whether she could continue and she responded that she could not.”

The judge also heard that other partygoers entered the bedroom after the incident and directed gendered and sexualised insults at the teenager while the two men did nothing to intervene.

Although Cowan described their failure to intervene as regrettable, he said he did not consider it an aggravating factor in sentencing.

The teenager promptly reported the assault and sought medical attention. Her facial bruise was photographed, while her testimony showed the psychological and emotional impact of the incident.

She told the court that the assault had affected every aspect of her identity and life, leaving her with feelings of powerlessness, depression, difficulty showing affection and vulnerability, and fears that the hurt might never end.

Both offenders had no previous criminal records and enjoyed the support of their families, friends and potential employers.

The judge also noted that both were skilled hockey players, adding that one had hoped to earn a living as an elite athlete before the offence and subsequent court proceedings derailed those ambitions.

The Crown had sought seven-year prison terms for both men, while their defence lawyers requested sentences at the lower end of the penitentiary range, citing their young ages and otherwise good backgrounds.

However, Cowan sentenced each of them to 40 months in prison, stressing that sentences for youthful first offenders should emphasise rehabilitation and, where necessary, individual deterrence.

He told the victim that the shorter sentences than those requested by the Crown did not diminish the suffering she had experienced or the profound effects of the offence.

The judge thanked her for putting her trust in the criminal justice system.

The two men will be eligible for full parole after serving one-third of their sentences, while they can qualify for day parole six months before then.

Tinubu confers GCON on Olanipekun

By Punch Punch 4d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

President Bola Tinubu on Thursday conferred the country’s second-highest national honour, the Grand Commander of the Order of the Niger (GCON), on legal luminar

 He described him as a phenomenon who had served as lead counsel to four successive presidents of the Federal Republic.

 The conferment was made at the public presentation of a six-volume publication authored by Olanipekun in Abuja, marking his 75th birthday, 50 years at the Bar and 35 years as a Senior Advocate of Nigeria.

 Special Adviser to the President on Information and Strategy, Bayo Onanuga, revealed this in a statement he signed on Thursday titled, ‘President Tinubu Confers National Honour, GCON, on Wole Olanipekun at Public Presentation of His Books.’

 “As President of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, I proudly celebrate Chief Wole Olanipekun, Senior Advocate of Nigeria, a phenomenon in Nigeria and an outstanding advocate who has fittingly stepped into the shoes of the acclaimed legends of the legal profession.

 “He did not merely fill those shoes but is walking with them into new territory,” the President said.

 Tinubu eulogised Olanipekun for his years of commitment, hard work, resilience, mentorship of younger generations and advancement of the legal profession, as well as his community development, scholarship and social empowerment work in Ekiti State and across Nigeria.

 Tinubu acknowledged a decades-long personal and professional bond with the celebrant, disclosing that Olanipekun had led his legal team for decades and had remained a constant presence through the most consequential chapters of his political life, some of which, he noted, are documented in the volumes presented to the public on Thursday.

 “On a personal note, I celebrate decades of loyalty, friendship, commitment and reciprocity between Wole Olanipekun and me.

 “He has led my legal team for decades. In politics, friends come and go, but Wole came and stayed,” the President said.

 He noted that Olanipekun had also led the legal teams that successfully defended three of his predecessors when their elections were challenged, making him the only lawyer in the country’s history to have served as lead counsel to four sitting presidents since 2007.

 Tinubu said, “Wole’s loyalty is not luck. It is trust, and it is kept at the highest level of our nation. I commend you as a worthy Nigerian.

 “This nation is very, very proud and honoured. As one of the finest advocates beyond the courtroom, I recently had cause to acknowledge your community development initiatives and philanthropic interventions across critical sectors in Ekiti State and Nigeria at large.”

 “We thank God for you. 50 years at the bar, and you are still raising the bar.

 “You have positively impacted Nigeria and humanity. You have mentored many, including myself. May God continue to spare your life, enrich you, protect you, strengthen your mind and enrich your intellectual inquisitiveness to do more for humanity the way we have been doing. Congratulations and happy birthday,” the President said.

 Tinubu used the occasion to reaffirm his administration’s commitment to judicial independence, describing a credible judiciary as not merely a constitutional requirement but an economic imperative.

 “The Rule of Law is a priority for our administration. This is not only because I am a constitutional democrat.

 “This is because of who we are as a nation, the giant of Africa. It is also because we firmly believe that a viable, trustworthy, competent, resourceful and independent judiciary is indispensable to the economic prosperity and to the rights and liberties of our citizens. Investors not only read our balance sheet, but they also read our judgments.

 “That is why we continue to support initiatives that present Nigeria as a destination and business-friendly country, one that respects the rights of all who live in the land and the sanctity of the law of every contract and that the new Nigeria contracts must be sacred, not seasonal,” the President said.

 He cited the Judicial Officeholder Salary and Allowances Act 2024 as evidence of that commitment, saying it reversed over 15 years of stagnation by delivering a 300 per cent increase in the salaries of superior court judges, while acknowledging that more remained to be done.

 “We are not yet at our desired designation. But significant work is in progress, and the brighter future is now in closer view. We will be there, my Lordship.

 “Our administration respects court judgments, even the ones we do not enjoy reading. We do not denigrate, attack or intimidate the judiciary,” the President reassured.

 Chairman of Heirs Holding, Tony Elumelu, delivering the keynote address titled ‘The Rule of Law as a Catalyst for Private Sector Growth,’ reinforced Tinubu’s message, arguing that the quality of a country’s judicial system was the primary condition for attracting and retaining investment, ahead of power supply, tax incentives or regulatory frameworks.

 “Capital goes where it is safe and stays where it is respected. Before power, before rules, before tax incentives, the first condition is a court judgment that is fair, impartial and timely. We know that justice delayed is justice denied,” Elumelu stated.

 Senate President Godswill Akpabio lauded Tinubu for his commitment to justice, democracy and national development, and assured him that the National Assembly would continue to support all legal instruments that promote justice, democracy and the rule of law.

 He also commended Olanipekun for his contributions to the legal profession.

 Chief Justice of the Federation, Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, praised Olanipekun for his immense contributions to the legal profession, saying the six volumes being unveiled would “further contribute to the intellectual depth, advocacy and enrichment of the nation’s jurisprudence.”

 Meanwhile, Attorney-General of the Federation and Justice Minister Prince Lateef Fagbemi also paid tribute to the celebrant, recalling several occasions they had collaborated in court.

 “As a lawyer, you don’t want to be on the opposite side of Chief Olanipekun,” he said.

 Ekiti State Governor Biodun Oyebanji eulogised Olanipekun for enriching the legal profession, saying Ekiti State was proud of him. He also commended him for his contributions to the state in education, health, human capital development and mentoring of the younger generation.

 Minister of the FCT Nyesom Wike also delivered a goodwill message.

 For his part, Olanipekun recalled his personal relationship with Tinubu over the years, commending the President for his steadfastness, advocacy for democracy and sacrifices.

 He revealed that at one such meeting, the President had taught him the difference between legal and political jurisprudence.

 The event was attended by justices of the Supreme Court and the Court of Appeal, former chief justices of the federation, former governors, members of the Federal Executive Council, traditional rulers and the governor of Ondo State.

 Also present was Israel Adeniyi, the overall best graduating student at the 2026 Call to the Bar at the Nigerian Law School, who was introduced as a panellist to the audience.

CBN, NNPC sign MoU to strengthen forex inflows, energy financing

From Adanna Nnamani, Abuja

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) and the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPC) have signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to strengthen foreign exchange inflows, improve energy financing and deepen collaboration between the financial and energy sectors.

Speaking at the signing ceremony in Abuja on Wednesday, the CBN Governor, Olayemi Cardoso, said the agreement would strengthen macroeconomic management and support economic growth, noting that NNPC’s operations were central to Nigeria’s foreign exchange inflows, reserves and fiscal revenue.

He said the CBN, as custodian of key operational foreign exchange accounts, played a critical role in ensuring the timely, secure and transparent settlement of oil and gas proceeds into the Federation Account and other designated government accounts.

Cardoso said efficient account management, transfers and liquidity support would improve fiscal revenue and provide more predictable foreign exchange inflows into the market.

He added that the apex bank would leverage its convening power to attract global financial institutions to support financing in the gas sector.

The CBN governor said the MoU would formalise and strengthen the longstanding relationship between both institutions, adding that regular engagements involving the NNPC, oil-related companies and regulators had produced positive outcomes.

He said the framework would cover consultations, information exchange, oil and gas asset monetisation, operational approvals, payment automation, digital innovation, cybersecurity, capacity development, regulatory alignment, ethics and risk management.

He said: “Today’s occasion marks a significant milestone in the development of stronger macroeconomic management. We believe this MoU will further the goals of providing enduring economic stability and sustainable prosperity for all Nigerians.

“The oil and gas sector is an important driver of Nigeria’s economic growth. The operations of NNPC Limited therefore remain central to Nigeria’s external reserves, fiscal revenue and foreign exchange inflows, and contribute significantly to economic activity.

“As custodian of NNPC Limited’s key operational foreign currency accounts, the Central Bank of Nigeria plays a critical role in ensuring the timely, secure and transparent settlement of oil and gas proceeds into the Federation Account and other designated government accounts.

“Our continued collaboration in efficient account management, funds transfer and liquidity support directly contributes to macroeconomic stability by ensuring the availability of revenue to achieve fiscal mandates and predictable inflows into the foreign exchange markets.

“As the financial system adapts to the global push for energy transition, the CBN is well positioned to collaborate with NNPC Limited by exercising its convening power to attract global institutions with financial structures that can help develop Nigeria’s vast natural gas resources.”

Also speaking, the Group Chief Executive Officer of NNPC Limited, Bayo Ojulari, said the agreement would provide a structured framework for collaboration between the national oil company and the monetary authority.

Ojulari said the energy and financial systems were interconnected, stressing that crude oil and gas remained major sources of foreign exchange for the country.

He noted that, following the Petroleum Industry Act (PIA), NNPC had become a commercial entity and needed stronger collaboration with the monetary authority to support its operations, based on transparency and accountability.

According to him, the MoU would deepen trade, improve foreign exchange inflows, support the repatriation of export proceeds and create financial structures to enable NNPC to raise capital using its balance sheet and assets.

He said the CBN’s convening role would also help the company engage financial institutions and develop financing opportunities.

Ojulari added that the agreement would cover digital payments, innovation, cybersecurity, data protection, regulatory compliance and risk management.

He said both institutions would establish a joint steering committee and working committee, co-chaired by their representatives, to monitor implementation and track agreed key performance indicators.

“Today’s signing is about one simple idea: Nigeria’s energy sector and its financial system are not separate stories. They are one story.

“Crude oil and gas remain among the most important sources of foreign exchange for our nation, with NNPC Limited designated as the national oil company.

“A commercial national energy company needs a clear, structured relationship with the country’s monetary authority, built on transparency rather than one-off requests. This MoU provides that structure, setting out the principles and framework through which our two institutions will collaborate at the meeting point between energy and finance.

“The anticipated collaboration will create opportunities for economic growth and financial stability, helping NNPC position its initiatives in line with national monetary and energy objectives. It will explore credible opportunities to deepen trade and address challenges affecting the enhancement of foreign exchange inflows, which will significantly support the flow of export proceeds into the Nigerian economy.

“Both organisations will collaborate in the areas of energy financing and the monetisation of hydrocarbons, working together on financial structures that will help NNPC raise capital on the strength of its own balance sheet and assets,” Ojulari stated.

Atiku knocks defence ministry for promoting APC campaign rally on social media

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has questioned the use of the Federal Ministry of Defence’s official social media platforms to publicise the All Progressiv

Atiku, through his Media Adviser, Paul Ibe, criticised the ministry in a statement on Sunday for using its official Facebook page and X handle to announce the rally and publicise the Minister of Defence, General Christopher Musa (retd.), inspecting the venue.

The rally was held on Saturday at Mapo Hall, Ibadan, Oyo State, where Tinubu canvassed support for his re-election and promised improved electricity supply.

On Saturday, the defence ministry, via its X handle, shared details of the minister’s inspection.

The ministry’s post, which includes photographs of the minister’s visit, reads, “Minister of Defence, General Christopher Gwabin Musa (Rtd), OFR, Friday night conducted an on-the-spot inspection of the venue as part of strategic preparations ahead of the upcoming presidential campaign rally in the state.

“During the visit, General Musa toured the premises and surrounding perimeters to evaluate security readiness, crowd management logistics, and overall infrastructural preparedness. President Bola Tinubu GCFR, and key party officials would be on ground for the rally.”

The post had been deleted when our correspondent checked on Sunday afternoon.

PUNCH Online reports that both Musa and Minister of State for Defence, Bello Matawalle, are on the security team of the All Progressives Congress 2027 Presidential Campaign Council released in August 2026.

Questioning the ministry’s involvement in publicising the event, Atiku said the development raised concerns about the use of government communication platforms for partisan purposes.

“Why is the Ministry of Defence’s official Facebook page and X handle publicising an APC presidential campaign rally for Bola Tinubu’s 2027 re-election bid?” he asked.

He said the ministry’s post announced the rally and described Musa’s inspection of the venue as part of preparations for the event.

Atiku said providing security for a President’s visit was a legitimate responsibility of the ministry but argued that using its official platforms to publicise the President’s re-election campaign compromised the neutrality expected of public institutions.

“Providing security for a President’s visit is a legitimate responsibility. Turning the Ministry’s official platform into a publicity outlet for his re-election campaign compromises the institutional neutrality Nigerians should expect,” he said.

He added that the ministry was funded by taxpayers across political divides and should use its communication platforms to serve all Nigerians.

“The Ministry of Defence is funded by taxpayers of every political persuasion. Its platforms must serve all Nigerians,” he said.

Atiku said Nigerians facing insecurity deserved information about efforts to protect their communities and support security personnel, rather than publicity for a political campaign.

He also questioned the confidence opposition parties and voters could have in the impartiality of state institutions when an official defence platform publicised activities linked to the ruling party’s campaign.

The former Vice-President urged the minister to explain the publication, ensure that partisan campaign publicity was removed and maintain a clear distinction between official responsibilities and party activities.

The criticism followed a report by the Foundation for Investigative Journalism.

In its report published on Saturday, FIJ raised questions about the publication under Section 99(2) of the Electoral Act 2026, which states that “State apparatus including the media shall not be employed to the advantage or disadvantage of any political party or candidate at any election.”

The Ibadan rally was the seventh Tinubu had attended since his re-election campaign began in Kaduna on October 24.

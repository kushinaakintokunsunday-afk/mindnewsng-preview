₦70,000 Transport Controversy: FG Constitutes Committee To Review New Minimum Wages For Workers

By Channels TV Channels TV 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Channels TV

The Federal Government has approved the constitution of a new committee to comprehensively examine and address the issues raised by the Trade Union Congress (TUC) regarding a new minimum wage for Nigerian workers.

The development was revealed by the TUC’s Secretary-General, Comrade General N.A. Toro, in a statement on Tuesday, following a meeting with the Federal Government in response to a letter dated 8th October, 2026, titled “Request for an Urgent Meeting to Address Critical National Economic, Security and Labour Challenges,” submitted by the Union.

While the meeting was chaired by the Secretary to the Government of the Federation (SGF), Senator George Akume, the TUC delegation was led by the President, Comrade Engr. Festus Osifo, and comprised Comrade General N.A. Toro, Secretary-General; Comrade Shehu Mohammed, Deputy President; Comrade Akintola Benjamin, Trustee; and Comrade Kamal Ibrahim, Auditor II.

Also in attendance were the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation and the Permanent Secretary in the Office of the Secretary to the Government of the Federation, Ibrahim Kana.

While providing further explanation, TUC noted that it had insisted on a salary increment rather than a mere one-off N70,000 six-month transportation package.

“In the course of the deliberations, the TUC made its position unequivocally clear on the proposed ₦70,000 transport intervention for a period of six months, which it rejected, preferring instead a salary increase pending the commencement and conclusion of discussions on a new National Minimum Wage.

“This position, alongside the other demands and concerns raised by Congress, will form part of the issues to be thoroughly interrogated by the High-Powered Committee”, the statement read in part.

According to the statement, the government has already attended to some of the demands raised by the TUC.

“The Committee will therefore undertake a comprehensive review and reconciliation of the issues, establish the extent of actions already taken by the Government, identify outstanding matters and make appropriate findings and recommendations”, the statement said in part.

The composition of the High-Powered Committee includes three representatives from the TUC, led by the Secretary-General, Office of the Secretary to the Government of the Federation (OSGF), Permanent Secretary, Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation (OHCSF), National Salaries, Incomes and Wages Commission, and the Federal Ministry of Labour and Employment.

The High-Powered Committee is expected to commence work immediately and submit its report to the Secretary to the Government of the Federation within two weeks.

Balotelli: From ‘Why always me?’ to talent that never fully delivered — Report

By Punch Punch 6d ago 6 min read

From crashing his Audi R8 to providing the assist for Manchester City’s historic Premier League title-winning goal in 2012, Italian footballer Mario Balotelli has had a career marked by extraordinary talent and repeated off-field controversies.

In a report published on Tuesday, BBC Sport examined the 36-year-old’s eventful career, which has taken him to 13 clubs across six countries and included an assist for arguably the most famous goal in Premier League history.

The report traced Balotelli’s journey from Italian third-tier side Lumezzane to Inter Milan, Manchester City and several other clubs, highlighting how his disciplinary problems and unpredictable behaviour often overshadowed his footballing achievements.

The Italian footballer first came across Roberto Mancini, the Italian manager, who was hired to manage Inter Milan, during which the club signed Balotelli.

Regarded as his “great mentor,” Balotelli’s relationship with Mancini was likened to that of a father and son.  Under the Italian coach’s tutelage, Balotelli flourished.

The report said, at the age of 17, Balotelli had won the 2007-08 Serie A title with the club and reached the Coppa Italia final. Before then, he scored a brace against Juventus to send Inter into the semi-finals.

Life changed for Balotelli with the arrival of Jose Mourinho in the weeks after that campaign ended.

Following the arrival of the Portuguese manager, there were concerns about likely disagreements and disciplinary action over Balotelli’s attitude.

According to a report by The Guardian in 2009, following Inter’s 1-1 draw against Roma, Mourinho said, “Balotelli? In my opinion, he came close to a zero rating. I didn’t like how he played. He is 19 but could have done much better.”

According to World Soccer in a 2012 publication, the platform quoted Mourinho as saying on CNN, “The most problematic players were funny. Mario was good fun. I could write a book of 200 pages of my two years at Inter with Mario, but the book would not be a drama; it would be a comedy.

“I remember one time we went to play Kazan in the Champions League. In that match, I had all my strikers injured. No Milito, no Eto’o, I was really in trouble, and Mario was the only one.

“Mario got a yellow card in the 42nd minute, so when I went to the dressing room at half-time, I spent about 14 minutes of the 15 available speaking only to Mario.

“I said to him, ‘Mario, I cannot change you; I have no strikers on the bench, so don’t touch anybody and play only with the ball. If we lose the ball, no reaction. If someone provokes you, no reaction; if the referee makes a mistake, no reaction.’ The 46th minute, red card.”

BBC Sport noted that Balotelli still won three Serie A titles and one Champions League with Inter, but his “reputation as a supposed loose cannon was already beginning to overshadow his feats as a player.”

In August 2010, Balotelli joined his former boss at Manchester City.

The report noted that Mancini set out to show that the “unpredictable ways of Balotelli could be harnessed for the team’s good,” aiming to get more out of him than Mourinho could.

“A hardline stance on discipline often appeared to soften when Mancini dealt with Balotelli, who by spring 2011 had garnered a reputation in England as a fantastic footballer who could not stay away from controversy.”

At City, the Italian player was known for many controversies, including a car crash with his Audi R8, throwing a dart at a youth-team player and attempting a backheel trick shot when through on goal during a pre-season friendly against LA Galaxy.

Balotelli was also instrumental in helping City win the FA Cup in 2011, ending their 35-year major trophy drought.

According to the report, after the trick shot against Galaxy, Mancini questioned Balotelli’s mentality, saying: “If you are serious, you can play 90 minutes. If not, you can come and sit by me on the bench.”

Sometimes the audacity paid off – like the famous shoulder goal and subsequent nonchalant celebration against Norwich; on other occasions, not so much, said the report.

In October 2011, the report said firefighters visited Balotelli’s home in Cheshire county, Northwest England, in an emergency service to put out a fire at his home after a firework was reportedly set off in his bathroom.

Days later, Balotelli was back in the spotlight, this time for his performance on the pitch.

This time, Balotelli scored City’s first goal in a 6-1 win over Manchester United.

He raised his jersey over his head, with an inscription on the inner wear that read ‘Why always me?’.

In April 2012, Balotelli was sent off against North London side Arsenal in the league campaign. This, the report stated, “seemed like a season-defining moment” that could end City’s title bid.

However, he later made history, providing the assist for Sergio Aguero’s 93rd-minute winner in City’s 3-2 victory over QPR.

“It was his only Premier League assist that season,” the report noted.

Mario Balotelli scored 30 goals in 80 games for Manchester City

In January 2013, Balotelli’s relationship with Mancini hit rock bottom. They had an argument at the training ground, and  Balotelli consequently left for AC Milan later that month.

“He is not a bad guy, and a fantastic player, but I’m very sorry for him as he continues to lose his talent and his quality. I don’t have any words for his behaviour.

“I hope for him he can understand he is in a bad way for his future,” Mancini said of his former player.

However, “affection remained on both sides, but the relationship had become too fraught.”

One of Balotelli’s teammates at City, Micah Richards, stated that Balotelli was “crazy” and “brilliant.”

“He was crazy in a good way. He was brilliant to have around the squad – a cult hero not just for the nonsense he did off the field but on it as well.

“The best thing about him is he’s a lovely guy – he’s not arrogant, or how the papers try to perceive him; he’s just a bit mad,” Richards, an English right back at City, said.

Another teammate, Nedum Onuoha, noted that while Balotelli’s career might have fallen short of expectations despite his talent, it was still better compared to some others.

“Once upon a time, Mario did genuinely believe he was one of the best players in the world.

“There are lots of stories about him from his time at City, but the one I remember is at our old training ground in Carrington when someone was talking about Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo, and Mario said he was as good as them – and he was deadly serious.

“It obviously hasn’t worked out that way, but he still has done so much more with his career than most players could even dream of,” Onuoha said.

Balotelli was briefly recalled for Italy by his old boss in 2018.

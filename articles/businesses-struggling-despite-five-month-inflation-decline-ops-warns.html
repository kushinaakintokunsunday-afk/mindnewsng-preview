Businesses struggling despite five-month inflation decline, OPS warns

By Punch Punch 18 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Despite five consecutive months of declining inflation, businesses are still shutting down in Nigeria, while the prices of essential goods and services remain h

Private sector leaders welcomed the further decline in headline inflation to 14.06 per cent in October, 2026, but warned that improved macroeconomic indicators had yet to translate into meaningful relief for businesses and households.

They called on the Federal Government to intensify interventions to reduce the cost of living, support struggling enterprises and ensure that inflation management aligns more closely with poverty reduction objectives.

The National Bureau of Statistics disclosed on Monday in its October 2026 Consumer Price Index report that headline inflation declined to 14.06 per cent from 14.63 per cent in September, representing a 0.57 percentage point reduction.

Speaking in separate telephone interviews with The PUNCH, OPS leaders, including the President of the Association of Small Business Owners of Nigeria, Dr Femi Egbesola, described the inflation slowdown as encouraging but said the development had yet to reflect in the prices of goods and the survival of businesses.

Egbesola said, “The situation is consistently coming down, and that’s our prayer. It shows that we are having macroeconomic change, which is good for the country, and will also engender investments.

It also means that the reforms of government are yielding positive fruits.

“But beyond that, if you go to the markets, you see that the prices of goods and commodities are still very high. A true test of the success of inflation is actually the reduction in prices. Rather than coming down, you also see that many of these commodity prices are still where they are, and some of them are even going up.”

He stressed that the continued difficulties facing businesses showed that the gains from economic reforms had yet to reach the productive sector, citing a recent report which indicated that 60 per cent of bankers were struggling.

Egbesola said, “You also look at it that businesses are still challenged. If you look at the report of last week from the Bankers Association, which said 60 per cent of bankers are still struggling, you know it means that businesses are still dying.

“And that’s how to really measure whether the reform is working fine, or translated to be effective or not. So when it is not reflected in households and in businesses, it means that there is a gap. And that’s why we are saying to the government that it needs to begin to intervene.”

He called for more sustainable support programmes for households and businesses, arguing that existing interventions remained inadequate to address the scale of economic hardship.

Also speaking, the National Vice-President of the National Association of Small-Scale Industrialists, Segun Kuti-George, described the declining inflation rate as a positive development, saying it indicated greater stability in the economy.

Kuti-George attributed the improvement to exchange rate and interest rate stability, as well as relative stability in the prices of petroleum products.

He said, “If that is happening, you know, it’s a positive development. It shows stability generally in the economy. And that can be accounted for by stable exchange rates, and stable interest rates. So, those are the things that could account for it. It means our monetary and fiscal policies are working positively.”

Meanwhile, the Chief Executive Officer of the Centre for the Promotion of Private Enterprise, Dr Muda Yusuf, called for a shift in inflation management towards affordability and household welfare, warning that declining inflation did not necessarily mean falling prices.

In a policy brief on the October inflation report, Yusuf said the moderation in headline and core inflation represented progress in economic stabilisation but warned that persistent pressures on essential household expenditure continued to undermine living standards.

He said, “Declining inflation does not necessarily imply declining prices. It primarily indicates that prices are rising at a slower rate. This distinction is particularly important for households already struggling with elevated living costs.”

Yusuf noted that food and non-alcoholic beverages contributed 5.63 percentage points to headline inflation, transportation 1.50 percentage points, housing and utilities 1.18 percentage points, while education and healthcare jointly contributed 1.72 percentage points.

He said these essential expenditure categories accounted for approximately 71 per cent of headline inflation, making their affordability particularly important to low-income households.

“The CPPE therefore stresses that inflation management must be more closely aligned with poverty reduction objectives. Government interventions should prioritise the affordability of food, transportation, housing, education and healthcare, given their disproportionate impact on the poor,” he said.

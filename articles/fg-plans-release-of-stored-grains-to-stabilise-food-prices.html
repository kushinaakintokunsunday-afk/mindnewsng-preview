FG plans release of stored grains to stabilise food prices

By Punch Punch 5d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The government also disclosed plans to distribute three million bags of fertiliser within the next month as part of measures to reduce farmers’ production costs and improve food availability across the country.

The Minister of Agriculture and Food Security, Abubakar Kyari, announced the measures on Wednesday during the presentation of the 2026 Wet Season Agricultural Performance Survey Report in Abuja.

According to a statement issued by the ministry, Kyari said the release of stored agricultural produce would form part of the government’s strategy to stabilise food prices while protecting farmers’ incomes.

“The same crops that we have that were stored under the guaranteed minimum price programme under the Bank of Agriculture will be released very soon to stabilise the market prices,” the minister said.

He disclosed that the government would announce the modalities for distributing the commodities, although he did not specify the quantity of grains available for release or the proposed distribution date.

Kyari explained that increased agricultural productivity, expanded cultivated land and improved access to farming inputs were expected to ease pressure on food prices.

He said a comparison of commodity prices between 2025 and 2026 indicated reductions in the prices of some agricultural products, particularly maize and dairy products.

“Just within the one year, we have seen a drop in dairy, maize and so forth that has come down,” he said.

The minister disclosed that the Presidential Fertiliser Initiative was preparing to release three million bags of fertiliser within the next month, following an intervention announced by Vice President Kashim Shettima.

He explained that the initiative would help reduce production costs and encourage farmers to expand cultivation.

Kyari also highlighted the National Agricultural Development Fund and the recapitalisation of the Bank of Agriculture as measures intended to improve farmers’ access to financing.

He said the Guaranteed Minimum Price programme provided farmers with a market for their produce, encouraging agricultural production while supporting price stability.

The minister attributed the expansion of cultivated farmland partly to improved security, explaining that military operations had enabled farmers to return to previously inaccessible agricultural areas.

On mechanisation, Kyari disclosed that the government had deployed 2,000 tractors, while deliveries under the second phase of the programme were expected within two to three months.

He also announced growing investment interest in agricultural processing, including cocoa, sesame, cassava and cashew nuts.

According to him, the government had signed a $33m investment agreement with a Turkish company for greenhouse projects under Nigeria’s Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones.

Meanwhile, the Executive Director of the National Agricultural Extension and Research Liaison Services, Ahmadu Bello University, Zaria, Yusuf Sani, disclosed that production of 13 out of the 17 major crops assessed was projected to increase in 2026.

“Nineteen multidisciplinary teams conducted field assessments across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory between August 31 and September 6, 2026, in collaboration with state ministries of agriculture, agricultural research institutes, Agricultural Development Programmes and development partners,” Sani said.

He identified Niger as the leading rice-producing state, Kano as the largest producer of sorghum and Yobe as the leading millet-producing state.

Sani called for stronger storage facilities, improved post-harvest handling and better market access to prevent agricultural losses.

Kyari clarified that the assessment covered only wet-season production, adding that a separate dry-season survey had been commissioned to determine Nigeria’s overall agricultural output for 2026.

The announcement comes amid easing monthly food prices, although annual food inflation remains elevated.

According to the National Bureau of Statistics’ October 2026 Consumer Price Index report, released on Monday, food inflation stood at 19.13 per cent, compared with 19.59 per cent in September and 16.30 per cent in October 2025.

This means annual food inflation declined by 0.46 percentage points month-on-month but remained 2.83 percentage points above its level a year earlier.

On a month-on-month basis, average food prices fell by 0.75 per cent in October, following a larger 1.55 per cent decline in September. The figures indicate that food prices continued to fall, although the pace of the decline moderated.

Reps divided over proposal to write off farmers’ loans

By Punch Punch 6d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A motion seeking the cancellation of outstanding loans obtained by Nigerian farmers under government-backed agricultural intervention programmes has divided mem

The motion, moved on Wednesday by the member representing Yamaltu/Deba Federal Constituency, Gombe State, Inuwa Garba, sought a waiver of outstanding agricultural loans, citing insecurity, climate-related disasters and prevailing economic hardship that have undermined farmers’ ability to repay their debts.

Garba argued that many beneficiaries were unable to meet their repayment obligations because circumstances beyond their control had severely affected their farming activities, investments and incomes.

Over the years, the Federal Government, through the Central Bank of Nigeria and other financial institutions, introduced several agricultural financing initiatives, including the Anchor Borrowers’ Programme, the Commercial Agriculture Credit Scheme and NIRSAL-supported facilities, to boost food production, empower farmers and strengthen national food security.

Leading the debate on the motion, Garba said, “The House is concerned that widespread insecurity, banditry, insurgency, farmer-herder conflicts, flooding, drought, climate change and other natural and human-induced disasters like soil degradation, poor soil fertility, poor irrigation infrastructure and inadequate veterinary and disease control services have severely disrupted agricultural activities, resulting in substantial crop failures, food insufficiency, loss of investments and diminished farm incomes.

“The House is concerned that many beneficiaries of government-sponsored agricultural loans have been unable to meet their repayment obligations, not necessarily due to unwillingness to repay, but because circumstances beyond their control have substantially undermined their productive capacity and financial stability.

“We are worried that the continued accumulation and enforcement of outstanding agricultural debts, including principal, interest and penalties, could push already distressed farmers into deeper poverty, expose them to asset seizures and discourage their participation in future agricultural activities,” he said.

He further argued that compelling financially distressed farmers to repay the loans under prevailing economic conditions could undermine the objectives of the intervention programmes.

According to him, such pressure could worsen food insecurity, unemployment and poverty in rural communities rather than strengthen agricultural production.

However, opposing the proposed blanket waiver, the member representing Ede South/Ede North/Ejigbo/Egbedore Federal Constituency, Osun State, Bamidele Salam, said an audit report by the Auditor-General for the Federation before the House had exposed widespread abuse of the agricultural financing schemes.

Salam alleged that some beneficiaries who accessed more than N1tn under the Anchor Borrowers’ Programme diverted the funds into businesses and investments unrelated to agriculture, including real estate acquisitions.

He called for a comprehensive investigation into the intervention programmes to establish how the loans were utilised and identify beneficiaries who genuinely invested the funds in farming.

He said, “Over a trillion naira, given up as loans under the Anchor Borrowers Programme, a CBN intervention programme for Nigerian farmers, was largely abused by beneficiaries. People came, approached the CBN or their money deposit banks, and obtained loans for the purpose of farming, but diverted those monies into some other area of business endeavour, buying real estate in Abuja, in Lagos, and some in Dubai.

“The Bank of Agriculture, which unfortunately was set up as that special purpose vehicle for supporting farmers, has today become virtually immobile as a result of a sum of N795bn borrowed by persons, most of whom diverted that money into other purposes.”

Salam, who chairs the House Committee on Public Accounts, said granting a blanket waiver without first establishing the circumstances surrounding the loans would set a dangerous precedent.

“If we come as a parliament and recommend a holistic waiving of all loans given out to persons who are purportedly to be farmers, we will be setting a very, very dangerous precedent for people to come, access government facilities, some of them interest-free, some of them single-digit interest, only for them to go and use those monies for other things,” he said.

He consequently called for the constitution of an investigative committee or the outright rejection of the motion.

Also opposing an immediate blanket waiver, an Ogun State lawmaker, Isiaka Ibrahim, cited instances of beneficiaries who allegedly obtained government-backed loans but spent the money on personal engagements, including marrying additional wives.

He, however, said a thorough investigation would help identify farmers genuinely affected by economic hardship and unable to meet their repayment obligations.

Similarly, another All Progressives Congress lawmaker from Lagos State, Paul Kalejaiye, urged the House to exercise caution, stressing the need to investigate how beneficiaries utilised funds obtained under the various agricultural intervention programmes before considering any debt relief.

The disagreement prevented the House from reaching a resolution on the motion, prompting lawmakers to defer further consideration of the matter until Tuesday, November 24.

The proposed waiver has brought into focus the challenge of balancing relief for farmers whose livelihoods have been disrupted by insecurity and other economic pressures with accountability for public funds committed to agricultural development.

The outcome of the legislative consideration could have implications for the recovery of government-backed agricultural loans, the credibility of future intervention schemes and efforts to improve food production in the country.

MREIF mortgage: What you need for the ₦100m FG-backed housing loan

By Punch Punch 5d ago 6 min read

Photo: Punch

The Ministry of Finance Incorporated Real Estate Investment Fund, established to address housing affordability and expand access to mortgage financing, also requires applicants to meet specific documentation and income verification conditions before they can access the facility.

According to the Frequently Asked Questions section on the MREIF website, borrowers must be at least 21 years old but not older than 60 years at the expiration of the loan.

“The person should have a satisfactory credit score. Borrowers must contribute at least 20% of the value of the housing unit,” the fund stated.

The requirements apply to prospective homebuyers seeking financing under the scheme, which has a maximum loan limit of ₦100m under its pension-backed mortgage arrangement.

The fund, established by the Ministry of Finance Incorporated, the investment arm of the Federal Government, is designed to address challenges in Nigeria’s housing sector by tackling both supply and demand constraints.

Explaining its objectives, MREIF said it was established to provide affordable mortgage financing, support housing development, generate employment and deepen local manufacturing of building materials.

It also aims to create up to two million jobs, generate up to $10bn in mortgage assets and contribute up to $20bn to economic growth.

Requirements for employed applicants

Employed Nigerians seeking to access the mortgage facility are required to submit personal, employment and financial documents as part of the application process.

According to the fund’s application requirements, applicants must provide the following:

Personal documents

* Loan application letter.

* Completed application form.

* Bank Verification Number (BVN) printout.

* Recent utility bill not older than three months.

* Signed sales or provisional offer letter.

* Passport photograph.

Employment and financial documents

* Appointment or employment letter, or last promotion letter.

* Curriculum vitae.

* Staff identity card.

* Salary account statement covering one year.

* Payslips covering one year.

* Tax clearance card or Tax Identification Number (TIN).

* PENCOM statement (optional).

Additional requirements for joint applications

Employed applicants submitting joint applications with their spouses must also provide:

* Spouse’s consent letter for the joint application.

* Valid means of identification.

* BVN printout.

* Passport photograph.

* Salary or business account statement.

* Tax clearance card or TIN.

* Spouse’s appointment or employment letter, or last promotion letter.

* Spouse’s CV and staff identity card.

* One-year salary account statement and payslips.

* PENCOM account statement (optional for Retirement Savings Account holders).

The fund also lists the following additional documents for applicants to upload after submitting their initial applications:

* Letter of introduction from the applicant’s human resources department.

* Two reference forms completed by current account holders with commercial banks. The accounts must be at least six months old and must not be salary accounts.

* Preferred Primary Mortgage Bank (PMB) account opening form.

* Evidence of equity contribution.

Requirements for self-employed applicants

Self-employed Nigerians are also eligible to apply but must provide additional documents to establish their business operations and demonstrate their financial capacity.

The requirements include:

Personal documents

* Completed loan application form.

* Loan application letter.

* Signed sales or provisional offer letter.

* Recent utility bill, such as an electricity or water bill.

* Passport photograph.

* Three years’ personal bank account statements.

* CV.

* PENCOM account statement (optional).

Business documents

* Copy of Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) registration documents.

* Three years’ company bank account statements.

* Company profile, including a list of clients.

* Evidence of trade association payment and endorsement.

* Tax certificate or TIN.

Additional requirements for joint applications

Self-employed applicants submitting joint applications with their spouses must provide:

* Spouse’s consent letter.

* Copy of CAC documents.

* Valid means of identification.

* BVN printout.

* Passport photograph.

* Three years’ company bank account statements.

* Evidence of trade association payment and endorsement.

* Tax certificate or TIN.

* Spouse’s CV.

* PENCOM account statement (optional).

Other documents required include:

* Two references.

* Board resolution authorising the borrowing and guaranteeing of the facility.

* Evidence of running contracts and contract renewals.

* Preferred PMB account opening form.

* Three years’ audited or management accounts.

* Evidence of equity contribution payment.

Diaspora applicants’ requirements

Nigerians living abroad can also access the mortgage facility, although they are required to make a higher initial contribution.

According to MREIF, diaspora applicants must provide evidence of an equity contribution of at least 40 per cent of the property’s value.

Other application requirements include:

Personal and residence documents

* Completed loan application form.

* Loan application letter.

* Signed sales or provisional offer letter.

* Passport photograph.

* CV.

* Copy of residence permit and work permit.

* Utility bill not older than three months.

* Evidence of tax payment.

* Evidence of equity contribution of at least 40 per cent.

Income verification documents

* Bank account statements covering 12 months.

* Payslips covering 12 months.

* Employment letter or letter of last promotion.

Additional documents

* Two Nigerian references.

* PENCOM statement (optional).

* At least two credit search reports from the country of residence.

* Letter of introduction from the employer.

One property per borrower under mortgage scheme

Although MREIF allows Nigerians to purchase multiple houses with their personal resources, the fund limits the number of properties an individual can acquire through its mortgage financing.

“Home buyers can buy multiple houses with their own resources, but can only buy one unit through MREIF mortgage financing,” it stated.

The fund also said its housing projects would cover states across Nigeria where there were available homebuyers.

On the mortgage products available, MREIF listed its pension-backed mortgage and a non-interest mortgage option, which it said was coming soon.

The fund explained that its intervention was designed to address Nigeria’s housing deficit, limited access to affordable mortgage financing and the need to provide offtake guarantees for qualified housing developers.

It said it intended to improve housing affordability by offering mortgage financing to homebuyers and offtake guarantees to housing delivery partners through a technology-driven platform.

According to the fund, the platform would allow Nigerians to view available housing units and complete purchase transactions.

MREIF also said it would employ due diligence, active asset management and a dedicated risk management team to mitigate market, liquidity, operational and regulatory risks.

Multi-Trex posts N1.03bn pre-tax loss despite revenue surge

By Punch Punch 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Multi-Trex Integrated Foods Plc recorded a 79 per cent rise in pre-tax loss to N1.03bn for the financial year ended 30 April 2026, compared with a restated N577

According to the company’s audited financial statements filed on the Nigerian Exchange and audited by Baker Tilly Nigeria, revenue surged 6,392 per cent year-on-year to N3.11bn from N47.90m in the corresponding period of 2025.

In the report, the directors noted that while the firm’s principal activities span cocoa bean trading, industrial cocoa processing and consumer product manufacturing, only cocoa bean trading took place during the year under review, with all N3.11bn generated from export sales compared with N9.48m in export revenue and N38.42m in local sales in 2025.

However, the top-line expansion was largely offset by cost of sales, which jumped from N6.82m in 2025 to N3.10bn in 2026 due to N3.09bn spent on direct materials. As a result, gross profit declined 79.1 per cent to N8.58m from N41.08m a year earlier.

Other operating income also fell 48.4 per cent to N198.13m from N384.31m in 2025, reflecting the absence of the N281.35m unrealised foreign exchange gain recorded in the prior year, even as scrap sales proceeds rose to N195.38m from N100.64m.

Administrative expenses climbed 28.3 per cent to N1.17bn from N911.36m in the preceding year, driven largely by N734.01m in depreciation and amortisation charges on idle productive assets, N90.25m in realised foreign exchange losses, N88.37m in staff costs and N60.34m in repairs and maintenance. Consequently, the company’s operating loss widened 98.2 per cent to N962.96m from N485.96m in 2025.

Although interest expenses on borrowings declined  23.3 per cent to N70.60m from N92.01m, pre-tax loss deepened to N1.03bn. Following a deferred tax credit of N136.50m, loss after taxation rose by 55 per cent to N897.06m from N578.62m in 2025, pushing basic and diluted loss per share to 14 kobo from nine kobo.

Given the accumulated losses sustained over the years, the Board of Directors did not recommend a dividend for the 2026 financial year.

On the balance sheet, total assets contracted 4.5 per cent to N13.79bn as of 30 April 2026, compared with N14.44bn in 2025.

While property, plant and equipment increased 15 per cent to N13.20bn from N11.48bn following N3.01bn in capital additions aimed at plant refurbishment, current assets plunged 82.9 per cent to N490.18m from N2.87bn as cash and cash equivalents dropped to N297.97m from N2.04bn and trade and other receivables fell to N132.96m from N830.60m.

Total liabilities rose 4.4 per cent to N6.21bn from N5.95bn in 2025, driven by a 33.7 per cent rise in trade and other payables to N1.71bn from N1.28bn, including N760.19m owed to related party N-Foods Universal Concept Limited.

Shareholders’ equity fell 10.7 per cent to N7.58bn from N8.49bn as retained deficit widened to N5.56bn from N4.69bn, leaving the company with a negative working capital of N2.37bn compared with a positive working capital of N411.73m in 2025.

In its disclosures on events after the reporting period, the company revealed that on 10 July 2026, it reached a final settlement with the Nigerian Export-Import Bank over its defaulted 14 per cent term loan facility. Under the agreement, Multi-Trex paid off the outstanding principal of N470.04m in full, while NEXIM Bank granted a full waiver of N595.90m in accumulated interest, discharging the company from all indebtedness under the facility.

The company also disclosed retrospective restatements to its 2025 financial statements in line with International Accounting Standard 8 to correct under-depreciation of property, plant and equipment between 2022 and 2025, alongside reclassifications of deferred building repairs and share issue costs.

Meanwhile, the management maintained that ongoing plant refurbishment and backing from strategic investors position the company to sustain operations and achieve full operational recovery.

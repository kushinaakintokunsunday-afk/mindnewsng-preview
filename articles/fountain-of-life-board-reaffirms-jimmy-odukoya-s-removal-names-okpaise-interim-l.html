Fountain of Life board reaffirms Jimmy Odukoya’s removal, names Okpaise interim leader

By Vanguard Vanguard 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Vanguard

The Board of Trustees of Fountain of Life Church has reaffirmed its decision to remove Pastor Jimmy Odukoya as senior pastor, appointing Pastor Rotimi Okpaise t

The board said the interim arrangement would remain in force pending consultations and the completion of processes for appointing a substantive leadership structure in accordance with the church’s constitution and established procedures.

The latest decision comes amid an escalating leadership and succession dispute between the board and Jimmy, son of the church’s late founder, Pastor Taiwo Odukoya, who had earlier rejected his removal.

In a statement on Tuesday, the trustees insisted that their November 16, 2026 resolution withdrawing recognition of Jimmy as senior pastor remained valid.

The board also alleged that individuals linked to Jimmy had taken control of the church’s social media platforms without its authorisation and were using them to publish what it described as inaccurate and misleading accounts of the dispute.

“The Board stands by its earlier statement in its entirety, and its resolution of 16 November 2026 remains in force,” it said.

The trustees explained that the decision followed a review of the church’s leadership and succession arrangements, as well as what they described as longstanding governance concerns and current doctrinal and moral controversies.

They said Jimmy had previously been asked to step down before the board resolved to withdraw its recognition of him as senior pastor with immediate effect.

“He is required to step down forthwith from the office and all functions of Senior Pastor,” the statement added.

The board said the decision was intended to create an atmosphere conducive to restoring peace, rebuilding trust and resolving the church’s leadership and governance challenges.

It stressed that the action was not motivated by vindictiveness, humiliation or personal condemnation but was considered necessary to protect the church’s spiritual heritage, institutional integrity and public reputation.

The trustees also warned that statements appearing on the church’s social media platforms should not be regarded as official positions of the board.

They said only statements issued and signed on behalf of the board should be recognised as official, adding that steps were being taken to restore the church’s communication channels to authorised control.

Background to succession dispute

Providing details of the succession disagreement, the board said Fountain of Life Church had built its reputation over three decades on biblical truth, sound doctrine, spiritual integrity and Christian leadership under its founding pastors.

It said responsibility for ensuring an orderly leadership transition rested with the board following the death of Taiwo Odukoya in 2023.

According to the trustees, Jimmy had informed them, and subsequently the congregation, that his late father had handed the leadership mantle to him.

The board said it relied on that representation when it appointed him senior pastor in accordance with the church’s constitution and the succession arrangements it understood to be in place.

However, it disclosed that a video recording made by Taiwo in December 2021, in which he expressly identified his preferred successor, was not presented to the board until late December 2025.

The recording, which the board said was witnessed by John Odukoya, Taiwo’s older son, did not identify Jimmy as the preferred successor.

It further claimed that the recording was not disclosed when it considered and approved Jimmy’s appointment as senior pastor.

“The fundamental issue, therefore, is not simply the identity of the person appointed, but whether the board’s decision was reached on the basis of full, accurate and transparent disclosure of all material information relevant to the succession process,” the trustees said.

The board maintained that it could not remain indifferent while, in its view, the church’s spiritual heritage, institutional integrity and public reputation faced continuing erosion.

Outrage as Kano court jails TikToker for ‘insulting’ Hisbah

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The arrest of a 19-year-old TikTok user, Zainab Abubakar, by the Kano State Hisbah and her subsequent conviction by a Sharia court over alleged insults against

Abubakar, an indigene of Ogun State, was reportedly arrested alongside two others, Ahmad Usman and Muhammad Aliyu, after they featured in a video that went viral on social media, allegedly insulting Hisbah officials following the agency’s decision to stop them from organising an event along Zoo Road in Kano.

Abubakar was on Wednesday sentenced to five months imprisonment and 10 strokes of the cane by the state Sharia court.

Reacting to the conviction, Amnesty International described Abubakar’s sentence as an attack on freedom of expression and called for her “immediate and unconditional release.”

The human rights group also condemned the detention of Aliyu, who is currently being held in prison pending trial over an alleged offence.

In a statement on Friday, Amnesty International faulted the conviction, arguing that no one should be prosecuted or punished for expressing an opinion, even when such views are considered offensive.

“It is flabbergasting that someone can be made to appear before a court and stand trial, let alone serve a sentence and 10 strokes of cane for simply having expressed a strong opinion,” the organisation said.

The group described Abubakar’s trial and sentencing as the latest development in what it called the Kano authorities’ sustained repression of young people.

It said the five-month jail term was an indication of what it described as the increasing excesses of Hisbah officials against young people, particularly those from less privileged backgrounds.

The organisation maintained that the right to freely express one’s views, including opinions considered shocking or offensive, was protected under international human rights law.

It, therefore, called on the government to quash Zainab’s conviction and ensure her immediate and unconditional release.

In a separate statement on Saturday, Amnesty International also demanded Aliyu’s release, describing his detention as disproportionate and a violation of freedom of expression.

“Criticising public institutions or officials is not a criminal offence under international law, and no one should stand trial, let alone face imprisonment and strokes of cane for just doing that,” the group said.

When contacted, the Director-General of the board, Abba Sufi, declined to comment, saying, “I’m sorry, I cannot comment on the issue for now. I’m currently at the hospital and I can’t speak to you now.”

Efforts to get the state government’s reaction through the Commissioner for Information, Sunusi Bature, were also unsuccessful, as he did not respond to phone calls and messages sent to him.

Meanwhile, many netizens have criticised Hisbah and called for justice.

A Facebook user, Abdulkarim Ishiyaku, said freedom of expression was a fundamental human right, arguing that criticism or disagreement should not automatically attract criminal punishment.

He said, “No young person should face imprisonment or corporal punishment simply for expressing an opinion. Authorities must respect human dignity, fundamental rights and the rule of law. Zainab deserves justice.”

Similarly, a Facebook user, Hazmaf Arzet, described the punishment as excessive, arguing that the teenager needed guidance rather than imprisonment.

The user also questioned the basis for the five-month jail term, calling on the court to explain the Islamic provisions under which she was sentenced.

An X user identified as Abuja queen alleged that Abubakar’s identity as a non-indigene might have influenced her treatment.

Another user, WizkidEmpire, accused the Hisbah of selectively enforcing its rules, alleging that ordinary citizens were more likely to face punishment than the children of influential people.

Also reacting, an X user, belel_yusuf, called for the arrest of anyone involved in physically assaulting the teenager, describing her treatment as a violation of human rights and dignity.

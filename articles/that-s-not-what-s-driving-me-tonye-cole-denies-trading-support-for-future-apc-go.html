‘That’s Not What’s Driving Me’: Tonye Cole Denies Trading Support For Future APC Governorship Ticket

Former Rivers State governorship candidate of the All Progressives Congress (APC), Tonye Cole, has denied that his decision to support the party’s political arrangement ahead of the 2027 elections was motivated by a promise of a future governorship ticket.

Cole said his priority was to change the perception of Rivers State as a place associated with violence and political strongmen, arguing that peace and security were essential to attracting investment to the state.

He spoke on Friday while appearing as a guest on Channels Television’s The Morning Brief, where he was asked whether he had been promised an opportunity to pursue his governorship ambition in the future.

“No, and that is not what is driving me,” he replied.

“What is driving me is that if you cannot bring peace and security and change the narrative of Rivers State, investment will not come to Rivers State. So the first aspect of it is that we must change the mindset of everyone that when they talk about Rivers State, violence, strongman, no gentlemen, loud, all of that tagline must change.

“If I cannot be the face of what the future of Rivers State politics should look like, then we have a problem,” he added.

Cole, who emerged as the APC governorship candidate in 2019 and 2023, withdrew from the party’s 2027 governorship race in May following consultations and efforts to reconcile rival factions within the state chapter.

He subsequently aligned with the Rainbow Coalition, a cross-party political arrangement led by the Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Nyesom Wike, which is backing President Bola Tinubu’s re-election bid.

Explaining his decision to remain with the APC group that predated the current political arrangement, Cole said he wanted to ensure that members who had worked to build the party were not sidelined.

“For me, abandoning those who were in APC who worked was not the question. So I decided that I was going to stay with them so that they would have a voice. They cannot be removed, so I stayed there to represent the voice of the APC that built forth and kept Rivers State whole,” he said.

He added that the decision to bring different political interests together was informed by the need to prevent divisions that had previously weakened the APC in Rivers State.

‘No Differences With Wike’

Wike holds near-total control over the formal political institutions and party structures in Rivers State. There was intense, public friction and even a defamation lawsuit between Cole and the FCT minister before they reconciled to form the Rainbow Coalition.

Cole, speaking further, said he and Wike had put their past disagreements behind them and were now working towards a common political objective.

“I believe politics is about sacrifice. There is no politician who does not have scars. But you have to be looking at the future. So today, Nyesom Wike and I do not have any differences.

“What was in the past was in the past. We settled it towards the future. Now we have a common goal that the APC win the ballot in 2027,” he said.

The coalition, on October 27, unveiled its endorsed candidates for the 2027 elections at the Yakubu Gowon Stadium in Port Harcourt, with Kingsley Chinda emerging as its preferred governorship candidate under the APC.

Cole said the objective was to change the state’s political image and make it more attractive to investors.

“We want to remove that tagline that Rivers State is a violent state,” he added.

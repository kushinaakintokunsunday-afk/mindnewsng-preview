Tinubu Receives State Police Bill, Prepares For Assent

By Channels TV Channels TV 6d ago 3 min read

President Bola Tinubu has received the Constitution (Sixth Alteration) Bill, which provides for the establishment of State Police in Nigeria.

Chief of Staff to the President, Femi Gbajabiamila, confirmed this in a statement on Wednesday.

Gbajabiamila said that the bill was formally transmitted to Tinubu on Wednesday by his Special Advisers on Senate and House of Representatives Matters, Senator Basheer Lado and Wasiu Olanrewaju-Smart.

He said that the President would review the Constitution Alteration Bill in consultation with the Attorney-General of the Federation, Lateef Fagbemi, and assent to it at a formal signing ceremony.

Meanwhile, Gbajabiamila, who is the Chairman of the Presidential Working Group on State Police, said the working group, with the support of the Policy Advisory Committee, has undertaken extensive stakeholder engagement and nationwide consultations towards developing a draft National Policing Bill.

He noted that the draft bill had been submitted to the Federal Ministry of Justice for legislative drafting validation.

“Upon receipt of the validated version, the Working Group will review it and present it, alongside its report, to Mr President for consideration.

“President Tinubu expresses his appreciation to the President of the Senate, the Speaker of the House of Representatives, members of the National Assembly, the Clerk to the National Assembly, the 36 State Governors, the State Houses of Assembly, and all Nigerians who have demonstrated a shared commitment to addressing the nation’s security challenges,” he added.

The Senate had earlier directed the clerk to the National Assembly to transmit the Constitution alteration bill providing for the establishment of State Police to President Tinubu for assent.

The upper chamber had also asked the Executive to forward the National Policing Bill to the National Assembly for legislative scrutiny and expeditious passage to establish the operational framework for subnational policing.

The resolutions followed a motion sponsored by the Senate Leader, Opeyemi Bamidele, during plenary on Wednesday.

The Clerk to the National Assembly, Kamoru Ogunlana, announced on Monday that all 36 State Houses of Assembly had approved the proposed constitutional amendment to establish state police, clearing the way for the bill to be transmitted to the President.

The approval, he said, exceeded the constitutional requirement of at least 24 state assemblies under Section 9(2) of the 1999 Constitution, as amended.

Community Policing

Persistent security challenges in Nigeria have intensified calls for a policing system that brings law enforcement closer to communities, improves intelligence gathering and enables faster responses to local threats.

Supporters of state police argue that decentralising the current centralised structure would allow security agencies to better understand the peculiar challenges of different communities.

They also believe state-controlled forces could strengthen grassroots intelligence and complement existing security arrangements.

However, the proposal generated concerns about accountability, political interference and funding.

In September 2025, House of Representatives member Yusuf Gadgi argued that properly funded community policing would be more effective than state police, warning that placing police commands under governors could worsen insecurity.

But existing community-based security outfits, such as Amotekun in the South-West, have also faced operational limitations, including restricted access to superior weapons, despite their role in local security.

Human rights lawyer Femi Falana supported decentralised policing but warned that clear jurisdictions and legal safeguards are essential to prevent political intimidation and clashes between security agencies.

He also cautioned that state police alone cannot resolve insecurity without addressing poverty and unemployment.

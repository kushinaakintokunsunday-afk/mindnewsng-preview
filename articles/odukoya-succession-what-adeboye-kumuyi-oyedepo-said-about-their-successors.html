Odukoya succession: What Adeboye, Kumuyi, Oyedepo said about their successors

By Punch Punch 5d ago 10 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The leadership crisis at The Fountain of Life Church, following the disputed removal of Pastor Jimmy Odukoya as Senior Pastor, has brought renewed attention to

A section of the church’s Board of Trustees announced that it no longer recognised Jimmy Odukoya as Senior Pastor and appointed Pastor Rotimi Okpaise as interim spiritual and administrative leader. The trustees cited governance concerns and information about the late founder, Pastor Taiwo Odukoya’s succession preference.

However, Jimmy Odukoya and his supporters rejected the announcement, insisting that he remained the duly appointed Senior Pastor and Chairman of the Board of Trustees. They described the trustees behind the decision as a group acting outside the church’s constitutional framework.

The disagreement has renewed discussions about how leadership should pass from one generation to another in churches built around influential founders and long-serving spiritual leaders.

Three prominent Nigerian clerics, the General Overseer of the Redeemed Christian Church of God, Pastor Enoch Adeboye; the General Superintendent of the Deeper Christian Life Ministry, Pastor William Kumuyi; and the Presiding Bishop of the Living Faith Church Worldwide, also known as Winners’ Chapel, Bishop David Oyedepo, have previously spoken about succession in their respective ministries.

While Adeboye has said he is waiting for God to reveal his successor, Kumuyi has rejected the idea of handing the ministry to any of his biological children, insisting that the church belongs to Jesus Christ. Oyedepo, on his part, has described a formal succession procedure contained in his church’s governing document, ‘The Mandate’.

God will reveal successor — Adeboye

Adeboye, who has led the RCCG since 1981, addressed speculation about his successor during the church’s ministers’ conference at the Redemption Camp, Ogun State, in August 2018.

At the time, reports had circulated that he might hand over the leadership of the church to Oyedepo, Pastor Joe Olaiya or one of his sons.

According to Tribune in 2017, the RCCG leader dismissed the speculation, saying he had not received any revelation from God about who would succeed him.

He explained that, based on his understanding of biblical succession, God would inform him before revealing the person to others.

“Elijah knew before Elisha knew that he would succeed him; Moses also knew that Joshua would succeed him and likewise Eli knew before Samuel and not the other way round and therefore, God will tell me first before any other person about who will become my successor as a general overseer,” he said.

Adeboye also addressed claims that Oyedepo, Pastor Joe Olaiya or one of his sons would take over from him.

“Some even said I would hand over to Bishop David Oyedepo, some said to Pastor Joe Olaiya because the two are very close to me and they do minister during our major events such as the conventions, Holy Ghost congresses and yet another quarter said one of my sons is the one succeeding me,” he said.

He urged members and the public to stop spreading speculation about the matter, insisting that no successor had been revealed to him.

“So, let me say it loud and clear again that God has not told me who my successor is.

“What God had told me several years ago was that He would tell me who my successor would be at least five years before I would be called home, and as at this now, He had not told me anything as regards that, which means He is not ready to call me home in the next five years,” Adeboye said.

He also acknowledged the demands that would come with leading the church, saying whoever eventually succeeded him would have to make significant sacrifices.

“Because I know how many hours I pray a day and I can count how many days I eat in a year too.

“So, whoever that will take over from me, such person will have to surpass me and God will have mercy on the person,” he said.

Adeboye’s comments followed an earlier leadership transition in January 2017, when he stepped down as General Overseer of the RCCG in Nigeria and Pastor Joseph Obayemi was appointed National Overseer. Adeboye, however, retained his position as General Overseer of RCCG Worldwide.

Church is not family inheritance — Kumuyi

Kumuyi has also repeatedly rejected the idea that leadership of the Deeper Christian Life Ministry should be transferred to one of his children.

In June 2026, PUNCH Online reported that the cleric addressed speculation that one of his biological sons could eventually succeed him, stressing that the church was not his personal property to be handed over to a family member.

During a church service, he said the ministry belonged to Jesus Christ and that its next leader should emerge through divine direction.

“Our attention has been drawn to the fact that some people are saying, ‘Is he going to hand over the church to his son?’ It’s the church of the Lord Jesus Christ and nobody has the right to hand it over to his son,” he said.

Kumuyi also cited biblical figures, arguing that the early leaders of the Christian faith did not transfer the church to their biological children.

“Peter did not hand over the church to his son. Paul did not have any son or wife, but Paul did not hand over the church to his son. And John did not hand over the church to his son,” he said.

The cleric had addressed the succession question earlier, in August 2025, when he expressed displeasure over what he described as subtle agitations within the church about his successor.

Speaking during a Bible study programme, PUNCH Online reported that Kumuyi questioned whether some members were already tired of his leadership and warned against attempts to pressure him into stepping aside.

“Are you tired of me?” he asked, prompting a response from members of the congregation.

The cleric was particularly concerned about comments by an unnamed young preacher who, according to him, had repeatedly said there was no leadership without a successor.

“The brother who taught the other Tuesday kept saying there is no leadership without a successor.

“He said it twice. I felt that was too much, more so since your father is still here. God will give a successor at His own time,” Kumuyi said.

He maintained that members should not attempt to dictate when he should leave office, adding that the issue of his successor was in God’s hands.

“You are not going to fight your shepherd, your pilot. I reject any member of the church, whether you are a worker, choir member, or security person, who tries to control me,” he said.

He further warned against attempts to undermine his conviction.

“Don’t try to kill my conviction. It will only make me resist you so that when you get to the other side, you won’t say I did not tell you.

“I will keep on telling you that under your resistance until I go,” he added.

Kumuyi, however, acknowledged that the ministry would eventually have a successor, but insisted that the choice would not be dictated by pressure from members.

“When the time comes, God will choose a man after His own heart. Don’t try to drive me away, even though I know you cannot,” he said.

Winners’ Chapel has succession procedure — Oyedepo

Unlike Adeboye and Kumuyi, who have publicly emphasised divine direction in the choice of their successors, Oyedepo has spoken about a formal procedure for leadership transition in the Living Faith Church Worldwide.

In a sermon reported by Daily Times Nigeria in June 2020, the bishop said the succession process was contained in The Mandate, a document he described as the church’s governing guide.

According to him, the procedure requires the presiding bishop to submit three names to the Council of Priests upon attaining the age of 70.

The candidates are then examined by the church’s secretariat to determine whether they meet the required qualifications.

Oyedepo explained that if none of the nominees qualified, the names would be returned to the presiding bishop, who would have another opportunity to submit a fresh list. If the second submission also failed, the Council of Priests would take over the process, with the Board ratifying the eventual decision.

He described the arrangement as a defined process designed to prevent uncertainty when a church leader leaves office.

“When you are 70 as leader of this Church, you present 3 names to the Council of Priests for them to see if any of them is qualified by the Secretariat. If they are not qualified, throw it back to you, you have one more chance to make another presentation and after that, you are out of it. The Council takes it, the Board goes to rectify. It is a process, not that somebody dies and then we don’t know what to do,” he said.

The cleric also said the procedure was documented and available to members of the church.

“If you read the book ‘The Mandate’ you will see the succession procedure of apostleship in this ministry. Open, it’s in the hand of everybody,” he said.

Oyedepo further explained that the transition was expected to take place within a specified period, saying the process was designed to ensure that the church would not be left without a clear leadership direction.

He also warned against treating churches as personal or family property, arguing that such an approach undermined the purpose of the ministry.

“If you want to leave a church as an inheritance for your family, the Church has died,” he said.

He reinforced the position by warning that a church should not become an enterprise owned by its founder.

“If church becomes your personal property, you have lost it, if it becomes a family property, it is dead,” Oyedepo said.

He also cautioned founders against compelling their children to take up ministry roles, saying they should be allowed to pursue the paths for which they were suited.

“Don’t call your sons and daughters to ministry, you may frustrate their destiny, don’t,” he said.

Although the three clerics have expressed their views in different ways and at different times, their public comments indicate opposition to the automatic transfer of church leadership to biological children.

Adeboye has maintained that God would reveal his successor to him at the appropriate time, while Kumuyi has insisted that the church belongs to Jesus Christ and should not be treated as family property.

Oyedepo, meanwhile, has outlined a succession process involving the submission and assessment of nominees by designated church bodies, while warning against turning a ministry into a family inheritance.

Their positions highlight different approaches to succession planning in large religious organisations: divine revelation, the rejection of hereditary leadership and a documented institutional procedure.

In the case of the Fountain of Life Church, the trustees who announced Jimmy Odukoya’s removal maintain that their decision stands, while the pastor and his supporters insist that the process was not constitutionally valid. Both sides have presented competing claims about who has the authority to determine the church’s leadership.

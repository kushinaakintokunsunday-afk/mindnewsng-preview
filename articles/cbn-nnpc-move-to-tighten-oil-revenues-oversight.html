CBN, NNPC move to tighten oil revenues oversight

By Punch Punch 5d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Central Bank of Nigeria and the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited have signed a Memorandum of Understanding to strengthen oversight of oil and gas

The agreement, signed on Wednesday at the CBN headquarters in Abuja, will also facilitate energy financing, automate payment systems and strengthen regulatory compliance between the two institutions.

Speaking at the ceremony, CBN Governor, Olayemi Cardoso, said the partnership would improve the management and settlement of oil proceeds, which remain critical to Nigeria’s fiscal revenue and foreign exchange stability.

“As custodian of NNPC Limited’s key operational foreign currency accounts, the Central Bank of Nigeria plays a critical role in ensuring the timely, secure and transparent settlement of oil and gas proceeds into the Federation Account and other designated government accounts,” Cardoso said.

He explained that predictable oil revenue inflows were essential to maintaining monetary stability, particularly during periods of economic volatility.

“Our continued collaboration in efficient account management, funds transfer and liquidity support directly contributes to macroeconomic stability by ensuring the availability of revenue to achieve fiscal mandates and predictable inflows into the foreign exchange markets,” he added.

Cardoso disclosed that the agreement would cover the monetisation of hydrocarbon assets, faster operational approvals, payment automation, cybersecurity, regulatory alignment and information sharing.

He also announced plans to attract international financial institutions to support Nigeria’s gas development through appropriate financing arrangements.

“The CBN is well positioned to collaborate with NNPC Limited by exercising its convening power to attract global institutions with financial structures that can help develop Nigeria’s vast natural gas resources,” he said.

The governor clarified that the agreement formalised an existing relationship, noting that the CBN, NNPC and other oil-sector institutions had been meeting regularly to resolve operational challenges.

In her welcome remarks, the CBN Deputy Governor, Operations, Emem Usoro, disclosed that Nigeria’s gross external reserves had exceeded $55bn, supported by higher oil production and sustained diaspora remittances.

“Today, while our gross external reserves exceed $55bn, supported by higher oil production and sustained diaspora remittances, we are confident that this MoU will help preserve and strengthen our reserves through improved export earnings and foreign exchange inflows,” she said.

Usoro explained that the agreement would establish clearer governance arrangements for international trade, energy financing, liquidity support and risk management.

The Group Chief Executive Officer of NNPC Limited, Bayo Ojulari, said the partnership would strengthen financial discipline and help the company raise capital using its assets and balance sheet.

Ojulari also revealed that NNPC faced severe financial difficulties when he assumed office in April 2025, including weak liquidity, troubled projects, substantial loans and refinery challenges.

“At the starting point, NNPC was in a very challenging situation. Our liquidity position was extremely weak, and the majority of our projects were heavily challenged,” he disclosed.

He explained that management subsequently reviewed proposed asset divestments and existing loans to address the company’s financial difficulties.

According to him, the agreement will establish joint steering and working committees to monitor implementation against agreed performance indicators.

“We are here not just for the optics. We are here to build a working partnership that delivers results,” Ojulari said.

Also speaking, NNPC’s Group Chief Financial Officer, Adedapo Segun, said the agreement would replace the previous practice of handling financial issues through irregular, transaction-specific arrangements.

“For too long, NNPC Limited and the CBN have cooperated on an ad hoc basis, transaction by transaction and crisis by crisis. That model will no longer serve Nigeria,” he said.

Segun explained that improved coordination would help attract the investment needed to increase Nigeria’s crude oil production from 1.8 million barrels per day to three million barrels per day.

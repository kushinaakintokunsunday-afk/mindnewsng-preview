Bauchi 2027: Leko defends wealth, dismisses allegations over properties outside state

From Paul Orude, Bauchi

The All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate for Bogoro, Dass and Tafawa Balewa Federal Constituency, Hon Jafaru Gambo Leko, has dismissed allegations concerning his acquisition of properties outside Bauchi State, insisting that he has legitimate sources of income and an unblemished record.

Leko, who was elected to the House of Representatives in 2023 and is seeking re-election in 2027, said he was not intimidated by criticism of his personal wealth, and challenged those making the allegations to establish their claims.

He spoke on Saturday during the ongoing interview series organised by the Correspondents’ Chapel of the Nigeria Union of Journalists (NUJ), Bauchi State, where he also highlighted some of his legislative interventions and national policy initiatives.

Responding to allegations that he had acquired properties outside Bauchi State while attracting projects to other states, the lawmaker said he had nothing to hide, citing his 23 years of service as a civil servant as part of his background.

“Let him go and buy his own house. Is it his money? If it is alleged, let him go and look for money. I am not a thief. I have a record. I have an antecedent. I worked for 23 years,” Leko said.

He added that he was not afraid of anyone questioning his wealth, arguing that individuals should not begrudge others their financial achievements.

“If God has blessed me, even if you give me money to buy a voucher, I will buy a voucher and nobody can stop that,” he said.

Beyond defending his personal wealth, Leko used the interview to highlight what he described as his contributions to national economic policy through legislative motions, particularly on Nigeria’s trade and financial relationship with China.

He claimed that, shortly after his inauguration into the National Assembly in 2023, he moved a motion advocating the adoption of the Chinese yuan as a supplementary currency for Nigeria’s foreign exchange reserves and trade settlements.

According to him, the initiative followed research he conducted during a legislative visit to Rwanda, where he studied measures adopted by some African countries to strengthen their economies.

Leko said he observed that countries such as Rwanda and Tanzania were using the Chinese currency in some trade transactions and believed that Nigeria could benefit from a similar arrangement, given its extensive commercial relationship with China.

“When I went there, I was not only sitting down; I browsed through a lot of things in Rwanda. I saw what they were doing to improve their economy,” he said.

He claimed that he subsequently presented the motion on the floor of the House of Representatives and that the Federal Government, in collaboration with the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), adopted the yuan in December 2024 as an additional reserve and trade settlement currency alongside the United States dollar.

The lawmaker further attributed the relative stability of the exchange rate after the adoption to the inclusion of the yuan in Nigeria’s official reserves.

Leko also cited another motion concerning six houses reportedly forfeited by a former governor of the Central Bank of Nigeria, arguing that such properties should be deployed for the benefit of ordinary Nigerians.

He said his position was informed by the constitutional responsibility of government to promote citizens’ welfare, particularly in the areas of shelter, food security and improved living conditions.

Referencing Section 16(2)(d) of the 1999 Constitution, Leko argued that government at all levels had an obligation to ensure the welfare of Nigerians.

He said he therefore advocated that the forfeited houses should be put to public use rather than left idle.

The lawmaker’s comments come as political activities gradually gather momentum ahead of the 2027 general elections, in which he will seek another mandate to represent Bogoro, Dass and Tafawa Balewa Federal Constituency.

His defence of his wealth and presentation of his legislative record formed part of a broader engagement with journalists on his public service and political ambitions.

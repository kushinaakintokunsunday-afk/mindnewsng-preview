Reps probe naira deductions from students’ fees

By Punch Punch 6d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The House of Representatives has resolved to investigate the cancellation of the 2026 admission exercise of the National Teachers’ Institute, Kaduna, by the Fed

The House also called for the suspension of further action on the cancellation of admissions into the institute’s Nigeria Certificate in Education and degree programmes pending a comprehensive review and the conclusion of the legislative process to clarify its academic mandate.

The resolutions followed the consideration of a motion of urgent public importance sponsored by Niger lawmaker, Mr Saidu Abdullahi on Wednesday.

Presenting his concerns, Abdullahi drew attention to a November 7, 2026 press release by the Federal Ministry of Education announcing the cancellation of 2026 admissions into NTI’s NCE and degree programmes, alongside the termination of all university affiliations and degree-awarding arrangements.

The lawmaker noted that the directive came while the National Teachers’ Institute Act (Amendment) Bill, 2025 which seeks to expressly empower the institute to award NCEs, undergraduate and postgraduate degrees, was still undergoing legislative consideration.

He recalled that the bill was transmitted to the Senate for concurrence on July 1, 2025, raising concerns over the timing of the ministerial directive and the uncertainty it had created for the institution and prospective students.

“The House notes that the development could expose thousands of applicants to financial losses, psychological distress and disruption of their educational aspirations.

“The House is concerned that the cancellation of programmes serving underserved communities and teachers in rural areas, particularly in Northern Nigeria, could undermine efforts to strengthen teacher education and lead to the underutilisation of public infrastructure developed over five decades,” Abdullahi said.

Beyond the admissions controversy, the motion raised questions about the financial management of the institute, particularly deductions made from gross student-fee receipts as operating surplus and subsequent remittances into the Federation Account.

“The House is concerned that the deductions had allegedly depleted NTI’s operational funds, leaving the institute struggling to pay facilitators, produce essential learning materials and conduct examinations effectively,” he added.

It also noted that the NTI Staff Union, in a statement issued on November 9, 2026, raised concerns about the implications of the directive for job security and called for a fair administrative process that would protect the interests of students already enrolled.

Following the abduction of the motion, the lawmakers urged the Federal Ministry of Education to stay further action on the cancellation of the 2026 NCE and degree admissions pending the outcome of a comprehensive review and the conclusion of the legislative process on the NTI Amendment Bill.

They also called on the Federal Ministry of Finance and the Office of the Accountant-General of the Federation to suspend deductions of operating surpluses from student-fee receipts and refund any unlawful deductions made from March 2026 to date.

The House said the intervention would enable the institute to meet its obligations to students and facilitators and sustain its academic operations.

In another resolution, the lawmakers urged the Senate to expedite consideration of the NTI Amendment Bill to establish a clear legal framework for the institute’s academic mandate.

To ensure accountability, the House mandated its Committees on Federal Colleges of Education and Finance to investigate the legality and implications of the Education Ministry’s directive.

The committees are to engage the Federal Ministry of Education, National Universities Commission, National Commission for Colleges of Education, Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board and NTI management as part of the investigation.

They are also to establish the legal basis for treating student-fee receipts as operating surplus, examine the deductions and recommend measures to protect the institute’s operational funds.

The committees are expected to submit their findings within four weeks for further legislative action, while the Committee on Legislative Compliance was mandated to monitor the implementation of the resolutions.

Established to promote teacher education and professional development through distance learning and collaboration with tertiary institutions, NTI has played an important role in expanding access to teacher training, particularly for serving teachers and learners in communities with limited access to conventional institutions.

The controversy has raised wider questions about the institute’s statutory powers, the authority of the executive to discontinue its academic programmes while an amendment to its enabling law is before the National Assembly, and the financial arrangements governing the institution’s operations.

The outcome of the House investigation is expected to clarify these issues and determine the implications of the admissions cancellation for prospective students, existing learners, staff and the future of teacher education through the institute.

In another development, a motion seeking the cancellation of outstanding loans obtained by Nigerian farmers under government-backed agricultural intervention programmes has divided members of the House of Representatives, with lawmakers disagreeing over whether debt forgiveness would offer relief to struggling farmers or reward beneficiaries who diverted the funds.

The motion, moved on Tuesday by the member representing Yamaltu/Deba Federal Constituency, Gombe State, Inuwa Garba, sought a waiver of outstanding agricultural loans, citing insecurity, climate-related disasters and prevailing economic hardship that have undermined farmers’ ability to repay their debts.

Garba argued that many beneficiaries were unable to meet their repayment obligations because circumstances beyond their control had severely affected their farming activities, investments and incomes.

Over the years, the Federal Government, through the Central Bank of Nigeria and other financial institutions, introduced several agricultural financing initiatives, including the Anchor Borrowers’ Programme, the Commercial Agriculture Credit Scheme and NIRSAL-supported facilities, to boost food production, empower farmers and strengthen national food security.

Leading the debate on the motion, Garba said, “The House is concerned that widespread insecurity, banditry, insurgency, farmer-herder conflicts, flooding, drought, climate change and other natural and human-induced disasters like soil degradation, poor soil fertility, poor irrigation infrastructure and inadequate veterinary and disease control services have severely disrupted agricultural activities, resulting in substantial crop failures, food insufficiency, loss of investments and diminished farm incomes.

“The House is concerned that many beneficiaries of government-sponsored agricultural loans have been unable to meet their repayment obligations, not necessarily due to unwillingness to repay, but because circumstances beyond their control have substantially undermined their productive capacity and financial stability.

“We are worried that the continued accumulation and enforcement of outstanding agricultural debts, including principal, interest and penalties, could push already distressed farmers into deeper poverty, expose them to asset seizures and discourage their participation in future agriculturAl activities.”

He further argued that compelling financially distressed farmers to repay the loans under prevailing economic conditions could undermine the objectives of the intervention programmes.

According to him, such pressure could worsen food insecurity, unemployment and poverty in rural communities rather than strengthen agricultural production.

However, opposing the proposed blanket waiver, the member representing Ede South/Ede North/Ejigbo/Egbedore Federal Constituency, Osun State, Bamidele Salam, said an audit report by the Auditor-General for the Federation before the House had exposed widespread abuse of the agricultural financing schemes.

Salam alleged that some beneficiaries who accessed more than N1tn under the Anchor Borrowers’ Programme diverted the funds into businesses and investments unrelated to agriculture, including real estate acquisitions.

He called for a comprehensive investigation into the intervention programmes to establish how the loans were utilised and identify beneficiaries who genuinely invested the funds in farming.

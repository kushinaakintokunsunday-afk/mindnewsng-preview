Wike tasks APC campaign council to stop ADC, NDC from winning 25% FCT votes

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 16 Nov 2026 5 min read

From Juliana Taiwo-Obalonye, Abuja

The Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Nyesom Wike, has challenged the All Progressives Congress (APC) Presidential Campaign Council and the Rainbow Coalition to ensure that opposition parties, particularly the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), fail to secure 25 per cent of the votes in the territory in the January 16, 2027 general election.

Wike, who is the coordinator of the FCT Presidential Campaign Council, gave the charge on Monday during the inauguration of the APC Presidential Campaign Council and Rainbow Coalition at the Bola Ahmed Tinubu International Conference Centre, Abuja.

He urged members of the campaign council to intensify grassroots mobilisation across the six area councils, insisting that the achievements of President Bola Tinubu’s administration provided sufficient evidence to convince voters to support the APC.

The minister, however, cautioned party members against taking electoral victory for granted, stressing that they must demonstrate the administration’s achievements to residents rather than rely on political rhetoric.

“You don’t need to go and see a prophet. You don’t need to go and see any doctor or anybody to tell you what to do. It is obvious; it is factual. Look at what Mr President has done for the people of the FCT. These are the tools you need to campaign,” he said.

Wike argued that Tinubu had demonstrated an unprecedented commitment to the development and political inclusion of the FCT, particularly through institutional reforms and infrastructure projects.

He said that, 50 years after the creation of Abuja in 1976, Tinubu had become the first president to ensure that the FCT was represented at the Federal Executive Council, giving the territory a voice in national decision-making.

He also listed the establishment of the FCT Civil Service Commission, the appointment of a Head of Service and the elevation of FCT civil servants to permanent secretary positions as major achievements of the administration.

The minister further cited the creation of the Mandate Secretariats for Women Affairs and Youth Development, saying they had provided opportunities for women and young people to participate more actively in governance.

On financial autonomy, Wike said the removal of the FCT from the Treasury Single Account arrangement had given the territory greater financial independence and enabled it to access loans for critical infrastructure development.

He challenged opposition politicians to compare the infrastructure projects executed under previous administrations with those delivered since Tinubu assumed office in 2023.

Wike particularly highlighted the administration’s record in the issuance of Certificates of Occupancy (C of O), claiming that previous administrations between 1999 and 2023 had issued only about 7,000 to 8,000 certificates, compared with more than 20,000 issued by the Tinubu administration within three years.

“How can governments from 1999 to 2023 sign only about 8,000 C of Os, while the government of 2023 to 2026, in three years, has signed over 20,000? How?” he queried.

He urged campaign council members to take the administration’s record to communities, wards and polling units, particularly in satellite towns, where residents could assess the impact of government projects.

Earlier, the FCT APC Chairman, Ishaku Yamawo, expressed confidence that the party would secure an overwhelming victory for Tinubu and its National Assembly candidates in the forthcoming elections.

Yamawo described the infrastructure transformation under Wike as a major campaign advantage, saying the projects had strengthened public confidence in the APC-led administration.

“Your projects have already become our strongest campaign message. The goodwill and confidence you have earned among our people, both indigenes and residents of the FCT, are exceptional,” he told the minister.

He assured the party leadership that the APC and the Rainbow Coalition were fully mobilised to deliver victory on January 16, 2027.

Yamawo also described the political cooperation among stakeholders in the FCT as unprecedented, urging members of the campaign council to sustain the unity and take the party’s message to every ward and polling unit.

He said the inauguration should serve as a call to greater responsibility, adding that the campaign must be inclusive, disciplined and firmly connected to the grassroots.

Also speaking, the FCT PDP Chairman, Ismaila Mohammed Dogara, expressed confidence that political leaders and stakeholders in the territory would mobilise support for Tinubu and other endorsed candidates, even in Wike’s absence.

Dogara commended the minister for building a political structure capable of sustaining mobilisation and coordinating electoral activities without his physical presence.

He described the cooperation between APC and PDP leaders under the Rainbow Coalition as a significant political development, expressing confidence that the alliance would translate into electoral victory.

“Even if you are not in the FCT, our executive chairman does not allow any space, not even for one minute, before taking it from where you stopped. That shows that on the faithful day of the election, if the Honourable Minister is not in the FCT, those leaders are ready and capable of delivering Mr President, Senator Philip Aduda, Tanimu and others,” Dogara said.

He expressed optimism that members of the coalition would return to the conference centre on January 17, a day after the election, to celebrate their anticipated victory.

“The big day is January 16, and the return day is January 17. By the special grace of God, we are going to return to this hall to celebrate,” he declared.

NEC approves additional N130bn for Police training, raises funding to N230bn

The National Economic Council (NEC) has approved an additional N130 billion for the rehabilitation of police training institutions across Nigeria, bringing the total funding for the programme to N230 billion.

The additional allocation is expected to speed up rehabilitation work, upgrade training facilities and prepare police institutions for the proposed recruitment of 50,000 personnel.

The approval followed a presentation by the NEC Ad-Hoc Committee on the rehabilitation of police training institutions, chaired by Enugu State Governor, Peter Mbah.

The committee informed the council that rehabilitation work was ongoing at 13 locations nationwide, with about 30 per cent of the projects completed.

NEC had earlier approved N100 billion for the programme after the committee was established in October 2025 on the recommendation of President Bola Tinubu.

According to a statement issued on Monday by Stanley Nkwocha, Senior Special Assistant to the President on Media and Communications in the Office of the Vice-President, the new funding would strengthen the capacity of the institutions to handle increased recruitment and training demands.

The statement said the intervention would also support preparations for the recruitment of 50,000 police personnel and improve the country’s security training infrastructure.

The approval was among the major decisions reached at the council’s 162nd meeting on Monday, where members also endorsed two development projects expected to receive a combined $850 million in proposed World Bank financing.

The projects are the $350 million Accelerating Resilience through Investment Systems and Engagement (ARISE-Nigeria) Project and the $500 million State-Led Early Childhood Development Programme.

The ARISE-Nigeria Project is a six-year initiative designed to strengthen Nigeria’s capacity to prepare for and respond to natural disasters, including floods, droughts and fires.

According to the statement, the programme will support the development of emergency operations centres, early warning systems, weather monitoring and disaster response financing at federal and state levels.

Between five and 10 states are expected to participate in the initial phase, with selection based on their exposure to disaster risks, institutional readiness and commitment to emergency preparedness.

The second project is designed to improve access to healthcare, nutrition, early learning and protection services for children across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory.

It will establish early childhood development hubs to connect primary healthcare facilities with childcare, preschool education and other essential social services, particularly in underserved communities.

The proposed $500 million World Bank financing for the programme is expected to be complemented by a potential $30 million grant from the Global Financing Facility.

On the economy, the Minister of Finance and Coordinating Minister of the Economy, Taiwo Oyedele, presented an update showing that Nigeria’s real Gross Domestic Product grew by 4.43 per cent in the second quarter of 2026, compared with 4.23 per cent in the corresponding period of 2025.

The report also indicated that headline inflation declined for the fifth consecutive month to 14.06 per cent in October, while the country’s external reserves stood at $54.87 billion as of November 12, 2026.

Nigeria’s trade surplus also increased from N10.2 trillion in the first half of 2025 to N20.1 trillion in the corresponding period of 2026.

Similarly, net revenue distributed through the Federation Account Allocation Committee rose by 44 per cent, from N15.2 trillion in 2024 to N21.9 trillion in 2025.

Despite the improvements in key economic indicators, Oyedele noted that households and businesses were yet to experience sufficient relief, particularly in food prices, electricity costs, access to affordable credit and employment.

The statement added that food inflation stood at 19.13 per cent in October, while high lending rates and energy costs continued to place pressure on businesses and household incomes.

The council also called for stronger cooperation between the National Identity Management Commission and state governments to accelerate national identity registration and the integration of the National Identification Number into public services.

On pension reforms, NEC approved a proposal presented by the Director-General of the National Pension Commission, Omolola Oloworaran, titled “Securing the Retirement of Nigerian Workers While Strengthening State Finances.”

The council urged state governors to establish timelines for implementing the Contributory Pension Scheme and directed their administrative and finance teams to work with PenCom on the necessary arrangements.

Earlier, Vice-President Kashim Shettima, who chairs the council, said the success of the Tinubu administration’s economic reforms would depend on the development of strong institutions capable of sustaining their benefits.

Shettima called on federal ministries and state governments to work together in addressing practical challenges, while ensuring that the impact of government policies on citizens remained a priority.

He also urged states to learn from successful practices in other jurisdictions while taking into account differences in their capacities and circumstances.

Speaking about the ARISE-Nigeria Project, the Vice-President said the initiative was intended to reduce the economic and social damage caused by disasters, particularly to families, businesses and public services.

He stressed that communities affected by development interventions should also have opportunities to contribute to the planning and implementation of such projects.

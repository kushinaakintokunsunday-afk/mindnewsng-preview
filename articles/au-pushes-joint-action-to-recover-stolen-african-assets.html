AU pushes joint action to recover stolen African assets

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The African Union is seeking stronger cooperation among member states to improve the recovery of stolen assets and ensure that proceeds recovered from corruptio

The Director of Special Services at the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission, Hassan Mohamed, disclosed this in a statement on Sunday ahead of an African asset recovery meeting.

Mohamed, who is also the Chairman of the Local Organising Committee for the event, said asset recovery had become an integral part of the global fight against corruption, requiring countries to harmonise their approaches and strengthen collaboration.

He said the meeting would provide an opportunity for African countries to share experiences, discuss recovery efforts and develop common strategies for tackling corruption.

“All over the world, asset recovery has become an integral part of the fight against corruption.

“It is to be sure, to ensure that what was recovered is put to use for the benefit of the generality of the masses. That is an aspect of the fight against corruption, which the African Union is advancing,” he said.

Mohamed explained that the initiative was not necessarily driven by concerns over the handling of recovered assets but by the need to ensure that such assets served the public interest.

He said the meeting would also enable participating countries to examine practical approaches to asset recovery and strengthen their collective response to the challenges associated with the process.

“The purpose of the programme is to harmonise the fight against corruption, especially now that asset recovery has become an integral part of what we do, of what anti-corruption agencies do all over the world.

“So here is to synergise, synthesise, come up with strategies, common ways to deal with this menace. Essentially, that’s what we’re doing,” he said.

The ICPC official also pledged that the commission would continue to disclose information about assets recovered by the agency, including their status, to promote transparency and accountability.

“Definitely, ICPC will do that. Nothing is hidden. It’s a public document. Anything recovered is for the public. And so, ICPC will definitely declare anything and whatever has been recovered,” he said.

He, however, declined to provide figures on assets recovered in Nigeria ahead of the meeting, saying he did not have the details available at the time of the briefing.

Mohamed said participating delegates would also have the opportunity to visit locations where some recovered assets were being put to use in Nigeria.

He added that the meeting would bring together representatives from across the continent, with about 52 to 54 African countries expected to participate.

Mohammed said the forum’s maiden General Assembly, scheduled to hold in Abuja from November 17 to 20, would provide an opportunity for African countries to develop practical strategies for addressing the challenges confronting asset recovery institutions.

He said participants would review progress in implementing the Common African Position on Asset Recovery, strengthen institutional collaboration and adopt governance instruments and strategic action plans.

He added that the meeting would also examine gaps in national and continental frameworks and identify ways of improving cooperation in the recovery and repatriation of illicitly acquired assets.

NUPRC opens bidding for 40 oil, gas blocks

By Punch Punch 3d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission has opened its portal for the 2026 oil and gas licensing round, offering 40 blocks to investors as the Fed

The registration portal, br2026.gov.ng, went live on Friday, 20 November, with interested companies given until 15 January, 2027, to complete their registration and prequalification processes.

The 40 blocks are spread across three categories, comprising 17 onshore blocks, 14 shallow water blocks and nine deep offshore blocks.

The Commission disclosed this in a statement issued by its Head of Corporate Communications and Media, Eniola Akinkuotu, on Saturday, urging prospective investors to visit the portal to access the full list of available blocks, bidding guidelines and frequently asked questions.

It said the website had been designed to provide users with a seamless experience throughout the licensing exercise.

The statement read, “The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission wishes to inform the general public that the 2026 licensing round portal is now live. On offer are 40 oil and gas blocks across three terrains. The blocks are grouped as follows: Onshore (17), shallow water (14) and deep offshore (9). Interested bidders are urged to visit the site to view the full list of the 40 blocks, as well as the guidelines and frequently asked questions.”

The 2026 licensing round will be conducted in five stages, beginning with registration and prequalification, followed by access to data, submission of technical and commercial bids, technical evaluation and a commercial conference.

The opening of the portal marks the transition from the announcement and publicity phase of the exercise to the formal participation of prospective investors.

According to the commission, the next major activity will be the pre-bid conference scheduled to be held in Lagos on 16 December, 2026. The conference is expected to provide prospective bidders with an opportunity to obtain further information about the available assets, the bidding process and the requirements for participation.

The commission said the licensing round was first announced by its Chief Executive, Oritsemeyiwa Eyesan, on 6 October, 2026, following approval by President Bola Tinubu, who is also the Minister of Petroleum Resources.

It added that the announcement was accompanied by statutory advertisements in local and international newspapers in accordance with the provisions of the Petroleum Industry Act.

The commission said, “Registration on the portal, br2026.gov.ng, began on November 20, 2026 and will close on January 15, 2027.”

It further stated that, “On offer are 40 oil and gas blocks across three terrains. The blocks are grouped as follows: Onshore (17), shallow water (14) and deep offshore (9).”

The latest exercise comes as Nigeria seeks to increase crude oil and gas production, attract investment into its petroleum industry and improve the country’s competitiveness in the global energy market.

The licensing round also follows the 2025 oil and gas licensing exercise, which offered 50 blocks and attracted interest from 143 companies. The exercise recorded 200 bids, with 31 companies emerging as successful bidders for 37 blocks, while 13 blocks did not receive bids.

The 2026 round provides another opportunity for indigenous and international oil companies to secure petroleum assets and expand exploration and development activities in Nigeria.

The availability of blocks across onshore, shallow water and deep offshore terrains is expected to provide investment opportunities for companies with different technical and financial capacities.

The exercise is particularly significant as Nigeria continues to grapple with production challenges, including crude oil theft, pipeline vandalism, underinvestment and declining output from some mature oilfields.

The government has repeatedly identified increased exploration, field development and private-sector investment as critical to achieving its production ambitions and improving revenue from the petroleum industry.

WPRF 2026: Young PR professionals urged to build credibility through results, not age

From Juliana Taiwo-Obalonye, Abuja

Young public relations professionals have been challenged to build reputations anchored on integrity, competence and measurable results, rather than rely on age, titles or self-proclaimed expertise to earn credibility.

The Chief Executive Officer of Dynasty Africa, Dayo Adedeji, gave the charge at the Global Youth Public Relations Summit, held as part of the World Public Relations Forum (WPRF) 2026 in Abuja, where he urged young Africans to abandon limiting perceptions and develop the capacity to build globally competitive businesses and influence.

Speaking on the theme “Be Someone Worth Believing”, Adedeji said credibility is established not by confidence or claims of excellence but by tangible achievements that demonstrate an individual’s value.

“If you want people to believe in you, your results do the explaining,” he said.

Drawing from his entrepreneurial journey, the Forbes Africa 30 Under 30 honouree explained how Dynasty Africa evolved from an influencer marketing agency five years ago into a full-service 360-degree marketing agency with operations and engagements across several African countries.

He said the company was recognised by Google in its second year as one of the African agencies with the largest influencer networks, before expanding into markets including Senegal, Côte d’Ivoire, Kenya, South Africa, Mozambique, Angola and Benin Republic.

According to him, the agency subsequently recorded more than 100 million impressions for its partners and clients and won a global Cannes Dolphins Award, marking what he described as a year of international recognition.

Adedeji said the transformation began when he stopped asking why anyone should believe in him and instead challenged himself with the question, “Why not me?”

He urged young professionals to adopt the same mindset, insisting that age should not determine the scale of ambition or achievement.

“There is no universally accepted age for great things. Success cannot be traced to age,” he said, citing the example of Colonel Harland Sanders, who founded the Kentucky Fried Chicken business in his 60s.

The marketing strategist also challenged young Africans to discard the perception that businesses and achievements originating from the continent are necessarily local, arguing that global relevance is driven more by ambition, quality and standards than by geographical location.

He cited the international success of African music and culture, Aliko Dangote’s business expansion and Nigeria’s hosting of the World Public Relations Forum for the first time as evidence of the continent’s growing global influence.

Adedeji said young professionals must learn to recognise opportunities within their immediate environments and build solutions capable of competing internationally.

He further stressed that personal perception influences ambition, decisions and, ultimately, the results people produce.

“Your sight shapes everything: your experience, your belief, your actions and your understanding,” he said, explaining that people must learn to see possibilities where others see limitations.

Earlier, the Chairman of the Youth Work Team and Special Adviser to the Anambra State Governor on Youth Reforms, Dr Nelson Omenugha, said the future of responsible communication rested significantly on the shoulders of young professionals and students.

Welcoming participants to the summit, Omenugha said the future of public relations would not be determined by technology alone but by the values, ethics, competence, courage and character of practitioners.

He noted that the growing influence of artificial intelligence, digital disruption and misinformation required young communication professionals to move beyond being consumers of change to becoming responsible creators, innovators and leaders.

“Technology can amplify our voices, but only integrity can give these voices credibility,” he said.

Omenugha urged participants not to wait for opportunities to be handed to them but to develop the competence and capacity to shape the future of the profession.

“To the PR students and young professionals here today, do not wait for the future to give you a seat at the table. Build competence, character and capacity to shape the table,” he said.

He also commended the President of the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations (NIPR), Dr Ike Neliaku, for creating platforms to nurture emerging communication professionals.

He highlighted initiatives including the Young Professional Hub, the University of Communication and Leadership, unveiled during the forum, and a technology innovation challenge sponsored through the Global Alliance partnership with NIPR.

According to him, the initiatives represent long-term investments in building institutions, developing talent and creating opportunities for the next generation of communication practitioners.

The Global Youth Public Relations Summit brought together students, young professionals, industry leaders and communication practitioners from Nigeria and other countries to examine the future of responsible communication and prepare emerging professionals for leadership in the industry.

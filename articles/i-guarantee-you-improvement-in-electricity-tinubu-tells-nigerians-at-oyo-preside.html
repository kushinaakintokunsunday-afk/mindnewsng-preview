‘I Guarantee You Improvement In Electricity,’ Tinubu Tells Nigerians At Oyo Presidential Campaign Rally

By Channels TV Channels TV 3d ago 4 min read

President Bola Tinubu has assured Nigerians of improved electricity supply, promising to address the country’s persistent power challenges.

The President gave the assurance on Saturday during the All Progressives Congress (APC) campaign rally at Mapo Hall in Ibadan, Oyo State, ahead of the 2027 general elections.

“We will continue to bring industry, you will see, and I guarantee you an improvement in electricity. Joseph Tegbe (Minister of Power) is working really hard with his team. You voted overwhelmingly for APC last time; you must stay the course for the sake of progress, prosperity of our country, development for the children and our great-grandchildren. Our future children must benefit from the hope of Nigeria. We must give them the right education and invest in Agriculture,” he said.

The President also announced plans to revive the defunct Leyland automobile factory in Ibadan through an agreement with Chinese bus manufacturer Yutong.

“For Oyo State, we have signed an agreement with Yutong of China to reopen the old Leyland factory in Ibadan. We are reopening it to produce public transport and automobile assembly plant there,” he said.

The agreement, signed on November 19, 2026, involves the Federal Government, through the Presidential Initiative on Compressed Natural Gas and Electric Vehicles (Pi-CNG & EV), Yutong and its local partner, YP Automobile.

Electricity Challenges

Tinubu’s latest assurance comes amid growing frustration among Nigerians over inadequate electricity supply, with households and businesses grappling with prolonged outages in different parts of the country.

The shortages have renewed questions about the effectiveness of the Band A, B and C electricity tariff classification, which links customers’ payments to the minimum hours of electricity supply they are expected to receive.

Customers on Band A, who are entitled to a minimum of 20 hours of electricity daily, have also complained about inadequate supply.

Intense heat has compounded the situation, leaving many residents struggling to cope without electricity to run fans and air conditioners.

Several Nigerians, including figures like Kate Henshaw and Cute Abiola, have expressed frustration over persistent power outages, lamenting that households have become “mini-factories” forced to generate their own electricity.

Meanwhile, rising petrol and diesel prices have made generator use increasingly expensive, while the cost of installing solar panels, batteries and inverters remains beyond the reach of many households and small businesses.

Minister of Power Tegbe recently attributed the prolonged blackouts to planned maintenance at the Egbin Power Station, gas supply shortages and vandalism of transmission infrastructure.

Tinubu’s 2023 Electricity Promise

The President’s latest assurance has also brought renewed attention to his 2023 campaign promise to tackle Nigeria’s electricity challenges and end estimated billing.

During his campaign, Tinubu told members of the Organised Private Sector in Lagos that Nigerians should hold him accountable if he failed to deliver on the promise.

“Whichever way, by all means necessary, you will have electricity, and you will not pay for estimated bills anymore. A promise made will be a promise kept. If I don’t keep the promise and I come for a second term, don’t vote for me, unless I give you adequate reasons why I couldn’t deliver,” he said.

The pledge returned to public discussion this week following comments by former Lagos State Governor and former Minister of Works, Babatunde Fashola, during an interview on Channels Television’s Politics Today.

Fashola argued that Tinubu did not specifically promise Nigerians 24-hour electricity during the 2023 campaign, saying his remarks had been taken out of context.

He explained that the President’s statement contained a qualification that voters should not re-elect him if he failed to deliver, unless he provided adequate reasons for the failure.

Fashola also urged Nigerians to assess the administration’s performance based on their personal experiences with electricity supply and the progress made in ending estimated billing.

His comments, however, attracted criticism from opposition figures and the campaign organisation of Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde, who argued that the debate over the wording of Tinubu’s promise did not address the continuing power challenges facing Nigerians.

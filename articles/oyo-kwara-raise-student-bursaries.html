Oyo, Kwara raise student bursaries

By Punch Punch 6d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Oyo and Kwara State governments have increased financial support for tertiary institution students through higher bursary payments, with Oyo approving award

In Oyo State, the government has commenced the implementation of its 2025/2026 bursary scheme for final-year students of the state studying in tertiary institutions across Nigeria.

The screening exercise, coordinated by the Federation of Oyo State Students’ Union National, is underway for eligible students in universities, polytechnics, colleges of education and other specialised institutions.

The development was contained in a statement released by the Special Adviser (Media), Dr. Sulaimon Olanrewaju, and made available to journalists in Ibadan on Wednesday.

Speaking during the screening exercise at the Oyo State Ministry of Education, Science and Technology, the 10th National President of FOSSU, Distinguished Senator Yekinni Sodiq, said the bursary programme reflected Governor Seyi Makinde’s commitment to supporting students and easing the financial burden of higher education.

According to him, 5,000 final-year students across the country successfully registered for the scheme.

Sodiq explained that the programme was open to final-year students of Oyo State origin studying in accredited tertiary institutions, provided they met the stipulated requirements.

He urged beneficiaries to make judicious use of the funds, noting that the bursary would support their academic pursuits and assist students engaged in small-scale businesses and other productive ventures.

Also speaking, the Head of Government to the FOSSU National President, Hon. Kamil-Bello Umar-Farouk, commended Makinde for sustaining the bursary scheme since assuming office and expanding opportunities for Oyo State students.

Farouk disclosed that students enrolled in Nigeria Certificate in Education programmes, who were excluded from the previous edition, had been included following appeals to the governor.

“Last year, NCE students were not captured under the bursary scheme. We appealed to the Governor because they are also stakeholders in the education sector and deserving beneficiaries. We are grateful that His Excellency approved their inclusion this year, and they will now benefit from the programme,” he said.

He further disclosed that Makinde had approved an increase in the bursary awards following requests from student representatives.

According to him, while beneficiaries received a flat bursary of N100,000 in the previous edition, the revised structure provides different amounts across categories of students.

“NCE students will receive N100,000 each, while polytechnic students will receive N150,000. Students in universities and other tertiary institutions will benefit from N200,000 each. Medical and clinical students, law graduates in Law School, as well as aviation students, will receive N500,000 each,” Farouk stated.

He appreciated the Senior Special Assistant to the Governor on Student Affairs, Hon. Victor Olojede, for his efforts towards the implementation of the programme, and commended the FOSSU leadership for advancing the interests of Oyo State students.

“On behalf of Oyo State students across Nigeria and beyond, we express our profound appreciation to Governor Seyi Makinde for this intervention. We remain grateful for his unwavering commitment to the welfare and educational development of students in the state,” he added.

In Kwara State, Governor AbdulRahman AbdulRazaq has announced an increase in the annual bursary for students from N20,000 to N50,000.

The governor made the announcement on Wednesday during a meeting with the leadership of the National Association of Nigerian Students, Joint Campus Committee, at the Government House in Ilorin.

He said payment of the new amount would begin before the end of November.

In a 52-second video obtained by The PUNCH, AbdulRazaq said his administration had reconsidered the existing amount after further discussions on providing more financial support for students.

“The fund is just to support the students. We approved N20,000 in bursary for each student. We’ve had discussions among ourselves and resolved that we must give our students N50,000,” the governor said.

“Payment will start before the end of this month,” he added.

The increase represents a 150 per cent rise from the previous N20,000 allocation and brings the state’s bursary to ten times the N5,000 paid to students in 2019.

The announcement came on the same day that the National Association of Kwara State Students pledged the support of its more than 50,000 members for the governor, citing his administration’s interventions in education and student welfare.

The association’s National President, Muhammed Issa, disclosed this during a radio programme monitored by our correspondent, where he identified the increase in bursary payments and the provision of two new buses for the association as some of the administration’s notable interventions.

Issa, who appeared on the programme alongside the association’s Public Relations Officer, Muhammed Baba, and Senate President, Yahaya Oladimeji, said the students’ endorsement followed a recent gathering at the Kwara State House of Assembly.

According to him, students drawn from across the country unanimously passed a vote of confidence in AbdulRazaq in recognition of his administration’s contributions to their welfare.

“The increase in bursary from N5,000 in 2019 to N20,000 and the provision of two new buses for our association are some of the interventions we appreciate this administration for,” Issa said.

He, however, urged the government to extend more assistance to Kwara indigenes studying outside the state, particularly those in the northern and southern parts of the country, who he said were struggling to meet their tuition obligations.

“We want the government to do more for our students studying outside Kwara State, especially those in the northern and southern parts of the country, who are finding it difficult to meet their tuition obligations,” he said.

The NAKSS president also called for sustained investment in education, describing AbdulRazaq as a youth-friendly governor.

He insisted that the association’s position was based on its assessment of student welfare rather than political affiliation.

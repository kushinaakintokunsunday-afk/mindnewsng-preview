Sokoto gov’s aide advocates home-grown technology to fight insecurity

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Special Adviser on Security Matters to the Sokoto State Governor, Col. Ahmed Abdul Usman (retd.), has called on the Federal Government to harness the expert

Usman said the country could no longer rely solely on conventional security methods, stressing that emerging threats required innovative responses driven by technology and local expertise.

He spoke on Friday after receiving a Platinum Award from the Nigerian Association of Technologists in Engineering at its 42nd Conference of Technologists in Engineering and Annual General Meeting in Port Harcourt, Rivers State.

The conference, held from November 11 to 13, 2026, at the Obi Wali International Conference Centre, recognised his contributions to national development, particularly in security administration and the application of technology.

In his remarks, made available to ArewaPunch, the retired military officer said the recognition should encourage stronger partnerships between security agencies and professionals in the engineering and technology sectors.

He said such collaborations would help develop practical solutions tailored to Nigeria’s peculiar security challenges.

“Technology has become an indispensable tool in modern security operations. We must continually explore innovative ways of deploying technology to strengthen our security architecture and respond effectively to emerging threats,” he said.

Usman said Nigerian engineers and technologists possessed the expertise required to contribute meaningfully to national security, industrial growth and development.

“Nigeria must increasingly harness the expertise and ingenuity of its indigenous engineers and technologists to develop home-grown solutions capable of addressing our security, industrial and developmental challenges,” he added.

Describing the award as a call to greater responsibility, the governor’s aide said he considered the honour both an appreciation of his contributions and an encouragement to continue serving the country.

“I see this honour not only as an appreciation of my modest contributions to national development but also as a renewed call to greater service,” he said.

The conference was held under the theme, “Safety, Regulation and Enforcement in Engineering: A Panacea for Industrial and Technological Growth in Nigeria.”

Usman also emphasised the need for professionalism, accountability, effective regulation and compliance with safety standards in the deployment of technology.

The Rivers State Governor, Siminalayi Fubara, was represented at the event by his Chief of Staff, Mr Sunny Ewule.

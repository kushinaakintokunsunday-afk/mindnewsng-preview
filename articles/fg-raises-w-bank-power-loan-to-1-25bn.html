FG raises W’Bank power loan to $1.25bn

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government is seeking an additional $500m loan from the World Bank to expand its renewable energy programme, a move that would raise the bank’s tota

The proposed financing will add to the $750m previously approved by the World Bank for the Nigeria Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up project, known as DARES, which is being implemented by the Rural Electrification Agency.

According to a World Bank Project Information Document obtained by The PUNCH on Sunday, the additional $500m loan is scheduled for consideration by the bank’s Board of Executive Directors on 18 December, 2026, three years after the original financing was approved.

“This is a proposed Additional Financing in the amount of $500m for the Nigeria Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up Project. DARES was approved on December 14, 2023 and reached effectiveness on November 15, 2024. The Project Development Objective is to increase access to electricity services for households, public institutions, and micro, small and medium enterprises with private sector-led distributed renewable energy generation,” the document read.

The document, prepared on 13 November, 2026, showed that the government intends to expand electricity access to households, businesses, schools, healthcare facilities and underserved communities through solar-powered systems and mini-grids.

The bank explained that the additional financing would accelerate the deployment of renewable energy infrastructure, particularly in communities that remain disconnected from the national electricity grid.

It said, “The proposed AF will enable the scale-up and expansion of electricity services through mini grids and standalone solar systems, while sustaining the momentum of private sector-led access delivery.”

The fresh borrowing proposal comes amid persistent electricity supply challenges in Nigeria, where households and businesses continue to experience outages and depend heavily on petrol and diesel generators.

The World Bank estimated that 87.2 million Nigerians lacked electricity access in 2024, despite improvements in the national electrification rate over the preceding decade.

According to the report, electricity access increased from 52.5 per cent in 2015 to 61.2 per cent in 2024. However, population growth meant that the absolute number of people without electricity had barely declined.

The situation was worse in rural communities, where only 32.9 per cent of residents had access to electricity, leaving more than two-thirds without power.

The bank observed, “Nigeria continues to face a significant electricity access gap despite recent progress.”

It added that the existing DARES financing was designed to provide electricity connections to more than 18.8 million people, representing only about 22 per cent of the country’s electricity access deficit.

The report estimated that Nigeria would require approximately $23bn to achieve universal electricity access by 2030, with distributed renewable energy solutions potentially providing electricity to more than 60 million people at a lower cost than conventional alternatives.

The latest proposal follows the World Bank’s approval of the original $750m DARES financing on 14 December, 2023, during the administration of President Bola Tinubu.

The project became effective on 15 November, 2024, about 11 months after approval, with the objective of expanding electricity access through private sector-led renewable energy investments.

The existing financing comprises $600m in shorter-maturity loans from the International Development Association and another $150m in IDA credit.

Under the proposed expansion, the IDA credit component would increase from $150m to $650m, while the $600m shorter-maturity loan component would remain unchanged.

This would bring the total World Bank financing to $1.25bn if the additional request receives board approval.

An analysis of the financing arrangements showed that the overall DARES project cost would also increase from $2.06bn to $2.94bn, representing an additional $880.2m or 42.71 per cent.

The proposed increase comprises $500m from the World Bank, $116.2m from the French Development Agency and $264m in expected commercial financing.

Consequently, total commercial financing under the programme is projected to rise from $1.028bn to $1.292bn, while contributions from other development partners would bring total co-financing from those sources to $350m.

The financing package also includes $193.8m from the Japan International Cooperation Agency, $49.1m in trust fund resources contributed through a United States Department of Justice-supported fund and $40m in other development partner financing.

The World Bank’s Country Director for Nigeria, Taimur Samad, endorsed the latest project document on 13 November, 2026, while the appraisal date was listed as 23 October.

Meanwhile, an analysis of the World Bank’s transaction records for the existing project showed that $128.44m had been disbursed from the original $750m financing commitment, representing approximately 17.13 per cent.

The records, covering transactions up to 22 October 2026, indicated that the disbursements were recorded against one of the financing agreements supporting the project.

This implies that approximately $621.56m, representing 82.87 per cent of the original financing, had yet to be recorded as disbursed in the transaction data reviewed.

The original financing was divided into three commitments of $350m, $250m and $150m, approved on 14 December, 2023.

The position raises questions about the pace of financial implementation, particularly as the Federal Government seeks additional borrowing before the existing financing has been fully utilised.

However, the World Bank explained that access to some of the funds depended on the achievement of performance conditions, including regulatory reforms and institutional improvements.

According to the report, $300m had been unlocked after the government met two performance-based conditions.

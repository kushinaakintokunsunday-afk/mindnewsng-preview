Australia's 2-Year Training Visa (Subclass 407): Who Can Apply and What It Allows

Australia’s Training visa (subclass 407) allows approved applicants to stay in the country for up to two years to take part in structured, workplace-based training or professional development. Here is a plain-English guide to who can apply and what the visa actually allows.

Who can apply

  • You need an approved Australian sponsor: an Australian organisation must nominate you and be approved to sponsor under the 407 programme.
  • The visa is training-based, not a general work visa: your main purpose must be participating in an approved training or professional development activity — not simply filling a job vacancy.
  • Qualifications and experience matter: sponsors are generally expected to show that the training matches your qualifications, skills and career stage.
  • Age, health, character and insurance: standard requirements apply, including health checks, character requirements and adequate health insurance for your stay.

What the subclass 407 allows

  • Stay of up to two years, usually matched to the length of the approved training programme.
  • Work only as part of the approved training: most work rights are tied to the training activity, so the visa is not an open work permit.
  • Multiple entries while the visa is valid, so you can travel in and out of Australia during your programme.
  • Bringing some family members may be possible in certain cases, depending on the programme and rules in force.

Streams commonly referenced

The programme is generally described around streams such as workplace-based occupational training, structured professional development, and capacity-building options for overseas organisations. Exact wording and conditions should be checked directly with official sources.

What Nigerians should check before applying

  • Verify the sponsor is genuinely approved and the training plan is real — do not rely on unlicensed agents.
  • Confirm current requirements and fees on the Department of Home Affairs website, as programmes and procedures change over time.
  • Understand that the 407 is temporary and training-focused; it is not by itself a pathway guaranteeing permanent residency.

This article is general information, not immigration or legal advice. Always confirm current rules via official Australian government sources or a qualified, registered migration adviser.

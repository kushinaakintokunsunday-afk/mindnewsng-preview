2027: ‘Reject rice politics, don’t sell your votes for food’ — Ex-Anambra gov, Obiano warns Nigerians

Former Anambra State Governor, Willie Obiano, has urged Nigerians to reject what he described as “rice politics”, warning against allowing politicians to exploit poverty and hunger to secure electoral support.

Obiano said voters must look beyond the distribution of food items and carefully assess the character, competence and track records of candidates before electing them into public office in the 2027 general elections.

He also called on Nigerians to demand accountability from political leaders rather than celebrate the provision of basic necessities as exceptional achievements.

The former governor made the remarks while delivering the keynote address at the 11th Chinua Achebe Literary Festival and Memorial Lecture in Awka, Anambra State.

Speaking on the theme, “The Writer as a Nation-Builder: Achebe’s Literary Philosophy and the Quest for a Better Society,” Obiano urged Nigerians to embrace the legacy of the late literary icon, particularly his commitment to confronting injustice, questioning political authority and advocating responsible leadership.

He argued that Achebe did not write merely to entertain readers but used literature to expose injustice, challenge political failures, defend African identity and stimulate conversations about the future of society.

Obiano challenged Nigerian writers and intellectuals to speak out against corruption, insecurity, poor governance, historical distortion and other challenges confronting the country.

He also warned against allowing outsiders or individuals with ulterior motives to control the narration of Nigeria’s history and experiences.

Describing Achebe as an exceptional intellectual institution, the former governor said the literary icon gave Africans the confidence to tell their stories from their own perspectives while challenging misconceptions about the continent.

“Achebe remains a representative of a typical Igbo Man with great wisdom,” he said.

Obiano maintained that Achebe’s greatness went beyond his literary achievements, noting that his courage to question leadership failures and defend the dignity of African people distinguished him.

He cited Achebe’s globally acclaimed novel, Things Fall Apart, published in 1958, as one of the works that transformed international perceptions of African literature.

The former governor also recalled Achebe’s rejection of Nigeria’s national honour of Commander of the Order of the Federal Republic (CFR) on two occasions, in 2004 and 2011, describing the decisions as evidence of his commitment to principles over personal recognition.

According to Obiano, Achebe rejected the first honour under former President Olusegun Obasanjo because of concerns about political instability, lawlessness and the crisis in Anambra State.

He said the literary icon also declined the second offer under former President Goodluck Jonathan because he believed concerns about corruption, accountability and governance had not been adequately addressed.

Obiano further recalled the political crisis that engulfed Anambra between 2003 and 2004, including the abduction of then Governor Chris Ngige and the destruction of government facilities in Awka and other parts of the state.

He said Achebe’s actions demonstrated that patriotism required more than public declarations of love for one’s country, stressing that it also demanded the courage to question those entrusted with political authority.

The former governor also referenced Achebe’s 1983 book, The Trouble with Nigeria, particularly its identification of leadership failure as the country’s central problem.

He noted that issues highlighted by the late writer more than four decades ago, including corruption, tribalism, nepotism, religious intolerance, indiscipline and the celebration of mediocrity, continued to undermine Nigeria.

Obiano said the continued relevance of Achebe’s observations should concern Nigerians, particularly writers and intellectuals, who have a responsibility to examine the country’s challenges and contribute ideas capable of changing its direction.

He also advocated the restoration of history to Nigeria’s educational system and public discourse, warning that societies that neglect their past risk losing the knowledge needed to understand the present and prepare for the future.

He insisted that young Nigerians deserved accurate accounts of the country’s history, including difficult and contested periods such as the Nigerian Civil War, rather than distorted narratives, half-truths or deliberate silence.

Referencing Achebe’s famous observation that until the lions have their own historians, the history of the hunt will continue to glorify the hunter, Obiano urged Nigerians to take responsibility for documenting their experiences.

Returning to the 2027 elections, the former governor said politicians should be assessed based on their ability to provide security, quality education, employment opportunities, functional institutions and other basic necessities, rather than their distribution of food items.

He also questioned the tendency to celebrate the discharge of basic government responsibilities as extraordinary achievements, urging Nigerians to demand sustainable solutions to insecurity, poor infrastructure, unemployment, electricity challenges and rising living costs.

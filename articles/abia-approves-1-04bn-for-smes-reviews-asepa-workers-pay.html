Abia approves ₦1.04bn for SMEs, reviews ASEPA workers’ pay

By Steve Oko

UMUAHIA — Abia State Government has secured approval for ₦1.041 billion in intervention funding for small and medium-scale enterprises (SMEs), in a move aimed at expanding businesses, stimulating job creation and improving access to affordable credit.

Of the amount, ₦382.9 million has already been disbursed to 29 micro-enterprises and two small and medium-scale businesses, while another ₦206.7 million in approved applications is undergoing documentation ahead of disbursement.

The state government also announced a review of the remuneration of ad-hoc workers engaged by the Abia State Environmental Protection Agency (ASEPA), with the new payment structure taking effect from November 2026.

Commissioner for Information, Prince Okey Kanu, who disclosed the developments during the weekly briefing on the State Executive Council meeting at Government House, Umuahia, said the SME intervention was being implemented through the Bank of Industry (BOI).

Kanu, represented by the Special Adviser to the Governor on Media and Publicity, Mr Fredinald Ekeoma, said the initiative was part of Governor Alex Otti’s administration’s efforts to strengthen the state’s productive sector and create sustainable employment opportunities.

Providing further details, Commissioner for Industries and SMEs, Mazi Mike Akpara, said the outstanding approved facilities would be released once beneficiaries fulfilled the necessary conditions, including documentation and provision of guarantors and collateral, where applicable.

He stressed that access to the intervention was not restricted by political affiliation, insisting that any genuine business operating in Abia was eligible.

“Whether you belong to the Labour Party, PDP or APC, we are not concerned about that. Once you are doing business in Abia and can provide evidence of your business operations, you are eligible to benefit.

“SMEs are the engine of the economy, and this initiative is intended to encourage and strengthen them,” Akpara said.

He said interested businesses could access the programme through the Ministry of Industries and SMEs at the Nnamdi Azikiwe Secretariat, Umuahia, or the BOI office in Aba.

Akpara described the facility as a major relief for local entrepreneurs, given its relatively low interest rates of 8.5 per cent for overdrafts and 7.5 per cent for other facilities, with a three-year repayment period.

He contrasted the rates with commercial bank lending rates, which he said could rise to between 28 and 30 per cent, arguing that the intervention would ease the financing burden on businesses and improve their capacity to expand.

In another development, Kanu said the Ministry of Industries and SMEs had verified and documented more than 110 workshops and 120 shops at the Ohiya-Umuogo-Nsukwe Industrial Cluster.

He said the exercise aligned with Goal Eight of the Sustainable Development Goals, which focuses on inclusive economic growth, productive employment and decent work.

On the forthcoming AfrICANDO 2026 conference, the Information Commissioner announced that Governor Otti would formally declare the global event open on Tuesday, November 17, at the International Conference Centre, Umuahia.

He said the conference would attract local and international participants, offering opportunities for investment partnerships, trade expansion and business development across different sectors.

Kanu urged the media to give the event adequate coverage, noting that Abia was open to investors seeking viable business opportunities.

The Commissioner also dismissed any suggestion that ongoing political activities ahead of the 2027 general elections had slowed down governance in the state.

He said the machinery of government remained fully operational despite the distractions associated with electioneering, adding that preparations for the 2027 fiscal year were nearing completion.

“In this connection, we affirm that the Abia State 2027 Budget is almost ready for presentation to the Abia State House of Assembly,” he said.

INEC picks 598 polling units for mock election exercise in Delta

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

From Enweliku Kent, Asaba The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) will conduct a mock accreditation and result upload exercise in 598 polling units

From Enweliku Kent, Asaba

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) will conduct a mock accreditation and result upload exercise in 598 polling units across the 25 local government areas of Delta State as part of its preparations for the 2027 general elections.

The exercise, scheduled for Saturday, November 28, 2026, is aimed at testing the functionality and reliability of the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS), assessing election-day procedures and identifying potential operational challenges ahead of the polls.

The Resident Electoral Commissioner (REC) in Delta State, Sir Etekamba Udo Umoren, disclosed this on Friday during a press briefing in Asaba.

Umoren said the simulation was part of INEC’s broader efforts to strengthen electoral processes and ensure credible, transparent, efficient and seamless elections in 2027.

He explained that the exercise would enable the Commission to evaluate BVAS performance under different environmental and operational conditions before its deployment for the general elections.

The REC emphasised that the exercise was strictly a simulation and not an actual election, adding that no winners or losers would be declared.

According to him, 598 polling units have been selected across the state’s 25 local government areas, with two polling units in each registration area designated for the exercise.

He said the polling units would open at 7:30 a.m., while voter accreditation would commence at 8:00 a.m. and end at 2:30 p.m.

Umoren noted that the selection was designed to ensure adequate geographical representation and provide an opportunity to assess the Commission’s operational capacity at the grassroots level.

The simulation will cover the testing of electoral technology and equipment, particularly BVAS, as well as procedures for voter accreditation, voting, result recording and transmission, alongside other election-day activities.

He added that any technical challenges encountered would be documented and addressed before the 2027 general elections.

On personnel readiness, the REC said the exercise would also assess electoral officials’ understanding of polling procedures, the handling of election materials, accreditation, voting, counting and result management.

He urged participating officials to demonstrate professionalism, discipline and neutrality while adhering strictly to established electoral guidelines.

The Commission will also use the exercise to evaluate its logistical arrangements, particularly the timely deployment of personnel, election materials, equipment and other essential resources to the designated polling units.

Umoren called on political parties, security agencies, media organisations, civil society groups, community leaders and other stakeholders to support the exercise by sensitising the public about its purpose and encouraging peaceful conduct in the selected polling units.

He specifically urged media organisations to ensure accurate and responsible reporting, stressing the need to educate residents that the exercise was solely intended to test electoral processes and did not constitute an actual election.

He assured residents that INEC would collaborate with relevant security agencies to maintain peace and order throughout the exercise. Political actors, community leaders, youths and members of the public were also urged to cooperate with electoral officials and avoid actions capable of disrupting the process.

The REC further disclosed that the Commission would closely monitor and evaluate the simulation to identify operational strengths, weaknesses and areas requiring improvement.

Feedback from electoral officials, observers, stakeholders and technical personnel would be analysed and used to improve operational procedures, enhance staff performance and strengthen logistical arrangements ahead of the 2027 elections.

Reaffirming INEC’s commitment to credible elections, Umoren said the Commission remained determined to deliver polls that were free, fair, transparent, inclusive and peaceful.

He described the forthcoming simulation as an important milestone in the Commission’s preparations for the 2027 general elections, noting that its success would depend largely on the cooperation and support of all stakeholders.

He urged the media to use the opportunity to educate residents on the importance of the exercise and its role in strengthening Nigeria’s electoral system, while reiterating INEC’s commitment to continuous improvement and stakeholder engagement to ensure that every eligible voter could exercise their franchise in a peaceful, secure and credible environment.

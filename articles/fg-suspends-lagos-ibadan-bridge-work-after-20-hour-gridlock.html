FG suspends Lagos-Ibadan bridge work after 20-hour gridlock

By Punch Punch 5d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has suspended the ongoing construction of a pedestrian bridge at the Aseese area of the Lagos-Ibadan Expressway following a gridlock that

The construction work, being carried out by the Federal Ministry of Works in collaboration with CBC Construction Company, was scheduled to run from Wednesday, November 18, to Sunday, November 22, to facilitate the installation of the pedestrian bridge.

The Ogun State Traffic Compliance and Enforcement Agency had earlier announced that the exercise would involve a temporary diversion of inbound Lagos-bound traffic towards the Lagos carriageway at Aseese, before motorists rejoined their normal route at a designated exit point opposite Christ Embassy.

However, the diversion triggered a prolonged traffic jam that began at about 2pm on Wednesday and lasted until about noon on Thursday.

Several motorists were stranded for hours, while many commuters trekked long distances to their destinations.

PUNCH Metro had reported that some commuters who boarded vehicles from Ibadan shortly after 11am did not arrive in Magboro until about 5.30pm. Another motorist lamented spending eight hours travelling from Mowe to Magboro.

Many frustrated motorists abandoned their vehicles and trekked several kilometres to their destinations.

Commercial motorcycle operators also took advantage of the situation to increase their fares, with one rider demanding as much asN6,000 from a passenger travelling from Mowe to Ibafo.

The gridlock was further worsened as some drivers resorted to driving against traffic to escape the congestion.

Lamenting the situation on Wednesday night, the Special Adviser to President Bola Tinubu on Media and Communications, Sunday Dare, criticised the timing of the construction work.

Dare, in a post on his Facebook account, said the prolonged gridlock had left thousands of commuters stranded, with many forced to abandon their vehicles and trek to their destinations.

He questioned why the Chinese contractor handling the project chose to carry out the work during peak traffic hours instead of at night when vehicular movement was lighter.

Dare wrote, “Tonight – Lagos -Ibadan Expressway. I have been in a traffic gridlock for over five hours trying to get out of Lagos to Ibadan for multiple radio and television appearances ahead of the APC Presidential Rally coming up on Saturday in Ibadan.

“It beats me why a Chinese Contractor would choose the busiest hours of vehicular traffic to construct the bridge and road around this exit point. Stranded cars, thousands of people trekking to get home.

“What happened to doing construction work during the obituary hours when traffic is lean? This is unnecessary pain. It needs to be corrected.”

Other road users also took to social media to share their experiences.

A Facebook user, Adiatu Adeoye, wrote, “My truck spent 12hrs in the gridlock.”

Another user, Ojewale Adewale, lamented, “Got to Interchange from Abeokuta enroute Lagos by 3pm, I am just getting home now 9.59pm.”

Kemi Adewuyi also wrote, “I met the traffic yesterday afternoon on my way from work, still met it this morning on my way to work, it was terrible. Imagine paying N500 from car park C to Mowe bus stop this morning.”

Monitoring the situation on Thursday morning, our correspondent observed that the gridlock had persisted overnight and into the afternoon.

Some commuters also abandoned their journeys after commercial drivers increased their fares to as much as N3,000 from Mowe to Berger, compared with the usual fare of between N700 and N800.

Commercial motorcyclists also raised their fares to as much as N5,000 for the same journey.

However, at about noon on Thursday, traffic had eased and movement was restored along the affected section of the expressway.

Following the development, TRACE announced that the construction work has been suspended indefinitely.

The agency stated that the suspension followed the traffic challenges experienced along the corridor from Wednesday to Thursday morning.

TRACE spokesperson, Babatunde Akinbiyi, said the development necessitated a review of the traffic management arrangements surrounding the construction activities.

“Consequently, all motorists are hereby advised that the temporary traffic diversion associated with the pedestrian bridge construction is suspended, pending further directives from the relevant authorities.

“TRACE regrets the inconveniences, delays and discomfort this development may have caused motorists and other road users. We sincerely appreciate the patience, understanding and cooperation of the motoring public,” he stated.

Also, the acting Controller of the Federal Ministry of Works in Ogun State, Richard Adeleye, confirmed that the bridge work was suspended due to the traffic situation when contacted on Thursday.

“Yes, it was suspended due to the traffic situation,” Adeleye said in a telephone conversation with our correspondent.

Meanwhile, some road users called for the construction and rehabilitation of inner roads linking the expressway to provide alternative routes during traffic congestion.

A commuter, Kunle Adeleke, told our correspondent that functional inner roads would have provided alternative routes for motorists during the gridlock.

“If the inner roads are good, they would have served as the alternative routes for motorists during traffic situation. Ogun State Government should fix the inner roads and end this gridlock suffering every time,” he said.

Another commuter, Jamiu Akinsola, urged the Federal Government to monitor contractors handling projects along busy corridors to prevent a recurrence.

“Infrastructural development is good but the welfare of the people must be taking into consideration.

“The FG must call its contractors to order and inform them to put the wellbeing of the citizens first. We should be doing better than these at this stage of our national development,” he said.

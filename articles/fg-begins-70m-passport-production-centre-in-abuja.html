FG begins $70m passport production centre in Abuja

By Punch Punch 4d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government on Friday announced plans to produce six million passport booklets annually from a new $70m manufacturing facility in Abuja. The plan als

The plan also includes ambitions to scale output to 10 million booklets per year and position Nigeria as a passport supplier to other African countries currently dependent on Europe and the Americas.

The Minister of Interior, Dr Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo, disclosed this at the groundbreaking ceremony for the National Passport Booklet Production Centre at the Nigeria Immigration Service headquarters in Abuja.

“The requirement of Nigeria is roughly between 1.8 to 2 million passports a year, but by May 2027, we will have the capacity for six million passports a year production, which we are going to scale to 10 million booklets a year,” Tunji-Ojo said.

He said the facility, developed with technology partner Iris Smart Technologies, was designed to meet ECOWAS travel document standards and could make Nigeria a continental exporter.

He said: “What we and our partners Aresmart are doing is to build a passport that will be able to service ECOWAS and even be able to export to other countries. All other countries in Africa, they are buying from Europe, from the Americas. But we believe Nigeria, being the giant of Africa, that is a market. Nigeria must lead.”

Tunji-Ojo said President Bola Tinubu would commission the facility in May 2027, adding that the project, sitting on 1.1 hectares of land and to be run entirely off-grid on solar power, was financed without government funds.

He said: “This project is about $70m without a dime from government.”

He noted that the initiative formed part of a broader plan since 2023 to domesticate the entire passport value chain, citing the completion of a centralised passport personalisation centre at NIS headquarters in September 2025 and an 8.3-petabyte AI-driven data centre.

He said Nigeria would become the first country in West Africa to manufacture e-passports domestically, joining South Africa, Ethiopia and Algeria as the only African countries with in-country passport manufacturing capacity, and the first to produce biometric e-passports.

He said: “In the whole of Africa, in the whole of West Africa, what we are doing today, by the time we deliver it in May, Nigeria will be the first country in West Africa to do it.”

He added that the original contract with Iris Smart Technologies dated to 2003, but President Tinubu inserted a “backward integration” condition requiring local manufacturing before the contract could continue.

Speaking at the event, the senator representing Edo North, Adams Oshiomhole, who performed the groundbreaking, said the ceremony marked a shift from procurement to production, arguing that historic reliance on foreign passport printing reflected a failure of imagination rather than capacity.

The Senate Committee on Interior chairman said: “For the first time in Nigerian history, under the leadership of President Bola Tinubu, we are going to start to manufacture passport of the finest quality, right here in Abuja.”

Oshiomhole, a former president of the Nigeria Labour Congress and former governor of Edo State, said the project embodied his lifelong argument that production must be domestic, contrasting it with what he called the procurement mentality of earlier administrations.

“Rather than going to sign agreement in a foreign country to be producing Nigeria passport, today we will manufacture it here,” he said.

He also cited earlier passport reforms as evidence of change, noting that members of his family had benefited directly.

“Nigerians who used to cry and do all sorts of lamentations about the dysfunctionality of the Nigerian state, that they can’t even have their passport renewed, and therefore their plan to visit family at home was aborted by reason of the fact that the Nigerian passport has expired.

“We saw there whether they were in America, they were in Europe or they were in Asia. Within months of the honourable minister’s assumption of office, these issues were dealt with and dealt with satisfactorily, including my own grandchildren.

“They tell me, ‘Grandpa, now I have Nigeria passport.’ So these are not in the advert; they are real stories that speak to what is changing the real meaning of renewed hope,” he said.

The former governor praised Tunji-Ojo as one of the most consequential ministers in the Tinubu cabinet.

He also commended NIS Comptroller-General Kemi Nandap for her leadership, saying she had shown that women could break new ground in security agencies without affirmative allocation.

Breaking new ground in passport production

In her remarks, the NIS Comptroller-General, Kemi Nandap, said the ceremony marked not just the foundation of a building but the foundation of a more sovereign passport system.

She acknowledged years of booklet shortages that left students missing admissions and parents unable to travel, saying the lesson was that “a nation cannot run a dependable passport system without control over the critical parts of its own supply chain.”

She outlined three expected benefits of the manufacturing centre: enhancing security through national oversight of sensitive travel documents; strengthening supply chain resilience and local expertise in secure document production; and improving service delivery to Nigerians.

“History will not judge us by the promises we make at this groundbreaking ceremony, but by the institutions we build, the standards we uphold and the results we deliver,” she said.

Meanwhile, Acting Assistant Comptroller-General of Customs Doris Okonkwo, who represented Comptroller-General Bashir Adeniyi, congratulated the Immigration Service and said the project aligned with the government’s broader institutional modernisation agenda.

“This landmark project represents a strategic leap forward in strengthening Nigerian domestic passport production capacity, enhancing document security, and ensuring sustainable, self-reliant passport administration,” she said, adding that ending external reliance in passport booklet production would conserve foreign exchange and strengthen national security.

Infrastructure Concession Regulatory Commission Director-General Dr Jobson Ewanife cited the facility alongside recent concession signings and commissionings as evidence of momentum in the administration’s infrastructure drive.

He said: “What we are witnessing today is a product of the bold reform of His Excellency Mr. President.”

Ewanife added that the ICRC would monitor compliance with contractual obligations to ensure delivery to specification.

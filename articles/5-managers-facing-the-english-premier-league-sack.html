5 Managers Facing The English Premier League Sack

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 10h ago 6 min read

Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The revolving door for Premier League managers is almost as entertaining and unpredictable as the football itself – and it seems a sacking is around the corner.

The revolving door for Premier League managers is almost as entertaining and unpredictable as the football itself – and it seems a sacking is around the corner.

Despite all 20 remaining in their position heading into November, there are a number of managers that look to be teetering on the edge.

Leading sports betting website Bets.ng have taken a look at the five most likely candidates to be the next Premier League manager to lose their job.

Matthias Jaissle (Newcastle)

Matthias Jaissle is the first name on the chopping blocking list, and it is no wonder why he is one of the favourites to go first.

Nine matches have culminated in three wins, two draws and four defeats. With 11 points in the bank, the Magpies may only be four points short of the European places, but the dull and uninspiring brand of football they are playing is not currently worth the entry fee.

Eddie Howe may not have been the flashiest name, but he was solid. Howe had a clear idea of how he wanted to play.

Making Newcastle tough to beat may not bring them the same sort of press as an all-action plan in which goals come freely would, it did bring them the 2024/25 EFL Cup after a brilliant run in which they beat Liverpool in the final at Wembley.

Fast forward to today, and the Magpies languish in the bottom half of the league after a poor start that has seen them lose to the likes of Leeds, Coventry and Everton.

The Magpies have been toothless across the start of the season, and if there are no major improvement quickly, then Jaissle will likely be looking for work very soon.

Oliver Glasner (Nottingham Forest)

Oliver Glasner left Crystal Palace following a superb run of results that saw him guide his side to the FA Cup at the end of 2025 and the Conference League in 2026.

A run like this tends to see a manager looking for a change after they have seriously overachieved with their side.

However, Glasner’s switch to Nottingham Forest is yet to ignite. One win, three draws and five defeats in nine matches sees Nottingham Forest spared propping up the table by Sunderland’s worse start.

Worrying for Glasner will be Forest’s track record of sacking managers of late. Since Nuno Espirito Santo was dismissed in September of 2025, four managers have come and gone as Forest have failed to find the remedy for their woes.

Finishing five points clear of the relegation zone in the 2025/26 season felt like a very close call, and it seems unlikely that this sort of close call will go down well with the Forest hierarchy once again.

Regis Le Bris (Sunderland)

The 2025/26 Premier League season was one that completely defied expectations for a number of sides, but none more so than Sunderland. A total of 14 wins, 12 draws and 12 defeats saw the Black Cats amass 54 points and qualify for the UEFA Europa League.

However, exceptional results lead to higher expectations.

This season, Sunderland prop up the Premier League table after nine matches. A single win, two draws and six defeats, coupled with just seven goals and 17 conceded has been a recipe for disaster. While the season is still incredibly young, fans will worry that the bubble may have burst.

Their European exploits have included wins over AZ Alkmaar and Torreense preceded a draw with Lech Poznan.

Is Europe to blame for their stuttering start to the season? Sunderland are not blessed with the sort of resources that Manchester City, Arsenal or Liverpool can call upon to compete across fronts, so it is likely a factor.

However, Regis Le Bris is starting to feel the pressure of a poor run of results. While Sunderland’s fans still have faith in him to turn the fortunes of the club around, how long will those in power hold off before pressing the panic button?

Pierre Sage (Crystal Palace)

Pierre Sage’s success at Ligue 1 side Lens saw him come in as the replacement for Oliver Glasner when Crystal Palace’s manager departed for Nottingham Forest.

However, arriving following success in France means little when the start to a new managerial venture comes with as much scrutiny as Sage’s has at Palace.

After nine matches, Palace have won twice, drawn once and lost six times. With nine goals scored and 21 conceded, Palace have one of the lowest returns up front while they have the worst defensive record.

While Palace have lost to Everton and Manchester City in games they were expected to lose, they have also lost to Ipswich, Newcastle and Tottenham, with each of the three teams they could have beaten considering their disappointing records.

The big humiliation came as they were completely played off the pitch by arch-rivals Brighton in a stunning 5-0 loss. While Brighton are enjoying a stunning start to their season, Palace and their fans will not accept a humiliation on this sort of scale from their biggest rivals.

Throw in the fact that Glasner was able to guide Palace to an FA Cup triumph in 2025 and the Conference League crown in 2026, and you have all of the ingredients for a Premier League side that have changed their manager and with this switch, suddenly their exploits on the field have changed drastically.

Michael Carrick (Manchester United)

A single win does not save a manager their job and Michael Carrick will know that he is still very much up against it if he is to still be the man leading Manchester United when the November international break comes to an end.

The bookmakers make Carrick the outright favourite to be the next Premier League manager to go, and there are no prizes for guessing why.

Since their 4-0 win over minnows Sabah in the Champions League on September 10, United have lost to Manchester City, Brighton, Atletico Madrid, Como, Bournemouth and Chelsea. They have also drawn with Fulham, Tottenham and Leeds.

Sat in 17th in the Premier League and 17th in the Champions League, trophies in either of them two biggest competitions in United’s sights seems unlikely.

Does securing a Champions League berth save Carrick’s job? Will winning the FA Cup be enough if United’s league season ends in an underwhelming campaign? Time will tell whether United are looking for their 13th new manager or caretaker manager since Sir Alex Ferguson in 2013, with this weekend’s match against Aston Villa potentially crucial.

FG pushes regional deal to prevent internet disruptions

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The proposed agreement would establish common procedures for rerouting internet traffic during cable failures, strengthen cross-border telecommunications infrastructure and reduce dependence on limited international connectivity routes.

The Minister of Communications, Innovation and Digital Economy, Bosun Tijani, advanced the proposal during engagements with member states of the International Telecommunication Union at its 2026 Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha, Qatar.

According to a statement issued by the ministry on Monday, Tijani “advocated for a shared regional protocol for digital infrastructure resilience, including mechanisms for rerouting network traffic and coordinating repairs when subsea cables fail.”

The ministry said the initiative formed part of Nigeria’s efforts to strengthen regional digital infrastructure and improve the reliability and affordability of internet services across West Africa.

It disclosed that Nigeria was collaborating with Chad, Burkina Faso and the Republic of Benin to improve cross-border connectivity, with plans to extend the cooperation to Cameroon.

Nigeria is also working with Togo on plans to establish a regional data park to expand access to computing capacity for researchers and businesses across West Africa.

The ministry said the initiatives would strengthen regional economic integration and reduce vulnerabilities arising from dependence on limited international telecommunications infrastructure.

It noted that submarine cables carry more than 99 per cent of international data traffic, making their protection essential to the functioning of the global digital economy.

Nigeria’s involvement in submarine cable protection gained prominence in 2024 when Tijani was appointed Co-Chair of the ITU International Advisory Body for Submarine Cable Resilience, established in partnership with the International Cable Protection Committee.

The country subsequently hosted the International Submarine Cable Resilience Summit in Abuja in February 2025, bringing together government officials, industry leaders and technical experts to address vulnerabilities affecting critical subsea infrastructure.

The advisory body’s work culminated in the adoption of recommendations in July 2026, following further international engagements, including a summit in Porto, Portugal.

The recommendations addressed cable protection, risk mitigation, infrastructure redundancy, timely deployment and repairs, and diversification of submarine cable routes.

Meanwhile, the ministry announced that Nigeria had secured re-election to the ITU Council for the 2027–2030 term.

Nigeria emerged among 13 African countries elected to the 48-member council, extending its participation in international telecommunications governance, which dates back to 1973.

The statement read, “The Federal Republic of Nigeria has secured re-election to the Council of the International Telecommunication Union for the 2027–2030 term, reaffirming the country’s growing influence in global telecommunications governance and its commitment to advancing inclusive digital development.

“The re-election was secured at the ITU Plenipotentiary Conference 2026 (PP-26), holding in Doha, Qatar, where Nigeria emerged among the 13 African countries elected to serve on the 48-member Council.

“The victory represents another important milestone in Nigeria’s longstanding engagement with the ITU and strengthens its voice in shaping international policies and decisions that will influence the future of global connectivity and digital development.”

Reacting to the development, Tijani said, “Nigeria’s re-election to the ITU Council is a strong affirmation of the confidence the international community has in our country’s leadership and contribution to the global digital agenda.”

He added, “Under the visionary leadership of President Bola Ahmed Tinubu, we are positioning Nigeria to play an increasingly influential role in shaping the policies, partnerships and innovations that will define the future of telecommunications.”

“We will use this renewed mandate to advance meaningful connectivity, strengthen Africa’s voice and ensure that emerging technologies create opportunities for our people.”

The minister was elected Vice-Chair of the ITU Council in June 2025 and subsequently chaired its 2026 session in Geneva. During his chairmanship, the council considered emerging technologies, stakeholder engagement, sustainable funding arrangements and institutional reforms.

It also endorsed a revised draft strategic plan for consideration at the Doha conference and approved the union’s operational plan for 2027. Beyond telecommunications infrastructure, the ministry highlighted Nigeria’s participation in international efforts to strengthen child online protection.

It disclosed that Stella Chubiyo Erebor served as the African regional Vice-Chair of the ITU Council Working Group on Child Online Protection, contributing to discussions on online safety policies and regulatory practices.

The ministry linked these efforts to domestic initiatives, including the Nigerian Communications Commission’s establishment of a multi-stakeholder technical working group and the development of the Nigerian Child Online Protection Policy in 2014.

Tijani also called for inclusive access to artificial intelligence, arguing that people across different countries and communities should be able to develop technologies that reflect their languages and address local challenges.

The ministry credited the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, led by Bianca Odumegwu-Ojukwu, and Nigerian diplomatic representatives in Qatar and Geneva with supporting the country’s re-election campaign.

Other participating institutions included the Nigerian Communications Commission, Nigerian Communications Satellite Limited, National Space Research and Development Agency, Defence Space Administration and National Emergency Management Agency.

The Federal Government said Nigeria would use its renewed ITU mandate to strengthen international cooperation on affordable connectivity, cybersecurity, digital skills, technology transfer and responsible innovation.

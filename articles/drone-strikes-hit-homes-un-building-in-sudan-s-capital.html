Drone Strikes Hit Homes, UN Building In Sudan’s Capital

By Channels TV Channels TV 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Channels TV

Drones struck Sudan’s capital, Khartoum, ripping through homes and damaging a United Nations building on Sunday, witnesses and the world organisation said.

Explosions rang out in the area around Khartoum airport and other parts of the city from around 2:00 am (0000 GMT) until approximately 4:30 am.

A drone strike destroyed a house, a witness said, while a Sudanese army source said the drones had been launched from Ethiopia.

“The drone attack at dawn today is a continuation of Ethiopian aggression against Sudanese territory, as the drones were launched from Ethiopia’s Bahir Dar airport,” the source told AFP.

Sudan’s military has repeatedly accused Ethiopia of aiding the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF), against which it has been fighting since April 2023, including through drone attacks launched from Ethiopian territory.

Ethiopia denies the accusations.

AFP footage showed a house reduced to rubble, with a security perimeter around the site. Pieces of furniture, household items and clothing were scattered among the debris.

Al-Nour Adam, who lives in the area, told AFP that upon entering the house after the strike, “we found a person lying on a bed, wounded in his left arm, chest and head”.

‘Where Are We Supposed To Go?’

“The strikes are indiscriminate,” Adam said.

“A strike could hit while you are asleep. You might wake up to find your children gone… It is truly tragic. You cannot be certain of anything.”

Yasser Hassan, another resident, said that the occupants of the house “had only just moved in, after searching for shelter for a long time”.

“Then the drone struck them. Where are we supposed to go?”

Another witness reported that a drone hit the home of a mosque’s muezzin, the person who calls Muslims to prayer, in Jebel Aulia, around 45 kilometres (28 miles) south of Khartoum.

“People were trying to rescue family members trapped under the rubble,” the witness said.

The United Nations, meanwhile, said that “a UN premises with UN colleagues in the building was damaged”.

“This occurred in a civilian neighbourhood,” the UN’s humanitarian office (OCHA) said in a statement posted on X.

OCHA condemned the risk posed to humanitarian workers, saying it was “unacceptable” that UN personnel were being exposed to such danger.

Saudi Arabia, which has provided support to the Sudanese regular army in the country’s war, said on X that it “categorically rejects these brutal attacks”.

Egypt, which also backs Sudan’s military, said in a foreign ministry statement it “strongly condemns” the attacks.

Drone Warfare

Authorities did not immediately report on casualties.

The conflict between the RSF and the Sudanese army has killed more than 200,000 people, aid workers estimate, and triggered the world’s largest hunger and displacement crises.

The United Nations has repeatedly criticised both sides’ rampant use of drones, which killed more than 1,100 people in the first half of 2026.

Of the hundreds of child casualties this year, two-thirds were killed or injured by drone strikes, the UN said.

A Sudanese army drone strike killed 15 people, including four children, in a West Kordofan village days ago, as the army pushed through the state, recapturing territory from the RSF.

The rival forces have, for the last year, wrestled for control of the resource-rich southern Kordofan region, which connects the army-held centre and east to RSF-controlled Darfur.

In recent months, both the army and the RSF have increasingly relied on drones capable of striking targets hundreds of kilometres away without risking their fighters.

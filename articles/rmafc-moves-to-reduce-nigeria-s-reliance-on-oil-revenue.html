RMAFC moves to reduce Nigeria’s reliance on oil revenue

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Revenue Mobilisation, Allocation and Fiscal Commission has begun preparing a national policy framework to expand government revenue sources, reduce dependen

The commission disclosed that the proposed framework would emerge from a National Revenue Summit expected to bring together federal, state and local governments, revenue-generating agencies, private sector operators, academics and development partners.

According to a statement issued on Tuesday by the commission’s Head of Information and Public Relations, Maryam Umar-Yusuf, the initiative formed part of efforts to improve revenue mobilisation, strengthen compliance and identify alternative sources of funding for national development.

Speaking during a two-day strategic planning retreat at Zuma Rock Resort, Niger State, the Chairman of the Mobilisation and Diversification Committee, Victor Eboigbe, said the commission intended to develop a policy document to guide revenue expansion nationwide.

“We are hoping that this Summit will be able to come up with a policy framework that the nation can rely on,” Eboigbe said.

He explained that the initiative would enable the commission to fulfil its constitutional responsibility of advising the three tiers of government on revenue generation and diversification.

“One of our very important functions is to advise the three tiers of government on how to increase their revenue,” he added.

Eboigbe argued that Nigeria needed to reduce its dependence on oil revenue and identify sustainable funding sources to support infrastructure development and broader economic growth.

He noted that achieving President Bola Tinubu’s development objectives would require adequate and predictable government revenue.

Earlier, the Chairman of RMAFC, Mohammed Shehu, called for a coordinated roadmap that would identify specific responsibilities, implementation timelines and measurable outcomes for the proposed summit.

Shehu explained that the retreat, held from November 16 to 17, was designed to determine the summit’s objectives, scope, major discussion areas and expected outcomes.

He emphasised that the gathering should produce recommendations capable of improving Nigeria’s revenue system rather than merely serving as another policy discussion.

“It should be a platform capable of generating meaningful dialogue and practical recommendations on the nation’s revenue system,” Shehu said.

According to him, the proposed summit aligns with the Federal Government’s efforts to increase non-oil revenue and improve fiscal sustainability.

Also speaking, the Vice Chairman of the Mobilisation and Diversification Committee and Federal Commissioner representing Abia State, Nkechi Oti, advocated greater attention to revenue opportunities outside the petroleum sector.

She explained that the commission’s responsibilities included advising governments on revenue generation and reducing economic wastage.

Oti disclosed that the proposed diversification initiative would extend to all six geopolitical zones to encourage grassroots participation.

She added that government institutions, private businesses, young people and other stakeholders would be involved in identifying opportunities for economic diversification.

The Director of the Mobilisation and Diversification Department, Khadija Kumo, said the retreat marked the beginning of detailed preparations for the national summit.

“We are looking at how to improve the revenue base of the Federation, the essence of the retreat is to kick-start the preparation so that we will have a successful National Revenue Summit,” Kumo said.

She explained that improving government revenue was necessary to provide adequate resources for development projects across the country.

The commission said the retreat would also examine opportunities to strengthen revenue administration, improve compliance and develop actionable recommendations for consideration at the summit.

Other federal commissioners from Kwara, Katsina, Ekiti, Osun, Bayelsa, Akwa Ibom, Gombe and Nasarawa states attended the retreat.

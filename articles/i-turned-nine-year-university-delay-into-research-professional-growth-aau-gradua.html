I turned nine-year university delay into research, professional growth – AAU graduate

After nine years at Ambrose Alli University, Success Owolabi graduated with a CGPA of 4.71/5.00 despite academic setbacks and personal loss. The first class graduate speaks with TEMITOPE ADETUNJI about his journey and academic achievement

What inspired you to study Medical Laboratory Science?

It was inspired by my early fascination with medicine and the treatment of diseases. Growing up, I was exposed to the healthcare environment through my mother, who was a nurse. Watching her care for patients and contribute to their recovery naturally sparked my interest in the medical profession. She had always hoped I would become a medical doctor, but at the time, I was looking for a career in healthcare that would require a relatively shorter period of study. This led me to choose Medical Laboratory Science.

Interestingly, what I initially considered an alternative gradually became a passion of its own. I developed a deeper appreciation for the role of laboratory investigations in disease diagnosis, treatment monitoring and patient management. Although I had anticipated a shorter academic journey, I ended up spending nine years at the university.

Looking back, the journey was longer than I imagined, but it gave me the opportunity to grow, develop professionally and discover how much I genuinely love the science behind disease diagnosis.

In what year did you gain admission into Ambrose Alli University?

It was in 2017, but I officially resumed academic activities in January 2018.

Your university journey lasted nine years. What accounted for the extended period?

The extended duration was largely due to administrative and regulatory delays beyond our control. The university admitted students into the programme in numbers that significantly exceeded the approved admission quota. Consequently, the indexing process was delayed, affecting our eligibility and scheduling for the professional examinations.

Because of the large number of students involved, the indexing and professional examinations were eventually conducted in batches. This process took approximately three years, from 2023 to 2026, before we could complete the necessary professional requirements and proceed to induction.

How would you describe your experience as a student?

It was a deeply emotional and challenging experience. I would not necessarily say that delay is not denial because, over the years, I watched several opportunities pass me by simply because I could not move forward at the same pace as my peers. There were moments of uncertainty, frustration and discouragement when I wondered how much longer I would have to wait.

Navigating those years required tremendous resilience, patience and determination. I had to constantly find reasons to stay motivated, keep my ambitions alive and remind myself why I started in the first place. It was not always easy to maintain the same level of enthusiasm, especially when I watched my mates progress with their careers while I was still waiting to complete my professional journey.

Ultimately, the experience taught me that resilience is not always about being strong; sometimes, it is about finding the strength to keep going even when you are emotionally exhausted. It was a difficult chapter of my life, but one that shaped my character and strengthened my resolve.

What were some of the most difficult moments you experienced while pursuing your degree?

One of the most difficult moments was losing my mother just a few weeks before the commencement of my final semester in 2023. It was an incredibly painful period because I lost someone who meant so much to me, and I was suddenly faced with the challenge of navigating my final semester while grieving.

I had to sit for my final examinations barely three months after her burial. I resumed school a little late and had already missed the mid-semester test in a particular course, Molecular Biology and Biotechnology. At that point, I was still struggling emotionally because my worst fear had happened, and finding the strength to concentrate on academics was not easy.

However, I knew my mother would have wanted me to finish strong. I had to gather myself, confront the circumstances and keep moving. Before then, I was accustomed to studying for about eight hours daily, but I increased this to approximately 10 hours to make up for lost time and stay on track.

By God’s grace, I finished that semester with a perfect 5.00/5.00 GPA, my third perfect GPA during the programme. It remains one of my most memorable academic achievements, not just because of the grade, but also because of what I had to overcome to earn it.

You maintained an impressive academic record. What helped you remain consistent academically?

Maintaining academic consistency was not always easy, but I gradually discovered how to rekindle my motivation whenever I felt exhausted or discouraged.

One of the things that kept me going was reading the Campus Champion column in Saturday PUNCH. My dad was an ardent reader of the newspaper, and I vividly remember cutting out the Campus Champion pages from his stack of newspapers and keeping them in my travelling box before resuming in January 2018. I would read about other students’ academic achievements, their stories and successes. Whenever I felt weary, those stories reminded me of what was possible and reignited my desire to achieve something similar. Much later, I started following the column online.

I was also fortunate to have mentors, particularly Dammy, whom I looked up to from as early as my 100-level days. The guidance, encouragement and practical advice I received helped me navigate the academic demands of the programme and remain focused, even when things became difficult.

Beyond these, I learnt to be intentional about my studies and disciplined with my time. I understood that motivation could fluctuate, so I had to develop the discipline to keep going, even when the excitement was no longer there.

Looking back, it was a combination of personal determination, inspiration from other people’s achievements, mentorship and, above all, the grace of God that kept me going. I am grateful that it all worked out in the end.

How did you manage the pressure of academic work during periods of uncertainty and delay?

I managed the pressure by understanding myself, developing a study routine that worked for me and finding healthy ways to unwind. Whenever things became overwhelming, I listened to spiritual songs, prayed and joked around with friends. I also made time to relax. I doubt if a day passed without me watching a movie or doing something unrelated to academics to take my mind off studying.

By 300 level, I had become more strategic with my studies. Interestingly, I became less academically intensive during my first semesters in 300 level, deliberately allowing myself more time to rest and explore income-generating opportunities. However, I knew when to draw the line and refocus. I became particularly intentional about my academics during second semesters, when I committed myself more fully to studying.

During intensive preparation periods, I aimed for a minimum of eight hours of reading daily, although I did not necessarily achieve that every day. I had my cheat days, too, when I stayed away from books, went to the gym, attended parties or travelled. I understood the importance of balancing academic commitment with having a life outside school.

I also knew I did not have a photographic memory, so I sometimes had to read a topic five or six times before fully understanding it. Rather than become discouraged, I learnt to work with my learning pace and prepare accordingly. Interestingly, from 300 level through 500 level, I recorded a perfect 5.00/5.00 GPA in every second semester. That experience taught me that academic excellence is not necessarily about studying every waking hour, but about understanding yourself, managing your priorities and knowing when to give something your absolute best.

Did you ever consider giving up on your academic journey?

Honestly, giving up was never an option for me. Even though I did not necessarily feel the need to prove anything to anyone, I simply could not bring myself to let myself down or throw away the hard work I invested in over the years and semesters.

What does graduating with a CGPA of 4.71 out of 5.00 mean to you?

Graduating with a CGPA of 4.71/5.00 represents far more than an academic milestone to me. It is a testament to years of hard work, discipline, sacrifices and countless nights spent studying. I have come to understand that while some people are naturally gifted and grasp things effortlessly, others have to work four times as hard to achieve similar results. I belong to the latter category, and I am proud of the effort it took to get here. It certainly did not come by chance.

Beyond the academic achievement, it represents the contributions, encouragement and sacrifices of everyone who supported me along the way. My family, friends, mentors and everyone who stood by me at different stages of the journey all have a part in this achievement.

To me, it is not just a number on a transcript; it is a reminder that the years of effort were worthwhile and that I did not walk this journey alone. Above all, I am grateful to God for the strength and grace to see it through.

How did you feel after learning you emerged as the best graduating male student in your set and the second-best graduating student in your faculty?

It was an overwhelming feeling, and my induction remains one of the best days of my life. It felt like finally reaching the destination after embarking on a very long journey, during which I sometimes wondered if that day would ever come. Even as I walked into the hall, it still felt surreal, almost as though it would not happen after waiting so many years for it.

It was a rollercoaster of emotions because, alongside the excitement and gratitude, there was also a deep sense of missing someone who should have been there to witness and share that moment.

What year did you eventually graduate?

I completed my academic programme in May 2023, but my professional induction and oath-taking did not take place until October 28, 2026.

Who were the people that stood by you throughout your university journey?

My profound gratitude goes to my father, the Owokonirans and my older brother, Kingsley. I am equally grateful to Ife, Olamide, Kenny, Martins, Moses, Isobomuwa, SirmieD, Eghosa, Sammy, Sir Christian, Jane, AIJ, Iyobosa, Abby, Mavis, Obinna, Onazi, Bennycruz, Jennifer, Mudia, Dayo and Endurance.

I also want to appreciate my lecturers, Mr Ken and Madam Maureen, my HOD, Dr Sunny, my landlord and landlady, as well as Efosa, Halliday, Seunara and Bira.

Every single person mentioned here played a role in keeping me afloat. They pulled me through some of my lowest moments, stood by me through periods of uncertainty and ensured that I lacked nothing within their means.

I genuinely want them to know that I do not take their love, sacrifices and kindness for granted. My certificate may bear my name, but the journey was never mine alone.

What lessons did the nine-year experience teach you about navigating difficult circumstances?

Beyond teaching me perseverance, patience and resilience, the nine-year journey taught me one very important lesson: how to work while waiting. I realised that although I could not control the delays surrounding my academic and professional progression, I could control how I used the time available to me.

Rather than allow the waiting period to become unproductive, I invested in personal and professional development. I took online courses and earned five certifications. I also became actively involved in research, contributing to more than seven co-authored publications and publishing my own research as a first author.

Beyond research, I trained in professional writing and eventually built my own professional writing business.

Looking back, I would say the experience taught me that a delay does not have to translate into stagnation. Sometimes, while waiting for one door to open, you can equip yourself with the skills, knowledge and experience needed to walk through several others. Above all, I learnt to make progress even when my circumstances were not moving at the pace I desired.

Now that you have been inducted as a Medical Laboratory Scientist, what are your immediate plans and long-term career aspirations?

My immediate priority is to secure an internship placement where I can complete my compulsory one-year professional internship, gain further practical experience and strengthen my clinical competence as a Medical Laboratory Scientist, particularly in clinical microbiology.

In the long term, I aspire to become a leading researcher in infectious diseases and virology, contributing to research that advances disease prevention, diagnosis and treatment. I am particularly interested in infectious diseases, public health, and vaccinology, and I hope to secure a fully funded scholarship to pursue postgraduate studies and deepen my expertise in these fields.

Ultimately, I want to build a career that goes beyond clinical practice to include meaningful scientific contributions, research publications, and innovations that improve public health, particularly in infectious disease control and vaccine development.

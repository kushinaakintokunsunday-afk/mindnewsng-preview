2027: Ex-CJN warns against repeat of 2011 post-election killings

By Punch Punch 21h ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former Chief Justice of Nigeria and Chairman of the Council of the Wise of the Savannah Centre for Diplomacy, Democracy and Development, Justice Olukayode Ariwo

Speaking at a press conference in Abuja on Tuesday, Ariwoola raised concerns over increasing incidents of political violence, intimidation and destruction of property associated with political activities across the country.

Political violence involving candidates and supporters of major political parties appears to be escalating ahead of the 2027 general elections, with growing reports of attacks across the country.

The incidents have resulted in the destruction of property, loss of life, and injuries to several people.

In an exclusive report published by The PUNCH on November 11, the National Peace Committee revealed that 26 states and the Federal Capital Territory recorded 109 violations of the National Peace Accord during a 10-week monitoring exercise, raising concerns over the safety of political campaigns and the conduct of the 2027 elections.

The report, which covered the period between August 19 and October 27 and was obtained by The PUNCH, identified political violence and thuggery as the most frequent violations, with 33 incidents, followed by misinformation and disinformation, which accounted for 29 cases.

Reacting, the former CJN urged security agencies to ensure adequate protection at campaign venues and arrest and prosecute perpetrators of electoral violence. He also called on the Independent National Electoral Commission to enforce electoral laws without fear or favour.

The Savannah Centre’s Council of the Wise expressed deep concern over the wave of political violence and intimidation, which it said had led to loss of lives and destruction of property during ongoing political campaigns across Nigeria ahead of the 2027 general elections.

Ariwoola noted that, “Recent media reports indicated that in the past five weeks, Nigeria has witnessed a disturbing recurrence of politically motivated violence and attacks in many states of the federation, thereby dangerously threatening the safety of candidates, electorates, journalists and the general public.

“Since the commencement of political campaigns, reported attacks and disturbances thus far have affected political convoys of some candidates, rallies and gatherings of political parties in Kano, Katsina, Lagos, Edo, Delta, Enugu, Cross River, Sokoto, Enugu, Bayelsa and Rivers states as well as the Federal Capital Territory.”

The Council of the Wise also expressed concern over rising political violence ahead of the 2027 elections, citing deaths, injuries, destruction of property and alleged threats by political leaders against their opponents.

Ariwoola added, “The Council of the Wise therefore wishes to remind all Nigerians, particularly politicians and their supporters, that democracy thrives on the peaceful contestation of ideas and visions, not on bloodshed or coercion.

“Every Nigerian has a constitutional right to participate in the electoral process freely and safely, regardless of party affiliation. It is important to note that if this dangerous trend of political violence is not effectively checked, it will lead to more unpleasant consequences.

“The Council stresses that abiding by the governing laws is indispensable to electoral peace, protection of lives and the credibility of the democratic process. Political parties, candidates, supporters, public officials, security agencies and the citizens must respect the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 (as amended), the Electoral Act, 2026 and applicable INEC regulations and guidelines. Compliance must be consistent and impartial, as political ambition cannot justify unlawful conduct.”

The Council urged political parties and their supporters to ensure peaceful and responsible campaigns in line with the Constitution, warning that failure to address the situation could trigger a national crisis.

Ariwoola said, “The Nigeria Police Force, the Department of State Security Service, the Nigerian Security and Civil Defense Corps, the National Intelligence Agency, the Defense Intelligence Agency, as well as the Nigerian Armed Forces must deploy adequate personnel to secure all major campaign venues and political rallies across the Country. Perpetrators of political violence must be swiftly arrested and prosecuted without fear or favor. Ban and confiscate dangerous weapons such as knives, sticks, cutlasses, and guns at political gatherings nationwide.

“Leaders of all political parties, candidates, and their respective campaign councils must take full responsibility for the conduct of their supporters in accordance with the Electoral Act and the Law. Party leaders must publicly and continually denounce violence and call their irate followers to order, to strictly adhere to the Peace Accord they signed and abide by the electoral guidelines issued by INEC.

“They should desist from inflammatory, hateful, and insulting rhetoric in their utterances. They should promptly take disciplinary action against any of their members who engage in electoral violence, or threaten the use of violence. Members of their respective campaign committees should be trained in conducting peaceful campaigns in accordance with parties’ codes of conduct and the electoral laws.”

The Council urged governments at all levels to provide a level playing field for political parties by ensuring that opposition groups were not denied access to public or private facilities for peaceful campaigns.

Ariwoola continued, “The Independent National Electoral Commission should take steps to enforce the electoral laws without fear or favour. It should coordinate with the Nigeria Police on investigation and speedy prosecution of electoral offenders by its legal team in accordance with section 148 of the Electoral Act.

“The National Assembly should strengthen its oversight duty over the Security Agencies, INEC and other public institutions involved in the election process to ensure peaceful conduct of the elections.

“The judiciary should ensure speedy trials of electoral cases in order to end the culture of impunity.”

The Council urged citizens, particularly youths, to shun political thuggery and violence, stressing that elections should be peaceful and voters must respect the rights of others to participate in the electoral process.

Ariwoola continued, “Political competition should be a contest of ideas, not a justification for intimidation and violence. The Council of the Wise urges all Nigerians to choose peace over violence; dialogue over threats; and democracy over anarchy.

“It will be an unfortunate national shame if Nigerians collectively allow a repeat of what happened after the 2011 elections, where, according to Human Rights Watch, over 800 people were killed in the election-related violence. The 2027 elections must be won at the ballot boxes, not on the streets through intimidation and bloodshed.”

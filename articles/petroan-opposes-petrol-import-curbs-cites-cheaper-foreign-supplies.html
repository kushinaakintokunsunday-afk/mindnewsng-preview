PETROAN opposes petrol import curbs, cites cheaper foreign supplies

By Punch Punch 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The association’s National Public Relations Officer, Joseph Obele, said multiple supply sources remained necessary to guarantee adequate petroleum products and affordable prices, despite increasing contributions from domestic refineries.

Obele, an energy expert and lecturer at Ignatius Ajuru University of Education, Port Harcourt, made the call in a statement on Sunday, citing the October 2026 industry statistics released by the NMDPRA.

The figures showed that daily petrol imports increased by approximately 47.1 per cent to 15 million litres in October from 10.2 million litres in September, representing an additional 4.8 million litres daily.

The statement read, “The Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority should sustain the issuance of petroleum import permits to qualified marketers, insisting that healthy competition remains essential to achieving affordable fuel prices and protecting Nigerian consumers.”

He said the increase demonstrated the continuing importance of imported petroleum products in meeting domestic demand, arguing that Nigeria’s evolving refining capacity should not be protected from competition through restrictions on imports.

According to him, the estimated landing cost of imported petrol stood at N1,056.79 per litre, compared with Dangote Petroleum Refinery’s reported gantry price of N1,159 per litre as of 16 November, leaving a difference of N102.21 per litre in favour of imported supplies.

Obele argued that the price difference reinforced the need for a competitive market where suppliers could compete on pricing, product quality and operational efficiency.

He warned that restricting import permits while domestic refining capacity was still developing could limit supply options and create conditions for higher prices.

The PETROAN spokesman also called on the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited to accelerate the proposed equity partnership with two Chinese companies interested in investing in Nigeria’s refining industry.

He said the entry of the Chinese investors could introduce additional competition into the downstream sector, noting that their business models were often associated with large-scale production, aggressive pricing and relatively low profit margins.

Obele expressed optimism that the proposed investments could eventually push petrol prices below N700 per litre, although he acknowledged that achieving such a price would depend on investment costs, crude oil supply arrangements, production efficiency, exchange rates and prevailing market conditions.

He urged the NNPC to move beyond announcements and translate the proposed partnership into concrete investment commitments, operational refineries and increased domestic petroleum production.

The energy expert also maintained that existing domestic refiners, including Dangote Refinery, should be encouraged to compete through efficiency, affordable pricing, product quality and reliable supply rather than protection from other market participants.

He said, “Competition is not the enemy of local refining; it is the foundation of efficiency. Nigeria must open its petroleum market to credible investors, sustain import permits where necessary and encourage strategic partnerships that will expand refining capacity. Our ultimate objective should be to make petrol affordable, guarantee adequate supply and ensure that Nigerians benefit from the gains of deregulation.”

He urged the NMDPRA and NNPC to take deliberate steps to build a competitive, investment-friendly and consumer-focused petroleum industry.

Students’ Day: Foundation urges ethical AI use in schools

By Punch Punch 6d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The First Class Muslim Foundation has called on educational institutions, policymakers, and students across Nigeria and beyond to prioritise value-driven educat

The FCMF’s Board of Trustees Chairman, Dr Sulaiman Ogunmuyiwa, made the call in a statement on Tuesday to mark International Students’ Day.

International Students’ Day is observed annually on November 17.  The day honours student activism, academic freedom, and the multicultural diversity of students around the world.

Ogunmuyiwa said that while artificial intelligence and digital learning tools have expanded access to knowledge, technical proficiency alone is insufficient without a strong foundation of character, critical thinking, and integrity.

“Today, students have more technological power at their fingertips than any generation in history. However, tools like artificial intelligence can either become catalysts for genuine innovation or shortcuts that erode academic honesty.

“True progress requires education that shapes both the mind and the moral compass through equipping young people not just to use technology, but to use it responsibly,” Ogunmuyiwa said.

To advance this vision, he urged education stakeholders to embed practical lessons on digital ethics, data privacy, and intellectual honesty directly into curricula.

These, he said, would ensure students see emerging technologies as aids to original thought rather than substitutes for it.

Alongside ethical technology use, Ogunmuyiwa stressed the need to move away from purely exam-centric instruction toward experiential learning that cultivates problem-solving, civic responsibility, and principled leadership.

According to him, meaningful educational reform must include bridging infrastructure gaps so that students in underserved communities have reliable access to safe, modern learning tools and dedicated mentorship.

He also encouraged student leaders and academic administrators to intentionally foster campus cultures that reward originality, accountability, and service-oriented innovation throughout the academic year.

“Students are not merely passive recipients of the future; they are actively building it right now.

“By grounding their education in timeless values and technological integrity, we empower a generation capable of solving complex societal challenges without compromising their principles,” Ogunmuyiwa said.

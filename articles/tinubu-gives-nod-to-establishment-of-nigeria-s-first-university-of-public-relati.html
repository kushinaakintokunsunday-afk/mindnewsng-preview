Tinubu gives nod to establishment of Nigeria’s first University of Public Relations and Leadership

By VeryNigerian VeryNigerian 5d ago 3 min read

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has approved the establishment of Nigeria’s first University of Public Relations and Leadership, marking a new development in professional education and strategic communication in the country.

The approval was announced on Wednesday by Vice President Kashim Shettima, who represented the President at the ongoing World Public Relations Forum in Abuja.

Shettima described the proposed institution as a strategic investment in human capital development, professional training and the future of public relations and strategic communication in Nigeria.

The university is expected to provide specialised training and develop professionals with the skills and leadership capacity needed to advance the public relations industry in Nigeria.

The development was announced during the international gathering of public relations professionals and stakeholders, which brought together experts to discuss the growth and future of the profession.

He said the university would promote specialised education, research, professional development and leadership training, while preparing a new generation of communication experts for emerging global challenges.

According to him, the initiative is part of the Federal Government’s commitment to creating a lasting institutional legacy from Nigeria’s hosting of the international forum.

The Vice President commended the Nigerian Institute of Public Relations, NIPR, the Federal Ministry of Information and National Orientation, and the Nasarawa State Government for working together to actualise the project.

He also urged international delegates attending the forum to explore Nigeria’s investment opportunities, describing the country as a major economic force with enormous human and entrepreneurial potential.

Shettima challenged negative international perceptions about Nigeria, insisting that the country should not be defined by narratives surrounding internet fraud and other criminal activities.

He said, “Nigeria goes beyond 419. Nigeria is much much bigger than that.”

The Vice President highlighted the achievements of Nigerians in business, technology and other sectors, noting that the country has produced some of the wealthiest Black entrepreneurs in the world.

He also drew attention to Nigeria’s growing population and global influence, projecting that the country would become the world’s third most populous nation by 2050, with an estimated population of 440 million.

Shettima further described Nigerians in the United States as an exceptionally accomplished immigrant community, noting that about 60 per cent of Nigerian Americans have university degrees.

“The richest black man on earth is a bloody Nigerian. The second richest black man in the world is another bloody Nigerian. The third richest black man in the world is also a bloody Nigerian,” he said.

He urged foreign investors and international delegates to build partnerships with Nigerian institutions and businesses, stressing that the country offers significant opportunities beyond its portrayal on social media.

Shettima said the government expects participants to leave Nigeria with a better understanding of its economic prospects and renewed interest in investing and collaborating with local businesses.

He added that the administration recognises the growing importance of communication professionals in strengthening public trust, democratic governance, national development and international relations.

The Vice President also called on public relations practitioners to maintain high ethical standards, transparency, accountability and professionalism, particularly amid the growing challenges of misinformation, disinformation and artificial intelligence.

Very Nigerian reports that the university would be located in Nasarawa State, Nigeria.

Northern leaders have failed region, need collective development agenda – Kaduna Gov

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Kaduna State Governor, Senator Uba Sani, has challenged past and present leaders of Northern Nigeria to accept responsibility for the region’s development chall

 He said northern leaders, including himself, had failed the people and must be prepared to tell themselves the truth rather than exploit ethnic, religious and regional sentiments to seek political support.

 The governor made the call on Wednesday at a summit on Good Governance, Inclusivity and Sustainable Development organised by the Sir Ahmadu Bello Memorial Foundation in Kaduna.

 He urged the foundation to convene past and present leaders of northern extraction to develop a comprehensive regional development blueprint similar to the vision of the late Premier of Northern Nigeria, Sir Ahmadu Bello.

 Uba Sani said the late premier envisioned a united, peaceful and self-sufficient Northern Nigeria but lamented that subsequent generations of leaders had deviated from that vision.

 He said, “We must look ourselves in the mirror and tell ourselves some home truths. All of us, including myself, have failed the people of Northern Nigeria in one way or another.

 “We must come together, irrespective of our political affiliations, religious beliefs and ethnic backgrounds, to fashion out a collective agenda for the development of the North.”

 The governor also criticised politicians campaigning ahead of the 2027 general elections by appealing to regional sentiments without explaining their contributions to the development of the North while holding public offices.

 He said political leaders should be judged by their records in office and their ability to address the challenges confronting the people rather than where they come from.

 Uba Sani, however, attributed some of the achievements recorded by his administration to the support of President Bola Tinubu, whom he thanked for his interventions in Kaduna State.

 “I want to seize this opportunity to thank the President for all that he has done for Kaduna State in the last three and a half years,” he said.

 The governor recalled that he approached Tinubu over the completion of the Abuja-Kaduna-Kano highway, describing the road as a major gateway linking the northern and southern parts of the country.

 He said the President released billions of naira for the completion of the project, which had previously suffered delays.

 “Today, the road is almost 95 per cent completed,” he said.

 On the proposed Kaduna Light Rail project, Uba Sani said he and the Speaker of the House of Representatives, Tajudeen Abbas, had met with Tinubu to secure the President’s approval for the initiative.

 “I am happy to say that President Bola Ahmed Tinubu, knowing the history of Kaduna, decided to approve $865m for the Kaduna Light Rail project. The contract has been signed.

 “By the grace of God, in the next two weeks, he will be coming to Kaduna to perform the groundbreaking. That will be the second light rail project in the whole of Nigeria,” he said.

 The governor also reeled out achievements of his administration since assuming office on May 29, 2023, including interventions in pensions, transportation, agriculture, education and security.

 He said his administration inherited pension liabilities of about N24bn but had paid more than N21bn to 9,683 state and local government retirees and families of deceased workers.

 On transportation, Uba Sani said 100 buses had been deployed under the state’s free Compressed Natural Gas bus service, adding that the scheme had recorded 5.2 million passenger trips and saved commuters more than N5bn.

 He said insecurity had previously prevented children in some communities from attending school and farmers from accessing their farmlands.

 According to him, the Kaduna Peace Model has enabled farmers to reclaim more than 500,000 hectares of previously inaccessible farmland.

 “There is no single school that has remained closed in Kaduna State due to insecurity at the moment,” he said.

 The governor further disclosed that his administration had increased the annual agricultural budgetary allocation from N1.48bn to more than N100bn to strengthen food production and support farmers.

 He said more than 69,000 smallholder farmers had received improved agricultural inputs, while over 1,400 truckloads of fertiliser had been distributed to more than 500,000 farmers across the state’s 23 local government areas.

 Uba Sani added that the government had facilitated access to more than 200 tractors through partnerships and enrolled over 100,000 farmers in crop insurance schemes.

 “We have also supported 5,000 livestock farmers with equipment and production inputs. Through the Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones and the Rural Access and Agricultural Marketing Project, we are linking farms to processors and markets, where better access can reduce losses and improve returns,” he said.

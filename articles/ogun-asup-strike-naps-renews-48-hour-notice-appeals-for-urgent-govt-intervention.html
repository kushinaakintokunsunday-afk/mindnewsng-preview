Ogun ASUP strike: NAPS renews 48-hour notice, appeals for urgent govt intervention

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 17 Nov 2026 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The National Association of Polytechnic Students (NAPS), Ogun State Liaison Council, has renewed its 48-hour notice to the Ogun State Government to intervene in the ongoing strike by the Academic Staff Union of Polytechnics (ASUP) in state-owned polytechnics.

The Chairman of NAPS Ogun State Liaison Council, Comr. Boluwatife Joshua Opatunmibi, announced the renewed notice at a press conference on Monday.

He said the fresh 48-hour notice commences at 12:00 noon on Monday, November 16, and ends at 12:00 noon on Wednesday, November 18.

Opatunmibi said, “We hereby issue a fresh 48-hour notice, commencing at 12:00 noon on Monday, 16th November 2026, and ending at 12:00 noon on Wednesday, 18th November 2026.

“Within this period, we appeal to the Ogun State Government and all relevant stakeholders to demonstrate clear commitment towards resolving the ongoing dispute through meaningful engagement with ASUP.”

He disclosed that the council had on Thursday, November 12, 2026, issued an initial 48-hour notice calling for urgent intervention, but renewed it after the strike persisted.

He said academic activities across Ogun State-owned polytechnics have been disrupted, academic calendars affected, and students left uncertain about their academic future.

“As the representative body of polytechnic students in Ogun State, we consider it our responsibility to speak up.

“Let me state our position clearly: We stand for the interest of students. We are not here to fight ASUP. We are not here to fight the Ogun State Government. We are not here to create political tension or encourage violence. We are here to appeal for a solution,” he stated.

Lecturers deserve welfare, students deserve uninterrupted education

Opatunmibi said NAPS believes lecturers deserve their legitimate welfare and entitlements, institutions deserve adequate funding, and students deserve uninterrupted education.

“We believe lecturers deserve their legitimate welfare and entitlements. We believe our institutions deserve adequate funding. But we equally believe that students deserve uninterrupted education. These interests should not be in conflict. They should be protected together,” he said.

He lamented the rising cost of education, noting that many political leaders in authority today received education when it was free or considerably less expensive.

“Education has become increasingly expensive. Parents and guardians are paying more. Students are making greater sacrifices. Families are stretching their resources to ensure that their children receive an education. Yet, despite these increased costs, we are still struggling to enjoy uninterrupted academic activities,” he said.

He added that while ordinary students endure disruptions, some children of those in authority have access to private institutions where academic activities continue without interruption.

“Our concern is not where anybody’s children attend school. Our concern is that the ordinary Ogun State student should not receive less attention simply because their parents cannot afford private alternatives,” he said.

Understanding ASUP dispute

Opatunmibi said following engagement with ASUP representatives, NAPS understands the dispute involves outstanding welfare and funding concerns, including the implementation of applicable allowances under the renegotiated 2026 Federal Government-ASUP agreement, reportedly signed on April 8, 2026.

He listed ASUP’s concerns in Ogun State to include implementation of applicable allowances including CATA and EAA, improved funding of state-owned polytechnics, and outstanding pension deductions and contributions.

“Among the demands communicated by the union is an increase in monthly subvention from ₦5 million to ₦100 million per polytechnic,” he said.

He said NAPS acknowledged that state-owned institutions may operate under different financial and administrative arrangements from federal institutions, but expects government to engage the union and clearly communicate its position.

“Let us be clear: we are not asking lecturers to abandon their legitimate demands. We understand that the welfare of academic staff is essential to the quality of education. A poorly supported lecturer cannot be expected to deliver the best possible educational experience. But we also insist that the resolution of these concerns must not continually come at the expense of students,” Opatunmibi said.

Appeal to Governor Abiodun

Opatunmibi made a direct appeal to Governor Dapo Abiodun to personally ensure relevant authorities engage ASUP leadership with urgency.

“Your Excellency, we recognise the responsibilities that come with governing a state. We recognise that government must balance competing demands and financial obligations. However, education is too important to be left in prolonged uncertainty.

“We appeal to Your Excellency to personally ensure that the relevant authorities engage ASUP leadership with the urgency this situation deserves. We appeal for an open and constructive negotiation that will address the outstanding concerns of lecturers while protecting the academic interests of students,” he said.

He also appealed for a clear position on funding, outstanding staff entitlements, and a realistic timeline for resolving the dispute.

“We are not asking for promises that cannot be fulfilled. We are asking for sincere engagement, concrete commitments, and a lasting solution,” he said.

Not a protest notice

Opatunmibi clarified that the renewed notice is not a declaration of hostility, an invitation to violence, or an announcement of a protest.

He said, “Let nobody misunderstand the purpose of this notice. It is not a declaration of hostility. It is not an invitation to violence. It is not an announcement of a protest. It is an appeal backed by a renewed deadline because students cannot continue to wait indefinitely.

“Our preferred approach is peaceful negotiation. We want government and ASUP to reach an understanding. We want our institutions to function. We want our students back in their classrooms.”

He said NAPS does not celebrate protests and its first choice has always been dialogue, consultation and peaceful engagement.

“We would rather sit around a negotiation table and announce a successful resolution than stand before the press repeatedly explaining why students are still out of school,” he said.

“We want negotiations, not confrontation. We want solutions, not street battles. We want our education to continue.”

PVC is power

Opatunmibi also used the briefing to encourage eligible students to obtain their Permanent Voter’s Card (PVC).

“The PVC is not merely an identification card for elections. It is an instrument of democratic participation, accountability, and the ability to influence the direction of government,” he said.

“Our vote is our voice. We must become a generation that understands its rights, accepts its responsibilities, and participates actively in shaping the society it wants to live in. This is not a call for partisan politics. It is a call for civic awareness and responsible democratic participation.”

In his final appeal, Opatunmibi urged students to remain calm, united and law-abiding.

“To the Ogun State Government, we appeal once again: please act within this renewed 48-hour window. Let this matter be resolved through sincere negotiation before the situation becomes more difficult. To ASUP, we recognise your concerns and encourage continued constructive engagement,” he said.

I started dreaming of flying planes since I was 11 – Capt Tolu Ita, MD, Enugu Air

Captain Tolu Ita is a pilot and the Chief Executive Officer of Enugu Air. She describes herself as one that has had a fair deal from Nigeria, noting that the country happened to her in great ways, noting that the country trained her to be a pilot.

“We were one of the batches sponsored by the Federal Government. Nigeria has been good to me,” she told the reporter.

In this interview with Saturday Sun, she spoke on her career growth, life generally as well as the overshooting of the runway incident t of one of the aircraft in her company’s fleet, among other issues.

How has your journey to becoming a pilot been?

The journey to being a pilot was that I went to flying school at the Nigerian College of Aviation Technology (NCAT) in Zaria, Kaduna State. We were one of the batches sponsored by the Federal Government. Nigeria has been very good to me. We went with the process of qualifying and I was successful and became one of the people privileged to be sponsored. We were the famous class called Standard Pilot Course 22 (SP22) and that was how I became a pilot.

What was the attraction? Where did the dream to become a pilot as a young girl come from?

There was no relative role model per se. At about 11-12 years old, I was just a young girl who was curious, something mysterious about being a pilot which birthed an interest in me. I recall then, when people ask that regular question of ‘Tolu what do you want to be in future,’ I would respond. I want to be a pilot. Then I had an uncle who countered me and said, ‘No, females are not meant to be pilots. He rather questioned why I would not study a course like Computer Science which I studied eventually from University of Ilorin. But I knew that when a child dreams, God honours it. In reality it was a thought that came to me because I remember seeing a plane flying overhead my house years ago in Ikeja, that was where it started from. Then it lingered and the thought kept settling. So from there, I convinced myself that one can be a pilot and that was it. Well again, it was not just a dream because I remember vividly that while growing up, everyone in my household knew I wanted to be a pilot. With that, my family started seeing me as a pilot because I have said it many times. Once my mum teased me that, I hope you will not sleep on your passengers with the rate at which you sleep. I loved sleeping as a young girl. Repeatedly, I would say I would fly my grandmother and that prophecy did not come to pass because the same day I got a job with Aero Contractors as a pilot was the same day my grandmother died. But I lived the dream of flying my mother.

Please tell us how she felt about it and the experience

My mother was very proud of me as her daughter. I flew her from Lagos to Port Harcourt. When we landed and she alighted from the flight; it was a proud mother-daughter moment for her. She kept saying, that’s my daughter!

Being a pilot over the past 20 years, how has it been?

As soon as I rounded off my National Youths Service Corps, I went to flying school and graduated in year 2004. I flew for some time and did studies on management back in my Aero Contractor days. I got into fleet management and became Head of Trainings and Standards. I also delved into overseeing trainings and had the opportunity to set up an airline for a company but we had to postpone that project at that time. Again, I worked with NCAA as an inspector and went into managing a partnership with a foreign company. With my experience in management, when the opportunity came, I was brought on board for the Enugu Air project. That is where I am today. My studies in Computer Science, Aviation and management make me technology savvy.

Within the year, Enugu Air had an issue at the Benin airport. Most Nigerians were afraid and called it a crash. What really happened and how could a reoccurrence be averted?

Well, I will to go by what the Nigerian Safety Investigative Bureau (NSIB) said; even though the report is on YouTube. NSIB released a report as they brought out the black box and were able to go through the different phases of the flight. One could see that what happened was that the pilot touched down a bit late, they did not touch down on time. They touched down about the 2/3 of the runway which was wet with a tail wind, so, everything that could not work could not work. And then, it was difficult for the plane to stop. Instead, it ran into the bush. The greatest miracle was no life was lost. All of us from the pilot, aircraft and aero plane have been under the radar. I am not saying there is any perfect airline, but Enugu Air has not been found liable in any way to have been at safety risk. It is just that no one can commend anyone in an accident. Truth is that all our records keeping are all clean as they could see everything. Everyone was very cooperative because as an airline, we want to learn. The only way to get better is to learn from mistakes and we have done all the needful. The pilots that were involved have been retrained. I know that someone would say, the pilots should be sacked, but the truth is that our staff are our investment. We have been training them. It’s not just about us but about the aviation industry as a whole. I appreciate the fact that they themselves are willing to go through all the processes of correction. Some actions were taken amidst their training, so they can be better for themselves and the company. I recall vividly when I was in flying school, I was involved in an accident which was weather related too as a flying student. I was alone because it was a solo qualifying flight, thought it was a training aircraft TB9. Now, knowing what I went through and knowing how it helped shape my career, there are some decisions I do not take in a hurry. That incident shaped my career, made me more grounded. I thanked the visionary instructors who decided to give me another chance, I went through it again and thankfully, I finished strong. So this is me showing the similar grace to someone else. I know through the process of correction, they will come out stronger and that is why we took that decision to retrain them.

Has that incident affected the business aspect of the airline?

Interestingly, Nigerians showed us solidarity. Our Enugu citizens and Nigerians supported us. Our revenue, of course, no matter what, would have been affected. There are some people that will still have it at the back of their mind. But what Enugu Air stands for is excellent service. As much as we can, our on-time departure is really speaking for itself. Our services gave people a chance to try us again and here we are. That same day of that incidence, I appreciated all our operational team and everyone continued working hard and well. What normally would have broken an airline helped to mould us better. We had our internal discussions, appreciated feedbacks and improved on our systems. It helped us to be a better organisation. Most Nigerians already know that an Enugu Air experience is an unforgettable experience. Every service of Enugu Air is carefully thought out to make sure the passenger does not just fly, but fly with us again and again because of the service and ambience. We keep to our promises as much as we can to have an on-time performance. We go overboard, not to meet regulations but looking after our passengers with even the way we handle difficult challenges. The frequency of cancelling flights happens but not too often. There are reasons why flights are cancelled; so when we cancel, delay or reschedule, we give options of other available flights and proceed to refund immediately since we got our Airline Operators Certificate (AOC) in March 2026 and started operating on our own.

As a female pilot, are you afraid when you are 33,000 feet above the ground?

Female pilots are very brave. We have had wonderful ladies that have made success from the everyday job. Capt. Ruth Adebanwo flew an aircraft from Canada to Nigeria. In Enugu Air, our female pilots who I brought from the US, Capt. Queency and Capt Grace flew the planes all over the oceans themselves from America to Nigeria and landed safely. It was amazing, we celebrated them quietly. They did not fly straight on; they flew from America to Canada, to Spain to Mauritania, fuelled, then landed in Nigeria. It was a two-day journey because the maximum hour to fly in a day is 8hrs by regulation. I was happy with how Ibom Air celebrated Capt. Ruth Adebamwo as she flew from Canada to Nigeria. It was a huge break.

How are you managing the business with and without competitions?

It is tough. The truth is that there is over capacity in the market and it is the customer that is enjoying it. Airlines are complaining that they are not able to meet their cost. But because of the competition, customers have become very price sensitive; it is simply a tough season for Nigerian airlines. Everyone is just hanging in there while hoping that the fuel price would come down. When Enugu Air started, we had an entry strategy as new people to sell our tickets at a lower amount just to introduce ourselves into the market and break even, other competitors took the price below us and scattered the business. Today all of us are crying.

Does Enugu Air have the big aircraft that can airlift 300 or more passengers at once?

Not yet, our Embryo carries between 100 and 124 passengers for now.  

When will Enugu Air start its West Coast and international routes?

We have started regional in Douala, Cameroon and will start London soon.

With where you are today, where do you see the Aviation industry in Nigeria in the next 10years?

I still see more people coming in, especially the state governments. The truth is that Nigerian Aviation industry needs some levels of consolidation. If we will be able to consolidate and have one big front like the Ethiopia, Kenyan and Rwanda Airlines, they will be able to compete properly on the continent.  Our airports are congested. Passengers are kings who must be treated nicely because of capacity. The goal is to hope that out of the small airlines, some will be able to sum up and compete on the continent.

Being the CEO of a busy airline, a wife and mother, how do you manage the home front?

My children are not young and they are in school. My husband and I are in the same industry and manage ourselves. We met in school and he has been my number one chair leader and critic as well. As a couple in the same industry, we chose to have some sanity in the house, which is the reason we face the home front mainly when we are back from work.

How was growing up?

Growing up was tough. I lost my dad when I was nine. My mother was a fulltime house wife with seven children, two of her siblings and my half-brother, making us 10 in all. My dad worked with Exxon Mobil, joined politics and became a local government chairman. His presence was missed and felt immediately after his demise. We had to adjust and adjusted well. Today all my siblings, the seven of us, are all doing well in our various professions.

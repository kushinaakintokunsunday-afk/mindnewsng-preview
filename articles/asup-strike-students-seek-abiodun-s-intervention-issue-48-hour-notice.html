ASUP strike: Students seek Abiodun’s intervention, issue 48-hour notice

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The National Association of Polytechnic Students, Ogun State Liaison Council, has renewed its 48-hour notice to the Ogun State Government, demanding urgent inte

The association, which described the prolonged disruption of academic activities as increasingly unbearable for students, called on Governor Dapo Abiodun to personally intervene in the dispute and ensure a peaceful resolution that would facilitate the immediate resumption of academic activities.

Chairman of NAPS, Ogun State Liaison Council, Joshua Opatunmibi, made the appeal on Monday during a press conference at Moshood Abiola Polytechnic, Abeokuta.

Opatunmibi announced that the association had renewed its 48-hour notice, which would commence at 12 noon on Monday, November 16, and expire at 12 noon on Wednesday, November 18, 2026.

He explained that the renewed notice was intended to compel the relevant authorities to demonstrate tangible commitment towards resolving the disagreement between the state government and ASUP, stressing that students could no longer afford to lose valuable academic time.

The NAPS chairman lamented that the industrial action had disrupted academic activities across Ogun State-owned polytechnics, leaving students uncertain about their academic futures while affecting institutional calendars and opportunities for learning and personal development.

He said, “We are here today because we can no longer overlook the consequences of the ongoing industrial action by the Academic Staff Union of Polytechnics (ASUP) in our state.

“The academic activities of students across Ogun State-owned polytechnics have been disrupted. Academic calendars are being affected, students are uncertain about their academic future, and the time that should be invested in learning and personal development is gradually being lost.

“As the representative body of polytechnic students in Ogun State, we consider it our responsibility to speak up. Let me state our position clearly: We stand for the interest of the students.

“We are not here to fight ASUP. We are not here to fight the Ogun State Government. We are not here to create political tension or encourage violence. We are here to appeal for a solution.

“We believe lecturers deserve their legitimate welfare and entitlements. We believe our institutions deserve adequate funding. But we equally believe that students deserve uninterrupted education.These interests should not be in conflict. They should be protected together”.

The student leader expressed concern over the increasing financial burden of education on parents, guardians and students, arguing that the growing cost of accessing tertiary education should be accompanied by improved institutional stability and uninterrupted academic activities.

He observed that many political leaders occupying positions of authority today benefited from free or relatively affordable education, which provided them with opportunities to acquire qualifications, build careers and eventually attain positions of leadership.

According to him, it was unfair for the present generation to face increasing educational expenses while continuing to experience disruptions in the public institutions they depend on.

On the issues underlying the industrial dispute, Opatunmibi said the association had engaged with relevant information and ASUP representatives to understand the concerns responsible for the ongoing strike.

He identified the implementation of applicable allowances under the renegotiated 2026 Federal Government-ASUP agreement, improved funding of state-owned polytechnics, and outstanding pension deductions and contributions as some of the issues raised by the union.

The NAPS chairman specifically referenced the renegotiated agreement, reportedly signed on April 8, 2026, noting that the union was concerned about the implementation of relevant provisions, including the Consolidated Academic Teachers Allowance and Earned Academic Allowance.

He also disclosed that among the demands communicated by ASUP was an increase in the monthly subvention to each state-owned polytechnic from N5 million to N100 million.

While acknowledging that state-owned polytechnics might operate under different financial and administrative arrangements from their federal counterparts, he urged the government to clearly communicate its position on the demands and engage the union towards an acceptable resolution.

Addressing the governor, Opatunmibi said that “Your Excellency, we recognise the responsibilities that come with governing a state. We recognise that government must balance competing demands and financial obligations.

“However, education is too important to be left in prolonged uncertainty. Thousands of students across our state-owned polytechnics are looking up to the government for intervention.

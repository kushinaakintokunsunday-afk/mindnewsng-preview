‘Democracy is not warfare’ – Peter Obi condemns Kwankwaso convoy attack

…Kano govt dismisses attack claims

Presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), Peter Obi has condemned a reported attack on the convoy of his running mate, Senator Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso, in Bichi Local Government Area of Kano State.

Some political thugs allegedly attacked Kwankwaso and members of his entourage on Sunday evening as they were leaving the area after a series of political engagements across parts of the state.

However, the Kano State Government has dismissed the reported attack as a fabrication by the Kwankwasiyya Movement to curry public sympathy.

In a Monday statement, Obi said he was deeply saddened by reports of the attack on Kwankwaso and members of his entourage.

“I strongly condemn this unfortunate incident. Reports indicate that several members of the entourage sustained injuries, while about seven vehicles were vandalised. This is unacceptable and must not be allowed to become part of our political culture,” he stated.

Obi said democracy is not warfare and political differences should never lead to violence.

The former Anambra governor stated, “Democracy is not warfare. Political differences should never be a reason to resort to violence, intimidation or the destruction of property. Every Nigerian, regardless of political affiliation, has the constitutional right to participate in the democratic process without fear of attack.

“We must not allow violence to take the place of dialogue, nor intimidation to silence those who hold different political opinions. Our democracy can only grow when we respect one another, uphold the rule of law and recognise that political competition is not a battle against fellow Nigerians.”

He called on the Nigeria Police Force and all relevant security agencies to immediately investigate the incident, identify those responsible, including anyone found to have sponsored the attack, and ensure they are brought to justice.

“No individual should be above the law, regardless of political position or influence,” Obi said.

He expressed solidarity with Kwankwaso, the Kano State NDC leadership and everyone affected by the incident, and wished those injured a speedy recovery.

“Our commitment to building a new Nigeria remains unwavering. We believe that change must come through peaceful engagement, persuasion, ideas and the ballot, not violence.

“Nigeria belongs to all of us. We may belong to different political parties, but we remain one people with a common destiny. No political ambition is worth the life of any Nigerian,” Obi said.

Meanwhile, the Kano State Government dismissed the reported attack as a fabrication.

In a Sunday post on Facebook, the state Commissioner for Information, Sanusi Bature Dawakin Tofa, accused the Kwankwasiyya movement of inventing the alleged incident in Bichi and challenged its members to provide evidence to substantiate the claim.

He said, “In an attempt to generate public sympathy, the Kwankwasiyya team has framed themselves a fictitious attack in Bichi, no pictures, no videos.”

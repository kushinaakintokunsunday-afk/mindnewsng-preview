Tinubu seeks Reps’ approval to remove 268 obsolete laws

By Punch Punch 4h ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

President Bola Tinubu has asked the House of Representatives to approve the revised and consolidated Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (LFN 2025), following a c

The review covered 932 federal enactments and 731 subsidiary legislations, in what the President described as an effort to modernise Nigeria’s legal framework and make federal laws more coherent, accessible and relevant to contemporary realities.

The Speaker of the House of Representatives, Tajudeen Abbas, read the President’s letter, dated November 18, 2026, during plenary on Tuesday.

The letter, titled ‘Transmission of the Revised and Consolidated Compendium of the Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (LFN 2025) for Consideration and Approval’, followed the recommendations of a technical committee constituted under the supervision of the Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, Lateef Fagbemi.

Tinubu said the exercise was necessary to address the long gap since the last official consolidation of Nigeria’s federal laws in 2004, noting that the country had undergone significant legislative, constitutional, institutional and technological changes in the intervening years.

In the letter addressed to the Speaker, the President said: “I write to formally inform you of the conclusion of the review, update and consolidation of the Laws of the Federation of Nigeria, and to forward herewith the proposed Revised compendium of the Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (LFN 2025) for the kind consideration and approval of the House of Representatives of the Federal Republic of Nigeria.

“You may wish to recall that the last official consolidation of the Laws of the Federation was undertaken in 2004. Over the past twenty (20) years, Nigeria has witnessed profound legislative, constitutional, institutional, and technological transformations.

“These developments have necessitated the enactment of numerous new statutes, substantial amendments to existing laws, and a significant accumulation of subsidiary legislation, making the consolidation of an updated, coherent, and accessible body of Federal laws an urgent national imperative.”

According to Tinubu, a specialised committee was established on October 24, 2024, to conduct a comprehensive review and update of federal laws, identify obsolete and spent enactments, resolve statutory inconsistencies and overlaps, and produce an authoritative modern edition of the country’s laws.

He said the committee examined 932 federal enactments and 731 subsidiary legislations before validating 664 principal enactments for inclusion in the LFN 2025, while recommending the removal of 268 others.

The President added: “In discharging this mandate, the Committee rigorously examined a total of 932 Federal enactments and 731 subsidiary legislations. Following extensive legal research, technical consultations, editorial scrutiny, and quality assurance, 664 principal enactments alongside their relevant subsidiary legislation were validated for inclusion in the LFN 2025, while 268 enactments were identified as obsolete, spent, or repealed.

“An updated, accurate, and easily accessible compendium of Federal laws is indispensable to our democratic and economic growth. Upon enactment, the LFN 2025 will serve as a reliable legal reference for the judiciary, legal practitioners, government institutions, domestic and foreign investors, and the general public, thereby enhancing legal certainty, institutional efficiency, and public confidence in our justice system.”

Tinubu described the exercise as a major deliverable of his administration’s Renewed Hope Agenda, particularly its commitment to justice sector reform, strengthening the rule of law and establishing a legal framework capable of supporting national development.

He urged the lawmakers to expedite consideration and approval of the revised compendium, emphasising its importance to governance and the administration of justice.

“This milestone represents a flagship deliverable of our administration’s Renewed Hope Agenda, particularly as it relates to reforming the justice sector, strengthening the rule of law, and establishing a responsive legal framework that supports sustainable national development.

“In view of the critical importance of this instrument to national governance and the administration of justice, I earnestly urge the Honourable Members of the House of Representatives to expeditiously consider and approve the Revised Edition of the Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (LFN 2025),” the President added.

Revealed: How Atiku’s choice of Okowa triggered feud with Wike

By Punch Punch 3d ago 7 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A new book by TheCable publisher, Simon Kolawole, has shed light on the breakdown of relations between former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar and former Rivers St

In excerpts from ‘Winning Formula: The Inside Story of Nigeria’s 2023 Presidential Election,’ made available to PUNCH Online on Sunday, Kolawole attributed the fallout to Atiku’s decision to choose former Delta State Governor Ifeanyi Okowa as his running mate despite reported assurances to Wike and a committee recommendation favouring him.

The author argued that Atiku’s remarks while announcing Okowa on June 16, 2022, further aggravated the disagreement by leaving Wike feeling humiliated.

He stated that Atiku announced Okowa as his running mate after the Peoples Democratic Party’s presidential primary. In his statement, he said he had consulted widely with party stakeholders and made his selection criteria clear.

He said his running mate must have “the potential to succeed me at a moment’s notice, that is, a president-in-waiting. In other words, the person must have the qualities to be president.”

Kolawole argued that the statement was an indirect slight against Wike, who had also sought the party’s vice-presidential ticket.

“By picking Okowa, he had virtually declared that Chief Nyesom Wike did not have presidential qualities. This was below the belt.

“And that was how a full-scale war broke out,” the author wrote.

The book said Wike, who felt hurt and humiliated, became determined to retaliate, while Atiku remained confident that he could win the election without the Rivers governor’s support.

Kolawole described the ensuing disagreement as a case of “mutually assured destruction”, noting that neither man eventually achieved his presidential ambition.

“Wike wanted a fight to the finish. Atiku was fully set for it. It was a case of mutually assured destruction (MAD).

“But rather than MAD being a deterrent doctrine as is the case with the world’s nuclear powers, it was the tonic for an all-out war between Atiku and Wike. Neither would end up as president or vice-president. The destruction was mutually assured,” the book stated.

The author also explained why Atiku was confident of winning the 2023 election despite the disagreement with Wike, citing his position as the leading northern candidate, his alliances in the South and the alleged support of some allies of former President Muhammadu Buhari.

Kolawole wrote that Atiku believed he had a strong chance of winning because “he was the strongest candidate from the north” and had “a considerably large coalition of allies in the south”.

The book also recounted a series of meetings between the two politicians and their associates in the weeks following the PDP presidential primary in May 2022.

One of the meetings took place at Wike’s residence in Guzape, Abuja, on May 30, 2022, after Atiku and the party’s National Chairman, Iyorchia Ayu, had paid a visit to former Sokoto State Governor Aminu Tambuwal.

Kolawole said Wike was unhappy that Atiku’s first visit after the primary was to Tambuwal, whose withdrawal from the contest had helped Atiku emerge as the party’s presidential candidate.

At the meeting in Wike’s residence, former Minister Adamu Maina Waziri represented Atiku’s camp, while former Ekiti State Governor Ayo Fayose and former Attorney-General of the Federation Mohammed Bello Adoke spoke on behalf of Wike’s camp.

Wike reportedly raised concerns about the PDP’s decision to field a northern presidential candidate despite an understanding among southern governors that power should shift to the South.

The two politicians later held a private discussion in Wike’s study, which they reportedly agreed would remain confidential.

According to Kolawole, Atiku expressed interest in working with Wike as his running mate during the meeting.

A bemused Wike reportedly asked, “In what capacity?” before Atiku responded, “as my running mate”.

Wike, however, reminded Atiku that he had already been told Okowa was the preferred choice.

“But I was told you already promised Okowa,” Wike was quoted as saying, while Atiku reportedly replied, “No, I have not promised anybody.”

The author said Wike’s mood improved following the exchange, and he promised to support the campaign with resources and grassroots mobilisation.

Kolawole wrote that Wike’s optimism was further encouraged by an arrangement for both men to travel in the same vehicle to the PDP national secretariat in Abuja for Atiku’s presentation with his certificate of return on June 1, 2022.

However, the arrangement was reportedly changed on the day of the event, with Wike informed that the governors would travel together in a bus while Atiku would ride separately. Wike subsequently found an excuse not to attend the ceremony.

The book stated that the development made Wike suspect that Atiku was changing his mind about choosing him as running mate.

Another meeting was arranged for June 8, 2022, during which Atiku reportedly told Wike that he would constitute a committee to assess prospective running mates and make a recommendation that would be binding on him.

Kolawole also detailed how the committee’s recommendation reportedly favoured Wike but did not translate into his selection.

“Adoke and Nwogu later briefed Wike on the discussions but encouraged him to still meet with Atiku before taking his next steps. Mallam Abdullahi Nyako, Atiku’s private secretary, put another meeting together on 8 June 2022, the day the APC announced the result of its own primary.

“At the meeting, Atiku explained to Wike that when he picked Mr Peter Obi as his running mate for the 2019 election, senior members of the PDP were unhappy and accused him of acting alone. This time, he said, he was going to constitute a committee that would assess all the prospective candidates and make a recommendation that would be binding on him.

“The 17-member committee, headed by Ambassador Umar Iliya Damagum, the party’s deputy national chairman, voted, and 13 of them, including Tambuwal, picked Wike. But Atiku still did not choose Wike, reportedly at the behest of the PDP elders from the north who did not have a high opinion of the governor,” the book stated.

The author said the decision to pick Okowa, despite the committee’s reported recommendation, further widened the rift between the two politicians.

He explained how the announcement of Okowa’s selection finally ended Wike’s hopes of joining Atiku’s ticket.

“As the announcement of the running mate drew closer, it was clear that Wike was not going to be the choice. Picking Okowa was one thing, but the framing of the announcement more than injured Wike’s ego.

“At this stage, Wike seemed to still be open to some form of accommodation in Atiku’s plans, but it was all a façade. He had lost the presidential primary and had also been overlooked in the choice of a running mate. It was all over,” the book stated.

Kolawole said Wike subsequently decided to stay away from Atiku’s campaign and work against his presidential ambition.

“He began to weigh his options and, by all accounts, decided to snub Atiku and shun the nationwide campaign. Not only would he shun the campaign; he would also work against Atiku with every ounce of energy in him and every kobo in his pocket,” the book stated.

The book, ‘Winning Formula: The Inside Story of Nigeria’s 2023 Presidential Election,’ published by CableBooks, is scheduled to go on sale on Monday, November 23, 2026.

The book description stated that it examined how Bola Tinubu outmanoeuvred former President Muhammadu Buhari, how Atiku and Wike undermined each other’s presidential ambitions, and how Peter Obi and the Obidient movement emerged as a political phenomenon.

It described the publication as “built on insider briefings, court filings and polling data, and told with the pace of a newsroom and the eye of a data analyst”.

The description further stated that the book explored “a story of naivety, cat-and-mouse games, cold calculations, and subterfuge”.

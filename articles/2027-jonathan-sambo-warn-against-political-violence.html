2027: Jonathan, Sambo warn against political violence

By Punch Punch 6d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Former President Goodluck Jonathan and former Vice-President Namadi Sambo have expressed concerns over growing political intolerance ahead of the 2027 general e

Jonathan, founder and chairman of the Goodluck Jonathan Foundation, gave the warning on Tuesday at the 2027 Peace and Security Conference in Abuja, themed “Safeguarding Peace and Strengthening Social Cohesion in a Competitive Political Environment.”

He cautioned that political intolerance, thuggery and ethnic rivalry could undermine national peace and stability.

The former president also raised concerns over conflicting judicial pronouncements, particularly decisions by courts of coordinate jurisdiction, warning that they could undermine public confidence in the judiciary and push citizens towards self-help.

He stated, “As we gradually approach the 2027 general elections, certain developments in our political environment are becoming increasingly worrisome.

“As a former president, I receive calls from Nigerians and friends of our country across the world who express concern about the direction Nigeria is heading. Rather than the confidence and optimism we should be inspiring, there is growing apprehension about the likelihood of crises that may follow the elections. We must not ignore these concerns.

“Among the issues demanding urgent attention is the growing culture of political intolerance. There appears to be an increasing unwillingness among political actors to accommodate opposing views or recognise the legitimate right of others to compete for political power.

“This is compounded by political thuggery, the exploitation of our young people as instruments of violence, and acts of political brigandage. Equally disturbing are controversies surrounding some judicial pronouncements, particularly those involving courts of coordinate jurisdiction.”

Jonathan warned that declining public confidence in the judiciary could lead to citizens taking the law into their own hands, with potentially disastrous consequences for the country’s stability.

Jonathan noted that while the judiciary’s responsibility is to interpret the law and adjudicate disputes, “conflicting decisions on similar matters can undermine public confidence in the institution and create unnecessary tension.”

“We must protect the integrity and independence of our judiciary,” he demanded.

He also cautioned against the use of ethnic sentiments in the 2027 elections, warning that political rivalry could deepen divisions and threaten national unity.

The former President added, “These are precisely the concerns that inform this Conference. We seek to hold a mirror before our political leaders and citizens, encourage reflection and promote a change in conduct that respects our laws, democratic values and shared responsibilities.

“As I observed a few days ago in Lagos, if Nigeria must make meaningful progress, we must review our political recruitment process. Our present system remains vulnerable to ethnic, religious and other primordial considerations, which often prevent competent and credible individuals from emerging as leaders.

“As we approach the 2027 elections, Nigerians will be choosing leaders at both national and subnational levels. We must therefore be deliberate about the quality of people we recruit into public office at both the state and federal levels. The future of our country depends on the choices we make. Leadership is very critical in the growth of any nation

“Let us choose competence over prejudice, service over personal ambition, and national interest over narrow considerations. Our democracy must produce leaders capable of uniting our people and advancing our collective development.”

Earlier in his remarks, Sambo, who is the Chairman of the occasion, urged political leaders to view political differences as opportunities for constructive engagement rather than sources of division.

Sambo called for inclusive governance, responsible leadership and respect for the rule of law, stressing the need to build bridges across ethnic, religious and political differences to strengthen national cohesion.

He stated, “As a nation, we must continually remind ourselves that political differences should be viewed as opportunities for constructive engagement rather than sources of division. Our collective responsibility is to promote tolerance, inclusive governance, responsible political leadership and respect for the rule of law.

“Having had the privilege of serving our country at the highest level of government, I strongly believe that national cohesion must transcend political affiliation and personal interest.

“We must deliberately build bridges across our ethnic, religious and political differences and create an environment where every Nigerian feels valued, protected and represented.”

Sambo expressed optimism that the discussions and recommendations at the conference would strengthen public trust, democratic institutions and national unity, stressing that “Our democracy must be competitive, but our nation must remain united.”

In her remarks, the Executive Director for the Goodluck Jonathan Foundation, Ann Iyonu, warned that insecurity, economic pressures and declining public trust could heighten political tensions ahead of the 2027 elections.

She cited an Afrobarometer survey showing that fewer than four in 10 Nigerians believe the 2027 polls will be free and fair, warning that electoral violence could threaten regional stability.

Political attacks on candidates and supporters of major parties have intensified as Nigeria prepares for the 2027 general elections.

The convoy of President Bola Tinubu, the All Progressives Congress presidential candidate, came under attack on November 13, 2026, shortly after he observed Jumu’ah prayers in Garki, Abuja, with some individuals reportedly throwing stones and other objects at it.

On November 15, 2026, suspected thugs attacked the campaign convoy of Nigeria Democratic Congress vice-presidential candidate, Senator Rabiu Kwankwaso, and its Kano governorship candidate, Aminu Gwarzo, in Bichi Local Government Area, leaving several vehicles damaged and supporters injured.

Earlier in November, the convoy of Nigeria Democratic Congress presidential candidate, Peter Obi, was attacked in Calabar, Cross River State, while some of his supporters were reportedly assaulted at a Lagos town hall meeting last week.

Also, Supporters of African Democratic Congress presidential hopeful, Atiku Abubakar, have also reportedly faced attacks in Edo, Kaduna and Cross River states.

Similarly, African Action Congress presidential candidate, Omoyele Sowore, and his entourage were attacked on October 30, 2026, at a restaurant in Ado-Ekiti, Ekiti State. The Allied Peoples Movement and other parties have also reported attacks on their supporters.

NNPC, Renaissance seek fresh funding for pipeline infrastructure

By Vanguard Vanguard 6d ago 4 min read

By Obas Esiedesa, Abuja

The Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPC) and Renaissance Africa Energy Company Limited have called for innovative financing mechanisms, stronger partnerships and improved security to attract investments into Nigeria’s pipeline infrastructure.

They said reliable pipeline networks were critical to strengthening the country’s energy security, maximising its hydrocarbon resources and driving industrial development.

The call was made at the 2026 Nigeria International Pipeline Technology and Security Conference (NIPITECS), organised by the Pipelines Professionals Association of Nigeria (PLAN) in Abuja.

Speaking on behalf of the NNPC Group Chief Executive Officer, Engr. Bayo Ojulari, the Managing Director of NNPC Gas Infrastructure Company (NGIC), Mr Audu Ibrahim, described pipelines as a critical link in the oil and gas value chain.

He warned that inadequate transportation infrastructure could undermine Nigeria’s efforts to harness its hydrocarbon resources, noting that the upstream, midstream and downstream segments of the industry were interconnected.

According to him, gas fields without pipelines to power plants, refineries without adequate feedstock and fertiliser plants without reliable gas supply could become stranded economic opportunities.

Ibrahim highlighted progress on major pipeline projects, including the 105-kilometre Oben-Ajaokuta-Obigbo (OB3) pipeline, the 640-kilometre Ajaokuta-Kaduna-Kano (AKK) pipeline and the West African Gas Pipeline.

He said the challenging River Niger crossing on the OB3 project was completed on February 26, 2026, while pre-commissioning activities were concluded in August, positioning the facility for first gas delivery.

On the AKK pipeline, designed to transport 2.2 billion standard cubic feet of gas daily, Ibrahim said mainline welding had been completed, with the pipeline reaching Abuja and undergoing commissioning.

He put the project’s completion level at approximately 95 per cent, adding that NNPC was targeting first gas delivery to Abuja before the end of 2026, followed by Kaduna and Kano.

The NGIC managing director disclosed that NNPC had raised about $1.5 billion against projected pipeline revenues, demonstrating the potential of properly structured pipeline infrastructure to attract financing independently of upstream and downstream projects.

He further disclosed that Nigeria’s gas production had reached 7.5 billion standard cubic feet per day, against a national target of 10 billion standard cubic feet daily, with the country holding more than 215 trillion cubic feet of gas reserves.

Ibrahim attributed improved pipeline performance to stronger collaboration among operators, host communities and security agencies, saying the country had achieved 100 per cent pipeline availability for the first time in two decades.

Also speaking, the Vice President, Projects and Engineering, Renaissance Africa Energy Company Limited, Mr Abimbola Tijani, who represented the company’s Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer, Engr. Tony Attah, said pipelines should be regarded as strategic economic infrastructure rather than merely technical assets for transporting oil and gas.

He identified three priorities for attracting investments into the sector: recognising pipelines as critical national infrastructure, building investor confidence through integrity, security and partnerships, and developing a diversified financing ecosystem supported by predictable regulatory frameworks.

Tijani said Nigeria’s ambition to sustainably increase crude oil production to three million barrels per day and expand domestic gas utilisation would depend significantly on investments in reliable pipeline networks.

He reaffirmed Renaissance’s commitment to securing Africa’s energy future and supporting Nigeria’s industrialisation, expressing optimism that the country could develop an extensive gas network connecting communities and households to cleaner energy.

Earlier, PLAN President, Engr. Ngozi Adeleke, said the theme of the conference, “Financing the Future: Unlocking Capital for Resilient Pipeline Infrastructure in Nigeria,” reflected the urgent need to mobilise investments to support increased hydrocarbon production, domestic gas utilisation and industrial growth.

She noted that pipeline development required substantial capital, innovative financing solutions, regulatory support, advanced technology and effective security frameworks.

Adeleke said PLAN would continue to promote professionalism, operational excellence, safety, infrastructure integrity and capacity development.

She also called for stronger collaboration among industry operators, financiers, regulators, technology companies and host communities to strengthen Nigeria’s pipeline infrastructure and improve the sector’s capacity to support economic growth.

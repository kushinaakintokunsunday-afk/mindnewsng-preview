Bayelsa tragedy: Senate demands nationwide audit of boats, ferries

By Punch Punch 17h ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Senate has called for an urgent nationwide audit of boats and ferries operating on Nigeria’s waterways following the death of 29 people in a boat accident i

The lawmakers blamed the November 20 disaster on overloading and poor safety standards, urging the Nigerian Inland Waterways Authority and other regulatory agencies to strengthen inspections and enforce safety regulations to prevent further accidents.

The resolutions followed a motion moved on Tuesday by Senator Benson Konbowei, representing Bayelsa Central, who described the incident as shocking and unfortunate.

Leading the debate, Konbowei said the ill-fated boat was carrying more than 300 passengers, far above its capacity, before its weak structure reportedly broke apart and capsized.

He said, “The boat had more than 300 passengers on board, which was more than its old and weak frame could comfortably carry, and it broke apart, capsized and threw the passengers into the river.”

The lawmaker disclosed that 29 bodies had been recovered, while divers and other rescue workers were still searching for missing passengers.

“After recovering 29 dead bodies so far, divers and other rescue and recovery workers are still combing the river beds in search of corpses,” he said.

He added that several survivors had been taken to hospitals across the state for treatment.

Konbowei said the accident highlighted the dangers of relying on poorly regulated water transportation, particularly in Bayelsa, where inadequate road networks had made boats the major means of transportation for many communities.

He called on the Federal Government to support critical road projects, particularly the proposed Yenagoa-Oporoma Road, which he said was recommended in the 1958 Willink Commission Report.

The senator commended Governor Douye Diri for his response to the disaster and President Bola Tinubu for sending Vice-President Kashim Shettima to the state.

Shettima had announced a donation of N250m to support the state government’s disaster management efforts and affected families.

Seconding the motion, Senator Simon Lalong, representing Plateau South, commended the state and Federal Governments for their response.

“We watched him [Diri] on television. I also commend the response of the Federal Government and particularly the visit of the Vice President to Bayelsa State that gave a lot of relief to some of those people that were affected,” he said.

Lalong, however, urged Nigerians to take water transportation safety seriously, particularly in communities where travelling by boat was unavoidable.

“We are always lamenting. This is a natural gift from God, water, but we must be very, very careful,” Lalong stated.

Also contributing, the Deputy Senate President, Barau Jibrin, urged regulatory agencies to conduct routine inspections of boats and ferries to ensure compliance with safety standards.

He said the country must move beyond expressing condolences after accidents and take practical steps to prevent their recurrence.

“It’s really sad, really sad, but what can one do when God has decided on what should happen? And once it happens, the only thing we can do is to look at ways to prevent the occurrence of such a situation in the future,” he said.

Barau said regular inspections would help identify unserviceable vessels and prevent overloading.

“I think if that was the case, if there were routine checks, I’m sure this kind of calamity will not be occurring at the rate they are occurring in our country,” he said.

He added, “We are not indicting them, but the fact is that they should do more in ensuring that things are done the way they should be done in terms of ensuring that regulations that guide the waterway transportation system are really adhered to.”

Similarly, Senator Sunday Marshall Katung, representing Kaduna South, called for a comprehensive investigation into the accident, saying it exposed weaknesses in the country’s water transportation regulatory system.

He said, “There are gaps in the regulatory framework that have given rise to situations such as this, and I want to urge that the relevant committee of the Senate needs to step in and conduct investigations.”

Katung also demanded an audit of all vessels operating in the country’s territorial waters to establish their condition and suitability for carrying passengers.

“The relevant authorities should step in quickly and ensure that there is an audit of all vessels that are operating within our territorial waters to ensure that they are fit and worthy of operating, particularly as they carry passengers,” he stated.

In his remarks, the Senate President, Godswill Akpabio, commended Tinubu for his response to the tragedy, saying the President had demonstrated concern for the welfare of Nigerians.

“I just want to personally commend the President for swiftly empathising with the people, showing compassion and also showing that he cares for the well-being of Nigerians and not just the politics of the country,” he said.

Akpabio also disclosed that a political rally had been cut short following news of the accident because the country was mourning the victims.

“Since we were already at the rally, there was no point cancelling it. We just went in, greeted the people and walked away because, as far as we were concerned at that moment, the nation was in mourning. There was no point continuing with the rally,” he stated.

The Senate President urged rescue teams to continue searching for missing passengers, particularly in the difficult-to-access mangrove areas of Bayelsa.

“We pray for the safe recovery of those who probably are still in the waters because they could swim and they are trying to get to safer areas. I hope that the rescue operation should still continue because some may still be within the mangrove swamps of Bayelsa and are not dead and waiting to be rescued,” he said.

Akpabio also blamed the accident on overloading, insisting that boat operators must observe approved passenger limits.

“This clearly was as a result of overloading, and if we do not carry overload and we restrict to the capacity of the boat, this would never have happened,” he said.

The Senate unanimously adopted seven prayers contained in the motion, including observing a minute’s silence for the victims and commending the Federal and Bayelsa State Governments for their interventions.

The lawmakers also urged the Federal Government to support the construction of the Yenagoa-Oporoma Road and other critical roads in the state to provide safer alternatives to water transportation.

They further called on NIWA and other regulatory agencies to enforce safety standards, while the National Emergency Management Agency was urged to provide relief materials and other assistance to survivors and affected families.

The chamber subsequently observed a minute’s silence in honour of those who died in the accident.

China, Nigeria strengthen cultural ties through music

By Punch Punch 2d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Nigeria and China have stepped up cultural diplomacy through music and people-to-people exchanges, with the Chinese Cultural centre disclosing that it has organ

The China Cultural Centre Counsellor, Yang Jianxing, disclosed this at the final of the 2026 Nigeria Chinese and English Singing Competition, on Saturday, where he described music as a powerful instrument for bridging cultural differences and strengthening bilateral relations.

Yang said the competition, which brought together Nigerian and Chinese participants, provided an opportunity for young people from both countries to showcase their talents, appreciate each other’s cultures and build lasting friendships.

“Music is a universal language of humanity and a bridge that connects different civilisations and brings people closer together. A song can transcend linguistic and national boundaries, conveying emotions, fostering mutual understanding and building lasting friendships,” he said.

He listed the cultural activities organised by the embassy and the China Cultural Centre in Nigeria to include the Happy Chinese New Year Temple Fair, Tea for Harmony: Yaji Cultural Salon and the China-Nigeria Food Festival.

According to him, the activities had created opportunities for citizens of both countries to interact, understand their respective cultures and strengthen bilateral friendship.

“These activities have created more opportunities for our two peoples to get to know each other and strengthen their friendship,” Yang said.

He added that the Chinese Embassy and the China Cultural Centre would continue collaborating with Nigerian institutions to organise more cultural programmes and deepen relations between the two countries.

Yang also described the competition as a practical demonstration of the Global Civilisation Initiative proposed by Chinese President Xi Jinping, which promotes respect for cultural diversity and stronger international exchanges.

He urged the contestants to sustain the spirit of friendship beyond the competition and contribute to advancing relations between both countries.

“I also hope that you will take this event as a new starting point and continue to make positive contributions, in your respective fields, to advancing the China-Nigeria comprehensive strategic partnership and bringing greater benefits to the peoples of our two countries,” he said.

Earlier, the Director-General of the China General Chamber of Commerce in Nigeria, Zhang Xinyu, thanked the Chinese Embassy for supporting the competition and commended the judges and organisers for their efforts.

He encouraged the contestants to relax, give their best performances and enjoy the opportunity to express themselves through music.

“Music knows no borders, and songs build bridges,” Zhang said, wishing the participants success.

Also speaking, the National President of the Society of Nigerian Artists, Muhammad Sulaiman, described the competition as more than a musical performance, saying it represented a meeting of hearts and an opportunity to strengthen understanding between the two countries.

He said the event demonstrated that people could build meaningful relationships despite differences in language, culture and geographical backgrounds.

Sulaiman expressed hope that the China Cultural Centre in Nigeria would sustain and expand its collaborations with Nigerian institutions.

He called for the spirit of unity demonstrated at the competition to be extended to communities and future generations, emphasising that music could promote understanding, friendship and peace.

“Because when we sing together, we heal together. We stand in the storm, we stand in all weather. We build a bridge that no wind can break. A bridge of music for friendship’s sake,” he said.

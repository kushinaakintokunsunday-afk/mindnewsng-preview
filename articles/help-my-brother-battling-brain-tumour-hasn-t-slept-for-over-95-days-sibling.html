Help! My brother battling brain tumour hasn’t slept for over 95 days – Sibling

By Punch Punch 3d ago 10 min read

Ayomide, the younger brother of a Lagos-based executive assistant, Oluwagbemiga Olajide, who has been battling frequent seizures and severe headaches as a result of an aggressive brain tumour, tells AYOOLA OLASUPO about the family’s painful ordeal and urgent need for medical and financial intervention

Can you take us back to the last time you noticed that something was wrong with him?

It started with a constant headache. He used to complain about having headaches, but we usually dismissed them because we thought they were normal, perhaps due to work-related stress. It wasn’t until September, when one side of his eye started closing up, that we became seriously concerned. That was when we decided to go to the hospital for further examinations. It was in the hospital that we discovered that the situation was much more serious than we had thought.

What kind of work did he do before the ailment started?

My brother worked as an executive assistant.

Aside from the headache, were there any other symptoms or signs that you noticed before his eye started swelling?

No, it was only the severe headache.

What transpired at the hospital when you got there?

At first, they thought the problem was with his eyes. They believed that perhaps some of the nerves in his eyes were affected. However, when the condition persisted, they asked us to undergo MRI scans. One of the MRI scans revealed that there was fluid affecting his eyes. Since then, we have been moving from one hospital to another. They would refer us to one hospital, and when we got there, they would tell us to go to another hospital. It has been a continuous process of referrals.

Can you remember how much you have spent on the tests, drugs and treatment so far?

We have done several tests. For instance, the first MRI scan was done. We also conducted several other tests, although I cannot remember all their names. While carrying out these tests, we were also asked to get several other things. Altogether, we have spent more than N3 million before they were finally able to detect the location of the tumour.

We spent all that money just to get to that point. Now, the hospital where he is currently receiving treatment is asking us to pay N10 million for the surgery and post-surgery care. But we do not know how to get such an amount. We’ve really tried our best, and also spent a lot already. Right now, my brother cannot sleep because the headache and seizure are very constant. He has been unable to sleep properly for more than 95 days now.

Can you remember the last time he was able to sleep?

I think it was a long time ago. That was the last time he slept properly.

How has he been managing and coping with the situation?

Honestly, it is only God who has kept him alive until now because he experiences seizures, and they are very frequent. He can have seizures as many as 10 times a day. His entire body will start shaking, and he will be unable to do anything. He will just be screaming, but he is really strong. Some people in similar situations would have passed away, but he is still alive today. They have certain medications they administer when they notice that the seizures are becoming severe. I do not know the names of all the medications. Sometimes, after two or three hours, the seizure starts again. They have told us that the tumour is very aggressive. One side of his face is now paralysed.

Right now, his wife is pregnant and currently carrying his first child, and the entire family cannot sleep. As a pregnant woman, she should be getting enough rest, but she cannot. She has been trying, but it has not been easy. We are begging everybody to please help us. The financial stress, the pain he is going through, and the emotional stress are all overwhelming. Everything has been difficult. Whatever assistance anyone can offer, we sincerely appeal to them to please help us.

What other efforts have the family made towards his recovery?

We have made several efforts. We even travelled to Ibadan before coming to Lagos. We tried to seek help through different channels, but we were told that getting attention through those channels could take about a month. Considering the seriousness of his condition, and how quickly it was deteriorating, we could not afford to wait that long. We knew that we could not keep him at home because he needed immediate medical attention. That was why we decided to take him to a private hospital here in Ibadan. We were fortunate because there was a neurosurgeon available that day. We were told that getting access to the surgeon was not always easy, but we were lucky to meet him. We explained everything to the neurosurgeon. At that moment, my brother was emotional and started crying because of the condition he had found himself in.

But the neurosurgeon explained some things to us and mentioned that another appointment would be necessary. My brother was concerned and asked why there would need to be another appointment when his condition was so serious, and he needed to be admitted. The neurosurgeon also discussed the possibility of carrying out a procedure. He explained that there were different options, but the situation was very complicated. Since then, we have been spending money on his treatment, including injections and other medications. As his brother, I hardly sleep. I look at my brother and see the pain he is going through, and there is nothing I can do to take that pain away. Nobody should have to live in pain every single day, for 24 hours, and then wake up the following day only to experience the same thing again.

That is the kind of life he is living now. Honestly, I would not wish such a situation on anybody, not even my enemy. He is in pain almost every moment of the day. He takes several injections and medications, but sometimes they do not seem to provide lasting relief because the tumour is so aggressive. A few days ago, something happened that gave us a little hope. He sent me a message in the middle of the night, around 4:30 a.m. I was worried because whenever I do not hear from him, I become anxious. But this time, he told me that he had slept very well. He was so happy because he had managed to sleep for several hours, possibly about seven hours.

I was also very happy because I knew how much he had been struggling to sleep. When we saw him the following day, he was talking and was relatively calm. He was still experiencing some pain, but it was not as severe as before. He seemed a little relieved, and he was happy. Unfortunately, things changed again. I received a call the following morning. I had been trying to get some of his injections and medications, and I was informed that he had experienced another seizure. I was worried because I had thought he was getting better. When I eventually saw him, he was very weak. He was shaking, and his eyes were not responding normally. One of his eyes was partially shut, while the other one was just looking at a spot without blinking. I was scared, so I called his name several times, but he did not answer. He was just staring at the floor, so I became confused and frightened. I kept wondering what I was supposed to do. Should I go and call the doctor? My brother was no longer responding to me. I tried to get him to speak, but he could not. The doctor had already explained to us that the tumour was very aggressive and that his condition was serious.

Before that, there was a point when he sent me a message explaining how he was feeling. He said his brain felt like it was closing up and that his breathing was not normal. I showed the message to the doctor and explained what my brother was experiencing. The doctor explained that his condition was critical and that anything could happen at this moment. They also explained that they were still trying to arrange the necessary procedure, and that we needed to act urgently. We are also trying our best but the situation is now overwhelming for us. That is why we’ve come out to start begging people to come to our aid. I don’t want my brother to die. The doctor told us that a biopsy was necessary, but the hospital did not have the facility to carry it out at the time. I asked whether there was another way we could get it done, and they explained that we would have to pay for the procedure. The biopsy involves taking a sample of the tumour for examination. They need to carry out that procedure before deciding on the next steps in his treatment.

Even when the doctor gave my brother options, should they draw the tumour out from his nose or open up his head? He chose the latter instead because he cannot withstand the pain anymore. He prefers that they should just open up his head and take away the entire tumour once and for all. When I look at him sometimes I also pity him because of the kind of pain he passes through every time.

How much is needed for the surgery procedure?

To be honest, we have been trying everything possible. We have spent so much money. We have spent up to N4 million so far. The daily expenses involved in taking care of him are enormous. We buy drugs, injections, and other medical supplies, and we pay for different procedures and services. What I want to do now is appeal to Nigerians for help. This is my brother, who just got engaged, and the family had been looking forward to a happy future for him.

His introduction ceremony was only a few months ago, and everyone in the family was happy.

He is a young, intelligent, smart, and responsible man who has never deliberately hurt anyone. Now, his wife is pregnant, and they are expecting their first child. I do not want to find myself in a situation where I have to raise my brother’s child because he is no longer around. I want him and his wife to live happily together and raise their child.

I want Nigerians to hear our appeal and help us in whatever way they can. We need financial assistance to meet his medical needs and ensure that he gets the treatment he urgently requires. He is intelligent, hardworking, and responsible. He has worked with different organisations and has always tried to provide for his family. His family has also been looking out for him, but this situation has become overwhelming for us all. We are putting God first and trusting Him for a miracle. We sincerely appeal to Nigerians, individuals, organisations, and anyone who can help us to please come to our aid.

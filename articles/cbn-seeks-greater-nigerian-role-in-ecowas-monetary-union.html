CBN seeks greater Nigerian role in ECOWAS monetary union

By Punch Punch 1d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Central Bank of Nigeria has called for greater Nigerian representation in the governance of the proposed Economic Community of West African States Monetary

The CBN Governor, Olayemi Cardoso, made the call on Friday at the ministerial session of the sixth Presidential Task Force Meeting on the ECOWAS Single Currency Programme in Abidjan, Côte d’Ivoire.

His position comes as West African countries intensify preparations for the phased introduction of the ECO single currency in 2027.

A statement issued by the CBN on Sunday read, “The Governor of the Central Bank of Nigeria, Mr Olayemi Cardoso, has called for equity, fairness and proportional representation in the governance structure of the proposed ECOWAS Monetary Union.

“He stressed that while regional solidarity and respect for the sovereign equality of member states must remain fundamental principles of the Union, these should be appropriately balanced with the relative financial commitments, responsibilities and risks borne by individual member states, particularly in determining participation in decision-making and governance.”

Cardoso argued that the governance structure must reflect the economic importance, financial commitments and risks borne by participating countries.

“While bigger economies continue to take up the burden, they should also be considered to play a significant role because of the risk and systemic importance they have in the sub-region,” Cardoso said.

“Above all, such economies need to defend the huge contribution they make before their respective parliaments and the civil society. Where obligations are shared but influence is not, arrangements may be agreed, but their long-term sustainability may be difficult to guarantee,” he added.

The governor maintained that regional solidarity and the sovereign equality of member states should remain fundamental principles of the proposed union but must be balanced against their financial responsibilities.

He explained that Nigeria’s position was not motivated by a desire to dominate other countries but by its responsibilities as the region’s largest economy and a major contributor to the monetary integration project.

According to him, Nigeria would commit substantial financial resources, foreign reserves and institutional capital to the proposed union, making it necessary to align governance participation with financial exposure.

Cardoso warned that decisions on the location of monetary institutions, leadership appointments and the allocation of authority would have long-term implications for the credibility and sustainability of the ECO.

“The decisions we take today will shape the future of the Union for decades to come,” he told delegates.

“They will influence its credibility in the eyes of investors and development partners, its legitimacy in the eyes of our citizens, and the confidence of Member States that their commitments are being matched by a meaningful voice within the institutions they are helping to build.”

He reaffirmed Nigeria’s commitment to the ECOWAS Monetary Cooperation Programme, citing its financial contributions, technical support and compromises made to promote regional consensus.

“Nigeria has and will continue to demonstrate solidarity and fairness, respecting the sovereignty of other member states. We remain fully committed to the successful establishment of the ECO and to a Monetary Union that is credible, sustainable and beneficial to all Member States,” Cardoso said.

He urged participating countries to negotiate with openness and mutual respect, warning against arrangements that impose substantial obligations on countries without giving them meaningful participation in governance.

The latest discussions follow years of delays in the proposed common currency, which is intended to deepen regional economic integration, facilitate cross-border transactions and reduce monetary fragmentation.

In July 2026, ECOWAS leaders reaffirmed their commitment to introducing the ECO in 2027, agreeing that implementation would begin with countries meeting the required macroeconomic convergence criteria.

The phased approach means that not every member state will necessarily adopt the currency at the same time. Countries that are not initially ready are expected to receive support to participate later.

The convergence requirements include conditions on inflation, fiscal deficits and monetary stability, intended to ensure that participating economies can operate under a common monetary framework.

Cardoso maintained that a sustainable monetary union must give every participating country a meaningful stake in its success while ensuring that financial responsibilities and decision-making powers are fairly distributed.

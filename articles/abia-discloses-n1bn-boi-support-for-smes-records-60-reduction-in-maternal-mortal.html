Abia discloses N1bn BOI support for SMEs, records 60% reduction in maternal mortality

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 18 Nov 2026 3 min read

From Okey Sampson, Umuahia

The Abia State Government has disclosed that the Bank of Industry (BOI) has so far given approval of N1.041 billion for businesses operating in the state under the SME support initiative of Governor Alex Otti’s administration.

The state has also recorded a 60 percent reduction in maternal mortality between 2024 and 2026, making Abia to emerge as the state with the second-lowest maternal mortality rate nationwide.

The Commissioner for Information, Prince Okey Kanu, made the disclosure at Government House Umuahia while briefing journalists on the outcome of this week’s state executive meeting.

The commissioner, represented by the Special Adviser to the Governor on Media and Publicity, Mr Ferdinand Ekeoma, said the fund was aimed at strengthening small businesses, creating jobs and expanding economic opportunities. He noted that the sum of ₦206.7 million represent applications that were approved but are currently undergoing documentations for drawing from the funds.

Kanu informed that in line with Goal 8 of the Sustainable Development Goals that speaks to the promotion of sustained, inclusive economic growth, full and productive employment, and decent work for all, the Ministry of Industries and SME’s has so far verified and documented over 110 workshops and 120 shops at the Ohiya- Umuogo- Nsukwe Industrial Cluster.

The information commissioner announced that the state government has approved a review of the remuneration of ad-hoc workers engaged by the Abia State Environmental Protection Agency (ASEPA), with the revised payment taking effect from November 2026. Prince Kanu also disclosed that the Abia State 2027 budget was almost ready for presentation to the state House of Assembly.

The Commissioner for Industries and SMEs, Mike Akpara, explained that out of the approved facilities, ₦382.9 million has already been disbursed to 29 micro entrepreneurs and two small and medium-scale enterprises across different sectors of the state’s economy, while about ₦206 million is awaiting the fulfilment of conditions precedent to drawdown.

Akpara explained that the outstanding facilities would be released once beneficiaries complete the necessary documentation, including the provision of guarantors and collateral, where applicable. He stressed that the intervention was designed to support genuine businesses in Abia, irrespective of their political affiliations.

Meanwhile, Abia State has recorded a 60 percent reduction in maternal mortality between 2024 and 2026.

Ekeoma, who spoke on behalf of the Commissioner for Information, disclosed that the development, which has made the state emerge as the state with the second-lowest maternal mortality rate nationwide, was as a result of the ongoing reforms in the health sector, stressing that it reflected the government’s commitment to strengthening healthcare delivery and expanding access to quality medical services.

He revealed that 190 out of the 200 Project Ekwueme Primary Healthcare Centres (PHCs) earmarked for renovation had been renovated, while 123 had been functionalised to provide essential healthcare services. He added that 27 of the centres had been solarised, with work ongoing on nine others, stating that all 77 World Bank Impact health projects had been completed.

The commissioner further announced that the National Nursing and Midwifery Council, in its accreditation report released on November 13, had increased the admission quota of the Aba Campus of the College of Nursing Sciences from 75 to 120 students.

He said the increase reflected growing recognition of the state’s investments and reforms in the health sector, particularly in strengthening healthcare manpower development.

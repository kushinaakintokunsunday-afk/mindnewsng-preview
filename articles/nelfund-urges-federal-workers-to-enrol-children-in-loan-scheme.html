NELFUND urges federal workers to enrol children in loan scheme

By Punch Punch 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Nigerian Education Loan Fund has launched a fresh drive to expand access to its interest-free student loan scheme, targeting children and wards of federal c

The Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of NELFUND, Akintunde Sawyerr, made this known on Wednesday during a sensitisation exercise at the Federal Ministry of Defence in Abuja, where he urged workers to encourage eligible children in public tertiary institutions to apply for the loans.

Sawyerr said the campaign was part of efforts to deepen awareness of the scheme across the 34 federal ministries before extending it to their departments and agencies.

He explained that the initiative was designed to remove financial barriers to higher education, reduce student dropouts and promote equal access to tertiary education.

According to him, NELFUND offers loans for institutional fees and monthly upkeep, with students allowed to choose whether to access both components or only the institutional fee support.

“While the institutional fee component is compulsory for applicants seeking support, the upkeep allowance is optional,” he said.

Also speaking, the Director of SERVICOM at NELFUND, Olusola Fowobaje, said the campaign would address misconceptions about the scheme and reassure Nigerians of its credibility.

He said the funds were drawn from public resources, including taxpayers’ contributions, and should be accessible to eligible Nigerians.

In a technical presentation, the Fund’s Director of Monitoring and Evaluation, Aliyu Mohammed, said applicants must be Nigerian citizens aged at least 18, have admission into a recognised public tertiary institution, and possess a National Identification Number and Bank Verification Number.

He disclosed that beneficiaries could receive N20,000 monthly as upkeep allowance, while approved institutional fees would be paid directly to their schools.

Mohammed added that repayment would begin two years after beneficiaries completed the National Youth Service Corps programme, provided they had secured employment.

Earlier, the Director of Education Services at the Ministry of Defence, Markus Kura, urged ministry staff to pay attention to the presentation, saying, “Our job today is to be enlightened because, they say, knowledge is power.”

The ministry’s Director of Reforms, Ogidi Paul, who gave the closing remarks, urged the workers to become ambassadors of the Fund by sharing information about the scheme with others.

The NELFUND scheme, established under the Student Loans (Access to Higher Education) Act, 2024, provides interest-free financial assistance to eligible students in public tertiary institutions to cover institutional fees and upkeep.

The programme has continued to expand since its application portal opened in May 2024. As of November 12, 2026, the Fund had disbursed N375.28bn to 1,675,474 students across 320 tertiary institutions, comprising N194.77bn for institutional fees and N180.51bn in upkeep allowances. It had also received about 2.45 million applications.

The latest campaign reflects the Fund’s efforts to enlist parents, guardians and government employees in promoting awareness of the scheme and expanding access to education financing.

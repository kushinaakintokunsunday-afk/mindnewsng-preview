FG unveils five-year drug control plan, seeks emergency response

By Punch Punch 1d ago 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has called for drug abuse and illicit drug trafficking to be treated as a national emergency, warning that the menace poses serious threa

Vice-President Kashim Shettima made the call on Monday during the official launch of the National Drug Control Master Plan 2026–2030.

Shettima, who was represented by the Special Adviser to the President on Special Duties, Office of the Vice-President, Modibbo Umar, said drug control must remain a national priority, stressing that the challenge extends beyond drug seizures and arrests of suspected traffickers.

He said, “That is why drug control must remain a national priority. As a matter of fact, it should be treated as a national emergency.

“The challenge before us goes beyond the consignments intercepted by our agencies or the criminal networks brought before our courts. It reaches into our homes, our schools, our workplaces, and our communities.”

Shettima noted that drug abuse affects families, workforce productivity, and the future of young Nigerians, adding that the country must confront the problem with determination and compassion.

According to him, the drug problem should be understood as a public health concern, security threat, development challenge and economic issue.

He said the response must go beyond enforcement to include prevention, early intervention, treatment, rehabilitation and recovery, while security agencies must intensify intelligence-led operations to dismantle trafficking networks.

“These dimensions are bound together. We cannot rely on enforcement alone and neglect prevention. We cannot promote treatment without improving access to appropriate services.

“We cannot disrupt trafficking networks while ignoring the financial structures that sustain them. And we cannot expect lasting results when institutions work in isolation or lack the resources to discharge their responsibilities,” he said.

The Vice-President said the new master plan builds on the experience of the 2021–2025 framework, urging relevant institutions to consolidate previous achievements, address implementation gaps and respond to emerging threats.

He stressed that the success of the plan would depend on effective implementation rather than the unveiling of the policy document.

“The worth of a national plan lies not merely in the priorities it sets, but in the change it produces—in how institutions conduct themselves, how resources are allocated, and how people’s lives are improved,” he added.

Shettima called on federal ministries, departments and agencies, state and local governments to take responsibility for implementing the plan, noting that the National Assembly, judiciary, health and education institutions also had critical roles to play.

He further identified traditional rulers, religious leaders, civil society organisations, the private sector, parents, teachers and young Nigerians as essential partners in tackling the menace.

“No single institution, however capable, can overcome this challenge alone,” he said.

He said the impact of the master plan would ultimately be measured by improved access to treatment, effective prevention, stronger institutional cooperation, disruption of criminal networks and better living conditions for Nigerians.

“Let this be the moment Nigeria moves from policy to action, from fragmented efforts to coordinated national impact, and from commitment on paper to measurable improvements in the lives of our people,” he said.

Speaking earlier, the Chairman of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency, Brig. Gen. Mohamed Buba Marwa (retd.), said the new master plan was designed to build on previous achievements while addressing identified gaps and emerging threats.

Marwa said the summative evaluation of the 2021–2025 National Drug Control Master Plan recorded 77,736 arrests, the seizure of more than 14.8 million kilogrammes of drugs and over 14,200 convictions.

He added that more than 32,400 people also benefited from treatment, counselling and rehabilitation services during the period.

According to him, the evaluation identified gaps in coordination, financing, service delivery, data management, monitoring and accountability, as well as weak institutional capacity at state and local government levels.

He said the new plan was designed “to keep what worked, fix what did not, and confront what is emerging.”

Marwa explained that the framework would better integrate drug supply and demand reduction while targeting the financial and technological systems sustaining illicit drug markets.

He said the approach would strengthen the agency’s capacity to confront sophisticated trafficking networks, synthetic drugs, new psychoactive substances and criminal syndicates exploiting encrypted communications and cryptocurrency.

Also speaking, the Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, Lateef Fagbemi, SAN, described the NDLEA as “the bedrock of our national drug response.”

He said the new master plan would consolidate the agency’s achievements into a whole-of-government framework integrating law enforcement with public health, education, social development, financial investigation and community participation.

Fagbemi commended the leadership and personnel of the agency under Marwa, assuring that the Federal Ministry of Justice would collaborate with the judiciary to facilitate the speedy determination of drug-related cases in line with the Administration of Criminal Justice Act.

He said the ministry would also ensure that convictions were accompanied by the confiscation of illicit assets and strengthen mutual legal assistance, extradition and cross-border asset recovery.

The AGF further praised the NDLEA’s practice of involving prosecutors early in investigations, noting that the approach helped preserve evidence and prevent procedural errors.

He stressed the need to distinguish between drug traffickers and people struggling with drug dependence, saying the latter required treatment rather than punishment alone.

“While traffickers must face the full weight of the law, those experiencing drug use disorders require evidence-based care, counselling, treatment, and community reintegration,” he said.

Fagbemi urged stakeholders to make the master plan their operational guide, adding that the NDLEA’s efforts to replace illicit drug cultivation with legitimate cash crops and other agricultural activities aligned with President Bola Tinubu’s development agenda.

In a goodwill message, the ECOWAS Commissioner for Human Development and Social Affairs, Professor Nassirou Bako-Arifari, commended Nigeria for the initiative, describing the master plan as consistent with regional commitments towards a safer and healthier West Africa.

He, however, said its success would depend on sustained political will, adequate funding and cooperation among governments, civil society organisations, communities, the private sector and international partners.

“The ECOWAS Commission will stand with Nigeria at every step, sharing experience, mobilising support and linking this national effort to the work of other Member States.

“Nigeria’s progress will guide the region, because no country can win this fight alone,” he said.

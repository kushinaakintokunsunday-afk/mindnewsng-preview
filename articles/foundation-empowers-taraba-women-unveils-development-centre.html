Foundation empowers Taraba women, unveils development centre

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Africa Nature Investors Foundation has commissioned a Women Development Centre and three schools in Gashaka Local Government Area of Taraba State as part of

The projects, supported by the European Union and implemented in partnership with the National Park Service, are expected to benefit residents of communities surrounding the Gashaka-Gumti National Park, Nigeria’s largest national park.

Located in Tonga (Bodel), the Women Development Centre will serve women from Serti, Bodel, Goje and Mayo Selbe communities, providing opportunities for vocational training, agricultural processing, skills acquisition and entrepreneurship.

Speaking at the commissioning ceremony on Friday, the Executive Director of ANI Foundation, Tunde Morakinyo, said the organisation had continued to attract development support, including funding from the European Union, to improve the livelihoods of communities around the national park.

He said the centre was established to provide women with a common platform to acquire skills, engage in productive activities and improve their economic circumstances.

Morakinyo disclosed that the foundation had also supported farmers through beekeeping initiatives and facilitated the vaccination of thousands of cattle to improve livestock health and strengthen rural livelihoods.

He added that the foundation planned to construct three blocks of classrooms in each of the communities across Gashaka Local Government Area to improve educational infrastructure and access to learning.

Also speaking, the Conservator of Gashaka-Gumti National Park, George Karngong, described the centre as another milestone in the partnership between the National Park Service and ANI Foundation.

He said the facility would serve as a hub for women’s savings groups established by the foundation, enabling beneficiaries to mobilise savings, exchange ideas, access training and establish small-scale businesses.

According to him, the initiative would strengthen women’s economic independence, improve household incomes and encourage greater community participation in conservation activities.

Karngong listed other interventions by the foundation to include alternative livelihood programmes, scholarships, classroom and teachers’ quarters construction, provision of portable water, cattle vaccination, volunteer ranger initiatives and environmental restoration projects.

He stressed that sustainable conservation required the cooperation of communities living around the park rather than reliance on enforcement alone.

“Conservation cannot succeed through enforcement alone; it requires the active participation and support of surrounding communities,” he said.

He explained that providing alternative livelihood opportunities, particularly for women, would reduce excessive dependence on the park’s natural resources and encourage residents to take greater responsibility for protecting the environment.

The conservator appealed to the Taraba State Government to provide the park with operational facilities, including patrol vehicles, motorcycles and communication equipment, to strengthen its capacity to protect the country’s important ecological asset.

He described the Gashaka-Gumti National Park as a strategic national resource with significant contributions to climate change mitigation, watershed protection, tourism, research, education and employment generation.

The Leader of the European Union Delegation, Iusan Liviu, expressed satisfaction with the intervention, describing it as a positive step towards community development and institutional strengthening.

Liviu called for sustained cooperation among the government, development partners, conservation authorities and host communities to ensure that the initiative delivered lasting benefits.

He assured that the European Union would continue to support ANI Foundation’s interventions aimed at improving community welfare and strengthening the management of the park.

Similarly, the Taraba State Commissioner for Heritage and Ecotourism, Titus Nagombe, commended the foundation for its contributions to communities around the park and urged women to take full advantage of the opportunities provided by the centre.

The Commissioner for Women and Child Development, Mary Sinjen, who was the special guest of honour, urged beneficiaries to cultivate a strong maintenance culture to ensure that the facility remained useful to present and future generations.

She also expressed her ministry’s readiness to collaborate with ANI Foundation in advancing women’s development and economic empowerment.

The Chairman of Gashaka Local Government Council, Umar Alluran Gari, described the foundation as a dependable development partner and urged residents to continue supporting its programmes and the National Park Service.

He, however, called on the foundation to intensify efforts to address the increasing influx of people into the park.

The Lamdo of Gashaka, Alhaji Zubairu Hammangabdo, who spoke on behalf of traditional rulers from Bodel and Mayo Selbe, acknowledged the foundation’s interventions and appealed to residents to sustain their support for the conservation partnership.

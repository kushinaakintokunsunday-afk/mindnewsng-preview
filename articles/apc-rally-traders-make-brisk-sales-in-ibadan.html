APC rally: Traders make brisk sales in Ibadan

By Punch Punch 3d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Traders on Saturday did brisk business ahead of the arrival of President Bola Tinubu for the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally at the

Items mainly on sale included customised APC caps and T-shirts bearing different inscriptions, as well as sachet and bottled water, soft drinks and snacks.

The News Agency of Nigeria (NAN) reports that the rally venue and its neighbourhoods, including Beere, Oja-Oba and Idi-Arere, were filled with thousands of APC members and supporters who came to hear from the President.

Sellers of brooms, the symbol of the APC, also recorded brisk sales, as did vendors of kolanuts and bitter kola.

Some of the traders who spoke with NAN expressed satisfaction with the sales recorded even before the start of the programme and the arrival of President Bola Tinubu.

Mrs Janet Yewande, a food vendor near the venue, said she had hired an additional six people to cope with the high volume of customers waiting to buy food from her shop.

She said the influx of customers had not made her increase her prices or reduce the quantity of food served.

Miss Rasheedat Bolade said the additional sales from sachet water would go a long way towards settling some pending bills and paying her school fees.

Bolade said her mother had bought enough bags of sachet water in preparation for the day’s business, adding that this had been her usual practice and means of survival whenever a major event was held at the historic Mapo Hall.

Also, Mr Akeem Ogunwale, a seller of customised caps, said he had made the necessary preparations by printing more caps and T-shirts in anticipation of high sales.

Ogunwale said he had sold no fewer than 60 customised caps and 12 T-shirts at the campaign ground.

A soft drink seller, Mrs Mercy Kingsley, said she had already sold 10 crates of soft drinks, noting that although many people preferred cold drinks because of the intense heat, some still bought drinks that were not chilled because of the high demand.

A participant at the campaign rally, Mr Kolade Ayinde, said the long hours spent in the sun had compelled him to drink up to three sachets of water, adding that many supporters did not mind the heat because of their support for the President.

Ayinde said the presence of traders was normal, as they catered to the needs of party supporters, especially given the intensity of the sun.

NAN reports that the large crowd in and around Mapo Hall was still awaiting the arrival of the President and his team as of the time of filing this report.

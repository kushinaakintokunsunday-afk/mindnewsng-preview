Political violence: Sowore rejects peace accord, questions peace committee’s relevance

By Punch Punch 8h ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Presidential Candidate of the African Action Congress, Omoyele Sowore, has questioned the relevance of the Kukah Centre, led by the Catholic Bishop of Sokot

Sowore announced his party’s withdrawal from the National Peace Accord signing, an agreement by politicians to avoid violence and ensure that their supporters do not undermine the electoral process.

The statement, titled ‘It is time to shut down Archbishop Matthew Hassan Kukah’s National Peace Committee,’ was issued by Sowore on Tuesday, a copy of which was obtained by PUNCH Online.

The AAC presidential candidate demanded the relevance of the peace accords, lamenting that political violence has claimed lives, while citizens are intimidated for exercising their democratic rights.

He also called for the National Peace Committee to be shut down.

“It is time to ask Bishop Matthew Hassan Kukah, founder of the Kukah Centre, and the leadership of the National Peace Committee to explain what purpose their so-called peace accords serve when political violence continues to claim lives, opposition candidates are attacked, and citizens are intimidated for exercising their democratic rights while the committee maintains radio silence.

“Nigeria cannot continue to celebrate ceremonial peace accords while political violence becomes an increasingly dangerous feature of our electoral process,” he stated.

The development comes amid growing concerns over political violence ahead of the 2027 general elections.

In an editorial published on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, The PUNCH urged the police to demonstrate their commitment to tackling electoral violence by arresting and prosecuting politicians threatening citizens with death, expulsion and suppression.

The newspaper also called on the Inspector-General of Police, Olatunji Disu, to strengthen intelligence gathering to identify and prosecute politicians and their associates involved in vote-buying.

Sowore cited instances of political violence from the August 15, 2026 Osun State governorship election, the October 30 attack on his team in Ekiti State, and recently, the gunmen attack on the campaign organisation of former Deputy President of the Senate, Ovie Omo-Agege, during a campaign tour in Delta State.

Sowore lamented that over 70 lives were lost in the Osun election, which he tied to the “struggle” between the All Progressives Congress and the Accord Party in the state.

On the Ekiti attack, Sowore questioned the actionable steps taken by the peace committee, despite describing the attack as an “assassination attempt” and having identified individuals believed should be “investigated for their alleged roles in its planning and execution.”

In an August 2026 report, The PUNCH reported that an election observer organisation, Kimpact Development Initiative, said 17 election-related violence incidents were recorded across 10 Local Government Areas of Osun State during the August governorship election, with more than 44 victims recorded.

KDI stated that the incidents, which included gunshots, physical assaults, intimidation and harassment, were documented through its Election Day Data Room, established to coordinate and analyse reports from accredited observers deployed across the state.

“Consider what happened in Osun State during the last gubernatorial election. The struggle between the APC and ACCORD party over the gubernatorial election in the state descended into deadly violence. Over 70 people were confirmed killed, several others injured, and communities thrown into fear.

“These were not imaginary casualties. They were Nigerians whose lives were lost in a political confrontation.

“What practical intervention did the National Peace Committee make to prevent such violence, demand accountability, or ensure that those responsible faced justice?” Sowore queried.

On November 16, 2026, Sowore petitioned the Ekiti State Police Command, demanding the immediate arrest, interrogation, and prosecution of an alleged participant in the October 30 attack on him and members of his campaign team in Ado-Ekiti, the state capital.

The petition alleged that Sowore and his team were attacked shortly after he concluded a radio interview at MIDAS FM 90.5 by “a group of more than twenty armed men” who allegedly used “guns, machetes and bottles”.

On this, Sowore stated on Tuesday, “Then consider what happened to us in Ado-Ekiti on October 30, 2026. Armed attackers descended on members of our campaign team after a radio interview.

“We have publicly described the attack as an assassination attempt and identified individuals we believe should be investigated for their alleged roles in its planning and execution. Yet the central questions about accountability remain unanswered.

“Where was the National Peace Committee when our lives were endangered? Did anyone from the committee publicly demand an independent investigation?

“Did anyone insist that political candidates must be able to travel, campaign, speak, and organise without facing violence?” he asked further.

Meanwhile, in an updated report on Tuesday morning, The PUNCH reports that the Nigeria Police Force confirmed that six people were killed and several others were injured during violence at a political campaign event in Uduere community, Ughelli North Local Government Area of Delta State.

The police confirmation came a day after Omo-Agege’s campaign organisation alleged that gunmen attacked his entourage during a campaign tour, killing four people and critically injuring four others.

The Force Public Relations Officer, CSP Ani Iniedu, disclosed the casualty figure in a statement released on Tuesday, saying preliminary police reports indicated that six people died in the violence.

Sowore stated further, “Now look at Delta State. On November 23, 2026, political violence erupted in the Uduere community of Ughelli North Local Government Area during a campaign visit by former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege. Four people were reportedly killed and others injured.

“Omo-Agege’s campaign described the incident as an assassination attempt, while local political actors have offered conflicting accounts of who initiated the violence.

“Whatever the outcome of an independent investigation, four human lives have reportedly been lost in circumstances connected to political activities. That alone should be sufficient to provoke national outrage and urgent action.

“Not a single statement from the “National Peace Committee” to shut down the cottage peace committee. As for the AAC, we will never participate any further in the charade known as the “Peace Accord” Signing.”

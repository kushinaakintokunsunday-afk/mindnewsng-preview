Visually Impaired Persons protest exclusion in Ondo, demand recognition

By Vanguard Vanguard 16 Nov 2026 3 min read

By Dayo Johnson

AKURE — Members of the Association of Technology Inclined Visually Impaired Persons of Nigeria (ATIVIPN), Ondo State chapter, on Monday staged a peaceful protest, demanding official recognition and inclusion in government programmes and interventions.

The protesters accused the Ondo State Ministry of Women Affairs of neglecting their association despite submitting relevant registration documents and several letters seeking official recognition.

They alleged that the state government recognises only the Nigeria Association of the Blind (NAB), thereby excluding other groups of visually impaired persons from government interventions and benefits.

Speaking during the protest, the state chairman of ATIVIPN, Comrade Omiyale Babatunde, said members had been excluded from government intervention programmes since the beginning of the year.

Babatunde said the association had submitted its Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) certificate and other relevant registration documents to the ministry but expressed disappointment over the alleged failure of the authorities to act on their request.

He said several members of the association were educated but remained unemployed, appealing to the state government to provide them with opportunities to become economically productive.

“We have to be included. We need jobs. Some of us are educated, we need something doing, we need palliatives,” he said.

Babatunde, who disclosed that he was formerly a member of the NAB from 2018, alleged that the association’s leadership had failed to adequately address the concerns of visually impaired persons in both rural and urban communities.

Also speaking, the youth leader of ATIVIPN, Bello Yusuf, said the association was seeking independent recognition and inclusion in government activities and programmes designed for persons with disabilities.

Yusuf said the association was established in April 2026 following disagreements with the NAB, whose leadership he accused of bias, alleged diversion of palliatives and failure to cater adequately for visually impaired persons at the grassroots.

“We need inclusion and recognition. That is our main point here. We are no more part of NAB because of the bias they actually bear, the cheating and diversion of palliatives,” he said.

He explained that the association had submitted its registration documents and other necessary papers to the Ministry of Women Affairs but was yet to receive official recognition.

Yusuf said the group had written several letters to the ministry and formally notified it of its intention to protest before staging Monday’s demonstration.

Responding to the protesters, the Permanent Secretary of the Ministry of Women Affairs, Kolawole Kayode, assured them that their complaints would be addressed within seven days.

Kayode, who acknowledged receiving two letters from the association, urged the protesters not to lose confidence in the ministry, promising that its executives would receive a positive response before the expiration of the seven-day ultimatum.

“We are working on it. I want to assure you that before the end of the seven days of the ultimatum you gave us to address your issue of inclusion, your executives will definitely hear from us,” he said.

However, Yusuf warned that the association would stage another mass protest if the government failed to address its demands within the stipulated seven days.

He said the group would intensify its peaceful demonstration and block access to government offices to press home its demands.

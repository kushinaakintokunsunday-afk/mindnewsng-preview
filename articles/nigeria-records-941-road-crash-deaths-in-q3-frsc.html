Nigeria records 941 road crash deaths in Q3 – FRSC

By Punch Punch 4d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Despite recording fewer road crashes and casualties in the third quarter of 2026, no fewer than 941 people died in road traffic accidents across Nigeria during

The figure represents a 12.6 per cent decline from the 1,077 deaths recorded in the corresponding period of 2025, while the number of crashes fell by 13.8 per cent from 2,434 to 2,098.

The FRSC also reported a 14.5 per cent reduction in road traffic injuries, from 6,856 in the third quarter of 2025 to 5,864 in the same period this year.

The figures, released by the Corps in a statement signed by its Corps Public Education Officer, Osondu Ohaeri, on Thursday, were presented at the end-of-year strategy session with commanding officers at the FRSC National Headquarters, Abuja.

Although the Corps described the performance as evidence of progress under its ongoing reforms, the figures show that the country still recorded an average of more than 10 deaths from road crashes every day during the three-month period.

The 2,098 crashes also amounted to about 23 incidents daily, while the 5,864 injuries underscored the continuing human cost of road accidents despite the reported improvement.

The FRSC said the reduction in crashes exceeded its 10 per cent target, attributing the performance to improved operational efficiency, preventive interventions, public enlightenment and enforcement.

However, the statement did not provide a breakdown of the crashes by state, identify the major causes of the accidents or indicate the categories of roads and vehicles responsible for the casualties. It also did not disclose how much of the reported improvement could be directly attributed to specific enforcement or technology interventions.

The figures were presented at the strategy session attended by the Secretary to the Government of the Federation, George Akume, who reportedly commended the Corps’ performance under the leadership of its Corps Marshal, Shehu Mohammed.

Akume said the results demonstrated progress in the Federal Government’s efforts to improve institutional efficiency and protect lives and property under President Bola Tinubu’s Renewed Hope Agenda.

But the persistent death toll presents a more sobering measure of the country’s road safety challenge, particularly as the FRSC prepares for increased highway traffic during the 2026 festive season.

Mohammed consequently directed commanding officers to intensify patrols along high-risk routes, improve traffic management and strengthen collaboration with other security and emergency-response agencies.

He also ordered the strategic positioning of ambulances, rescue teams and tow trucks, alongside sustained public enlightenment and improved personnel supervision.

The Corps Marshal warned against extortion, negligence and abuse of authority, charging commanding officers to take responsibility for the performance of their commands and ensure that available resources were properly deployed.

As part of its reforms, the FRSC also announced the completion of an upgraded Operations Monitoring and Control Centre at its national headquarters. The facility, commissioned by Akume, is expected to improve patrol monitoring, evidence management and coordination of operational responses.

According to the Corps, the centre will enable it to obtain, preserve and retrieve evidence against traffic offenders, while strengthening transparency and accountability in enforcement.

The FRSC said its road safety strategy was not limited to enforcement but also involved public education, intelligence gathering, technology and stakeholder collaboration.

It maintained that the ultimate measure of its performance should be the number of crashes prevented, injuries avoided and lives saved, rather than the volume of enforcement activities or personnel deployed.

The Corps expressed confidence that its reforms and improved operational capacity would sustain the reductions recorded, while acknowledging the need for further efforts to bring down road traffic casualties.

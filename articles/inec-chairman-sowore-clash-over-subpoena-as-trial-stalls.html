INEC chairman, Sowore clash over subpoena as trial stalls

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 15h ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

The failure of the African Action Congress (AAC) presidential candidate, Omoyele Sowore, to file court documents within the stipulated time stalled proceedings

The failure of the African Action Congress (AAC) presidential candidate, Omoyele Sowore, to file court documents within the stipulated time stalled proceedings yesterday his ongoing trial over alleged cybercrime offences before the Federal High Court, Abuja.

Sowore’s lawyer, Reuben Adakole, who represented him when the case was last heard on November 17, had undertaken to file his client’s response before the next adjourned date.

The response was to address an application filed by the Chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Prof. Joash Amupitan (SAN), seeking to set aside a subpoena issued on him at the instance of the defendant.

The politician is being prosecuted by the Department of State Services (DSS) over social media posts he allegedly published on X (formerly Twitter) and Facebook in August 2025, in which he described President Bola Tinubu as a “criminal” and accused him of lying about the level of corruption in the country.

When the matter was called on Monday, Sowore’s lawyer, Adeyinka Olumide-Fusika (SAN), told the court that his colleagues were experiencing difficulties filing the defendant’s counter-affidavit at the court registry.

Olumide-Fusika explained that he was informed that officials responsible for generating Remita payment details at the registry were experiencing technical difficulties.

He consequently sought a short stand-down to enable his colleagues to complete the filing process.

However, Amupitan’s lawyer, Alex Izinyon (SAN), expressed disappointment over the defence team’s decision to wait until Monday to file its response, insisting that the documents ought to have been filed earlier.

Izinyon recalled that at the previous sitting on November 17, Adakole had undertaken to file the defendant’s response before the next hearing date, questioning why the defence waited until the day of the hearing.

He further disclosed that rather than file the expected response, the defendant opted to file a notice of preliminary objection, which was served on him last Friday.

According to him, the development forced him to work throughout the weekend to prepare a reply, which he filed before the court commenced sitting on Monday and subsequently served on the defence.

While the lawyers were still deliberating on how to proceed, Justice Mohammed Umar suggested that the matter be adjourned until Tuesday.

However, shortly after the suggestion, one of Olumide-Fusika’s colleagues rushed into the courtroom with the newly filed documents, prompting the defence lawyer to hand a copy to Izinyon.

After examining the documents, Izinyon confirmed that they contained a counter-affidavit and a written address.

He, however, told the court that he would need time to study the processes and file a response, either before the court registry closed on Monday or early Tuesday before the commencement of proceedings.

Following the absence of any objection from the prosecuting counsel, Akinlolu Kehinde (SAN), Justice Umar adjourned the matter until Tuesday for hearing.

The counter-affidavit and preliminary objection filed by Sowore are responses to Amupitan’s application seeking to set aside the subpoena issued at the defendant’s instance.

Sowore wants the INEC chairman to appear in court as his witness and produce certain documents, including the academic credentials President Tinubu submitted to the electoral commission.

At the last sitting, Izinyon had argued that the subpoena should be set aside on the grounds of alleged irregularities.

He also faulted the claim by Sowore’s lawyer, Adakole, that his client had filed an application seeking to amend the subpoena.

Izinyon argued that the defence could not introduce another application to amend the subpoena without first addressing the alleged irregularities surrounding the original process.

He further maintained that Adakole needed to clarify whether Sowore intended to withdraw the original subpoena or proceed with the application to amend it.

The INEC chairman’s lawyer insisted that the subpoena could not be altered at Sowore’s instance without a proper order of the court.

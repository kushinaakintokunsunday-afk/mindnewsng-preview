I thought I would lose my leg after shooting – Broda Shaggi

Popular comedian and skit maker, Samuel Perry, popularly known as Broda Shaggi, has revealed that he feared losing the use of his leg after he was hit by a stray bullet in March 2026.

The entertainer also credited the Senior Pastor of Harvesters International Christian Centre, Bolaji Idowu, with helping him secure further medical treatment in Dubai after months of delays in Nigeria.

Shaggi recounted his ordeal on Sunday during a thanksgiving service at the church, where he expressed gratitude to the pastor for intervening when his recovery was uncertain.

He said he initially resisted travelling abroad for another surgery, despite being unable to walk for months after the shooting.

According to him, Idowu insisted that he seek treatment outside Nigeria and helped connect his team with a specialist, Dr Lushola.

Shaggi’s manager, Olufemi Oguntamu, popularly known as Penza, who also spoke at the service, said the comedian spent about a month in hospital before he was discharged and asked to return for weekly check-ups.

He said they initially expected another surgery to take place within two or three weeks, but the process was delayed for about four months.

The delay prompted discussions about seeking treatment abroad, although Shaggi was initially unwilling to travel.

Oguntamu said Idowu eventually intervened and insisted that the comedian needed further medical attention outside the country.

He explained that they considered treatment options in Dubai and the United Kingdom before settling for Dubai, with the pastor helping to connect them with the specialist and arrange support.

He added that Idowu remained involved throughout the process, regularly calling to check on them and praying for Shaggi’s recovery.

“At a point, Pastor Balaji would call me in the midnight. And I’d be like, ‘Ah, shit, this man no get wife?’ Every time, like, he would call me and pray for me. And he’d be like, ‘Samuel, don’t worry. You’ll be fine,’” Oguntamu said.

He also disclosed that the pastor travelled from London to Dubai to support Shaggi during the surgery.

Oguntamu recalled that Idowu had promised to mobilise church members to support the comedian wherever he eventually underwent the procedure.

“He was even saying that, ‘Oh, Shaggy, what’s the problem? Is it people that are going to take care of you?’ Because for those that don’t know, he’s a very private person.

“So, he didn’t want a lot of people into, you know, his matter. But Pastor Balaji would say, ‘Don’t worry. Anywhere you want the surgery, I’m going to ensure that I get members there that will support you and all of that,’” he said.

Responding to the testimony, Idowu disclosed that doctors in Dubai told them Shaggi’s injured leg was already shrinking when he eventually arrived for treatment.

“When they eventually got to Dubai, the doctor said if they had come late, the leg they shot was already shrinking. I’m telling you. And that’s the glory of God,” the pastor said.

Shaggi, who repeatedly thanked Idowu for his intervention, said he had feared he might never regain the use of his leg after the shooting.

Recounting the incident, he said it happened on March 19, 2026, while he and his team were returning from a brand activation assignment.

He said they were caught in traffic when a crowd gathered around his vehicle, cheering and calling his name. Suddenly, he heard a loud bang.

Shaggi said he initially thought policemen in the area were shooting but later realised he had been hit when he touched his hip and noticed blood.

“There was traffic, the policemen were in front, my team were at the back, my car was in the middle, then these area boys just gathered. They were all happy and shouting, ‘Shaggy!’ like they always do. And all of a sudden, all I heard was a loud bang.

“I thought maybe the policemen were shooting outside or something was happening outside. So I looked to the right. I saw people moving, I saw people talking, but I couldn’t hear a thing. It was just total silence. Then, when I touched my hip, I started seeing blood,” he said.

The comedian said the incident made him afraid of dying, despite previously believing he was prepared for death.

“Like, I don’t know, I’ve always said, when death comes, I’m ready. Like, I’m ready. But we got to that point, and the next thing I was just saying is, ‘Jesus, I don’t want to die. Jesus, I don’t want to die. Jesus, I don’t want to die,’” he added.

He said he was first taken to a hospital where the required treatment could not be provided before being transferred to another facility in Ikeja, where he received first aid and was stabilised.

Shaggi said the injury also raised fears about the future of his entertainment career, particularly because dancing and physical performances were important parts of his work.

He disclosed that he initially expected to spend only a few days in hospital and instructed his manager not to inform his mother about the incident.

However, as his hospitalisation continued, he eventually called her and told her he was receiving treatment. He said he did not initially disclose that he had been shot because he feared she would become distressed.

“I just had to call her. I didn’t even tell her I was shot. I just said I was in the hospital so that she wouldn’t shout wherever she was,” he said.

He described seeing his mother at the hospital as an emotional experience, saying it was the first time she had seen him in such a condition.

“She came, she saw me, she felt really bad. I felt so bad, too, because it was the first time she would see me in that position. It was the first time I would see myself in that position. I’m not going to lie, I thought I was going to lose it,” he added.

The comedian said he could not walk for months and depended on crutches and assistance from others for basic activities, including bathing.

“After four months, I couldn’t walk. It was just me on my crutches and this leg up like this. I was in the hospital. They bathed me. There was this very small girl. She would bathe me,” he said.

He added that he had prepared himself for the possibility of using crutches permanently but was grateful that his recovery took a different turn.

“For my fans, for those who follow me, for those who watch my videos, I love you too. I already made up my mind that whatever happens, I’m going to use that crutch as brother. But thank God, it didn’t go that way,” he said.

The comedian had earlier narrated his experience in a YouTube video, saying the injury kept him away from work for months and left him uncertain about his future in the entertainment industry.

He also spoke about his fear that audiences might forget him while he was unable to create content and perform.

In that account, Shaggi said he underwent an initial surgery in Nigeria before travelling to Dubai for further treatment and physiotherapy.

He said his condition improved after the procedure in Dubai, disclosing that he was able to stand with support on the second day and walk with crutches on the third day.

At Sunday’s thanksgiving service, Shaggi also led the congregation in praise songs, accompanied by the church choir, as he celebrated his recovery and thanked those who supported him through the difficult period.

FG backs efforts to develop Lassa fever vaccine in Nasarawa

By Punch Punch 3d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has pledged support for the Federal University Teaching Hospital, Lafia, Nasarawa State, in its efforts to develop a vaccine for Lassa fe

The Coordinating Minister of Health and Social Welfare, Muhammad Ali Pate, gave the assurance during an inspection visit to the hospital’s Centre for Vaccine Research and Biotechnology on Friday, expressing confidence in the facility’s efforts to develop locally produced vaccines.

This is as the hospital restated its commitment to develop a vaccine for the disease.

The Director of the Centre for Vaccine Research and Biotechnology at FUTH Lafia, Prof. Mustapha Imam, disclosed this to journalists in Lafia, the Nasarawa State capital, when the Coordinating Minister of Health and Social Welfare, Muhammad Ali Pate, paid an inspection visit to the facility on Friday.

According to Prof Imam, Lassa fever, which currently has no cure, has caused illness and claimed the lives of many Nigerians, including healthcare workers, hence the need to develop a vaccine to tackle the disease.

Imam lamented that despite the disease spreading to some West African countries, not much significant effort had been made to find a lasting solution to it.

He said, “We are talking about Lassa fever, which is primarily a Nigerian disease. Lassa fever started in Borno State. It still doesn’t have a cure.

“It has spread to some West African countries, but because it is not a Western disease, they don’t really care about finding a solution for it.

“So, we are working on it, trying to see if we can develop a vaccine. We are also working on dengue fever, which is also a problem in Nigeria and other African countries.”

Speaking on the minister’s inspection visit, the centre’s director expressed gratitude to Ali Pate for his commitment and support in ensuring that the centre had been able to deliver on its mandate since its establishment.

He noted that the minister’s visit would serve as an encouragement to the management and staff to do more, especially following his assurances of further support for the centre.

“We are really happy with the Minister’s visit because just like I have mentioned several times, this is like a baby center, and it requires all the support that it can get in terms of support, training, human capacity development, and more importantly, in terms of infrastructure.

“We are trying to fill a gap that critically exists in Nigeria. Most of the players in vaccine and drug research and development are usually in the later stages.

“So that means we need to wait for people in other countries to develop these drugs and we test them and use them. So now we are saying no to that. We don’t want to be in that trap. We want to discover the vaccines and develop them here in Nigeria,” he explained.

Earlier, the Coordinating Minister of Health and Social Welfare, Muhammad Ali Pate, reiterated the Federal Government’s commitment to supporting the centre in developing vaccines locally and reducing Nigeria’s dependence on imported vaccines.

Our correspondent reports that the minister visited the centre while attending the 67th National Council on Health, held in Lafia, Nasarawa State.

Ali Pate said he was impressed by the facilities, the vision of the hospital’s Chief Medical Director, Dr Hassan Ikrama, and the commitment of staff to developing local vaccine production capacity.

He commended the Nasarawa State Government, the CMD and researchers from various institutions for collaborating to establish the centre and develop local solutions to Nigeria’s health challenges.

“They already have vaccine candidates that are in early stages but appear promising. So, the Federal Government will continue to support them to succeed.

“I am very impressed, and the Federal Government will continue to support this effort by bringing in partners, globally experienced partners, that can help build the human capital that this institution has,” he said.

Ali Pate stressed the importance of research that addresses local needs, saying it should be tailored to the country’s peculiar health challenges to deliver meaningful impact.

“Research is important, but it is even more important if it helps solve local problems, and it has to be contextualised, because the young men and women that I have seen here really have a lot of promise, and we will support them,” the minister added.

On his part, the Chief Medical Director of the Federal University Teaching Hospital, Lafia, Dr Ikrama Hassan, expressed appreciation to the minister for the visit, describing it as a significant boost to the hospital’s research and development efforts.

He said the hospital management remained committed to providing the necessary institutional support and a conducive environment for the research centre to achieve its mandate of advancing medical research, innovation and the development of solutions to pressing public health challenges.

Dr Hassan noted that strategic partnerships with relevant national and international institutions would be essential in strengthening the centre’s research capacity, facilitating knowledge exchange and ensuring that its activities aligned with global best practices.

He assured the minister that the hospital would continue to work closely with the centre’s leadership to translate research findings into practical healthcare interventions that would improve disease prevention, diagnosis and treatment, particularly in addressing infectious diseases affecting communities.

The CMD also expressed optimism that the minister’s engagement would open new opportunities for collaboration, capacity building, infrastructure development and greater visibility for the research centre.

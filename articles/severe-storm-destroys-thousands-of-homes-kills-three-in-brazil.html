Severe Storm Destroys Thousands Of Homes, Kills Three In Brazil

A powerful storm with winds exceeding 100 kilometers per hour killed three people and destroyed around 5,000 homes Friday in southern Brazil, as authorities raced to rescue injured survivors and find more casualties in the wreckage.

Rain and high winds battered Tupancireta early Friday, tearing up buildings and about 100 trees in the municipality that is home to 20,000 people.

“It’s something never seen before, a movie scene,” Tupancireta Mayor Gustavo Herter told local media.

“Our hospital was blocked by the trees around it, the access roads to the municipality were blocked with trees. The city is finished,” he said. The hospital’s roof was also destroyed and 11 patients had to be transferred.

The city was without electricity and water after the storm hit at around 3 am and lasted less than an hour, Herter said.

Three people were found dead, according to the state government of Rio Grande do Sul, where Tupancireta is located.

Another 32 municipalities also reported damage.

President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva ordered emergency teams be sent to the region in response to the storm.

“We know there was a party in the municipality where the roof of that structure ended up collapsing,” Luciano Boeira, state coordinator of civil defense, told Radio Gaucha.

Brazilian meteorologists said winds reached 114 kilometers per hour (70 miles per hour) during the storm.

Civil defense described the storm as a “supercell,” which often precedes a tornado with strong upward air currents, and has not ruled out the possibility that a tornado hit the city.

State officials have allocated about $330,000 in local currency to respond to the disaster.

Southern Brazil is frequently hit by tornadoes.

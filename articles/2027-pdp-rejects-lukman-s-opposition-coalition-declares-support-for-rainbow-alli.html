2027: PDP rejects Lukman’s opposition coalition, declares support for Rainbow alliance

MINDNEWSNG
Photo: VeryNigerian

The Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) has dismissed claims that it has joined the opposition Coalition for Democracy being promoted by former All Progressives Congress (APC) National Vice Chairman, Salihu Lukman.

The opposition party also announced that it would not participate in the coalition’s proposed summit scheduled for November 30.

The PDP said it had not entered into any alliance with the group, authorised anyone to represent it at the meeting or agreed to be bound by decisions taken in its name.

The party maintained that its only recognised coalition arrangement was the Rainbow Coalition, which it said was backed by its National Leader, Nyesom Wike.

The position was made known in a statement signed by the PDP National Publicity Secretary, Hon. Jungudo Haruna Mohammed.

Mohammed explained that individuals being presented as representatives of the PDP at the coalition were expelled members who no longer had the authority to speak or act on behalf of the party.

The party’s clarification comes amid efforts by opposition figures to build a broader political alliance ahead of the 2027 general elections.

Jungudo said the individuals’ claims to the party’s leadership and representation had suffered defeats at the Federal High Court, Court of Appeal and Supreme Court.

He added that the courts had issued orders restraining Kabiru Tanimu Turaki, SAN, and his associates from parading themselves as PDP leaders, while imposing financial penalties exceeding N100 million over alleged abuses of court processes and frivolous litigation.

Questioning Lukman’s continued engagement with the individuals, Jungudo said, “It is therefore astonishing that Mr Salihu Lukman, who ought to be sufficiently informed about the legal status and recognised leadership of the PDP, would continue to engage these individuals and use them as a basis for associating the party with his so-called Toronto Coalition.”

Describing the proposed arrangement as a political project built on misrepresentation, the PDP declared it ‘dead on arrival’, particularly because those being presented as its representatives lacked the authority to speak on its behalf.

The party also threatened legal action against Lukman, his associates and other promoters of the Coalition for Democracy if they continued to use its name and institutional identity in their political activities.

“The party will not hesitate to pursue appropriate legal action against any person or group that continues to unlawfully associate the PDP with this arrangement,” Jungudo warned.

Reiterating its position, the party said the Rainbow Coalition, in line with Wike’s political direction and directives, remained its only recognised coalition arrangement.

It consequently urged Nigerians, political stakeholders, members and supporters, as well as media organisations, to disregard reports linking its national headquarters at Wadata Plaza, Abuja, to the November 30 summit or any other political arrangement being promoted by Lukman and his associates.

“The PDP will not allow its identity to be hijacked, its institutional authority misrepresented or its political interests compromised by individuals who have no mandate to speak or act on its behalf,” Jungudo said.

The party maintained that it would not be bound by any resolution or commitment purportedly made in its name by the promoters of the coalition.

202 days after China deal, NNPC under pressure to revive refineries

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 5d ago 4 min read

202 days after signing a strategic partnership agreement with two Chinese companies, the Nigerian National Petroleum Company (NNPC) Limited is under pressure to fast-track the rehabilitation of the Port Harcourt and Warri refineries.

This comes as the Petroleum Products Retail Outlets Owners Association of Nigeria (PETROAN) warned that escalating tensions between Iran and the United States could disrupt global petroleum supplies.

The association argued that any prolonged disruption around the Strait of Hormuz could trigger fresh shocks to crude oil supply, petroleum product availability, shipping costs and international energy prices, leaving Nigeria vulnerable because of its continued dependence on imported refined products.

National Public Relations Officer of PETROAN, Mr. Joseph Obele, who raised the concern in a statement yesterday, said the unfolding geopolitical tensions should serve as a wake-up call for NNPC Limited to move beyond agreements and technical assessments and translate its refinery revival plans into concrete action.

Obele noted that NNPC Limited signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on April 30, 2026, with Sanjiang Chemical Company Limited and Xinganchen (Fuzhou) Industrial Park Operation and Management Co. Ltd. for a proposed technical equity partnership covering the completion, operation, maintenance and expansion of the Port Harcourt and Warri refineries.

However, with exactly 202 days gone since the agreement was signed, he said Nigerians were yet to see the concrete implementation measures needed to bring the facilities back to productive operations.

Although plant assessments had reportedly been conducted on both refineries, the PETROAN spokesman insisted that such exercises must translate into clearly defined implementation timelines, investment commitments and commencement of rehabilitation and upgrade works.

“An MoU must not remain a ceremonial document. It must translate into measurable action, investment, infrastructure development and sustainable petroleum production,” he said.

He called on NNPC to make public the implementation status of the Chinese partnership, including the investment commitments, technical responsibilities, rehabilitation roadmap and expected delivery dates.

Obele also demanded the release of details of the plant assessment reports for both refineries, alongside clear performance targets and accountability mechanisms to ensure that the proposed partnership delivers on its objectives.

The PETROAN spokesman warned that Nigeria’s continued dependence on external refining capacity was a major vulnerability, particularly at a time when geopolitical tensions threaten the stability of international energy supply chains.

He said the consequences of a prolonged disruption in the Middle East would extend beyond the petroleum industry to transportation, food prices, manufacturing, businesses and household incomes.

Beyond the national energy security implications, Obele, an indigene of Ogoni, said the revival of the Port Harcourt Refinery carried significant economic and social implications for its host communities.

He lamented that thousands of Ogoni indigenes and other residents whose livelihoods depend directly or indirectly on the refinery had suffered economic hardship following its prolonged inactivity.

According to him, the shutdown has weakened employment opportunities, commercial activities and other livelihood sources, leaving families and businesses struggling.

He argued that restoring the refinery to productive operations would help revive local businesses, create jobs and rebuild economic confidence among host communities.

Obele urged NNPC to give special consideration to the social and economic importance of restarting the Port Harcourt facility as part of efforts to strengthen confidence between the oil industry and communities that have hosted petroleum operations for decades.

He further called for stronger strategic petroleum product reserves and improved domestic supply preparedness to cushion the country against possible international market disruptions.

“NNPC must recognise that refinery development is not merely a commercial undertaking; it is a critical component of Nigeria’s national energy security,” he said.

He maintained that Nigeria could no longer afford to remain heavily exposed to external refining capacity when its own refineries were yet to operate at their expected potential.

“ Nigeria has the crude oil. Nigeria has the market. What Nigerians urgently need is functional domestic refining capacity. The time for implementation is now. Another international petroleum crisis should not find Nigeria unprepared,” he added.

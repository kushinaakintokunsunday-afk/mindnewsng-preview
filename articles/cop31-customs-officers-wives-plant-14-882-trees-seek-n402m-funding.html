COP31: Customs officers’ wives plant 14,882 trees, seek N402m funding

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Customs Officers’ Wives Association says it has planted 14,882 economic trees and empowered more than 8,000 people in communities along Nigeria’s borders in

The association also announced plans to collaborate with the Office of the President’s Special Assistant on Climate Change Matters to organise what it described as one of the largest gatherings of environmental activists and green workers in Abuja on December 5, 2026.

The initiative, which is expected to attract Guinness World Records recognition, is part of efforts to increase public participation in environmental protection and strengthen community-led responses to climate change.

National President of COWA, Kikelomo Adeniyi, disclosed these at a side event organised by the association at the ongoing COP31 climate conference on Monday in Antalya, Turkiye.

She said the association returned to the United Nations climate conference with evidence of the achievements it promised at COP30 in Belém, Brazil, last year, including tree planting, women’s economic empowerment and climate education for children.

Adeniyi said, “Last year, when we came to Belém, Brazil, we made so many promises that we were going to plant trees, empower women in green areas and sensitise our children on climate change. We have achieved all of these and more.

“Here in Antalya, Turkey, we have come to show evidence of all these things that we have achieved. So far, we have planted over 14,800 economic trees. These are trees that will be beneficial to our women in the near future. They will harvest them, take them to the market and sell them to make money.”

She explained that the tree-planting programme was deliberately designed around economic benefits to ensure that environmental restoration also provided sustainable livelihoods for women in border communities.

According to her, COWA has also trained women in recycling, farming and other green economic activities, while introducing solar energy solutions to help communities maximise their resources.

The association has mobilised 682 Green Ambassadors, while its community education programme has reached 6,093 beneficiaries. Another 4,041 people have benefited from health outreaches, while 1,138 have received skills acquisition support.

Adeniyi said the wider intervention had reached more than 8,000 people across border communities, including Seme, Idiroko and Illela.

She explained that the programme was also intended to address the economic vulnerability that could push residents, particularly young people, into smuggling and other illicit activities.

“The plan is to empower them because we believe that in the border areas, when people do not have something legal to do, they will go into illicit business, and that is where smuggling comes in. The children will begin to go into smuggling, and the mothers will encourage them to do so.

“We are giving them jobs and creating opportunities for the children and their mothers so that they can have something sustainable to do instead of going into illicit businesses,” she said.

The COWA president, however, said the association needed additional investment to scale up the programme, disclosing that it was seeking about N402 million to complete its innovation centre in Abuja.

She said the centre, for which the association had already invested about N415m, would serve as a national hub for training, research and the development of green skills, including solar installation.

“We have started already. It is already at the finishing level. Now, we are seeking over N400m just to bring it to completion. This is a place where people will come for training and research, and women will have a space where they can learn even how to fix solar systems.

“We want to have a place where everybody can come from the North, South, East and West as a hub for COWA,” she said, appealing to development partners and investors to support the project and its proposed expansion across Nigeria’s geopolitical zones.

Speaking during a panel session at the event, the Director-General, National Environmental Standards and Regulations Enforcement Agency, Innocent Barikor, commended COWA for directing attention to border communities, which he said were often overlooked in environmental interventions.

Barikor said NESREA could support the association through environmental governance advice, technical guidance and monitoring to ensure that its interventions produced verifiable and lasting results.

He stressed that the success of the tree-planting campaign should not be measured by the number of seedlings planted but by how many survived over time.

The NESREA chief also cautioned against introducing inappropriate or invasive tree species, urging the association to ensure that the trees planted were suitable for their locations and did not threaten existing vegetation.

Also speaking, the Special Assistant to the President on Climate Change Matters, Yussuf Kelani, described COWA’s initiative as an important contribution to climate prevention and community resilience.

He said the association was demonstrating how non-traditional environmental actors could contribute to climate action, noting that security agencies were usually called upon to respond to disasters after they occurred.

“Whenever we have climate crises around the world, we have the paramilitaries and the military coming in whenever there is a bush fire, extreme weather conditions or storms in different communities. We call on the military to salvage the situation.

“But now, the fact that we are preaching that there is no way forward without prevention, that we must participate in prevention, and the Customs Officers’ Wives Association has come up with this initiative, where they are actually participating in prevention, is a very good one,” he said.

Kelani disclosed that the National Council on Climate Change and the Ministry of Environment were working with the association to support the implementation of climate policies and community-based interventions.

The event also featured the presentation of an award to the COWA President by the President of the Nigeria Community in Türkiye, Emre Magboh, on behalf of Nigerians living in Türkiye, in recognition of the association’s contributions to environmental sustainability and community development.

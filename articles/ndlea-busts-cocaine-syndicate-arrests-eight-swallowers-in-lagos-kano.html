NDLEA busts cocaine syndicate, arrests eight swallowers in Lagos, Kano

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 17 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: The Sun Nigeria

From Godwin Tsa, Abuja The National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) says it has dismantled a transnational cartel that moves cocaine from Lagos through Kano

From Godwin Tsa, Abuja

The National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) says it has dismantled a transnational cartel that moves cocaine from Lagos through Kano and across the desert via Niger Republic and Libya to Europe. Eight suspected members of the syndicate, all drug mules, were arrested in intelligence-led operations in Lagos and Kano.

The suspects were picked up between Friday, 30 October, and Saturday, 31 October 2026. Having swallowed cocaine pellets, they excreted 634 wraps weighing 13.795 kilogrammes over several days in custody.

The operation began on 30 October, when operatives of the agency’s Special Operations Unit arrested the first suspect, 57-year-old Okwudiba Ifeanyichukwu Damian, at Nice Touch Hotel, Ago Palace, Okota, Lagos. He excreted 74 wraps weighing 1.650kg.

The next day, four more suspects were intercepted at the MM2 Terminal of the Murtala Muhammed Airport, Lagos, as they prepared to board a flight to Kano:

Jackson Ahamefule, 43: 90 wraps, 1.950kg; Nwafor Osita Emmanuel, 47: 85 wraps, 1.750kg; Marcel Nnabuike, 38: 75 wraps, 1.655kg; and Onyenwuba Ignatius, 44: 75 wraps, 1.650kg. In all, the five mules arrested in Lagos excreted 399 wraps weighing 8.655kg.

Intelligence from the Lagos operation showed that other members of the cartel had already travelled to Kano. Officers of the agency’s Mallam Aminu Kano International Airport (MAKIA) Command were mobilised and intercepted three suspects at the arrival hall of the airport’s domestic terminal:

Chinweike Ephraim Ifechukwu, 48: 85 pellets, 1.853kg; Edezuno John Nwaorji, 45: 80 pellets, 1.752kg; and Chukwudi Chinaka, 31: 70 pellets, 1.535kg.

Body scans showed that all three had swallowed drugs, and they were placed under excretion observation. The Kano suspects said they planned to travel by road from Kano through Niger Republic to Libya, with Europe as the final destination.

The NDLEA Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, Brig. Gen. Mohamed Marwa (Rtd), commended the officers of the Special Operations Unit and the MAKIA Command for their “intelligence-driven synergy, speed and professionalism,” which he said ensured that none of the eight mules “slipped through our net.”

“This cartel thought it had found a back door to Europe through our northern borders and the Sahara,” he said. “Their arrests are a message to drug barons and their couriers that there is no safe route out of Nigeria. Whether they go by air, by road or through the desert, we will trace them, find them and bring them to justice.”

He appealed to young people not to sell their lives to drug barons. “Swallowing wraps of cocaine is a death wish,” he warned. “One burst pellet can kill a mule before the journey ends. No amount of money is worth that risk.”

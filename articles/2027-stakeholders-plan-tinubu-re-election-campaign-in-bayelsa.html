2027: Stakeholders plan Tinubu re election campaign in Bayelsa

By Punch Punch 5d ago 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Ogbuku said leaders, women, youths and other stakeholders from Brass, Nembe and Ogbia Local Government Areas had agreed to bury their political differences and work together to secure victory for the APC at the polls.

He spoke during a political stakeholders’ engagement involving supporters and leaders of the party from the three local government areas at the Government House, Yenagoa.

The NDDC managing director said the gathering was part of efforts to appreciate Tinubu for his administration’s infrastructure interventions in the district and mobilise support for the President’s re-election bid.

He explained that the organisers deliberately brought together traditional leaders, women, youths, parents and children to demonstrate their appreciation beyond newspaper publications and courtesy visits.

Ogbuku said, “That was why we said we were going to come out en masse for the world to see us, not only doing paper publications thanking you or coming to pay courtesy visits, but bringing out our mothers, our fathers, our youths and the children for the world to see and tell you thank you.”

He added that the people were prepared to translate their appreciation into electoral support for the President and other APC candidates.

“And the final thank you these people want to give is committing their votes to APC for the president and all other candidates of the party,” he said.

Ogbuku urged the people of the district to ensure that their commitment was reflected in the actual election results, saying Bayelsa must demonstrate its support through votes rather than mere political declarations.

He said the outcome would also strengthen the political standing of Governor Douye Diri among his colleagues at the national level.

“Whatever we are doing, we are going to support the government to ensure that when the governor sits amongst governors in Abuja, the result he is going to present will also be worthwhile,”  Ogbuku said.

The NDDC boss, who made reference to a statement credited to the Minister of the Federal Capital Territory, Nyesom Wike, cautioned against claiming electoral victory without actively participating in the political process.

According to him, Bayelsa East would not make claims of victory without working to achieve it. He also said that political leaders from the district had visited former President Goodluck Jonathan to seek his permission and blessings before embarking on their political mobilisation exercise.

He said Jonathan, who hails from Bayelsa East, blessed the movement and the candidates, including those seeking elective positions at the presidential and other levels.

The NDDC managing director said the visit demonstrated the importance the leaders attached to Jonathan’s influence and leadership in the state.

He further declared that the political differences that had divided leaders in the district had been set aside in the interest of a common political objective.

“For the first time, we have all buried our differences and we have decided to stand together. All the leaders in Bayelsa East are together,” he said.

On Tinubu’s infrastructure interventions, Ogbuku commended the President for the Ogbia access road, which he said had significantly improved transportation and connectivity in the area.

He described the approximately 46-kilometre road as a major intervention, recalling the difficulties commuters previously encountered in accessing Ogbia town.

He also listed solar-powered streetlights in communities and the second phase of the Nembe-Brass Road as other projects expected to improve the economic fortunes of the district.

According to him, the completion of the Nembe-Brass Road would end years of isolation for several communities and open up opportunities in the blue economy.

Ogbuku, however, acknowledged concerns raised by women over the absence of female candidates among those being presented for elective positions, describing the issue as sensitive.

He also described his appointment as NDDC managing director as a landmark achievement for Bayelsa East, noting that it was the first time someone from the senatorial district had occupied the position.

Meanwhile, the Bayelsa State Chairman of the APC, Warman Ogoriba, announced plans by the party to commence grassroots campaigns across the state as part of its preparations for the 2027 general elections.

Ogoriba said the ongoing engagements with stakeholders and supporters in Bayelsa East were preliminary consultations aimed at strengthening the party’s mobilisation structures ahead of the elections.

He clarified that the gathering was not the formal campaign but an opportunity for the party to interact with its members, appreciate their commitment and consolidate support for Tinubu and other APC candidates.

“This is not the main campaign. This is just our pre-campaign, and we are meeting the people. Wait for us very soon. We will start campaigning across all the wards,” the APC chairman said.

Ogoriba expressed confidence that Bayelsa Central and Bayelsa West would also participate in the mobilisation exercise, stressing that the party was determined to reach supporters across the three senatorial districts.

He thanked party members and stakeholders in Bayelsa East for their participation, describing the district as important to the APC’s preparations for the 2027 presidential election.

The APC chairman’s declaration signals the party’s intention to intensify its grassroots mobilisation and expand its political engagement across communities and wards in Bayelsa State ahead of the 2027 polls.

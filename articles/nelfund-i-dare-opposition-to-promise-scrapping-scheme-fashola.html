NELFUND: I dare opposition to promise scrapping scheme — Fashola

…Says students, beneficiaries would revolt against such proposal

Former Lagos State Governor, Babatunde Fashola, has challenged opposition politicians to campaign on a promise to scrap the Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND), saying beneficiaries and their families would resist such a move.

Fashola, who spoke on Tuesday during an interview on Channels Television’s Politics Today, described the student loan scheme introduced by President Bola Tinubu’s administration as one of the reforms that had eased the financial burden on families across the country.

NELFUND was established in April 2024 after Tinubu signed the Student Loans (Access to Higher Education) Act into law to provide interest-free loans to eligible students in tertiary institutions.

As of October 3, 2026, the fund said it had disbursed more than ₦372 billion to 1,635,676 beneficiaries across 312 tertiary institutions nationwide.

Speaking at the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally in Jigawa on November 10, Tinubu had described NELFUND as a weapon deployed by his administration to tackle poverty, insisting that the scheme would not be discontinued.

Supporting the President’s position, Fashola said the initiative was introduced to help Nigerian families cope with financial obligations, particularly rent, transportation, healthcare and education.

“This led to the creation of NELFUND to help families pay school fees and provide student allowances,” he said.

He noted that more than one million Nigerians were already benefiting from the scheme, adding that the number was increasing.

Fashola challenged opposition candidates to make the discontinuation of the programme part of their campaign promises, saying such a position would attract resistance from beneficiaries and their families.

“I challenge the opponents to promise to scrap NELFUND and see how beneficiaries and their families react. They won’t dare,” he said.

The former Minister of Works and Housing also addressed the rising cost of living, describing it as a global challenge whose effects were being felt in Nigeria.

He called for a broader understanding of the factors contributing to the economic hardship, pointing to disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic and subsequent global developments.

According to him, the pandemic disrupted economic activities worldwide, forcing governments to inject money into their economies to support businesses.

He said China, which he described as the world’s biggest production centre, took about two to three years to resume normal operations, contributing to supply shortages and rising prices.

Fashola also identified the Russia-Ukraine war as another factor that worsened the situation, citing disruptions to the supply of energy products, including diesel, and food commodities such as wheat.

He said increased shipping costs and war-related insurance premiums further contributed to rising prices, although the impact differed among countries depending on their locations and levels of development.

“Thirdly, there was the unexpected war in the Middle East, which further worsened the situation,” he said.

Fashola added that the conflict had brought renewed attention to the strategic importance of the Strait of Hormuz, with developments around the waterway influencing the cost of goods.

“Everybody now knows the Strait of Hormuz… even the tomato seller, and prices goods based on replacement costs,” he said.

NGX sheds 1.83% amid 43% surge in dollar trades

By Punch Punch 18h ago 7 min read

Nigeria’s financial markets recorded mixed performances in the week ended 20 November 2026, as equities declined while foreign exchange turnover rose sharply, driven by increased spot dollar transactions, JIDE AJIA reports

The NGX All-Share Index dipped 1.83 per cent to 247,660.74 points, with equity turnover falling over 42 per cent in value, and over 70 per cent in volume, to N106.36bn, whereas FMDQ FX turnover jumped 36.59 per cent to $4.67bn on the back of a 43.08 per cent rise in spot dollar trades.

Trading activity on the Nigerian Exchange recorded a notable downturn during the week as investors exchanged a total turnover of 2.366 billion shares valued at N106.363bn across 159,759 deals, marking a steep contraction from the 8.029 billion shares worth N185.358bn traded in 160,836 deals during the preceding week.

Daily trading volumes steadily declined throughout the five-day session, starting at 673.59 million shares valued at N26.10bn in 38,218 deals on Monday, 16 November, before tapering to 564.04 million shares on Tuesday, 451.10 million shares on Wednesday, and 372.01 million shares on Thursday. It closed the week on Friday with 305.62 million shares worth N16.55bn across 29,586 deals.

Sectoral performance, measured by volume, remained heavily concentrated in the Financial Services Industry, which dominated the activity chart with 1.717 billion shares valued at N67.579bn across 74,214 deals. The sector single-handedly accounted for 72.57 per cent of the total equity turnover volume and 63.54 per cent of the total turnover value.

The Consumer Goods Industry followed in second place with 174.538 million shares worth N9.052bn traded in 20,668 deals, while the Services Industry ranked third with a turnover of 158.306 million shares valued at N1.378bn across 8,975 deals.

Activity in the top three equities by volume, including Access Holdings Plc, FCMB Group Plc and NEM Insurance Plc, generated a combined turnover of 613.658 million shares, reported as 613.658 million in the exchange summary, worth N12.994bn across 8,726 deals, representing 25.93 per cent and 12.22 per cent of total equity volume and value, respectively.

In the Exchange Traded Products segment, investors transacted a total of 1.107 million units valued at N410.452m across 3,848 deals, reflecting a slight dip in unit volume but a substantial jump in transaction value compared to the 1.212 million units worth N278.965m traded in 3,351 deals the previous week.

VETBANK led the ETP board by volume with 286,081 units valued at N7.43m across 247 deals, while STANBICETF30 dominated by value and deal count, recording 229,738 units worth N210.75m in 1,644 deals.

Other active funds included VETGRIF30 with 197,247 units valued at N18.08m, VETGOODS with 108,885 units worth N4.49m, LOTUSHAL15 with 94,284 units valued at N12.03m, and SIAMLETF40 with 90,696 units worth N132.23m. Smaller volumes were recorded across VETINDETF, MERGROWTH, VSPBONDETF, MERVALUE, GREENWETF and NEWGOLD, with NEWGOLD generating N9.65m from just 128 units.

Fixed income market

Activity in the secondary debt market saw a contraction in volume and value despite an increase in deal count, with a total of 116,356 bond units valued at N122.704m changing hands across 49 deals, compared to 156,612 units worth N177.879m traded in 41 deals the prior week.

Trading was anchored by FG202034S2, which recorded 37,848 units worth N43.43m in four deals, closely followed by FGSUK2033S6 with 37,289 units valued at N37.99m across nine deals, and TAJSUKS2 with 22,090 units worth N22.09m in 11 deals.

Additional notable transactions occurred in FG142037S2 with 10,000 units valued at N10.09m, alongside lighter trades in FG202029S5, FGS202776, TAJSUKS1, FGSUK2031S4, FGS202770, FGSUK2032S5, FGSUK2027S3 and FGS202888.

Index, sectoral movements

Broad market sentiment turned bearish during the period under review, pushing the NGX All-Share Index and total market capitalisation down by 1.83 per cent to close at 247,660.74 points and N160.804tn, respectively, down from the previous week’s ASI close of 252,269.16 points.

While the ASI retains a strong year-to-date gain of 59.15 per cent, it now reflects a month-to-date decline of 2.61 per cent and a quarter-to-date dip of 1.41 per cent.

Almost all sectoral and thematic indices ended in negative territory, led downward by the NGX Industrial Goods Index, which fell 5.29 per cent to 11,726.62; the NGX MERI Value Index, which dropped 4.62 per cent to 22,991.14; and the NGX-AFR Bank Value Index, which lost 4.28 per cent to close at 4,559.13.

Other notable declines included the NGX Premium Index (-3.77 per cent), NGX Oil/Gas Index (-3.75 per cent), NGX AFR Div Yield Index (-3.70 per cent), NGX Banking Index (-2.75 per cent), NGX Lotus II (-2.66 per cent), NGX Commodity Index (-2.54 per cent), NGX Pension Index (-2.36 per cent), NGX Pension Broad Index (-2.07 per cent), NGX 30 Index (-1.95 per cent), NGX CG Index (-1.95 per cent), NGX MERI Growth Index (-0.37 per cent), NGX-Main Board Index (-0.36 per cent) and the NGX Sovereign Bond Index (-0.24 per cent).

Bucking the broader downward trend, only three indices closed higher: the NGX Insurance Index advanced 2.47 per cent to 1,098.77, the NGX Consumer Goods Index gained 2.35 per cent to reach 4,028.05, and the NGX Growth Index rose 1.10 per cent to 26,752.31.

Breadth, movers, listings

Market breadth weakened compared to the previous week, as 39 equities appreciated, down from 43 the prior week, while 43 equities depreciated, up from 39 previously, and 64 equities remained unchanged.

LivingTrust Mortgage Bank Plc topped the gainers’ chart with a 28.76 per cent surge from N2.33 to N3.00, followed by Chellarams Plc, which rose 20.62 per cent from N9.70 to N11.70, and International Breweries Plc, which climbed 20.24 per cent from N8.40 to N10.10.

Regency Alliance Insurance Plc gained 18.75 per cent to close at N0.76, Critical Minerals Financing Corp Plc added 17.33 per cent to reach N3.52, Sovereign Trust Insurance Plc rose 16.89 per cent to N2.56, and Legend Internet Plc advanced 16.13 per cent to N3.60.

Rounding out the top 10 gainers were International Energy Insurance Plc (+14.29 per cent to N2.00), Cornerstone Insurance Plc (+13.64 per cent to N5.00) and Omatek Ventures Plc (+11.54 per cent to N1.45).

On the decliners’ side, Meyer Plc suffered the steepest percentage loss, shedding 17.43 per cent to fall from N17.50 to N14.45, followed closely by R. T. Briscoe (Nigeria) Plc, which dropped 16.94 per cent from N9.15 to N7.60.

Lasaco Assurance Plc and Aradel Holdings Plc both declined 10.00 per cent, closing at N1.71 and N1,166.40, respectively, while heavyweight Dangote Cement Plc slipped 9.99 per cent from N1,469.90 to N1,323.00, exerting significant downward pressure on the benchmark index.

Other top decliners included Haldane McCall Plc (-9.91 per cent to N3.00), FTN Cocoa Processors Plc (-9.72 per cent to N6.50), John Holt Plc (-9.71 per cent to N7.90), Red Star Express Plc (-9.60 per cent to N11.30) and Learn Africa Plc (-9.41 per cent to N7.70).

Finally, on the corporate listings front, Nigerian Exchange Limited admitted an additional 323,805 units of the Chapel Hill Denham Nigeria Infrastructure Debt Fund to its Daily Official List on Monday, 16 November 2026. These supplementary units were issued pursuant to the Fund’s Q3 2026 scrip dividend distribution, increasing the total listed units of NIDF on the Exchange from 1,197,308,007 to 1,197,631,812 units.

FX market performance

In contrast to the downturn in equities, turnover in the Foreign Exchange Spot and Derivatives markets between FMDQ Dealing Member (Banks)/Authorised Dealers and their clients surged  36.59 per cent ($1,251.63m) week-on-week to reach a total of $4,672.49m for the week ended 20 November 2026, up from $3,420.86m in the preceding week.

Daily average turnover rose accordingly from $684.18m to $934.50m.

According to the FMDQ weekly market report, “The week-on-week increase in total turnover was solely driven by the 43.08 per cent ($1,398.37m) increase in FX Spot transactions, which recorded a total value of $4,644.30m compared to $3,245.93m in the week-ended November 13, 2026, offsetting the 83.88 per cent ($146.74m) decrease in FX Derivatives transactions (comprising FX Forward) for the week-ended 20 November 2026.”

FX Spot transactions accounted for 99.40 per cent of total market turnover with a daily average of $928.86m, increasing its share from 94.89 per cent in the prior week.

Conversely, FX Derivatives, representing FX Forwards turnover, contracted sharply from $174.93m (a daily average of $34.99m and a 5.11 per cent market share) to $28.19m (a daily average of $5.64m), narrowing its share of total weekly FX turnover to just 0.60 per cent.

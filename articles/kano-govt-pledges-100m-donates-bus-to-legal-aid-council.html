Kano Govt pledges ₦100m, donates bus to Legal Aid Council

By Vanguard Vanguard 6d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Vanguard

The Kano State Government has pledged ₦100 million and a brand-new operational bus to the Legal Aid Council of Nigeria to strengthen the delivery of free legal

Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf announced the support when he received the Chairman of the Governing Board of the Legal Aid Council of Nigeria, Justice Sulaiman Durosinlorun Kawu, alongside the Director-General, management team and other officials of the Council during a courtesy visit to the Government House, Kano.

The governor said poverty should not deprive anyone of access to justice, stressing that no indigent or vulnerable citizen should remain in detention without legal representation simply because of a lack of financial resources.

He commended the Council for its contributions to the administration of justice, particularly its provision of free legal representation and assistance to Nigerians who cannot afford the services of private legal practitioners.

Yusuf reaffirmed his administration’s commitment to strengthening its partnership with the Council, promising necessary logistical, administrative and financial support within available resources.

As part of efforts to improve the Council’s operations in the state, the governor directed the commissioners for Justice and Works to assess its office facilities for possible renovation and upgrading.

He also ordered improvements in office furniture and electricity supply to provide a more conducive working environment.

In addition to the ₦100 million pledged to the Council’s national headquarters, Yusuf assured its Kano State office of continued financial assistance and promised a new bus to facilitate its operations and outreach activities across the state.

The governor urged government institutions, particularly agencies in the justice sector, to cooperate with the Council to enable it to discharge its responsibilities effectively.

He also called on Nigerians to sustain peaceful coexistence, support national development and pray for the country’s leaders, while urging collective commitment to ensuring free, fair and peaceful conduct of the 2027 general elections.

Earlier, Kawu commended the governor for his commitment to the welfare and development of Kano State.

He explained that the Council’s visit was part of its working tour of the North-West geopolitical zone, aimed at strengthening partnerships with state governments, assessing the delivery of legal aid services and promoting wider access to justice.

Kawu disclosed that the Council provided legal assistance to approximately 2,500 people in Kano State between 2021 and 2023.

He added that the Council had also facilitated the release of about 20 inmates by paying fines and court-ordered compensation on behalf of individuals who could not afford the payments.

The chairman noted that Kano occupied a strategic position in the administration of justice in the North-West, particularly because of its large custodial facilities and the significant number of awaiting-trial inmates requiring legal representation.

He appealed to the state government to further support the Council through the provision of operational vehicles, improved office facilities, information technology equipment and annual financial subventions.

According to him, such assistance would enhance the delivery of legal aid services across the state.

Kawu maintained that access to justice should not be determined by an individual’s financial circumstances, reaffirming the Council’s commitment to protecting the constitutional rights of indigent Nigerians.

The statement was signed by the Kano State Commissioner for Information and Internal Affairs, Sunusi Bature Dawakin Tofa, and dated November 17, 2026.

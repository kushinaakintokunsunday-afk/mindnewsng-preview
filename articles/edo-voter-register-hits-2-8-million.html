Edo voter register hits 2.8 million

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Independent National Electoral Commission has put the number of registered voters in Edo State at 2.8 million following the conclusion of the Continuous Vot

The Edo State Resident Electoral Commissioner, Obo Effanga, disclosed this on Friday, adding that the latest registration exercise had increased the state’s voter population.

He said the final figure would be announced after the commission’s national headquarters completed its clean-up exercise.

INEC had earlier announced that 131,165 new voters were registered in Edo during the Continuous Voter Registration exercise conducted between August 2025 and July 2026.

Providing an update on the collection of Permanent Voter Cards, Effanga said 23,401 PVCs had been collected across the state since distribution commenced on October 9.

He explained that the figure comprised 6,319 old PVCs, 5,128 newly produced cards and 11,954 PVCs relating to transfers.

Effanga said: “The state currently has 2.8 million registered voters after the last registration exercise. However, the final figure would be released after the recent clean-up exercise by the national body.”

The REC disclosed that PVCs belonging to voters who registered during the first and second phases of the Continuous Voter Registration exercise were available for collection at INEC offices across the state’s 18 local government areas.

He recalled that the first phase of the registration exercise lasted from August to December 2025, while the second phase took place between January and April 2026.

On preparations for the forthcoming elections, Effanga said the commission was working towards ensuring a smooth electoral process.

Speaking on the mock accreditation exercise scheduled for November 28, he said it would be conducted in 384 polling units across the state.

He explained that Edo had 192 registration areas, also known as wards, with two polling units selected from each registration area for the exercise.

According to him, the exercise would provide an opportunity to test the commission’s electoral technology and assess the preparedness of its personnel ahead of the elections.

He added that the state office had submitted the list of proposed polling units to INEC headquarters for approval.

Effanga said the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System would be deployed during the exercise to demonstrate voter accreditation, result capture and transmission to the INEC Result Viewing Portal.

On the recruitment of ad hoc personnel, the REC explained that INEC did not have sufficient permanent staff to conduct elections across the state’s 4,519 polling units, making the recruitment of temporary personnel necessary.

He said recruitment would commence after the closure of the application portal, following which the commission’s headquarters would process applicants’ details and forward the names to the respective states based on their preferred locations of service.

Successful applicants would subsequently be invited for screening and training before deployment, he added.

The REC identified Supervisory Presiding Officers and collation officers as other categories of ad hoc personnel required for the exercise.

He explained that Supervisory Presiding Officers were usually selected from among senior civil servants, particularly federal civil servants who met the required grade-level qualifications.

Addressing concerns over the safety of ad hoc personnel, particularly in view of previous incidents of electoral violence, Effanga said INEC was working with security agencies to ensure adequate protection for everyone involved in the electoral process.

He emphasised that security arrangements would cover not only election officials but also voters, journalists and other stakeholders participating in the exercise.

According to him, the commission had been holding regular meetings of the Inter-Agency Consultative Committee on Election Security, with the latest meeting held on Tuesday.

He also disclosed that he would participate in a national summit on election security organised by the Office of the National Security Adviser in collaboration with INEC.

The REC, however, stressed that maintaining electoral security required the cooperation of all stakeholders, particularly politicians, whom he accused of sometimes encouraging violence through their actions and campaign rhetoric.

He said politicians must understand that elections were civil exercises and should not threaten or intimidate voters into supporting particular candidates.

Effanga condemned statements suggesting that people who refused to vote for certain candidates should not visit particular communities, describing such rhetoric as unacceptable.

On whether election personnel were covered by insurance in the event of injuries or other incidents during elections, the REC confirmed that the commission had insurance arrangements for those engaged in the electoral process.

He said the coverage included life insurance, adding that INEC had previously taken responsibility for the medical treatment and other necessary support of corps members injured while performing electoral duties.

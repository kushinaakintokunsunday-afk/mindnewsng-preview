S’African court rejects Nigerian’s N430m claim over 117-day detention

By Punch Punch 18 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

A South African court has rejected Nigerian Sunday Okoro’s bid to revive his R5.4m damages claim against the Minister of Police over his arrest and detention fo

A South African news outlet, IOL, reported on Tuesday that the Supreme Court of Appeal dismissed Okoro’s application for reconsideration after his attempts to secure leave to appeal against an earlier ruling failed.

Okoro was arrested without a warrant on July 3, 2002, after police discovered the body of a man hanging by a bedsheet in the shower of room 7 at Evaleigh Court, an apartment he was said to have rented days before the discovery.

The report said Okoro remained in custody until November 29, 2002, when the murder charges against him were withdrawn.

Following his release, Okoro sued the Minister of Police, seeking R5.41m in damages over what he described as unlawful arrest and detention, as well as the resulting loss and suffering.

According to Forbes Advisor, as of the time this report was filed, R5.41m was approximately N430m, at an exchange rate of R1 to N79.48.

However, the KwaZulu-Natal High Court in Durban dismissed his claim with costs, ruling that the police had reasonable grounds to suspect him of murder and that his arrest and detention were lawful.

After failing to obtain leave to appeal from both the High Court and the Supreme Court of Appeal, Okoro approached the president of the appeal court for reconsideration.

The matter was heard by a three-judge panel comprising Acting Judge of Appeal Mjali, Acting Judge of Appeal Molitsoane and Judge of Appeal Nicholls.

In its judgment, the majority, comprising Molitsoane and Nicholls, dismissed his application for reconsideration with costs.

According to IOL, the court heard that Warrant Officer Allen Mark Naidu and Warrant Officer Sunil Seosunker were conducting crime-prevention duties in Durban’s Point area when they encountered Okoro outside Evaleigh Court.

“Naidu testified that Okoro entered the building after seeing their police vehicle, prompting the officers to follow him.

“Naidu later saw Okoro emerging from room 7 in a distressed state, repeatedly shouting the name Francine.

“Okoro asked the officers to follow him into the room, where they discovered a man of Indian descent hanging by a bedsheet in the shower.

“The deceased’s body was cold and stiff, with no pulse. Naidu became suspicious that the hanging had been staged to conceal a murder.

“He testified that the deceased’s feet were not positioned close enough to the shower ledge to suggest that he had used it to hang himself.”

According to the report, the court found no evidence that the police had misled the prosecution or deliberately withheld or misrepresented information to ensure that Okoro remained behind bars.

It therefore concluded that there was no basis to hold the Minister of Police liable for his detention after his first court appearance.

Stop copying Western cinema, AMAA jury president tells African filmmakers

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 6 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The President of the 2026 Africa Movie Academy Awards jury, Dorothy Wenner, has urged African filmmakers to stop imitating Western cinema and develop original s

Wenner said filmmakers should resist the pressure to satisfy foreign expectations and instead have confidence in their ability to tell authentic African stories.

Speaking during an interview with ARISE NEWS on Sunday, she said African cinema needed a distinct identity rather than reproducing Western styles and narratives.

“This is a new way of telling a story that is not a spoof of Western cinema, but it comes from storytelling from an African background and culture, So authenticity is something that you don’t create. It is something where, you know, you come to yourself. And it’s not an imitation,” she said.

She advised emerging filmmakers against copying previously successful films, urging them to develop original ideas and explore their individual creative perspectives.

“Don’t imitate the winner from last year. Very often happening. What I think is to really think, what have I to say? And what is my new way of telling my story, not imitating something that was successful earlier,” she stated.

Wenner also encouraged filmmakers to take creative risks and explore subjects that genuinely interest them.

She further stated, “I say, well, surprise the jury. Be courageous. And when it comes to subject matters, do look out, ask your friends and trust, do have a trust in what interests you.”

African films face distribution barriers in Europe

The jury president also raised concerns about the difficulty African films face in securing mainstream distribution in Europe, saying distributors often consider them too risky to release.

Speaking from her experience in Berlin, Germany, she said African films across different genres struggled to gain access to regular distribution channels.

“I’m from Berlin, Germany, and I can only say it’s so tough to have, to bring a film of African descent, no matter if it’s a documentary, if it’s comedy or whatever genre it is, into regular distribution. The distributor says, but this is so old-fashioned.

“Sometimes it drives me nuts. So to say, oh, it’s such a risk. We are in such difficult times. It is too risky to bring an African film into distribution,” she observed.

She said the Africa Movie Academy Awards had a role to play in creating new opportunities for African cinema through strategic efforts to promote its films.

“So here is where I find AMA is really at the fore of fighting for new revenues. And not with aggression, but with clever films and with clever strategies,” Wenner noted.

Wenner further cautioned African filmmakers against tailoring their productions to what they assume European audiences expect from the continent.

She declared, “So sometimes it’s also, and I hope I don’t sound too critical, but sometimes it’s also the African filmmaker thinks, oh, this is what is expected from us by the Europeans.

“And I think this is an outdated thing. This is a trap that I’ve seen. Quite a lot also African filmmakers get into.”

Wenner praises Nigerian, Kenyan films

Assessing entries for this year’s awards, Wenner described the quality of films submitted as exceptional, highlighting the emergence of new filmmakers and artists from across the continent.

“This was an exceptional year. I think overall internationally the film industry is in kind of like where do we move to, what’s happening, who’s coming to the cinemas.

“And so having had the chance of watching all these films I was extremely happy and excited and especially I would say because there were a lot of surprises.

“In a such new names and new names filmmakers also in other artistic departments that really climbed to the top in lightning speed and the films that we got to see were surprising, innovative and they have a kind of, there is an urgency to say something and that we got films from all over the continent I think was making all of us very proud,” Wenner noted.

She singled out Nigeria’s The Herd and Kenya’s One Woman, One Bra as two outstanding entries.

Speaking about The Herd, she said, “This is a film that really, really took me by surprise. And I remember when I was watching it I didn’t know where it would go to.”

On the Kenyan film, she said, “The title is very provocative. It’s a satire. It’s a comedy. But I can’t really remember to having seen a film that would put as a topic international aid and how it affects communities in film, in a feature film. Stunning, stunning actress, lead actress.”

She also called for fresh approaches to documentary filmmaking, noting the growing influence of mobile phones on how people record and present their experiences.

“If you go into documentary, allow the new reality in which everyone is constantly having their phones and has become a director of his and her own film. Life, sorry, not sure. Director of your own film.

“So thereby you have to find new ways of storytelling within documentary. Observe. Observation is very important to create something like authenticity,” she said.

Wenner said filmmakers needed to trust their instincts and draw from their own experiences when developing stories.

Wenner said, “I think we have to find our own voice. As creative people, that’s very important because oftentimes creative people are the lead, you know?

“So you find your own voice and you have confidence in yourself and confidence in your ability to tell that story. And know that what you are telling is based on truth. Not someone else’s truth, but your own truth.”

She added that filmmakers did not necessarily need large budgets to communicate important ideas, but should make creative use of the resources available to them.

“It’s always a big issue, you know, no matter where we are. But how is it that you use the resources that you have in order to tell that story? How do you apply what you have to communicate those ideas that you feel are important and how you do that in a creative way?,” she concluded.

Meanwhile, PUNCH Online reported Monday that Nollywood actors Wale Ojo and Linda Ejiofor were among Nigeria’s biggest winners at the recently concluded 2026 Africa Movie Academy Awards.

The 22nd edition of the awards was held on Sunday, November 15, 2026, at the Marriott Hotel in Ikeja, Lagos State.

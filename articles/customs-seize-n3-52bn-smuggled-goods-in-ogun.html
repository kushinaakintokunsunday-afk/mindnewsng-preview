Customs seize N3.52bn smuggled goods in Ogun

By Punch Punch 3d ago 2 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Nigeria Customs Service, Ogun 1 Area Command, Idiroko, says it “seized various smuggled goods, including 6,037 wraps of cannabis, with a Duty Paid Value of N3.52bn in October.”

The Acting Area Comptroller, Mr Abdulhamid Ahmed, disclosed this at a news conference on Saturday in Idiroko.

Ahmed said the seizures underscored the command’s commitment to enforcing customs laws, preventing the importation and circulation of prohibited and harmful products, and protecting the nation’s economic interests.

He said the seizures were made during coordinated operations by customs officers at “various smuggling flashpoints across the state.”

According to him, the “operations were intelligence-led and supported by improved enforcement strategies and collaboration with sister security agencies.”

He added that the collaboration helped officers track smugglers, intercept contraband and prevent the illicit goods from entering the country.

Ahmed listed the “seized items to include 20,500 bottles of cough syrup, 125 live cartridges, 828,920 tablets of various brands, 627,320 capsules, 95 pills of prostate medication and 4,000 Enox injections comprising 2,850 ampoules.

“Other seizures included 5,173 bags of foreign parboiled rice weighing 50kg each, 6,037 wraps of cannabis sativa, 1,067 used tyres, 1,037 25-litre kegs of Premium Motor Spirit and 185 packages of sanitary pads and diapers.”

He also listed “two trucks and several vehicles, including a 2024 Toyota RAV4, Toyota Highlander, two Lexus vehicles, a 2000 Toyota Hilux, 2013 Toyota RAV4, Toyota Sienna, 2010 Toyota Venza, Opel, Mazda and 2012 Range Rover Sport.”

The acting comptroller said the total DPV of the seized items with available valuations stood at N3.52bn.

He explained that some smugglers abandoned their consignments and fled into nearby bushes upon sighting approaching customs patrol teams.

Ahmed reiterated the command’s commitment to intensifying anti-smuggling operations to protect the nation’s economy and safeguard public health.

Earlier, the Comptroller-General of Customs, Mr Bashir Adeniyi, urged personnel to “sustain their efforts in combating smuggling activities across the borders.”

Adeniyi also “appealed to residents of border communities to collaborate with the service by providing useful intelligence” and “supporting campaigns against economic and cross-border crimes.”

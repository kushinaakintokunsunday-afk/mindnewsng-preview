Abaribe urges leaders to embrace Azikiwe’s ideals

By Punch Punch 16 Nov 2026 5 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Senator representing Abia South Senatorial Zone, Enyinnaya Abaribe, has said towering leadership, radical change of mindset backed by a clear philosophy and

Abaribe said 30 years after the death of Nigeria’s first President and nationalist, Dr Nnamdi Azikiwe, many countries in Africa, especially Nigeria, were still in an unending search for leadership anchored on firm principles which Azikiwe had championed over 50 years ago.

He stated this on Monday at the 14th Annual Zik Lecture Series with the theme, ‘Zik’s vision for Renaissance: Lessons from emerging economies in Africa’, held at the Secretariat Complex of the Academic Staff Union of Universities, Nnamdi Azikiwe University, Awka, Anambra State.

The annual Zik’s lecture series is in honour of Nigeria’s first President and nationalist, the late Dr Nnamdi Azikiwe.

In the series, Zik’s virtues, leadership, legacies and love for the development of Nigeria and Africa are highlighted.

Abaribe, who gave the keynote address at the event, noted that the greatest problem facing Nigeria was leadership, adding that the late Azikiwe was a great nationalist, whose virtues, leadership and legacies should be imbibed for sustainable growth in the country.

He also listed uncensored exposure to social media, breakdown of family structure, unilateral focus of schools on grades and glorification of ill-gotten wealth as some of the key factors responsible for moral decadence in the country today.

He said, “For many people from my generation, Zik represents what towering leadership backed by a clear philosophy is about.

“For Zik, leadership was not just about political power, but mainly about using every available arsenal such as education, re-orientation, call for freedom, infrastructural development and precocious vision to direct the trajectory of the people.

“This, for me, is what leadership should be about. It is to our disadvantage that decades after the great Zik left this world, many countries in Africa, especially Nigeria, are still in an unending search for leadership anchored on the firm principles which Zik had championed over half a century ago.

“One critical thing which framed Zik’s vision and which became the motto of his West Africa Pilot was Dante Alighieri’s ‘show the light, and the people will find the way.

“Some of the things that have led us to where we are today are uncensored exposure to social media, breakdown of family structure, unilateral focus of schools on grades and glorification of ill-gotten wealth. Today, we glorify ill-gotten wealth.

“A society that applauds sudden wealth without questioning is losing its moral values. This is the greatest danger that Nigeria is facing today. If you reward integrity, you will produce statesmen.”

Addressing the students, the Senator advised them that the purpose of education is for them to have the value so that when they are given their certificate, they would be found to be worthy in character and learning.

“This was what the great Zik propounded many years ago, but today, education doesn’t seem to translate into moral values. We can lament how our young ones are drifting away, but we ought to know that what is happening today is a manner by which the young ones are learning from their leaders.

“State capture is a systematic capture of the institution of the state, not towards a public interest, but the interest of a few. The Nigerian state is gradually becoming an enterprise for a few. Policies, justice and interventions cannot be for a few, it must be for all.

“If we continue as we are doing, we would have entrenched immorality as a part of government, and we may no longer be able to correct it, when immorality will be the norm.

“Azikiwe understood that values make a man and therefore, he insisted that we must at all times put education and moral values first in making Nigeria get to that position it aspires to be. We must ensure that we imbibe the virtues of discipline, morality, and character. We must follow Zik’s footsteps to ensure that,” Abaribe added.

Also speaking, the Benefactor, Zik Lecture Series, Senator Ben Obi, said he took the initiative to establish the lecture series as a modest but purposeful contribution to the preservation and promotion of the ideals of Dr Nnamdi Azikiwe.

Obi said Zik was not merely a political leader, but a nationalist and advocate of African emancipation who understood that the liberation of a people must begin with the liberation of their minds through education, critical thinking, civic consciousness and responsible leadership in building a strong and prosperous nation.

He eulogised the late Azikiwe, saying he fought to ensure Nigeria remained one.

Earlier, in his welcome address, the Vice Chancellor, UNIZIK, Prof. Ugochukwu Anyaehie, said over the years, the Zik lecture series has established itself as a premier intellectual forum for interrogating critical socio-political, economic, and developmental issues affecting Nigeria and the African continent.

According to the VC, the 14th edition, alongside the school’s convocation ceremonies, is a reminder that a university is not merely a centre for the award of degrees, but a crucible of ideas designed to illuminate the path toward societal transformation.

“The theme chosen for this year’s lecture is both timely and deeply resonant. Long before the modern discourse on African economic integration and self-reliance gained global traction, Dr Nnamdi Azikiwe articulated a bold philosophy of Renascent Africa.

“He envisioned a continent liberated not only from political subjugation, but also from economic dependency, intellectual stagnation and social fragmentation.

“Zik believed firmly in the capacity of the African mind to innovate, to build resilient institutions and to harness indigenous resources for collective prosperity.

“Today, several emerging economies across Africa are demonstrating what is possible when visionary leadership, institutional discipline, investment in human capital, and strategic economic diversification converge.

\”From rapid strides in digital innovation and renewable energy to infrastructural renewal and regional trade integration, these economies offer practical lessons that align remarkably with Zik’s foundational ideals,” the VC added.

Govt deploys technology to expand access to education

By Punch Punch 17 Nov 2026 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Federal Government has unveiled a National Education Technology Strategy aimed at addressing the challenges of out-of-school children and teacher shortages

The National Coordinator, Special Programmes Operation and Implementation Unit at the Federal Ministry of Education, Adebayo Onigbanjo, disclosed this on Monday while outlining the strategy, which seeks to integrate 26 government initiatives into a unified digital education system.

Onigbanjo said the initiatives were designed to operate as interconnected interventions rather than separate projects, with the aim of strengthening teaching, learning, education management, skills development and financing.

He explained that the National Education Verification and Identification initiative would “enable the government to map children and assign them unique Learner Identification Numbers, making it easier to identify those outside the education system and direct interventions towards them.”

According to him, the data would support targeted programmes for out-of-school children, girls and learners enrolled in home education programmes.

He said the Inspire Live initiative would connect pupils in schools without qualified subject teachers to certified teachers through digital platforms, while zero-rated data would eliminate internet subscription costs as a barrier to accessing online lessons.

“The main message today is that these are not 26 separate projects; they form one connected system. The digital infrastructure is what connects them,” he said.

Onigbanjo said the strategy would also link education with employment and entrepreneurship through programmes such as the Digital Training Academy, Technical and Vocational Education and Training and entrepreneurship initiatives.

He identified the NELFund loans, Student Venture Capital Grant and Tertiary Institution Staff Support as interventions designed to ease financial constraints facing students and educators.

The coordinator said public portals and dashboards would also strengthen transparency by allowing citizens to monitor government commitments and assess the implementation of education programmes.

He explained that the strategy would be driven by five pillars, including data and interoperability, digital infrastructure, technology and procurement, teacher transformation and other measures aimed at strengthening education delivery.

On data management, he said the government would build on the Nigeria Education Data Infrastructure by adopting common data standards to enable existing education databases to exchange information automatically while preserving institutional autonomy.

He said priority would be given to data on schools, teachers, enrolment and education financing, with personal records protected under the Nigerian Data Protection Act.

On infrastructure, he said the government would prioritise reliable electricity, broadband connectivity and digital devices for schools, particularly in rural and underserved communities, while making provisions for the maintenance of such facilities.

He added that the strategy would build on existing national connectivity initiatives, including Project BRIDGE and the Universal Service Provision Fund.

Onigbanjo said the government would also adopt vendor-neutral standards and transparent, performance-based procurement systems to encourage Nigerian developers to produce technology solutions aligned with the national curriculum.

He said the National Teachers’ Digital Competency Framework would establish standards for teachers’ digital skills, certification and career progression, supported by a national teacher professional development portal.

The strategy, he said, aligned with President Bola Tinubu’s Renewed Hope Agenda and the Federal Ministry of Education’s vision of using education to drive national development, innovation and global competitiveness.

He acknowledged the contributions of the Federal Ministry of Communications, Innovation and Digital Economy, state governments, development partners, the private sector, academia and civil society to the strategy’s development, particularly the Mastercard Foundation and the World Bank Group.

The strategy comes amid persistent challenges in Nigeria’s education sector, particularly the large population of children excluded from formal education and shortages of qualified teachers in public schools.

A 2024 UNICEF report estimated that 18.3 million Nigerian children were out of school, comprising 10.2 million primary school-age children and 8.1 million junior secondary school-age children. The Federal Ministry of Education, however, has cited an estimated 15 million out-of-school children in its recent interventions.

Teacher shortages also remain a major concern. Data reported from the Federal Ministry of Education’s digital education database showed that 31.8 million learners in public schools were served by 592,077 teachers, representing a learner-teacher ratio of approximately 54 to one.

The Universal Basic Education Commission has estimated that an additional 194,876 qualified teachers are needed in basic education.

The strategy also builds on the Federal Government’s rollout of the Digital National Education Management Information System (DNEMIS), launched in July 2026 to improve education data collection, planning and accountability.

As of its initial nationwide rollout, the platform had captured data from 124,548 schools, covering more than 40 million learners and 1.19 million teachers.

The government said the new EdTech strategy would provide a coordinated framework for using technology to address these challenges and ensure that investments in digital education translate into improved learning outcomes.

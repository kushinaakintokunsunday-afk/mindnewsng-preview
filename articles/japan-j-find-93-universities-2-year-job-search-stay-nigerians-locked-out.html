Japan Publishes Official List of 93 Universities Whose Graduates Can Stay 2 Years to Search for Jobs - No African University Made the List

By Mindnewsng Mindnewsng 10 Nov 2026 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Mindnewsng

Japan's J-Find scheme gives graduates of 93 listed top world universities a 2-year 'Future Creation Talent' stay to hunt for jobs or prepare a startup - but eve

Japan has refreshed its official list of the overseas universities whose graduates qualify for the country's most generous job-search visa - and for African graduates the answer remains stubborn: none of the 93 universities is African.

What the J-Find scheme is

The Future Creation Talent system (J-Find), running since April 2023, grants graduates of top world universities the “Designated Activities (Future Creation Talent)” residence status once they arrive in Japan - letting them search for employment or prepare to launch a business for up to two years.

  • Your degree must come from a university ranked top 100 in at least two of the three major global rankings - QS, THE (Times) and Shanghai's ARWU - and you must have graduated within the last five years.
  • You must show about 200,000 yen (≈US$1,300) in living funds for the initial stay, plus a CV and schedule of planned activities.
  • The official list currently names 93 institutions (October 2026 update), dominated by universities in the US, UK, Europe, China and Asia.

The African exclusion - again

Even the continent's strongest universities - Cape Town, Cairo, Wits, and Nigeria's own University of Ibadan, UNILAG and Covenant University - do not feature. It mirrors the story at the UK's High Potential Individual (HPI) visa, whose list of 82 criteria-universities likewise contains no African institution, shutting out the continent's best graduates from both schemes.

Realistic routes for Nigerian graduates

  1. Study in a Japanese university first: graduates of Japanese universities get their own job-search route - 6 months of “Designated Activities (job hunting)”, extendable to one year, plus up to 28 hours/week part-time work with permission. Language schools feed into this pipeline.
  2. MEXT and university scholarships: Japan offers fully funded scholarships (undergraduate to PhD) that remove the cost barrier, after which the domestic job-search route applies.
  3. Specified Skilled Worker (SSW) visas: if you pass the skill and language exams, sectors like care work, construction, food service and hospitality accept foreign workers from any country - including Nigeria.
  4. Graduate from a top-100 university abroad (US, UK, China, EU) and your degree doubles as a J-Find ticket - in other words, the scheme rewards credentials Japan trusts.

This article is for general information only and is not immigration or legal advice. Eligibility rules and lists are updated by Japan's Immigration Services Agency - always verify the current list and requirements on moj.go.jp and studyinjapan.go.jp before making plans.

Sources: Japan Immigration Services Agency (J-Find/Designated Activities guidance); JETRO overview of the Future Creation Talent system; Yen.com.gh report on the October 2026 93-university list.

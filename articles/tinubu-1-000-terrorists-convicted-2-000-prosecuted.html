Tinubu: 1,000 terrorists convicted, 2,000 prosecuted

By Vanguard Vanguard 16 Nov 2026 5 min read

By Kingsley Omonobi

President Bola Tinubu has disclosed that more than 1,000 terrorists have been convicted, while over 2,000 suspected terrorists are currently undergoing prosecution as part of his administration’s efforts to dismantle terrorist networks across the country.

The President also said Nigerian security forces had killed or arrested four of eight identified senior global terrorist leaders, describing the development as evidence of the country’s growing capacity to dismantle terrorist command structures.

Tinubu, who was represented by the National Security Adviser, Mallam Nuhu Ribadu, made the disclosures on Monday at the opening of the 2026 Chief of Defence Intelligence (CDI) Annual Conference in Abuja.

He said the number of convictions and ongoing prosecutions placed Nigeria among countries with high rates of terrorism-related prosecutions globally.

Highlighting the operational successes recorded by security agencies, the President cited the elimination of senior Islamic State leader, Abu Bilal al-Minuki, in May 2026, as well as the arrests of other prominent terrorist figures.

According to him, the operations also targeted Ahmed Ohida’s ISWAP southern expansion network, while Abu Barra of Ansaru and Mamuda of Mahmudawa were arrested in 2025.

“Recent operational successes demonstrate our growing capacity to dismantle terrorist leadership, disrupt expansion networks and deny violent groups freedom of action.

“Under this administration, four of eight identified senior terrorist leaders have been killed or arrested. These gains include action against Ahmed Ohida’s ISWAP southern expansion network, the elimination of senior Islamic State leader Abu Bilal al-Minuki in May 2026, and the arrests of Abu Barra of Ansaru and Mamuda of Mahmudawa in 2025,” he said.

Tinubu, however, identified the remaining four senior terrorist figures as Ba Shuwa of ISWAP, Abdullahi Oro of IS-Sahel, Sadiku of JAS in the North-Central region and Bakura Doro in the Lake Chad Basin.

He said security agencies were sustaining operations against the remaining leaders and their networks, adding that senior bandit leaders, including Halilu Sububu, had also been killed alongside several of their fighters.

On developments in the Niger Delta, the President disclosed that incidents of illegal oil bunkering and vandalism of critical oil and gas installations declined from 32 in the first quarter of 2026 to 21 in the second quarter and 17 in the third quarter.

He said the figures represented a 46.88 per cent reduction between the first and third quarters.

Tinubu added that crude oil and condensate production averaged approximately 1.68 million barrels per day in August 2026, stressing the need to sustain the protection of the country’s energy assets and revenue sources.

He attributed the security gains to improved intelligence sharing and coordinated operations among the Department of State Services (DSS), Armed Forces, Police and other security agencies.

The President said the administration would consolidate the gains and sustain pressure on criminal and terrorist networks, particularly ahead of the 2027 general elections.

“Every Nigerian must be able to live, work and exercise their democratic rights in safety,” he said.

On the changing nature of security threats, Tinubu said technological advancements, particularly artificial intelligence, machine learning, cyber capabilities and unmanned systems, were creating new opportunities for intelligence gathering and security operations.

He said such technologies could help security agencies detect patterns, map criminal networks and generate early warnings of potential threats.

The President called for the integration of AI-assisted analysis, geospatial intelligence and unmanned surveillance through secure and interoperable systems capable of translating information into timely operational decisions.

He, however, cautioned that artificial intelligence outputs must be verified through accountable human judgement, while data protection and cybersecurity safeguards must be incorporated into the systems.

“Technology must strengthen the trusted human intelligence on which effective security depends,” he said.

Tinubu further disclosed that he had directed the Special Adviser on Homeland Security, Major General A.A. Famadewa (rtd), to coordinate the preparation of a Comprehensive National Threat Assessment and a Five-Year Rolling Strategic Defence Operations Plan.

He said the initiative was aimed at placing accurate, integrated and forward-looking intelligence at the centre of Nigeria’s defence planning.

The President also emphasised the importance of collaboration among the Armed Forces, Defence Intelligence Agency, DSS, National Intelligence Agency, Nigeria Police Force, Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps and other relevant institutions.

He said intelligence cooperation must extend to regional and international partners, subnational entities, technology companies and research institutions.

Tinubu listed the United States, France, the United Kingdom, China, the United Arab Emirates, Türkiye and Nigeria’s African neighbours among countries with which his administration had strengthened security partnerships.

He pledged to deepen intelligence sharing, training and technological cooperation while safeguarding Nigeria’s sovereignty and national interest.

The President also urged Nigeria’s Defence Advisers and Attachés abroad to strengthen strategic intelligence and defence relationships with their host countries and international partners.

He called on State Office Coordinators to intensify collaboration with local security agencies to improve intelligence gathering and coordination.

Tinubu commended the Chief of Defence Intelligence, Lieutenant General Emmanuel Akomaye Parker Undiandeye, and his team for their leadership and contributions to strengthening defence intelligence and strategic cooperation.

In his remarks, the Minister of Defence, retired General Christopher Musa, commended intelligence personnel for their professionalism and contributions to national security.

Musa urged them to remain proactive, stressing that credible, timely and actionable intelligence was critical to successful military operations.

He also reaffirmed the Federal Government’s commitment to indigenous defence production, describing local manufacturing as essential to developing sustainable military capabilities.

Similarly, the Chief of Defence Staff, General Olufemi Oluyede, described intelligence as fundamental to operational planning, strategic decision-making and effective military responses.

Oluyede called for increased investment in artificial intelligence, advanced data analytics, geospatial intelligence and unmanned systems.

He said the Armed Forces would continue to prioritise intelligence integration, capacity development and technological innovation.

The CDS stressed the need to shift security operations from largely reactive responses to proactive, intelligence-led engagements capable of anticipating and disrupting threats before they materialise.

The conference, organised by the Defence Intelligence Agency, is themed, “Optimising Intelligence through Technological Innovations for Effective Strategic Operations in a Complex Security Environment.”

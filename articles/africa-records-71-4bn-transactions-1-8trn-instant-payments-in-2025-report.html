Africa Records 71.4bn Transactions, $1.8trn Instant Payments In 2025 — Report

By Channels TV Channels TV 5d ago 4 min read

Africa’s instant payment systems processed 71.4 billion transactions worth $1.8 trillion in 2025, nearly three times the volume recorded in 2021, according to a new report by the AfricaNenda Foundation, the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa and the World Bank Group.

The fifth edition of the State of Inclusive Instant Payment Systems in Africa (SIIPS) report, published on Thursday, highlighted the rapid expansion of digital payment infrastructure across the continent but warns that access alone has not translated into widespread everyday usage.

According to the report, 89 per cent of African adults live in countries with access to an instant payment system.

However, a survey of 1,403 digital payment users across 15 markets found that 55 per cent still use cash for about half of their transactions.

It also found that more than half of respondents had experienced payment problems, including delays and transfers to the wrong recipients, while one in three affected users never reported such incidents, largely because they did not know how to do so.

“Five years ago, our first report mapped 29 instant payment systems; today there are 39, and one in two African adults live in a country served by an inclusive instant payment system IIPS,” said Sabine F. Mensah, who is Deputy CEO of AfricaNenda Foundation and lead author of the report.

“The question has changed. It is no longer whether Africans can pay instantly, but whether they trust the system enough to stop reaching for cash. Our research shows that trust is won in seconds, through a payment that goes through instantly, with preventive safeguards such as recipient name validation, and a payment confirmation message received in real time. It is kept by getting timely resolution and people’s money back when something goes wrong.”

39 Instant Payment Systems

The report identified 39 live instant payment systems across Africa, comprising 35 domestic systems in 29 countries and four regional systems.

Overall, 43 African countries now have access to instant payment capabilities through domestic or regional platforms.

Four domestic systems were introduced in the past year in Liberia, Burundi, Guinea and Mauritania, while a new regional system now serves the eight countries of the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU).

Another 17 countries have instant payment system projects underway.

Transaction volumes, according to the report, have grown by an average of 28 per cent annually since 2021, while the average transaction value has declined from $45 to $26, indicating increased use for smaller, everyday purchases.

Mobile money systems accounted for 73 per cent of all transaction volumes.

The report also identified instant payments, clear confirmation messages and recipient verification as the three major factors that build users’ confidence in digital transactions.

It noted that the handling of failed transactions also influences adoption, with 43 per cent of users who lost money reducing their use of digital payments, while those who received refunds often increased their usage.

Trust, Fraud Protection Challenges

On security, the report found that payment service providers are adopting artificial intelligence fraud prevention measures faster than instant payment system operators, while users largely perceive AI as a risk, particularly because of threats such as deepfakes.

It further observed that countries with operational instant payment systems record approximately seven percentage points higher active account usage.

According to the report, more inclusive instant payment systems also create opportunities to expand access to other financial services, including credit, through digital cash flow records.

“Africa has built inclusive instant payment infrastructure at remarkable speed, drawing on the talent of its own software engineers and designers,” said Robert Ochola, who is CEO of AfricaNenda Foundation.

“The next five years must be about making these systems affordable to use, safe and sustainable to run.”

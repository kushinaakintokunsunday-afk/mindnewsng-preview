Zamfara: Suspected bandits kill guard, abduct family members in fresh attack

By The Sun Nigeria The Sun Nigeria 6d ago 4 min read

By Philip Nwosu

Gunmen suspected to be bandits have killed a security guard and abducted several members of a family and their visitor during an attack on the residence of a businessman, Alhaji Babangida Sama’ila, in Sabuwar Kaura, Kaura Namoda Local Government Area of Zamfara State.

The attack, which reportedly lasted about an hour on Monday, came barely 24 hours after gunmen killed seven worshippers, including a senior aide to Governor Dauda Lawal, during an attack on a mosque in the same community.

The latest incident has heightened tension in Kaura Namoda, where residents have expressed growing concern over the worsening security situation and the apparent freedom with which armed groups operate.

A resident of Sabuwar Kaura, who spoke on the incident, said the attackers initially abducted three girls, their mother, two boys and a visitor to the family.

The mother was subsequently released, while the other abductees remained in captivity as of Monday.

The resident said three of the girls were minors, aged between 14 and 16, while the abducted visitor was also between 15 and 16 years old.

“They are my neighbours. The children are between 14 and 16 years old. One of those abducted was a visitor to the family and is also between 15 and 16,” he said.

According to the resident, the gunmen killed the security guard when he attempted to prevent them from gaining access to the residence.

He said the attackers subsequently used a makeshift structure to climb over the fence, while Sama’ila and one of his sons escaped by jumping over it.

The resident expressed concern that the attackers operated for about an hour without immediate intervention from security personnel, despite the residence being close to the General Hospital, Kaura Namoda.

He alleged that warning shots fired by soldiers stationed at the residence of the Rector of the Federal Polytechnic, Kaura Namoda, alongside local vigilantes, might have disrupted the attackers’ operation.

He also identified nearby settlements, including Gidan Sambo, Unguwar Sarkin Musulmi and Madira, as possible routes through which the bandits could have accessed the town.

The resident further alleged that some communities around Kaura Namoda had entered into reconciliation arrangements with bandits, warning that such agreements could create additional security risks.

He claimed that Unguwar Sarkin Musulmi was less than a kilometre from the camp of a notorious bandit leader identified as Dan Sadiya.

Another resident expressed suspicion that some bandits might be secretly living within the town, making it difficult for security agencies to identify and monitor their movements.

Death toll from mosque attack rises to seven

Meanwhile, the death toll from Sunday’s attack on worshippers at a mosque in the Sabuwar Kaura Low-Cost area of Kaura Namoda has risen to seven, while 15 others were reportedly abducted.

The attack occurred shortly after worshippers concluded their early morning congregational prayers.

Among those killed was Hon. Nasiru Sani Abubakar, an aide to Governor Lawal.

Other victims were identified as Lauwali Abubakar, Malam Abdulrazak, Malam Abubakar, Malam Musa, Malam Usman and Malam Lawal Abubakar.

A resident, Mallam Yusuf Bello Kaura, told Daily Trust that the gunmen opened fire on worshippers as they were leaving the mosque after prayers.

He said the incident had thrown the community into mourning, adding that although the area had experienced previous attacks, residents had never witnessed such a deadly incident.

Another resident, Auwalu Ibrahim, suspected that the attackers had laid an ambush around the mosque before the commencement of prayers.

He expressed concern that the attack occurred less than 10 miles from a military base, questioning how the gunmen managed to operate without resistance.

Muhammad Aliyu, another resident, said the remains of the seven victims were buried on Monday at about 11am.

He added that the bandits had yet to contact the families of those abducted as of Monday.

The killing of Governor Lawal’s aide and other worshippers had sparked public anger, with some residents reportedly booing the governor during the funeral prayers on Sunday.

Police deploy personnel

Meanwhile, the Zamfara State Commissioner of Police, Ahmad Bello, confirmed the mosque attack but gave a lower casualty figure.

Bello said one person was killed and four worshippers were abducted during the incident.

In a statement, the police commissioner said about 30 armed bandits, allegedly loyal to notorious kingpin Bello Wakkala, popularly known as Dan Sadiya, attacked the mosque in the Low-Cost area.

He said the motive behind the attack and the actual number of abducted persons were still being investigated.

Bello added that police personnel had been deployed to the community to prevent further attacks, while additional officers would be sent to the local government area to protect lives and property.

The two attacks within 24 hours have renewed concerns over the security situation in Zamfara, where banditry, kidnappings and attacks on rural communities continue to threaten residents.

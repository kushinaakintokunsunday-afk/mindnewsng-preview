Police probe officers over viral pregnant woman video in Abia

By Punch Punch 1d ago 3 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Abia State Police Command has stated that it is aware of and is investigating a viral video circulating on social media depicting an encounter between polic

In a statement issued on Sunday, the command’s Police Public Relations Officer, Maureen Chinaka, said on behalf of the Commissioner of Police that the video shows officers insisting the man be taken to the police station for allegedly driving a vehicle without a registration number plate.

“The command wishes to inform the public that the officers involved have been identified as officers of the Surveillance Patrol and Guard Team attached to Ubakala Divisional Police Station.

“An investigation into the incident has commenced to establish the circumstances surrounding the encounter and determine the extent of responsibility of the officers involved,” the statement read.

Chinaka further stated that preliminary findings indicate the man was driving a vehicle without a displayed registration number plate, contrary to applicable road traffic regulations 39(9) of the National Road Traffic Regulations, which require vehicles to display prescribed identification number plates.

The regulation of vehicle identification, she pointed out, is particularly important in view of prevailing security challenges, including vehicle theft and the use of unidentified vehicles for criminal activities.

The command, however, emphasises that the enforcement of traffic regulations must be carried out with sound judgement and consideration for human welfare.

According to the statement regarding the man’s claim that his wife was experiencing labour contractions and was about to give birth, “preliminary information indicates that she is fine and has not given birth as at the time of this release at 7.47 pm on Sunday.

“Furthermore, no medical evidence or documentation has so far been presented to substantiate the claim of imminent delivery. Nevertheless, the command maintains that the circumstances required the officers to exercise appropriate judgement, empathy and consideration for the welfare of the pregnant woman.”

The command regretted that while the officers were carrying out their official duties, “their apparent failure to demonstrate sufficient empathy towards the pregnant woman is strongly frowned upon by the command.”

The outgoing Commissioner of Police, AIG Wilfred Afolabi, reiterated that the Nigeria Police Force, in line with Section 4 of the Nigeria Police Act, is statutorily mandated to prevent and detect crime, protect lives and property, maintain law and order, uphold the rights of citizens and provide necessary assistance to persons in distress.

He further emphasised that the discharge of police duties must be guided by professionalism, empathy, restraint and respect for human dignity.

The CP, therefore, warned that while the command will not relent in enforcing extant laws and taking appropriate action against offenders, such responsibilities must be discharged with humanity, consideration and professionalism.

He advised members of the public to remain law-abiding and ensure compliance with vehicle registration and other applicable traffic regulations, as he also assured the public that the investigation will be thorough while appropriate disciplinary measures will be taken where misconduct is established.

France, Malaysia Boost Cooperation On Rare Earths, Defense

By Channels TV Channels TV 17 Nov 2026 2 min read

Photo: Channels TV

France and Malaysia agreed on Tuesday to deepen ties in the lucrative field of rare earth minerals and in defence, with visiting President Emmanuel Macron hailing a “new milestone” in relations.

Macron, who is on a two-day whistle-stop tour of the Southeast Asian nation, told a news conference that a joint rare earths project will include “the option for refining in France” — a “unique” agreement, according to Malaysia’s Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim.

Malaysia plays a major role in rare earth processing, courted by many countries as an important option outside China, which holds a monopoly on critical minerals used in products ranging from smartphones to fighter jets, electric cars and wind turbines.

The country already hosts the largest rare earths processing plant outside of China, and the planned project between French firm Carester and Malaysia’s Malaco Mining would add another 13,000 tonnes of processed minerals, including those needed for high-performance magnets.

Macron said the joint project was set for an operational launch early next year.

It will “not only encompass extraction, but also local processing of reserved volumes and capacities, including the option for refining in France, which was a key element for our entire value chain,” Macron said.

Anwar said that “we have done something which is unique”, adding: “We do not export raw materials. But with France, we’ll do so because you are committed to that — processing and working with Malaysians as a team”.

Carester in July announced plans with Malaco to build a rare earths separation plant in Malaysia’s northern Perak state under a 10-year joint venture.

Macron’s visit also saw the two governments sign agreements on defence, energy supply and digital cooperation.

This included the establishment of a regional centre for “countering hybrid threats” and the provision of Airbus-built search-and-rescue helicopters to the Malaysian armed forces.

“As we approach the 70th anniversary of our diplomatic relations, our two countries are reaching a new milestone with this visit,” Macron said.

Bilateral trade between France and Malaysia reached more than 4.9 billion euros ($5.4 billion) last year.

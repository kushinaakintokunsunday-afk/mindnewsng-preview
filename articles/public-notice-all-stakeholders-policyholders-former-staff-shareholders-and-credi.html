UPDATE ON STATE OF AFFAIRS/STATEMENT OF FACTS OF THE COMPANY AND DEVELOPMENTS RELATED THERETO — BY THE RECEIVER AND LIQUIDATOR

 It will be recalled that the undersigned did a publication on July 17, 2026, briefing all stakeholders of Niger Insurance, Plc. on state of affairs of the company, following a judgment of 5th June, 2026 in Suit No. FHC/ABJ/CS/887/2025, obtained in the name of the Company, without authority, by individuals who have no stake or shareholding in the company, but claiming to be Board Members of the company under Receivership/Liquidation.

For the purpose of those who must have missed the said publication, we shall restate in brief some salient facts contained therein, and thereafter state developments in respect of the company, vis-à-vis, position of the cases in the Appellate Courts, as at date:

  1. The operating license of Niger Insurance Plc. was cancelled by the official Regulatory Agency (National Insurance Commission), otherwise known as NAICOM, in June, 2022 for gross infraction against the prevailing Insurance Act and also for inability of the company to fulfill her core statutory obligations, i.e., payment of verified claims of its policy holders, since 2013 and inability to pay its staff salaries for well over a period of time, preceding 30 calendar months; after the company admitted those infractions in writing.
  2. In reaction to the license cancellation, the company immediately afterwards instituted an action at the Federal High Court Abuja, challenging the license cancellation and appointment of a Receiver/Liquidator for the company, by the Commission.
  3. The Federal High Court, Abuja, struck out the Suit for being incompetent and for lack of jurisdiction. The company immediately challenged that decision in an appeal to the Court of Appeal.
  4. The said Appeal was eventually dismissed by the Court of Appeal in 2024, following the filing of Notice of Withdrawal of the Appeal by the company.
  5. The company subsequently made a volve face and, engaged the services of another counsel (also a SAN) to file an application at the Court of Appeal to relist the dismissed Appeal, but the application was unanimously rejected and dismissed by the Court of Appeal sitting in Abuja, on the 27th of February, 2025.
  6. Dissatisfied with the decision of the Court of Appeal refusing to relist the dismissed appeal, the company went on further appeal to the Supreme Court; all the briefs of Argument have been duly filed and exchanged by the parties, but no hearing date has yet being given by the Apex Court.
  7. It was while the appeal on the subject matter of cancellation of the company license and appointment of a Receiver/Liquidator by the Commission was pending at the Supreme Court that the individuals who claimed to be Board members of the company in Receiver/Liquidation, initiated an action before the same Federal High Court, presided over, this time, by Hon. Justice J.K. Omotosho, vide an Amended Originating Summons dated 30th April, 2025 but filed on the 11th July, 2025; which action birthed the referenced judgment of 5th June, 2026 despite bringing to the attention of that Court the glaring issue of abuse of Court process involved.
  8. As mentioned above, despite the fact that attention of Justice Omotosho, via a Notice of Preliminary Objection, was pointedly drawn to the facts that the principal claims, issues and subject matters of the fresh case before him had been ruled upon by his fellow Judge of the same status ( Coram: Ekwo, J.) in January,  2023, and that the matter is now on appeal at the Supreme Court, awaiting determination on whether the Court of Appeal was right to dismiss the appeal of the company over the decision of Ekwo, J.,  the learned Justice Omotosho proceeded to hear the matter expeditiously, and gave judgment to the Plaintiffs on 5thJune 2026.
  9. The National Insurance Commission and the Receiver promptly filed their Notices of appeal against the judgement, of Omotosho, J. raising grounds of abuse of court process and also lack of jurisdiction/competence of the Presiding Judge, the fresh action having been barred by Statute of Limitation, having not been filed within the mandatory 3 months of occurrence of the alleged injurious actions of NAICOM, and their having to wait for over 2 calendar years after the said acts, before instituting the said fresh case in 2026, brought before Justice Omotosho.
  10. It should also be noted that both the Commission, (NAICOM) and the Receiver also filed along with their respective Notices of Appeal, Motions for Stay of Execution, by their Counsel, i.e., office of Prof Osipitan, SAN & Co, and Tochukwu, SAN & CO, at the Court of Appeal and the appeal, inclusive of the applications, are pending.
  11. It is also pertinent to note that the briefs of Argument had been filed by the parties Counsel, inclusive of Reply brief, and all being awaited now is hearing date from the Appeal Court Registry. Same goes with the earlier Appeal at the Apex Court in respect of the Court of Appeal dismissal of the challenge to the 2023 Ekwo, J’s judgment. The above stated scenario represents the undisputable development in the 3 cases cited above, all relating to Niger Insurance Plc, vis, a vis, efforts that had been made to circumvent due process of law, abuse of judicial process and the rule of law, by some known characters in this matter.
  12. It is pertinent to state that following the cancellation of the company’s operating license in June 2022 by the Commission/NAICOM and appointment of the Receiver, no shareholder of the company had opposed the actions taken by the Commission till date, albeit, gone to Court to challenge same, neither has any duly constituted Board of the company met to take any decision concerning the subject matter, or the company in general.
  13. The instituting of the case challenging the action of the Commission in June, 2022, was actually instigated and carried out on the instruction of a minority shareholder, without consultation with any of the company’s other shareholders.

The shareholder in question is merely one of the shareholders of the Plc company, and has only about 25% of Niger Insurance, Plc Shareholding, going by the last approved audited statement of the Company, and certainly not its majority shareholder. The authenticity of this fact can also be confirmed from the Commission, by any stakeholder.

  1. The very minority shareholder in question, after January 2023, i.e., following the striking out of Niger Insurance suit by Justice Ekwo of the Federal High Court, had approached the NAICOM and the Receiver, (verbally and in writing) requesting to not only buy but to be given the right of first refusal in the purchase of the 2 choicest properties of the company, i.e., its 10-storey building known as River Plaza and Mall, Abuja, as well as the Ikoyi property, popularly known as Polo House. The said deal could not go through following the ridiculous amount the minority shareholder of the company offered for purchase of the Rivers Plaza property, which of course, was vehemently rejected by the Receiver and the Commission (NAICOM).
  2. The advice by the then supervising Minister of the National Insurance Commission to the said shareholder at a meeting he conveyed in his office, to wit: not to short-change the company by buying its choicest property in Abuja, at an unreasonable price, was promptly turned down by the CEO of the said minority shareholder company.
  3. Niger Insurance, is a Plc, i.e., a Public Company duly registered under the CAC with 100% paid up share-capital, before its license was duly cancelled following its inability to meet up with its statutory responsibility for close to 10 calendar years, and having been serially given opportunities by the Commission to raise funds and recapitalise, and its failure to take advantage of the indulgence at the various times, up till June, 2022, when its license was eventually revoked.
  4. The last time the company paid claims of its policyholders was sometimes in 2016, and was then owing staff salaries for over 30 calendar months, prior to cancellation of its operating license by the National Insurance Commission (NAICOM).
  5. The last Actuary Valuation of the company showed it was owing close to 8,000 (Eight Thousand) policyholders, including Federal Government Agencies, in sum in the region of 2 digit billions of naira, aside its financial commitments to its numerous shareholders and other stakeholders.
  6. It should be noted by all stakeholders of the company, that Niger Insurance, being a public company, and assuming its licence had not been revoked by the Regulatory body, can only be recapitalised and come back to life, if the decision is supported by majority of its shareholders, at a meeting duly conveyed, pursuant to the mandatory procedure set out in CAMA, but certainly not at the instance of a company which does not hold majority of the shareholding of the company. We further state that even at that, the recapitalization exercise must be monitored by the Regulatory body, i.e., NAICOM and the SEC. The resolution of a simple majority of attendees will thereafter be tabled at the company’s Board meeting for further administrative process towards actual recapitalisation of the company.
  7. The last time Niger Insurance Plc. had a duly constituted Board meeting was in 2018. Since then, it did not have any recognised Board up to the time its operating license was cancelled. Before then, some of its Board members had resigned in writing and left the company in the hit of their challenges earlier mentioned herein, and had not been duly replaced before the company’s licence was revoked in June 2022.
  8. Indeed, some of such previous members of the Board whose names were used as Plaintiffs in the referenced Suit before Omotosho, J. have already gone to Court to ventilate their grievances over unauthorized use of their names in that Suit.
  9. EFFECT OF THE COURT CASES ON THE WORKS OF THE RECEIVER:It must be said that the pending cases, especially, the Omotosho, J. judgment of 5th June, 2026 have indeed constituted a bitter distraction to the Receiver/Liquidator, especially the unlawful resort to illegalities and unlawful operation at the instance of some faceless characters in in sealing off some of the company’s properties in Abuja and Lagos, thereby preventing its sales, and by extension, access of the Receiver to funds which he would have deployed into continued payments to policyholders, after verification of their claims.
  10. It should further be noted that the said illegal actions, purportedly on strength of the judgment delivered by Omotosho J, was carried out without any Order of the affected court, albeit, involvement of office of the Deputy Sheriff of the Federal High Court who ordinarily, is statutorily empowered and expected to carry out execution of any court judgment or Order, if any.

ON CURRENT LEGAL STATUS OF THE RECEIVER:

The undersigned states in unmistaken terms that his appointment remains valid and lawful, vis-à-vis, the statutorily activities and responsibility attached to the office, on the strength of justice I. E. Ekwo’s judgment, which is of the same hierarchy with that of justice Omotosho, and again, our having appealed against the latter’s judgment and filed a pending application for its execution.

It should also be noted that the Receiver since the January 2023 judgment in his favour by the Federal High Court (presided over by Hon. Justice Ekwo) which was upheld by dismissal of the Appellant’s Appeal against it, by the Court of Appeal Abuja, had continued in performance of his functions till date in the management of the company, and had indeed, since commenced payment to Individual Life Policyholders, whose claims had been duly verified and has also continued to attend to cases and claims against the company, among other assignments expected of him as the Receiver.

The undersigned wishes all stakeholders of the company to note that he has been ably strengthened and encouraged to continue in his statutory responsibility, having been guided by the extant Laws of the country and can therefore not be said to be in disobedience of any Court order or judgment, in any way, especially following the relevant judicial precedents laid down by our appellate courts in plethora of cases, for instances, the below captured cases:

(i)      BASIL OKOMA V. SUNDAY SAMUEL UDOH (2002) 1 NWLR 748 PG 438 (CA), where the Appellate court held thus: 

“Once an appeal is filed with a Motion for Stay, the applicant is not obliged to obey the judgment. Execution is suspended until hearing of Motion at least…This is because a party cannot be held to be in disobedience of the order he is appealing against and which he wants to be stayed. Nor can a party be stopped from exercising his constitutional right to appeal.”

(ii)      A.G. ANAMBRA STATE V. OKAFOR (1992) 2 NWLR (PT.224) 396 (P. 425, PARAS. G-H) (SC), where another appellate court also held as follows:

“It is an abuse of process for a respondent who knows that an appeal has been filed against a judgment in his favour and that a motion for stay of execution thereof is pending, to go ahead and execute the judgment until the motion for a stay of execution has been disposed of.”

Suffice to say that in this case, there was no Order of any court issued against the Receiver, whatsoever, till date.

IN CONCLUSION:

The Receiver urges all stakeholders of Niger Insurance, Plc, (Under Receivership) to exercise more patience with him and that he is committed to fulfilling his statutory obligations to them notwithstanding the aforesaid distractions he is confronted with, and shall in due course, continue with payment of claims to the policyholders after due verification, subject to availability of funds from sale of company’s assets, which he is vigorously pursuing.

Signed:

Otunba Sanya Ogunkuade, Esq.

(LL.B Hons, B.L, LL.D Hons)

Receiver & Liquidator,

Niger Insurance Plc

(Under Receivership)

2027: NES warns against sacrificing economic gains for politics

By Punch Punch 4d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

The Nigerian Economic Society has urged political parties, governments and other stakeholders to maintain fiscal discipline and policy continuity ahead of the 2

The President of the society, Dr Baba Yusuf Musa, made the call on Thursday at the 2026 National Growth and Development Forum, organised by Moneyline with Nancy on African Independent Television in Abuja.

The warning comes as Nigeria prepares for the 2027 general elections, a period traditionally associated with increased political spending, campaign-related expenditure and pressure on public finances.

Previous election cycles have raised concerns about the diversion of public resources to political activities, expansionary government spending and the risk of fiscal decisions being driven by short-term electoral considerations rather than economic priorities.

The PUNCH recently reported that members of the Central Bank of Nigeria’s Monetary Policy Committee warned that increased government spending ahead of the 2027 general elections could fuel inflation, weaken fiscal discipline and complicate efforts to maintain monetary stability.

Speaking on the theme, “The Nigeria We Want: The Economy, Your Money, Our Future,” Musa said political considerations must not override sound economic management as the country approaches another election cycle.

“As we approach the 2027 elections, I appeal, respectfully but earnestly, to all stakeholders, across every party and every tier of government, to protect these hard-won gains through fiscal discipline and policy continuity. Sound economics should be the common ground on which all who aspire to lead this nation can stand,” he said.

The NES president warned that Nigeria’s development challenges required disciplined public finance, credible institutions and consistent implementation of economic policies.

“Debt is a financing instrument, not a development strategy,” he declared.

He added, “And every naira of public spending should answer three questions: What problem will it solve? Who will benefit? And how will we know it worked?”

Musa acknowledged the Federal Government’s economic reforms, including petrol subsidy removal, foreign exchange market unification, bank recapitalisation and tax reforms, describing them as difficult but necessary decisions.

He cited the country’s 4.43 per cent economic growth in the second quarter of 2026, October inflation of 14.06 per cent, external reserves of approximately $55bn and the reduction in the monetary policy rate from 26.5 to 23 per cent as signs of improving macroeconomic conditions.

However, he cautioned that these gains had yet to translate into meaningful improvements in the living conditions of many Nigerians.

“Macroeconomic stability is necessary, but it is not yet shared prosperity. A stable exchange rate is not yet a full dinner table. Stability is the foundation; it is not the building,” Musa stated.

Citing World Bank estimates, he said approximately 63 per cent of Nigerians lived below the poverty line in 2025, while food inflation, weak industrial growth and widespread informal employment remained major concerns.

He identified five priorities for sustainable development, including job-creating growth, affordable credit, domestic production, investment in human capital and disciplined public finance.

Musa also proposed a national development compact involving government, businesses, universities, the media and citizens, supported by measurable indicators covering household incomes, employment, education, healthcare, electricity and poverty reduction.

“We must replace the politics of short-term applause with the economics of long-term results,” he said.

Also speaking, the Special Adviser to President Bola Tinubu on Economic Affairs, Tope Fasua, defended the administration’s reforms, arguing that sustainable improvements in household welfare depended on macroeconomic stability.

“If you don’t build a strong macro, you can’t go anywhere with the micro,” Fasua said.

He warned against excessive reliance on cash transfers and monetary financing, citing the previous administration’s Ways and Means advances.

The President of Capital Market Academics of Nigeria, Prof. Uche Uwaleke, similarly called for reforms that improve employment, purchasing power and poverty reduction.

He said the capital market had recorded year-to-date returns exceeding 60 per cent but stressed that investment opportunities must become more accessible to ordinary Nigerians.

The Managing Director of the Bank of Agriculture, Ayodeji Sotinrin, advocated affordable agricultural financing, mechanisation and improved seeds to raise productivity and strengthen food security.

Earlier, the forum’s convener and Executive Producer of Moneyline with Nancy, Nancy Illoh-Nnaji, emphasised the importance of financial literacy and public understanding of economic policies.

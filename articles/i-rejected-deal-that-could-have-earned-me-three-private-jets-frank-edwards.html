I rejected deal that could have earned me three private jets – Frank Edwards

By Punch Punch 3d ago 4 min read
MINDNEWSNG
Photo: Punch

Gospel singer Frank Edwards has revealed that he rejected an offer to remove the name of Jesus from his songs in exchange for international music distribution,

Edwards said the offer came shortly after he travelled to South Africa to shoot the music video for his song, ‘Beautiful’, at the early stage of his music career.

He disclosed this during a ministration at a concert, a video of which went viral on social media on Saturday where he recounted how some people offered to connect his music to a global audience on the condition that he replaced references to Jesus with God.

“I have also seen temptations that, if I had followed through, I probably would have had about three private jets by now.

“I’ve met people, I’ve seen people. Sir, I have been tempted. I have travelled, I have been tempted, and I’ve seen opportunities. These opportunities don’t look like you want to do evil. That’s why you have to be very careful,” he said.

The singer explained that the promoters argued that mentioning Jesus could offend some people and limit the reach of his music, while replacing the name with God would make his songs acceptable to a wider audience.

He recalled that they told him, “We can only distribute your music to a global audience if you remove Jesus and replace it with God because the name Jesus will offend many people. So, if you want to reach many people, you need to remove it and put God. It still means the same thing.”

Edwards said he initially struggled with the decision because he considered Jesus and God to be closely connected in the Christian faith, adding that the financial prospects attached to the offer were overwhelming.

He said he eventually became confused and decided to seek his mother’s advice.

According to him, his mother immediately rejected the proposal and instructed him to run away from the people making the offer.

“At some point, I really got confused because the offer was something else. Then, I called my mum.

“She didn’t even allow me to finish. My mum said, ‘We’ll go to school.’ When my mum calls you by your native name, just know that there’s a problem,” he recounted.

The singer said his mother, who was not physically with him in South Africa, instructed him to remove his slippers and run, a directive he obeyed.

He said the decision to heed her warning saved him from compromising his faith.

Edwards added that he later believed God promised to give him ten times what the promoters had offered if he refused to compromise.

“But when I did that, God said to me, ‘Because you did not fall for this temptation, whatever these people promised you, I’ll give you times ten of it,’” he said.

He further narrated how he eventually met a music personality who told him that he had all his songs on his phone and wanted to work with him.

According to Edwards, the encounter happened without him making any prior effort to contact the man.

“I met the man, and it was the man himself who told me, ‘Hey, I have all your songs on my phone.’ He said to me, ‘Anytime I see you, we have to do something together.’

“I said, ‘Eh? Me?’ And that was the beginning of it all,” he said.

The singer did not identify the promoters who made the initial offer during the ministration.

He also reflected on an earlier teaching from 2007, saying he had been told that a generation of gospel music ministers would emerge with an unconventional approach to music and face opposition but remain unstoppable.

“I said that a time is coming, you know, and by that time, I had released many albums. But God told me that a generation was coming that would be rugged, that would not understand religion. They were going to be crazy with their music. People would try to stop them, but they would be unstoppable,” he said.
